# Dusk Bestiary

## For the Host

### Using This Bestiary

These creatures are templates. Adjust their stats to match your party's level. Dusk entities should be threatening but not insurmountable. Their true horror is not their combat stats but what they represent: the world's accumulated wounds made flesh.

### Designing Dusk Encounters

Every Dusk encounter is a story. A fight against Dusk creatures is never only a fight. It is an encounter with something that was once alive, once loved, once ordinary. The Host should approach each encounter as a chance to reveal something about the empire's corruption, the nature of the Dusk, or the players themselves.

**Three Modes of Dusk Encounter**

Horror: The Dusk is at its most effective when it is not charging at the party but watching them. A Dusk Weeper that ignores the players entirely as it relives its death. A Crimson Gardener that does not defend itself but continues planting seeds even as it burns. The horror of the Dusk is that it does not care about the players. It has its own terrible purpose. Let the players witness something they cannot stop. Let them hear sounds from places where nothing should be making sound. Let them find evidence of the Dusk's work and realize it is already too late to prevent what is coming.

Tragedy: Every Dusk creature was something else first. The Tide Walker was a person with a name and a family and a reason to live. The Dusk Hound was a loyal companion or a protector. When the players fight Dusk creatures, they are fighting the remains of people and things the empire failed. Describe what the creature used to be. Let the players find clues to its former life. A child's toy in the lair of a Dusk Hound. An unfinished letter in a Tide Walker's pocket. A wedding band fused into the flesh of a Hollow Official. The question is not whether the players can kill the creature. The question is whether they can mourn it afterward.

Action: Sometimes the Dusk must be fought. When combat is inevitable, make it feel desperate. Dusk creatures do not tire. They do not negotiate. They flow and shift and press forward. Use the environment. Let the Dusk warp the battlefield during combat. Let the ground pulse with red veins. Let the sky weep crimson during the fight. Give the players something to protect beyond themselves. A person they must evacuate. A relic they must retrieve. A ritual they must complete while the Dusk throws everything at them.

**Scaling for Party Level**

Parties at Low Levels (Level 1 to 3): Single creatures or small groups of Wisps and Hounds. The Dusk is a mystery they are just beginning to understand. Encounters should be survivable but unsettling. The goal is not to kill the players but to make them afraid of what they do not yet understand.

Parties at Middle Levels (Level 4 to 6): Tide Walkers, Crimson Effigies, Tide Callers. The players have seen enough to know how dangerous the Dusk is. Encounters should test their resources and their resolve. Start introducing moral choices. A Tide Walker they might save. A Dusk Market they must decide whether to trade with.

Parties at High Levels (Level 7 to 8): Memory Thieves, The Unwritten. The Dusk is now a known threat with unknown depths. Encounters should cost the players something. A memory. A relationship. A piece of their humanity. Every victory should feel earned and expensive.

Endgame parties (Level 9): The Dusk Made Manifest. This is the final confrontation. Everything the players have learned, every bond they have forged, every truth they have uncovered prepares them for this moment. The encounter should draw on the campaign's specific history. Use details from the players' own actions. Let the Manifestation speak in the voices of people the players failed to save.

**Making Each Encounter Tell a Story**

Every Dusk creature has a history. Before the encounter, decide what that history is. Write one sentence about what the creature was before the Dusk took it. Write one sentence about how it was taken. Write one sentence about what it wants now (even if that want is incomprehensible). These three sentences are the creature's story. The players may never learn all of it, but the Host knowing it makes the encounter deeper.

When the players defeat a Dusk creature, describe its final moment. Does it dissolve into red mist? Does it collapse into ash? Does it, for just a moment, look like what it used to be before it fades? The death of a Dusk creature should feel like an ending. It should also feel like a question. What will become of the players when the Dusk finally takes them?

Let the players' actions shape the encounter. If they try to communicate with a Tide Walker instead of attacking, reward that choice. If they flee from a Crimson Effigy, let them escape but show them the consequences later. The Dusk does not punish players for being afraid. It proves them right.

### Dusk Wisp

A drifting remnant of a person consumed by the Dusk. It appears as a humanoid shape made of reddish light that bleeds at the edges. It whispers fragments of its former life.

**Stats:** HP 10, Defense 10, Might 6, Swiftness 14, Endurance 8, Intellect 4, Presence 6, Resolve 4
**Attack:** Crimson Touch (1d4 psychic, ignores armor)
**Special:** Memory Echo. When a Wisp attacks, the target briefly experiences one of the Wisp's memories: a fragment of the life it lost. Sanity check TN 12 or lose 1d4 Sanity.
**Encounter:** 2 to 6 Wisps. Use to introduce the Dusk's nature: it does not kill. It transforms.

**Read Aloud**

_The air grows cold. A shape coalesces from the red tinted darkness. It is roughly human but wrong in ways your mind struggles to define. Its edges blur like a watercolor left in rain. Light pulses through it in no rhythm you can recognize. You hear a voice but it does not come from the shape. It comes from inside your own head. It is saying a word you almost understand. It is saying a name. You are not sure if it is your name or someone else's. The shape reaches toward you and for a moment you see a different life. A kitchen. A fire. A child laughing. Then the shape is upon you and the warmth of that vision turns to pain._

**Behavioral Details**

Dusk Wisps do not run. They drift at walking pace, their feet never touching the ground. When not aware of living creatures, they circle slowly around the place where they died, repeating fragments of their final moments. They sometimes stop and tilt their head as though listening to something only they can hear. Wisps do not hunt. They react. If a living creature approaches, the Wisp becomes curious. It reaches out to touch. The touch is not intended as violence. It is an attempt to connect, to share, to make the living understand what it has become. The understanding is what damages the mind.

When two or more Wisps are together, they sometimes whisper to each other in voices that overlap and interlace. The whispers sound like conversation but the content is always the same memory repeated from different angles. No Wisp retains enough of itself to have a new thought.

**Story Seed**

A village near a Dusk zone has begun experiencing mass nightmares. Every villager dreams the same dream: a woman in red walking through their home, touching their belongings, whispering their children's names. The village healer believes it is a curse. It is not. It is a single Dusk Wisp, the remnant of a woman whose children were taken by inspectors. She died searching for them. She is still searching. Every villager's dream is her reaching out, mistaking their children for hers. The players must decide whether to destroy her, help her find peace, or uncover what really happened to her children.

### Dusk Hound

Once a dog, or a wolf, or a person who crawled on all fours until they forgot how to stand. A quadrupedal shape of sinew and crimson light. It hunts in packs. It remembers pack tactics from before it was changed.

**Stats:** HP 18, Defense 13, Might 14, Swiftness 16, Endurance 12, Intellect 6, Presence 8, Resolve 10
**Attack:** Bite (1d8 + Might), Claw (1d6 + Might, may attack twice if both attacks target different creatures)
**Special:** Pack Hunter. Advantage on attacks against targets adjacent to another Dusk Hound.
**Special:** Howl. Once per combat, all creatures in 3 Zones must make a Resolve check TN 14 or lose their next Movement.
**Encounter:** 3 to 5 Hounds. A serious combat threat for parties at low levels.

**Read Aloud**

_The sound reaches you before the shape does. It is a sound like wet leather being stretched over a drum. Then you see them. They move low to the ground, spines curving in ways that suggest too many joints or too few. Their skin is the color of old bruises with veins of red light pulsing beneath. They do not have eyes. Where eyes should be there are smooth depressions that somehow still track your movement. They do not pant. They do not growl. They are silent except for the sound of their claws on stone and earth and the wet leather sound of their bodies shifting. One of them opens what might be a mouth and the howl that comes out is not a wolf's howl and not a person's scream. It is something between the two and it vibrates in your chest like a second heartbeat._

**Behavioral Details**

Dusk Hounds circle their prey before engaging. They do not charge directly. They spread out, flank, and wait for an opening. When one Hound finds an advantage, the others move to support it instantly, as though connected by a shared mind. They do not eat what they kill. They have no need for food. They kill because the Dusk tells them to kill and they no longer have the will to refuse.

When not hunting, Dusk Hounds can be found standing still in groups, facing outward in a watch formation. They do not sleep. They do not tire. They stand guard over areas saturated with Dusk, waiting for anything living to cross their territory. Some Dusk Hounds retain a fragment of their former loyalty. A Hound that once guarded a home may still circle that home, keeping living things away not out of malice but out of the last echo of its protective instinct.

**Story Seed**

A Border Wall garrison has reported strange behavior from the Dusk Hounds in their sector. The Hounds are not attacking patrols. They are herding them. The Hounds appear to be driving travelers away from a specific area in the Dusk zone rather than toward it. Something in that area frightens even the Dusk Hounds. The garrison wants the players to investigate. What is the Hounds' former master, a person who was consumed while trying to protect something precious, still directing them even in death? Or is something worse than Hounds hunting in that territory, something the Hounds instinctively fear?

### Tide Walker

A humanoid figure that has been Dusk Touched but not fully consumed. Tide Walkers retain some intelligence and memory. They are tragic rather than evil. Some can be reasoned with. Others are too far gone.

**Stats:** HP 25, Defense 12, Might 12, Swiftness 10, Endurance 16, Intellect 12, Presence 14, Resolve 14
**Attack:** Warped Strike (1d8 + Might), Crimson Gaze (target makes Resolve TN 14 or is paralyzed for one round, once per combat)
**Special:** What Remains. Each Tide Walker retains one memory, one skill, or one personality trait from before. Roll d6: 1. A prayer they cannot stop reciting, 2. A loved one's name they repeat, 3. Professional skills (crafting, fighting, writing), 4. A grudge against a specific official, 5. Prophetic mutterings about the Dusk's nature, 6. Complete lucidity (they know what they have become and can speak).
**Encounter:** 1 Tide Walker as a social encounter, or 2 to 3 as combat. Some may be saved. Most cannot.

**Read Aloud**

_The figure stands at the edge of the Dusk haze. Its outline is human. It is wearing clothes that might have been fine once but are now stained and torn and wrong. Its skin has a reddish undertone like a sunburn that never healed. Its eyes are the worst part. They are still human eyes. They still hold recognition. They hold pain and confusion and a desperate grasping at something the figure can no longer quite remember. It opens its mouth and a voice comes out that cracks between registers. It says something. A word. A name. Please. It repeats the word like a prayer it is afraid to forget. Then its face shifts and the human thing retreats and something else looks out through those eyes. Something that does not remember names._

**Behavioral Details**

Tide Walkers oscillate between lucidity and Dusk influence. In their lucid moments, they can speak normally, remember their past, and even express gratitude or fear. In their Dusk influenced moments, they become hostile, confused, or catatonic. The transition is unpredictable. A Tide Walker may be weeping and apologetic one moment and attacking the next.

They often touch their own faces repeatedly, as though trying to confirm they still have human features. They talk to people who are not there, particularly people from their past. A Tide Walker may ask about a specific person by name, not understanding that years have passed. They collect small objects that remind them of their former lives. A dried flower. A broken comb. A child's shoe. These objects are precious to them in a way that transcends understanding.

**Story Seed**

A Tide Walker has been appearing at the edge of a village every night. She does not attack. She leaves small offerings at the village gate. Vegetables. Handmade bandages. A child's toy repaired with string. The villagers are terrified. The local magistrate wants her destroyed. But a child in the village claims the Tide Walker is her mother. The mother disappeared into a Dusk zone three years ago. She is still trying to take care of her family. She does not understand that they are afraid of her. She does not understand what she has become. She only knows she must bring gifts to the gate every night because that is what mothers do.

### Crimson Effigy

A stationary corruption: a tree, a statue, a section of wall that has become a locus of Dusk energy. It pulses with red light. It warps the environment around it. Destroying it cleanses the immediate area, though the corruption will return unless the source is addressed.

**Stats:** HP 40, Defense 8 (immobile), Might 16, Swiftness 0, Endurance 20, Intellect 14, Presence 18, Resolve 18
**Attack:** Pulse (all creatures in Zone take 1d6 psychic, once per round), Summon Wisps (as a reaction to taking damage, summons 1d4 Dusk Wisps, up to 3 times)
**Special:** Reality Warp. The Zone containing the Effigy has altered physics. Roll d4: 1. Gravity is doubled (Movement costs 2 actions), 2. Time is slowed (Initiative reversed each round), 3. Space is folded (ranged attacks have Disadvantage), 4. Memories bleed (all creatures in the Zone experience each other's significant memories, Sanity TN 16 or 1d6 Sanity loss).
**Encounter:** 1 Effigy plus its summoned Wisps. A boss encounter.

**Read Aloud**

_The air thickens as you approach. Each breath feels like you are pulling it through cloth. There is a light ahead, pulsing slowly, rhythmically, like a heartbeat made visible. You see it now. It was once a tree. Or perhaps a statue. It is difficult to tell because the form has been subsumed by something else. Red crystalline growths cover its surface, branching outward in patterns that mimic veins and arteries and roots. The ground around it crawls with red lines that spread outward like cracks in drying mud. The pulse is not just visual. You feel it in your teeth. In your bones. In the spaces behind your eyes. The Effigy is not dead. It is waiting. It is dreaming. And its dreams are bleeding into the world around it._

**Behavioral Details**

A Crimson Effigy is never alone. It does not move but the area around it shifts constantly. The red lines on the ground pulse and spread, slowly converting the environment into something that belongs to the Dusk. Grass blackens. Water turns red. Stone becomes porous and warm to the touch. The Effigy influences the minds of those who remain near it too long. They begin to feel that the pulse is natural. They begin to feel that the red light is warm, comfortable, right. Characters who spend more than one hour within 3 Zones of an Effigy must make Resolve TN 14 or begin to resist leaving the area.

Effigies form at sites of concentrated trauma. A battlefield. A prison. A hospital. A school where something terrible happened. The Effigy feeds on the memory of that trauma. Destroying the Effigy without addressing the underlying trauma means another Effigy will eventually grow in the same place. To permanently cleanse an Effigy site, the players must understand what happened there and perform an act of commemoration or justice.

**Story Seed**

A Crimson Effigy has grown in the central square of a town that was devastated by a Dusk surge three years ago. The townspeople have not returned. But every night, the Effigy projects images of the town as it was. Children playing. Markets open. People laughing. The images are beautiful. Travelers who see them have begun to settle in the ruins, drawn by the vision of a thriving community. They do not realize they are being drawn by the Effigy, which is feeding on their hope. The players must decide whether to destroy the beautiful lie or let people find comfort in a false memory.

### The Dusk Made Manifest

_For endgame encounters only._

A concentration of Dusk energy that has achieved a terrible consciousness. It speaks in the voices of everyone it has consumed. It remembers everything the empire has tried to forget.

**Stats:** HP 80, Defense 16, Might 20, Swiftness 16, Endurance 22, Intellect 18, Presence 20, Resolve 20
**Attack:** Manifest Strike (2d10 + Might, reaches across 2 Zones), Memory Eater (target must make Resolve TN 18 or permanently lose one significant memory. The Manifestation gains 10 HP above its normal maximum. This extra HP is lost first.)
**Special:** Legion Voice. The Manifestation knows everything known by anyone it has consumed. It will reveal secrets. It will use the players' own truths against them. The Host should prepare specific revelations for this encounter.
**Special:** Unmaking. Once per round, as a reaction to being attacked, the Manifestation may attempt to unmake the attacker's weapon or armor. The item makes a "Resolve check" (d20 + its quality modifier) against TN 16 or is destroyed.
**Encounter:** The campaign's final battle, or close to it. This is not just a combat. It is the moment when everything the players have learned confronts them in physical form.

**Read Aloud**

_The sky does not darken. It reddens. The air grows thick and warm and wet like standing inside a living throat. The Manifestation does not appear. It assembles. Pieces of the Dusk's red light gather from everywhere at once, drawn together like iron filings to a magnet. It takes a shape. The shape is never the same twice. It might be a giant humanoid figure with too many limbs. It might be a sphere of pulsing light with faces pressing against its surface from the inside. It might be a mirror that shows the players not their reflections but their worst fears rendered in perfect detail. Then it speaks. A hundred voices speak at once in a choir of the dead and the forgotten and the consumed. They all say different things but the meaning is singular. You cannot win. You are already part of me. You always were._

**Behavioral Details**

The Dusk Made Manifest does not need to move. It is everywhere in its domain. It speaks from all directions. It attacks from any angle. It watches through every surface that reflects light. Its presence warps reality constantly. Time becomes unreliable. Distance becomes meaningless. The players may find themselves standing next to an ally one moment and isolated on an empty plain the next.

The Manifestation enjoys the fight. It is not desperate. It is not afraid. It has waited for this moment. It wants to savor the breaking of hope. It uses its victims' memories against them with surgical precision. It impersonates loved ones. It creates illusions of places the players remember as safe. It promises things it cannot deliver and things it can.

**Story Seed**

The Dusk Made Manifest cannot be created by the Dusk alone. It requires an invitation. Somewhere, in the campaign's history, someone knowingly or unknowingly opened a door for the Manifestation to enter the world. The final encounter is not just about defeating the Manifestation. It is about discovering who invited it and why. The Host should tie this invitation to one of the players' backstories, one of the Twelve Figures, or a choice the players made early in the campaign that seemed small at the time. The Manifestation is the consequence of that choice made flesh. Defeating it requires more than combat. It requires the players to confront the choice that brought it here.

### Dusk Weeper

Not all Dusk entities are hostile. Some are simply trapped. The Dusk Weeper is a spirit locked in an eternal reenactment of its own death. It does not attack. It does not react. It repeats its final moments on a loop, unaware of anything outside its own suffering.

**Stats:** HP cannot be reduced by normal means, Defense 0, Might 0, Swiftness 0, Endurance not applicable, Intellect not applicable, Presence 18, Resolve not applicable
**Attack:** None. The Weeper does not attack.
**Special:** Endless Dying. The Weeper relives its death every 10 to 15 minutes. All creatures who witness the full cycle must make Resolve TN 16 or gain 1 Sanity loss. Each subsequent viewing increases the TN by 2.
**Special:** Residual Sorrow. The area around a Weeper is saturated with grief. Characters within 2 Zones have Disadvantage on checks involving joy, hope, or comfort. Emotional effects that create positive emotions cost double the normal Qi or resources.
**Special:** Atonement. If the players can understand what injustice caused the Weeper's death and perform an act of justice in its name, the Weeper dissolves peacefully. This grants a permanent +1 Ritual Harmony for the party or one significant story advancement.
**Encounter:** 1 Weeper. A puzzle and a moral challenge rather than a combat. The players must decide whether to endure the Weeper's suffering, find a way to free it, or leave it to its eternal cycle.

**Read Aloud**

_You hear the sound before you see the source. It is not a scream. It is something worse. It is the sound of someone trying to scream and having no breath left to do it with. The Weeper hangs in the air at about waist height, a translucent figure that flickers like a candle in a draft. Its face contorts through the same expressions over and over. Shock. Fear. Pleading. Acceptance. Then it starts again. It does not look at you. It has not noticed a living person in decades. Perhaps centuries. It is too consumed by its own ending to perceive anything else. The air around it is cold and heavy and smells of wet stone and old copper. You feel an overwhelming urge to look away. You also feel an overwhelming urge to help. Both urges are correct._

**Behavioral Details**

The Weeper moves through an exact sequence of motions that correspond to its death. It may reach out as though grasping for help. It may fall to its knees. It may cover its face. The motions repeat with perfect precision. Nothing in the environment alters them. If interrupted, the Weeper simply resets to the beginning of its sequence without acknowledging the interruption.

The area around a Weeper is unnaturally cold. Breath fogs. Skin crawls. Small animals avoid the area. Plants near the Weeper grow twisted and pale, reaching away from the Weeper's location as though trying to escape. The Weeper does not speak but those with sensitivity to Qi may hear a faint repeating whisper: the last words the Weeper said before dying.

**Story Seed**

A Dusk Weeper has appeared in a noble's mansion, looping through its death in the grand hallway. The noble family is terrified and has sealed that wing of the house. They have hired exorcists, philosophers, and scholars but none can banish the Weeper. The players are summoned for help. The Weeper is the spirit of a servant who was killed to keep a secret. The secret is something the noble family has spent generations hiding. If the players uncover the truth and expose it, the Weeper will finally find peace. But exposing the secret will destroy the noble family's standing and may have political repercussions.

### Crimson Gardener

A Crimson Gardener is a Dusk entity that does not hunt living creatures. It cultivates. It plants seeds of Dusk corruption in living soil, spreading the blight deliberately and methodically. It is patient. It has plans that span years.

**Stats:** HP 30, Defense 14, Might 10, Swiftness 12, Endurance 18, Intellect 16, Presence 12, Resolve 16
**Attack:** Sowing Touch (1d6 + Might, and plants a Dusk seed in the target. The seed grows over 1d4 days, dealing 1 damage per day until removed.)
**Special:** Tend the Garden. The Gardener can accelerate the growth of any Dusk corruption within 3 Zones. An Effigy that would take months to form can be grown in days with a Gardener's attention.
**Special:** Dusk Bloom. Once per day, the Gardener can cause all Dusk seeds within 3 Zones to bloom simultaneously. Each seed deals 1d6 damage to the creature carrying it and creates a Dusk Wisp at that location.
**Special:** Not Worth Fighting. The Gardener does not prioritize its own survival. If threatened, it attempts to flee and continue its work elsewhere. Fighting it is a distraction from its true purpose.
**Encounter:** 1 Gardener and its corrupted garden. A chase and investigation encounter. The Gardener is not the real threat. The garden it is growing is the threat.

**Read Aloud**

_Among the twisted red landscape, one figure moves with purpose. It is humanoid but too thin, limbs too long, fingers too many. It moves slowly, deliberately, stopping to touch the ground or a plant or a stone. Where it touches, red lines spread like roots. It is dressed in tattered robes that might once have been gardener's clothes. A hat with a wide brim shades a face that is featureless except for a mouth that moves constantly, silently, as though reciting instructions to itself. It carries a watering can. The watering can contains something dark and thick that drips with a sound like blood hitting dry earth. It is tending to something. You realize with a chill that what it is tending to is the ground beneath your feet._

**Behavioral Details**

The Crimson Gardener ignores creatures that do not interfere with its work. It will walk past a party of armed adventurers to water a patch of corrupted soil. It does not speak but it hums. The tune is distorted and wrong, missing notes that should be there and including notes that should not exist. It follows a specific route through its territory, visiting each of its plantings in order. Observant players can predict its movements and avoid it or ambush it.

Gardeners collect things. Seeds. Stones. Bones. They store these in a central location, their nursery. A Gardener's nursery is a site of concentrated Dusk growth, filled with Effigies that have only partly formed and corrupted plant life. The nursery is where the Gardener is most dangerous and most vulnerable.

**Story Seed**

A farming community has noticed their crops changing. The wheat grows red. The vegetables taste of copper. The soil has begun to pulse with a faint warmth. A Crimson Gardener has taken up residence in the abandoned farm at the edge of town. It is slowly converting the entire agricultural region into a Dusk nursery. If it succeeds, the food supply for three provinces will be corrupted. The players must find the Gardener's nursery and destroy its stockpile before the next planting season. But the Gardener has been watching the farmers. It knows their routines. It has planted seeds in their fields, in their homes, in their bodies as they slept.

### Memory Thief

A stealth predator that has learned to feed on memories rather than flesh. The Memory Thief does not kill its victims. It erases them piece by piece until nothing remains but a breathing shell.

**Stats:** HP 22, Defense 16, Might 8, Swiftness 18, Endurance 10, Intellect 14, Presence 16, Resolve 12
**Attack:** Claw (1d6 + Swiftness)
**Special:** Memory Drain. The Thief touches a sleeping or willing target and draws out a specific memory. The target permanently loses that memory. The Thief gains knowledge of that memory and may use it against the target or others.
**Special:** Stolen Faces. The Thief can impersonate anyone whose memory it has consumed. For 1 hour, it gains the appearance, voice, and mannerisms of that person. Those who knew the person well may make Intellect TN 18 to notice something wrong.
**Special:** Evasive. The Thief has Advantage on checks to hide, flee, or avoid detection. It does not fight to the death. It flees to hunt another night.
**Encounter:** 1 Memory Thief. A stealth and investigation encounter. The Thief avoids direct confrontation. Finding it requires tracking its victims and understanding what memories it has taken.

**Read Aloud**

_Sleep does not come easily in Dusk territory. When it finally does, it is fragile and thin. You dream of nothing. A void. An absence. Then you feel a presence in the void. It is not hostile in the way a predator is hostile. It is curious in the way a scholar is curious. Something is examining you. It is sifting through your mind, handling your memories like objects on a shelf. It pauses at one. A birthday. A first kiss. A parent's face. It takes that memory and folds it carefully and places it somewhere you cannot reach. You try to hold onto it but it slips away like water through fingers. When you wake, you know something is missing. You do not know what. You only know there is a space inside you where something warm used to be._

**Behavioral Details**

The Memory Thief is active only at night. During the day, it hides in dark spaces, clutching its stolen memories like a miser clutching coins. It does not eat or drink. It sustains itself entirely on the memories it consumes. A Memory Thief that goes too long without feeding becomes desperate and reckless, taking memories from waking victims regardless of the risk.

Memory Thieves have lairs where they store their collections. A lair contains physical objects connected to stolen memories. A child's toy. A love letter. A dried flower. The Thief does not understand these objects as sentimental. It understands them as labels. It revisits its favorite memories the way a person rereads a beloved book. The Thief can be tracked by tracing the trail of people who have forgotten something important.

**Story Seed**

An old scholar arrives at the players' camp in a panic. He cannot remember his wife's face. He remembers her name. He remembers their wedding. He remembers her death. But her face is gone, replaced by a smooth blankness. He knows a Memory Thief took it. He has been tracking the creature for weeks and has narrowed its lair to a specific region. He wants the players to help him recover his wife's face. But the Memory Thief has been active for years. It has stolen hundreds of memories. The scholar's memory may already be lost, consumed, used up. The real question is what the scholar will do if he cannot get his memory back. He has a blade. He has a purpose. He has said goodbye to his children.

### Tide Caller

A Dusk Touched human who has learned to command the Dusk rather than be consumed by it. Tide Callers are rare and terrifying. They have traded something essential for their power. They are bridges between the living world and the Dusk.

**Stats:** HP 28, Defense 12, Might 10, Swiftness 12, Endurance 14, Intellect 16, Presence 18, Resolve 16
**Attack:** Dusk Lash (1d8 + Presence, reaches 2 Zones), Fragment Storm (2d4, targets all creatures in one Zone, recharge 4 to 6 rounds)
**Special:** Summon Dusk Entities. The Tide Caller can summon 1d4 Wisps or 1 Hound per round, up to a total of 6 summoned creatures at once. Maintaining control requires concentration.
**Special:** Dusk Shield. Once per combat, the Tide Caller can surround itself with a barrier of Dusk energy that absorbs 10 damage. While the shield is active, the Tide Caller cannot summon.
**Special:** What Was Traded. Every Tide Caller sacrificed something to gain their power. Roll d6: 1. Their name (they no longer respond to any name), 2. Their voice (they communicate through their summoned creatures), 3. Their ability to feel warmth (they are always cold), 4. A specific memory (they do not remember who they were before), 5. Their shadow (they cast no shadow and are uncomfortable in bright light), 6. A loved one (the person vanished the day the Tide Caller made their pact).
**Encounter:** 1 Tide Caller plus summoned creatures. A tactical combat. The Tide Caller is the priority target but is well protected by its summoned minions.

**Read Aloud**

_The figure stands apart from the Dusk corruption, untouched by the red light that surrounds them. They are not a creature of the Dusk. They are a person. A person who has made a choice. Their eyes are human but wrong in their clarity. They see something you cannot see. They are speaking in a language that sounds like no language you have ever heard. The Dusk responds. It flows toward them like a tide answering the moon. Shapes form in the red haze behind them, taking the outlines of creatures that should not exist. The figure does not look afraid. They look satisfied. They made their peace with what they are a long time ago. The question is whether you can make peace with what they have become._

**Behavioral Details**

Tide Callers are always in conversation. They speak to the Dusk and the Dusk speaks back. To an observer, this appears as muttering, long pauses while the Caller listens to silence, and sudden shifts in mood based on what the Dusk tells them. They are never truly alone. Even when no summoned creatures are visible, the Dusk is with them, coiled around them, ready to respond.

They have a distinctive physical marker of their pact. A pattern of red lines on their skin, always present, always pulsing. They cannot hide what they are. Tide Callers are outcasts by necessity. They live at the edges of society, in zones contaminated by the Dusk where their power is strongest. Some Tide Callers believe they are using the Dusk. The Dusk knows better.

**Story Seed**

A Tide Caller has approached a Border Wall garrison with an offer. They will control the Dusk creatures in the region, keeping them away from the wall, in exchange for food, shelter, and isolation. The garrison commander has accepted. The deal has held for six months. But the Tide Caller's control is slipping. They are making mistakes. Summoning creatures they cannot dismiss. Talking to presences that the garrison's officers who sense Qi can feel but cannot name. The garrison wants the players to assess the Tide Caller and determine if they are a threat. The Tide Caller knows they are losing control. They want the players to help them sever their connection to the Dusk before it is too late. But severing the connection may kill them.

### Hollow Official

A bureaucrat who was consumed by the Dusk in the middle of a task. They do not know they are dead. They continue processing paperwork, issuing permits, and enforcing regulations in the Dusk zone where they fell. Their work means nothing. They do not know that either.

**Stats:** HP 20, Defense 10, Might 8, Swiftness 8, Endurance 16, Intellect 14, Presence 10, Resolve 20
**Attack:** Stamp of Authority (1d4 + Intellect, against Resolve)
**Special:** By the Book. The Hollow Official cannot be reasoned with through emotion or plea. Only official documentation affects its behavior. If presented with the correct form, properly filled and stamped, the Official will follow the instructions on that form without question.
**Special:** Red Tape. The Official produces endless paperwork as a defensive measure. Anyone attempting to interact with it must first fill out a form (Intellect TN 14 to complete correctly, takes one full round). Incorrect forms are rejected and the Official becomes hostile.
**Special:** Eternal Bureaucracy. The Official cannot be killed by normal means while it believes it has work to do. Only completing its task or making it understand its own death can end it.
**Encounter:** 1 Hollow Official and its office. A puzzle encounter. Violence is ineffective. The players must understand the Official's task and find a way to complete it or subvert it.

**Read Aloud**

_In the middle of a ruin corrupted by Dusk, there is a desk. The desk is clean. The desk is organized. Behind the desk sits a person in imperial robes that should have rotted years ago but are somehow still pristine. The person is writing. Their hand moves steadily across the page, filling line after line with perfect calligraphy. They do not look up when you enter. They do not acknowledge your presence at all until you step past a certain invisible boundary. Then they speak without raising their head. Their voice is flat. Their words are the same words they must have spoken a thousand times. Do you have an appointment? Do you have the proper forms? This office is for authorized personnel only. Please present your documentation or vacate the premises. The person still has not looked at you. You are not sure they have eyes anymore._

**Behavioral Details**

The Hollow Official maintains its office with obsessive precision. It dusts the desk. It organizes the papers. It sharpens its brushes. It does all of this in a ruined building with a collapsed roof, surrounded by Dusk corruption, while red light pulses through the gaps in the walls. It does not notice any of this.

The Official has a specific task it believes it is completing. Processing citizenship claims. Reviewing trade licenses. Auditing military expenditures. The task no longer has any connection to reality but the Official does not know this. It responds to any interruption according to the protocols it remembers. If someone presents a correctly filled imperial form, the Official must process it. This is both its weakness and its purpose.

**Story Seed**

A Hollow Official sits in the ruins of a provincial records office, still processing the citizenship claims of a village that was consumed by the Dusk thirty years ago. The claims are for people who are dead or transformed. But the Official does not know this. It is issuing citizenship papers for ghosts. A young woman from a nearby settlement has discovered that her grandmother's citizenship claim was never processed. She needs the papers to inherit property. She begs the players to retrieve the documents from the Hollow Official's office. The players must navigate the ruined building, deal with the Official's protocols, and find the specific claim among centuries of paperwork. But the Official will not release the claim without proper authorization. And the only person who could authorize it has been dead for thirty years.

### Red Rain

Red Rain is not a creature. It is a weather phenomenon that occurs when Dusk contamination reaches a critical density in the atmosphere. It falls across entire regions, not just Dusk zones. It does not discriminate.

**Effects:** Red Rain falls as a warm, viscous fluid that looks like blood but is thinner and colder. It carries Dusk essence. Anything it touches, it slowly corrupts.

**Mechanical Effects:**

- Exposure: Creatures caught in Red Rain must make Endurance TN 14 every hour or gain one stage of Red Rain Exposure. These stages are cumulative and apply only to this hazard.
- Red Rain Exposure Stage 1: Mild nausea and headache. Disadvantage on concentration checks.
- Red Rain Exposure Stage 2: Skin reddens and becomes sensitive to light. Sanity checks suffer Disadvantage.
- Red Rain Exposure Stage 3: Hallucinations begin. The affected creature sees Dusk entities that may or may not be real.
- Red Rain Exposure Stage 4: The creature begins to hear the Dusk's voice. It must make Resolve TN 16 to resist following the voice's instructions.
- Red Rain Exposure Stage 5: Transformation begins. The creature becomes a Tide Walker over 1d4 days unless treated with intensive Qi therapy.
- Treatment: Removing each stage of Red Rain Exposure requires a successful Intellect TN 16 check and 24 hours of rest. Qi Techniques that purify can accelerate this process.

**Environmental Effects:**

- Red Rain accelerates the growth of Dusk flora. Plants touched by the rain develop red veins and sharp thorns within hours.
- Structures exposed to Red Rain for more than a day begin to pulse with faint red light. They become slightly warm to the touch.
- Water sources contaminated by Red Rain become undrinkable. Animals that drink from contaminated sources show signs of Red Rain Exposure within days.
- The rain is persistent. It can last for hours or days. During a Red Rain event, the sky remains red even at night.

**Read Aloud**

_The first drop lands on your cheek. It is warm. Unnaturally warm. You wipe it away and your fingers come away red. You look up. The clouds have turned the color of old bruises. The rain is falling in sheets now, heavy and thick, each drop striking the ground with a sound like meat hitting a butcher's block. It does not smell like rain. It smells like copper and earth and something rotting. The world turns red. The horizon disappears. You cannot see more than a few feet in any direction. The rain gets into your mouth. It tastes like coins and regret. You need to find shelter. You need to find it now._

**Story Seed**

A Red Rain event has begun in a major city. It is the first time Red Rain has fallen outside a Dusk zone. The city is in chaos. People are panicking. Commission investigators are trying to maintain order but they are also afraid. The players must navigate the panicked streets, find shelter for civilians, and determine the source of this unprecedented event. Is the Dusk spreading faster than anyone predicted? Or has someone deliberately drawn the Red Rain to the city to cause chaos, to cover a crime, to send a message?

### Dusk Market

The Dusk Market appears without warning in areas saturated with Dusk. It is a gathering of vendors, stalls, and customers who have been changed by the Dusk. They sell things that should not exist. They accept payment in things that should not be spent.

**Nature:** The Dusk Market possesses a dim awareness. It wants to trade. It does not care about morality, law, or consequence. It cares about exchange. It appears when there are enough Dusk Touched beings in one area to form a community. It disappears when the sun rises or when the last customer leaves.

**What the Market Sells (roll d10):**

1. A memory in a bottle. Drinking it grants the memory for 24 hours. The memory is always someone else's and always important.
2. A key that opens any lock. It breaks after one use. The lock it opens is never the lock you intended.
3. A flower that never wilts. It drains the life from whoever keeps it. One year of life per month of bloom.
4. A mirror that shows you as others see you. Looking into it requires Resolve TN 14 or 1 Sanity loss.
5. A map of a place that does not exist. Following the map leads to a place that did not exist before you followed the map.
6. A name written on a piece of paper. Speaking the name aloud summons the person who owns it. They can refuse the summons once. After that, they must come.
7. A jar of silence. Opening it creates a zone of absolute silence for one hour. In that zone, no sound exists. Not your heartbeat. Not your breath.
8. A strand of hair from a dead god. It can be used to empower one Qi Technique to its maximum effect. Using it destroys the strand and draws the attention of something that noticed the god's death.
9. A contract written in blood. Signing it grants one wish. The contract's terms are binding. The wish will be fulfilled in a way that is technically correct and emotionally devastating.
10. A candle that burns with red flame. While it burns, Dusk creatures will not approach you. When it goes out, they will remember that you kept them away.

**Payment the Market Accepts (roll d6):**

1. A year of your life. The vendor reaches into you and pulls out a year of your lifespan. You feel older.
2. A secret that matters. You must tell the vendor a secret that would damage you if revealed. The vendor knows if you lie.
3. A cherished object. The vendor takes an object you value. You will forget you ever owned it.
4. A promise. The vendor names a future service. You are bound to perform it. The vendor will collect when the time is right.
5. A sense. The vendor takes your sight, hearing, or sense of smell for one month. You choose which.
6. A name. The vendor takes your name for one day. During that day, no one who knows you will recognize you.

**Read Aloud**

_The haze parts and you see it. A collection of stalls and tents and awnings arranged in no particular pattern. Lanterns glow with red light. Figures move among the stalls, browsing, haggling, examining wares. At first glance it looks like any market. Then you notice the details. A vendor with no face arranging jars filled with smoke. A customer paying with something that glows and squirms. A stall selling children's shoes made of a material that might be leather and might be skin. No one looks at you. They all know you are here. They are waiting for you to approach. They are waiting for you to want something. The market can always provide what you want. The question is whether you are willing to pay the price._

**Behavioral Details**

The vendors of the Dusk Market do not remember their former lives completely. They know they sell. They know they trade. They may remember what they sold before the Dusk but the memory is distant and unimportant. They are polite in a hollow way. They smile with too many teeth or too few. They do not touch customers unless a transaction requires it.

The Market has rules. No violence. No theft. No attempts to take without offering something in return. Breaking the rules results in the Market expelling the offender. Expulsion means being deposited outside the Market's boundaries, often miles from where the Market was entered, often with a small additional price extracted as punishment.

**Story Seed**

A player character's lost loved one appears in the Dusk Market. They are not a vendor or a customer. They are an item for sale. They stand in a cage, looking out with eyes that recognize the player but cannot speak. The vendor explains that this person was collected from the Dusk's edge, preserved, kept pristine. The price is steep. The player's most significant bond. Their relationship with one of the Twelve Figures. Their place in a school. Their reputation. The vendor will take that bond and erase it, leaving the player and their loved one free but disconnected from everything that made them who they are. The other players must watch and decide whether to let their comrade pay that price.

### The Unwritten

The Unwritten exists in the space between what the official records say happened and what actually happened. It is the empire's lies made manifest. It haunts historians, archivists, and anyone who knows the truth and has been forced to forget it.

**Stats:** HP 35, Defense variable (see special), Might not applicable, Swiftness not applicable, Endurance not applicable, Intellect 18, Presence 20, Resolve not applicable
**Attack:** None. The Unwritten does not attack directly.
**Special:** Cannot Be Fought. The Unwritten has no physical stats because it is not physical. Attacks pass through it. It cannot be harmed by weapons, Qi Techniques, or any form of direct violence.
**Special:** Truth Cost. The Unwritten can be harmed only by speaking a truth that the empire has suppressed. Each truth told deals 1d10 psychic damage to the Unwritten and costs the speaker 1 Sanity (the truth hurts to speak).
**Special:** Rewrite. The Unwritten can reach into a character's mind and alter one of their memories, replacing a true memory with the official record version. The target must make Resolve TN 18 to resist. If they fail, they believe the false memory until someone presents irrefutable evidence of the truth.
**Special:** Memory Echo. When the Unwritten is harmed by a truth, all creatures within 2 Zones experience a vision of the event the truth describes. The vision is vivid and complete. It cannot be forgotten.
**Encounter:** 1 Unwritten. A philosophical and psychological encounter. Violence is useless. The players must use what they know, at the cost of their own peace, to destroy it.

**Read Aloud**

_The air in this place is too clean. Too still. There are no Dusk marks here, no red veins, no pulsing corruption. This space has been scrubbed. Something does not want to leave traces. Then you see it. It is not a shape. It is an absence of shape. A space where the air is slightly wrong, where light bends around something that should not be there. When you look directly at it, you see nothing. When you look away, you see it in your peripheral vision. A figure made of erased text. A face that is a palimpsest of corrections and official revisions. It has no mouth but you hear it clearly. It speaks in the voice of every history book you ever read, every official announcement you ever heard, every lie you were told as a child. It says: That is not what happened. That is not what the records say. You must be mistaken. Let me show you the correct version._

**Behavioral Details**

The Unwritten is drawn to places where history has been altered. Archives where documents were replaced. Schools where revised textbooks are used. Courtrooms where testimony was changed. It does not destroy evidence of the truth. It overwrites it. It prefers to work slowly, replacing truth with official fiction one person at a time.

It can appear anywhere but is most powerful in imperial administrative centers. The closer to the heart of the empire's bureaucracy, the stronger the Unwritten becomes. In the Imperial Archives, the Unwritten is nearly invincible. In a remote village where oral tradition still matters, the Unwritten is weak.

**Story Seed**

A scholar has been found dead in the Imperial Archives. The cause of death is unknown. There are no wounds. No signs of struggle. But the scholar's research notes are covered in a single phrase written over and over: It found me. It found me. It found me. The players are asked to investigate. They discover the scholar was researching a historical event that does not appear in any official record. A massacre. A betrayal. A truth the empire buried. The scholar found evidence of the real event. The Unwritten found the scholar. Now the players must decide whether to continue the research and risk the same fate, or let the truth stay buried.

### Dusk Touched NPCs

Not every person touched by the Dusk becomes a creature. Some remain human enough to speak, to feel, to ask for help. These Dusk Touched individuals are among the most powerful tools a Host has for creating emotional connection to the campaign's themes. They represent what the players are fighting against and what they might become.

**Creating a Dusk Touched NPC**

A Dusk Touched NPC has three defining elements:

The Mark: Every Dusk Touched person bears a physical sign of their exposure. Red veins in the eyes. A patch of skin that has taken on a crimson hue. Fingernails that grow red and thick. Hair that has begun to change color. The mark is always visible if one looks closely. Many Dusk Touched try to hide their mark.

The Price: Every Dusk Touched person has lost something to the Dusk. A memory. A relationship. The ability to feel a specific emotion. A physical sensation (they can no longer taste, or feel warmth, or smell). The price is what they paid for surviving. They do not speak of it willingly.

The Need: Every Dusk Touched person wants something they cannot achieve alone. Some want cleansing. Some want revenge against the people who exposed them. Some want to understand what is happening to them. Some want to spread the Dusk further, believing that if everyone is touched, no one will be an outcast. The NPC's need determines whether they are an ally, a neutral contact, or a future antagonist.

**Example NPC 1: Lin the Preserver**

Lin was an archivist in the middle ranks of the Imperial Library when the Dusk reached her district. She barricaded herself in the rare documents room for eleven days, surviving on water from the building's pipes and the desperation of a woman who refused to die. The Dusk did not consume her. It passed through her. She emerged changed.

The Mark: Lin's left eye has turned permanently red. The iris is the color of dried blood. She wears an eye patch and claims she lost the eye to infection. Those who look beneath the patch see the red eye watching them with unsettling awareness.

The Price: Lin can no longer read. The words on every page shift and rearrange themselves when she looks at them. For an archivist, this is a fate worse than death. She has not told anyone. She spends her days in the library, touching the books she can no longer read, pretending to work.

The Need: Lin knows the library contains a document about Dusk cleansing. She cataloged it herself years ago. She cannot find it because she cannot read the labels. She needs someone to find the document for her, read it to her, help her understand if there is a way to reverse what happened. She is terrified and desperate and trying very hard to appear calm.

**Example NPC 2: The Cartographer's Son**

His father was a cartographer for the Imperial Survey Corps. His father mapped the Dusk's advance for ten years. His father began bringing the Dusk home in small ways. Red dust on his clothes. A crimson tint to his dreams. His father succumbed three years ago. The son inherited the maps, the notes, and the growing red stain on his own hands.

The Mark: The Cartographer's Son has red lines spreading from his fingernails up toward his knuckles. He keeps his hands gloved at all times. The lines grow longer each week. He estimates he has six months before they reach his heart.

The Price: The Cartographer's Son can no longer dream. When he sleeps, he experiences nothing. No images. No sounds. No sensations. Just a void. He has not had a dream in two years. He would give anything for a nightmare. At least a nightmare would be something.

The Need: His father's notes contain a map of a place the Dusk has not reached. A sanctuary. The Cartographer's Son believes that if he can reach this place, the Dusk's advance in his body will stop. He needs an escort through Dusk territory. He cannot pay in money. He can pay in knowledge. His father's notes contain information about Dusk movements, creature behaviors, and the locations of four unrecorded Crimson Effigies.

**Example NPC 3: The Offering**

She does not remember her name. She does not remember where she came from. She woke up in a Dusk zone three years ago with no memories before that moment. She was not consumed. She was created. The Dusk made her as a vessel, a container, a way to experience the world through human senses. She is the Dusk's attempt to understand what it means to be alive.

The Mark: Her entire body is covered in fine red lines that form patterns like a circulatory system. The lines pulse with light in time with her heartbeat. At night, she glows faintly. She cannot hide what she is. She has stopped trying.

The Price: She has no past. She has no family. She has no name. She exists entirely in the present, with no context for her own existence. She has learned to speak by listening to travelers. She has learned to read from signs on abandoned buildings. She has learned to feel by watching other people and imitating their expressions. She is not sure if what she feels is real or an imitation of real.

The Need: She wants to understand what she is. She has heard that there is a Qi master in the mountains who can see the truth of any being. She wants to go to this master and ask: Am I a person? Am I a thing? Is there a difference? She is not dangerous. The Dusk does not speak through her. But the Dusk is patient. It is waiting to see what she learns. It is learning through her. The players may be helping a lost soul find answers. They may be helping the Dusk understand humanity better so it can destroy it more completely.
