### Path: Alchemist

### School: Golden Orthodoxy

## Description

You are trained in the alchemical arts: the transmutation of substances, the brewing of elixirs, and the delicate craft of narrative shaping. The Bureau of Harmonious Narrative does not merely record events. It decides which events happened and which did not. You understand, better than most, that truth is a substance that can be refined, diluted, concentrated, and occasionally poisoned.

You wear robes of gold and alabaster. You carry vials of reagents that can heal, harm, or reveal. Your hands are stained with ink and something darker. You know things about how the Court's official record is produced that would terrify the public if they understood. You have made your peace with this. Mostly.

## Archetype Abilities

### Transmutation of Truth

_The alchemist's core art: turning one thing into another._

Once per session, you may attempt to alter the official record of a recent event. Describe what you want the record to say instead of what actually happened. Roll Intellect + Craft against TN 16. On success, the Bureau of Harmonious Narrative accepts your version. The false record becomes official. On a Critical Success, even physical evidence subtly shifts to support your version. On failure, someone notices the discrepancy.

### Elixir Preparation

_You can brew alchemical preparations with specific effects._

During a long rest, you may prepare a number of elixirs equal to your Intellect Modifier (minimum 1). Choose from the following preparations:

- **Clarity Elixir:** The drinker gains Advantage on their next Intellect check. Lasts one scene.
- **Fortifying Draught:** The drinker heals 1d8 HP. Cannot be used in combat.
- **Truth Serum:** The drinker must make a Resolve check (TN 16) or answer the next question truthfully. The drinker is aware of the effect afterward.
- **Shadow Essence:** The drinker gains Advantage on their next Stealth check. Their movements become slightly blurred.

Elixirs remain potent for 24 hours. Unused elixirs lose their effect.

### Narrative Authority

_When you cite doctrine, people listen._

When you invoke orthodox doctrine to support your position during a social encounter, you gain Advantage on the roll. You must actually quote or paraphrase the doctrine you are invoking. If you fabricate a doctrine that does not exist, roll Intellect + Deception against TN 16. On success, no one challenges you. On failure, someone in the room knows you are lying.

## Expanded Backstory

You were born in the Archive Quarter, the daughter of a junior archivist and a calligrapher who specialized in official death records. Your mother spent her days transcribing the names of the Harmonized, writing their final epitaphs in perfect brushstrokes that would be filed and forgotten by the end of the week. Your father catalogued petitions that would never be read. You grew up surrounded by paper and silence. The first smell you remember is the smell of old ink.

When you were seven, you discovered something you should not have. You were playing in a restricted storage room beneath the Bureau's west wing, hiding from your tutor, when you found a stack of records labeled for a year that had not yet occurred. The documents described events that had not happened. They were meticulous. They were complete. They were lies waiting to become truth. You told your father about it that night. He turned very pale. He told you never to speak of it again. You never did. But the discovery planted something in you: the understanding that the world was made of words, and words could be changed.

Your formal apprenticeship began at twelve. The Bureau selects its recruits early, before they develop loyalties to anything outside the institution. Your master was a woman named Prefect Yan, a senior narrative shaper with hands scarred by acid and a smile that never reached her eyes. She taught you the physical craft first: distillation, precipitation, the careful measurement of reagents that could burn through bone. She taught you that alchemy was not magic. It was chemistry applied to reality. She taught you that ink was the most powerful substance in the empire, because ink decided what was true.

The pivotal moment came when you were nineteen. Prefect Yan summoned you to her private workshop, a room you had never been allowed to enter. On her workbench sat a single document: a death certificate for a man you knew was still alive. You had seen him at the market that morning, buying oranges. Prefect Yan told you to prepare the ink. She told you that the man had been judged, that his Harmonization was a matter of doctrine, and that the record simply needed to catch up with reality. You prepared the ink. You watched her write the certificate. You did not stop her. The man appeared on the Harmonization rolls the following week. He did not appear at the market again.

Your greatest regret is that you said nothing. Not because it would have saved him. You know now that it would not have. The Bureau does not change its plans. But you could have retained the part of yourself that believed truth mattered. You gave it up for the safety of obedience. You have been trying to find it ever since.

Your secret hope is that one day you will write something that is both true and allowed. You do not know if such a thing is possible anymore. But you keep a private journal hidden in your quarters. It contains the real history of the last decade, as you witnessed it. You do not know what you will do with it. You only know that you cannot stop writing it.

## Sample Dialogue

**When asserting narrative authority in a negotiation:**
"The Golden Edict of Third Harmony states that 'the recorded witness of a duly appointed Bureau official supersedes all contrary testimony, including that of the witnessing parties themselves.' I was a duly appointed official. I was present. My account is the account."

**To an ally who questions the morality of their work:**
"You think I enjoy this? I did not make the rules of this world. I simply learned them well enough to survive. If you want to change things, you need to understand how they are built first. And they are built with paper."

**When caught in a lie and doubling down:**
"The documents you saw were preliminary drafts. The revision you are holding is the authoritative version. The preliminary draft was never approved. I am sorry for the confusion. It happens more often than you would think."

**A quiet observation to a trusted companion:**
"The most terrifying thing I have ever learned is that the official record of this empire could be completely wrong about everything and nobody would ever know, because the record is the only source anyone checks. There is no reality underneath the paper. There is only the paper."

**When confronted about their role in a cover up:**
"You want me to apologize for keeping my hands clean in a city that executes people for knowing the wrong things? I am not proud of what I do. I am proud that I am still alive to feel ashamed about it. That is more than most of my colleagues can say."

**To a junior scribe who shows talent:**
"Your calligraphy is beautiful. That is a curse. Beautiful work gets noticed. Noticed work gets scrutinized. If you want to survive in this Bureau, learn to make your lies look exactly as boring as the truth. Nobody reads the boring documents. Those are the ones that last forever."

**When a friend asks if they can be trusted:**
"I have spent my entire adult life learning how to make people believe things that are not true. I am very, very good at it. The question is not whether I can be trusted. The question is whether I have chosen to be. And I have, with you. Do not make me regret it."

**After a long night of alchemical work, to no one in particular:**
"The reagents are stable. The documents are filed. The narrative is consistent. Another day of making the world slightly less real than it was yesterday. Goodnight."

## Personal Connections

**The Duke of Eternal Night (Crimson Lineage Patriarch):**
You once prepared a batch of Clarity Elixir for the Duke at the request of the Crimson Lineage. It was a trivial favor, the kind of small debt that accumulates between schools. He received you in his private study, a room that smelled of old roses and older blood. He asked you how the Bureau was treating you. He asked it in a tone that suggested he already knew the answer. You have never been able to forget the way he looked at you: like you were a book he had already finished reading. You are terrified of him. You are also fascinated. When he speaks, you find yourself wanting to impress him, even though you know that his attention is the most dangerous thing you could attract. You suspect he knows about your private journal. You cannot prove it. But he mentioned, once, that he has always admired people who keep their own records.

**The Night Warbler (Assassin of the Bureau of Internal Harmony):**
You have crossed paths with the Night Warbler three times. The first was in a hallway where neither of you was supposed to be. The second was in a dead drop location you had both been sent to use. The third was during a Bureau function where they were posing as a junior clerk. You recognized them each time. They recognized you. There is a silent understanding between you: you work for different branches of the same system, and the system does not care which of you dies first. You do not trust them. They do not trust you. But you share the knowledge of how the Court really operates, and that knowledge creates a bond that is almost friendship. You have never spoken more than ten words to each other. You have never needed to.

**The Guest Among Forests (Verdant Path Representative):**
You admire the elf deeply and have never told them. During a diplomatic incident three years ago, the Bureau attempted to alter the record of a treaty negotiation to favor the Golden Orthodoxy. The Guest Among Forests noticed the discrepancy within hours. They did not accuse the Bureau. They simply provided their own account, written in perfect calligraphy, and let the two versions sit side by side. The Council was forced to investigate. The truth emerged. You were the one who wrote the Bureau's false version. The elf's quiet correction of your work was the most elegant professional defeat you have ever experienced. You want to apologize, but you cannot explain why you wrote the false version without implicating yourself. So you say nothing. You suspect the elf knows anyway.

**The Iron Calculation Artificer:**
You worked together on a joint project six months ago: a census of Qi distribution across the capital's industrial districts. You were there to ensure the numbers told an acceptable story. The Artificer was there to ensure the numbers were accurate. You clashed constantly. They accused you of fudging data to make the Golden Orthodoxy's policies look more effective. You accused them of having no imagination. But by the end of the project, you had developed a grudging respect. The Artificer is the most honest person you have ever worked with. Their honesty is terrifying. It is also refreshing. You have started meeting for tea once a month. Neither of your schools knows. You talk about nothing important. You think they are the closest thing you have to a friend outside the Bureau.

**The Bright Mirror Justicar:**
You have been interviewed by the Justicar twice. Both times were related to document authentication cases. The Justicar is the only person who makes you feel like your skills are useless. You can lie to anyone. You cannot lie to them. They do not have to do anything. They simply stand there, radiating judgment, and the truth falls out of you like water from a broken cup. You fear them more than you fear the Duke of Eternal Night, because the Duke's attention is a choice and the Justicar's attention is a force of nature. You have started preparing notes before every interaction with them, writing down exactly what you can and cannot say, memorizing your cover stories. You still stumble. They still notice. You do not know if they have reported you. You suspect they are waiting for you to cross a line they have not told you about.

**Your Former Master, Prefect Yan:**
Prefect Yan is still alive, still working in the Bureau's west wing, still scarred by acid and still smiling with her eyes. You see her once a week in the course of your duties. She speaks to you exactly as she did when you were her apprentice: with calm, professional warmth that contains no affection. You have never confronted her about the death certificate. You do not know if she remembers the specific one. You have often wondered if she was testing you that day, if she was waiting to see if you would refuse. You did not refuse. You have never forgiven yourself. You have also never stopped wanting her approval.

## Roleplaying Guide

Your internal conflict is the tension between safety and truth. You know the real history of the Court. You know that the official record is a carefully maintained fiction. You could reveal it. You have the skills to survive the fallout. But survival is not the same as living, and every day you stay silent makes it harder to speak. The longer you wait, the more complicit you become. The more complicit you become, the less your truth matters.

What keeps you up at night: the possibility that the Bureau knows about your private journal. You keep it hidden, but you are an alchemist. You know that everything hidden can be found. You also worry about the people whose names you have altered, whose deaths you have recorded before they happened, whose lives you have edited out of existence. You do not remember all their faces. You remember some. They visit you in dreams.

How you treat allies: carefully. You know from experience that the Bureau monitors everyone's relationships. An ally is a vulnerability. A close ally is a target. You keep people at a distance not because you do not care but because caring is dangerous for everyone involved. When you do let someone close, you are fiercely loyal, but you express it in practical ways: forged documents, altered records, false alibis. You cannot give people your heart. You can give them a revised version of reality in which they were never at the scene of the crime.

How you treat enemies: with respect for their competence, if they have it. You know that the people who threaten you are usually acting on information that someone else gave them. You do not blame the blade. You look for the hand that wielded it. If an enemy is simply cruel or stupid, you destroy them through paperwork. You file a report that creates complications. You adjust their budget allocation. You lose their transfer request. You are patient. You can wait years.

What you notice that others miss: the version number on a document. The timestamp on a seal. The quality of paper used for an official record. The fact that a signature was written with a different ink than the body of the text. You read documents the way a musician reads a score: you see the composition, the structure, the places where someone made a choice. Most people read for content. You read for the fingerprint of the author.

Your tell when you are lying: you become more specific. You add unnecessary detail. Real truth is often vague. Your lies, polished by years of practice, are beautifully precise. Someone who knows you well can spot the pattern: when you are being most detailed, you are being most false.

Your tell when you are being sincere: you pause. You look away. The words come slowly, because you are building them from real memory rather than from prepared scripts. You speak in fragments. You contradict yourself. Sincerity looks, from the outside, like incompetence. You hate showing it.

## Ability Examples

**Transmutation of Truth in a diplomatic crisis:**
The party has been accused of starting a fire in the Archive Quarter that destroyed several official records. The accusation is politically motivated: a Crimson Lineage faction wants your group discredited. You have no evidence to prove your innocence. So you create it. You file a new record showing that the fire was caused by faulty gas lamps, that the official who inspected the building the week before had noted the risk, and that the party was three blocks away at the time of the incident. You write the record with your own hands, in your own ink, using paper that matches the Bureau's official stock. You submit it through proper channels. By the end of the day, the alternative narrative exists. By the end of the week, it is the truth.

**Elixir Preparation in a tense interrogation:**
Your party has captured a Crimson Lineage operative who knows the location of a kidnapped official. He is trained to resist questioning. He has been subjected to truth serums before. You prepare something subtler. You brew a variation of Truth Serum, cutting the concentration by half and adding a mild sedative. When the operative drinks it, his resistance softens not into confession but into relaxation. He becomes chatty. He talks about his childhood, his training, his frustrations with his superiors. He does not realize he is giving you the information you need until he has already given it. By the time he understands what happened, you have already extracted the location and filed a report. He will remember the conversation as a pleasant exchange with a sympathetic listener. He will not realize he was compromised until he checks the official record.

**Narrative Authority in a Council chamber:**
A Golden Orthodoxy official is arguing against your party's proposal. They have the floor. They are persuasive. You wait. When they finish, you stand and quote the Third Precept of Celestial Governance: "A doctrine that cannot adapt to new evidence is not a doctrine. It is a tomb." The room goes quiet. The official did not expect you to use their own school's teachings against them. You continue, paraphrasing the Prefectural Commentary on the same passage, explaining that the original authors of the doctrine intended it to evolve. By the time you are done, the Council is nodding. The official is trapped. They cannot argue with their own faith. You sit down and let the vote proceed. You do not smile. Smiling would be unprofessional.

## Expanded Truths

Add these to the existing list:

- The Bureau keeps a separate, secret archive of the true history of the Court. You know where it is. You have never told anyone. You are not sure you should have been told yourself.

- You have started to suspect that your own memories have been altered. There are gaps in your childhood that feel artificial, like pages torn from a book. You do not know who did it or why. You are afraid to investigate.

Complications for existing truths:

**For "You have seen the workshop beneath the Spire where jade edicts are carved. You know the Emperor's proclamations are forged. You have told no one."**
Complication: One of the forgers recognized you. They have been leaving subtle signals in recent edicts, messages meant only for you. They want to talk. You do not know if they are friend or trap.

**For "A senior official's 'illness' was actually poisoning. You brewed the poison. You did not know what it was for until it was too late."**
Complication: The official did not die. They survived, paralyzed and unable to speak. Their family believes they are a vegetative invalid. You know the poison you brewed. You know what they must be experiencing inside their trapped body. You visit them sometimes, in disguise, and sit beside them in silence.

**For "You maintain a secret, accurate chronicle of events, separate from the official record. If it were found, you would be Harmonized within the week."**
Complication: Someone has found it. You do not know who. You discovered that your quarters had been searched three nights ago. The chronicle was not taken. But it was moved. Someone read it and put it back in the wrong place. They want you to know they know.

**For "Someone in the Bureau has been altering records to protect a specific school. You have noticed the pattern. You do not yet know who."**
Complication: You have now identified the pattern. The alterations always benefit the Verdant Path. But the alterations are too clean, too professional, too obviously traceable. You suspect you are being shown a false trail. The real culprit may be setting up the Verdant Path to take a fall.

## Daily Life

You wake before dawn, as you have done every day since you were twelve. The first thing you do is check your journal. The second thing you do is check that your journal is still hidden. You dress in your official robes, the gold and alabaster that marks you as a servant of the narrative. You eat a small breakfast alone in your quarters: rice porridge, pickled vegetables, tea. You do not eat with colleagues. Meals are when people talk. You have nothing to say over breakfast.

Your walk to the Bureau takes twenty minutes. You take the same route every day, not because it is efficient but because you have memorized every face along the way: the baker, the paper seller, the old woman who feeds stray cats, the guard who always stands at the same corner. You note when they are absent. You note when someone new appears. You note everything. The Bureau taught you to note everything.

Your workday is divided into three parts. Mornings are for correspondence: reading the official reports that arrived overnight, filing your responses, noting which documents require your attention and which can be safely ignored. Midday is for alchemy: brewing elixirs for official use, preparing reagents, maintaining your equipment. Afternoons are for the delicate work: altering records, drafting new narratives, shaping what will be remembered.

You leave the Bureau at sunset. You do not stay late. Staying late suggests you care too much. The Bureau does not trust people who care too much. You walk home the same route in reverse. You eat dinner alone. You write in your private journal. You read by candlelight until your eyes tire. You sleep. You dream of paper. You dream of ink. You dream of all the names you have written and all the names you have erased.

The task you dread most: the quarterly audit of death records. Every three months, you must cross-reference the Bureau's Harmonization list with the actual census data. The numbers never match. You are the one who makes them match. You do this work in silence, with a flask of strong tea at your elbow and a tightness in your chest that does not go away until the audit is submitted and approved.

## Expanded Advancement

**Level 3: Mass Transmutation.** You may use Transmutation of Truth on events you did not personally witness, as long as you have access to the relevant records.

What it feels like: You have always known the truth was malleable. Now you understand that you did not need to be present to shape it. Documents are not evidence of events. Events are evidence that someone wrote a document. This knowledge settles into you like a bone that has finally healed in the right position. You feel more powerful. You also feel more alone, because you now understand something that most people will never grasp: the past is not a place you visit. It is a text you edit. The weight of this understanding bends your spine a little. You stand differently now. You carry the Bureau's authority not as a badge but as a burden.

**Level 5: Perfect Elixir.** When preparing elixirs, one additional elixir of each type is created at no extra cost.

What it feels like: Your hands have achieved a precision you did not know they were capable of. The reagents respond to your touch like old friends. You measure without measuring. You pour without spilling. The elixirs brew themselves, almost. You have stopped thinking about the process and started thinking about the application. Your alchemy has become an extension of your body, like a calligrapher who no longer sees the brush. Your colleagues notice. They ask if you have learned new techniques. You tell them you have simply been practicing. You do not tell them that you have begun to dream in formulas, that the language of transmutation has become your first language and the Court's bureaucratic tongue your second. You do not tell them because you are not sure it is normal. You are not sure you care if it is normal.

**Level 7: Narrative Rewrite.** On a Critical Success with Transmutation of Truth, you may alter not just the record but the memory of a single witness. They remember your version as what actually happened.

What it feels like: The first time you use this power, it terrifies you. You watch someone's face shift from confusion to certainty as your version of events replaces their actual experience. They were there. They saw what happened. But now they do not remember. You have taken a piece of their reality and replaced it with something you manufactured. The power is immense. The cost is that you now know, with perfect certainty, that you cannot trust your own memories. How do you know your past has not been rewritten by someone more powerful than you? How do you know you are the original version of yourself? You become quieter. You spend more time alone. You start checking your private journal for consistency across entries. You look for gaps the way you would look for gaps in a forged document.

**Level 9: Philosopher's Secret.** Once per campaign, you may prepare the Philosopher's Elixir. Its effect is determined by the Host based on the campaign's needs. Legends say it can restore the dead. The legends do not mention the cost.

What it feels like: You have been working toward this your entire life without knowing it. The recipe is not written anywhere. It comes to you in fragments: a formula glimpsed in a dream, an ingredient that appears in your alchemical kit without explanation, a phrase your master said to you years ago that you did not understand until now. Preparing the Philosopher's Elixir is not a technical process. It is a ritual of surrender. You pour everything you are into the mixture: your skill, your guilt, your hope, your despair. The elixir requires a piece of you that cannot be regrown. When you prepare it, you lose something permanent. You do not know what until it is gone. The elixir glows with a light that looks like dawn but feels like dusk. You do not drink it yourself. You are not sure you could. It is not meant for you. It is meant for a moment that has not yet arrived. You carry it in a vial against your chest, warm as a second heartbeat, waiting for the moment when the cost of using it will be worth the cost of making it.
