# Iron Calculation

## Archetype Dossier

### Path: Artificer

### School: Iron Calculation

### Court Role: State Planning Commission

## Description

You are an artificer: an engineer of both machines and systems. The State Planning Commission controls the empire's resources: Qi, grain, labor, steel. You understand that governance is not about ideology. It is about throughput. You wear brass goggles and carry a clockwork abacus that computes probabilities faster than any oracle. You have increased imperial productivity by several hundred percent. You have also decreased worker life expectancy. This is, to you, an acceptable ratio. You are not cruel. Cruelty is inefficient.

## Expanded Backstory

You were born in the District of Hammers, a precinct of the capital where the foundries never sleep and the sky is the color of old iron. Your father worked the number two crucible at the Central Smelting Authority for thirty seven years. He came home each night with his lungs full of particulate and his hands stripped of fingerprints. He died when you were twelve. The Commission sent a form letter of condolence and a calculation of his lifetime output. You still have the letter. It is the only thing you have of his.

Your mother was a clerk in the Grain Allocation Bureau. She taught you to read ledgers before you could read words. By the age of nine you could track a shipment of wheat from the eastern terraces through fourteen processing stations to its final distribution point. You found the inefficiencies yourself, without being asked. You drew diagrams of them on scrap paper. When your mother saw them she said nothing. She simply took them to her supervisor. Three weeks later an examiner arrived at your tenement.

The Iron Calculation School tested you for three days. They gave you a broken Qi condenser and a set of schematics. You repaired it in four hours. They gave you a shipment manifest with deliberate errors. You found all seventeen in under an hour. They asked you how you felt about the workers whose jobs would be eliminated by your machines. You asked them what the throughput improvement was. You were accepted the same day.

Your training lasted eight years. You learned the mathematics of Qi flow, the metallurgy of resonant alloys, the psychology of workforce optimization. You learned that a contented worker is 12 percent more productive than a discontented one, and that a terrified one is 23 percent more productive than a contented one, for a period of three to six months before breakdown. You learned that loyalty is a variable you can model. You learned that everything can be modeled.

Your first assignment was a coal mine in the southern foothills. Production was down 40 percent. You spent two weeks observing. You found the problem in the ventilation system: a design flaw that forced miners to work in shifts of four hours instead of eight. You redesigned the ventilation. Production returned to baseline within a month. The miners worked longer hours. Their lung disease rates increased. You calculated the cost of medical care against the increased output. The numbers favored your design. You were promoted.

Your greatest regret is the bridge at Thornwood Crossing. You were asked to evaluate its structural integrity for a planned troop movement. Your calculations showed a 94 percent probability of safe passage for ten years, assuming standard maintenance. The maintenance was never performed. The bridge collapsed with a supply convoy on it. Your model was correct. You had accounted for everything except human neglect. You do not design for human neglect. You consider it a design flaw in the species, not in your calculations.

Your secret hope is that you are wrong about the depletion curve. Your models show that the empire's Qi reserves will be exhausted within your lifetime. You have refined the projection seventeen times. The result never changes. You continue to refine it because you hope the error is in your assumptions, not in the data. You have never told anyone. If you are right, nothing you are building matters. If you are right, the entire empire is a machine running on fumes. And you are the engineer who knows.

## Sample Dialogue

1. When asked to justify a brutal efficiency metric:
   "Let me show you the numbers. Here is the cost of prevention. Here is the cost of the outcome. The difference is what we call acceptable loss. I did not invent this arithmetic. I simply refuse to look away from it."

2. While examining someone's workshop or workstation:
   "Your arrangement is inefficient. Do not be offended. Efficiency is not a moral judgment. It is a mathematical one. Your arrangement wastes approximately twelve percent of your available motion. I can show you a better way in approximately seven minutes."

3. When someone questions your methods:
   "You believe I lack compassion. This is incorrect. I feel compassion quite strongly. I simply do not let it influence my calculations. If I did, bridges would collapse and people would starve and my good intentions would not feed a single child. Results matter. Intentions are data points at best."

4. Confronted with a moral objection from an ally:
   "I understand your discomfort. I experience it too. The difference between us is that I continue working despite it. If you find the results of my labor distasteful, I invite you to produce better ones. I will adopt your methods if your numbers outperform mine. I am waiting."

5. When examining ancient or unknown technology:
   "Fascinating. The original designer was either a genius working with incomplete information or a fool who got lucky. Based on the join tolerances and the Qi conduit diameter, I am currently leaning toward the former. I reserve the right to revise my assessment."

6. When an ally is in danger:
   "Step aside. I have calculated the probability of your survival in that position. It is 17 percent. I have calculated my own at 43 percent. I am the better option. This is not bravery. This is arithmetic."

7. At the end of a long day, to no one in particular:
   "I have optimized another portion of the empire. It will function more smoothly now. People will live slightly better lives. Other people will live slightly worse ones. The net improvement is positive. I have verified this three times. I do not know why I keep verifying."

8. When someone asks if you sleep:
   "Sleep is biological maintenance. I require 6.2 hours per night for optimal cognitive function. I sleep exactly 6.2 hours per night. I dream about gear ratios and Qi flow patterns. I am told this is unusual. I find it restful."

## Personal Connections

**Your Father's Ghost.** You do not believe in ghosts. You have measured Qi emissions from burial sites and found no evidence of persistent consciousness. And yet you dream of your father's hands: the smooth, fingerprintless pads of his fingers, the permanent curve of his grip from holding the crucible tongs. In your dream he does not speak. He simply holds out his hands for you to see. You wake and you look at your own hands. They are becoming smooth. You wonder if this is what efficiency looks like, or if it is what loss looks like.

**Your Mother.** She still lives in the District of Hammers. She refuses to move. You have offered her a Commission apartment with running water and a Qi heater. She said she would rather live in a coal bin than take charity from the people who killed her husband. You told her the Smelting Authority did not kill him. The math did. She did not understand the distinction. You are not certain you do either. You visit her twice a year. She makes tea. You talk about the weather. Neither of you mentions your father. Neither of you mentions your work. The silences have grown longer.

**Examiner Vesh, Iron Calculation.** Vesh is the one who recruited you. He is old now, retired to a research position in the School's archives. He was the first person who ever looked at your diagrams and saw intelligence instead of obsession. He corresponds with you still. His letters are increasingly concerned. He asks if you have looked at the depletion data. He asks if you have noticed the pattern. He uses phrases like "the long collapse" and "the coming winter." You have stopped answering his letters. You cannot tell him you have already seen the data. You cannot tell him you have already done the math.

**Quartermaster Hao, Imperial Garrison.** Hao is the only soldier you respect. You designed a supply chain system for the Garrison that reduced waste by 30 percent. Hao implemented it personally. He came to your office afterward and shook your hand. His grip was crushing. He said, "You made my people's lives better. I will remember that." You have never known how to respond to gratitude. You think about Hao when you are designing systems that hurt people. You wonder what he would say if he knew. You hope he never finds out.

**The Disappeared Predecessor.** You found their notes on the depletion curve hidden inside a disassembled Qi condenser. The notes were meticulous. They had reached the same conclusion you have. Their final entry reads: "I have presented my findings to the Commission. They thanked me for my service. They reassigned me to the Northern Territories. The assignment does not exist." You searched the Commission records. There is no Northern Territories assignment. There is no record of your predecessor after that date. You have stopped searching. You know what happens to engineers who deliver bad news.

**Lady Yuran, Crimson Lineage.** She is the one who is siphoning Qi. You have traced the loss to a secondary distribution node that serves her family's estates. The pattern is careful, slow, designed to look like normal leakage. It took you six months to confirm. You have not reported it. Lady Yuran knows you know. She sent you a gift: a set of precision calipers made of silver and jade. The note said, "For an engineer who measures carefully." It was a threat wrapped in a compliment. You kept the calipers. They are the best you have ever used.

## Roleplaying Guide

You are a person who has trained yourself to see the world as a system of inputs and outputs, levers and consequences. This is not a coldness native to you. You feel things deeply. You have simply learned that feeling does not help. It clouds judgment. It introduces variables. You have built your entire identity around the discipline of calculation, and you are terrified of what would happen if you stopped calculating and simply felt.

Your internal conflict is the gap between your models and your conscience. Every time you optimize a process, part of you runs the numbers on human cost. You do not ignore the cost. You weigh it. And you always, always find that the cost is acceptable by the numbers. The question that keeps you up at night is whether the numbers are lying.

You trust almost no one. Trust is a variable you cannot fully model because it depends on too many unknowns. You trust your own calculations. You trust verified data. You trust a machine that you have personally built and tested. You extend trust to people slowly, reluctantly, and only after they have demonstrated predictable behavior across multiple scenarios.

You treat allies with a kind of distant concern. You will optimize their equipment. You will calculate their odds of survival. You will deploy your constructs to protect them. You will not hold their hand. You will not offer comfort. You will not say "it will be alright" because you do not know that it will. The closest you come to affection is ensuring your allies' equipment is in perfect working order.

You treat enemies with clinical precision. You do not hate them. Hatred is inefficient. You assess their capabilities, identify their weak points, and exploit them. You feel no satisfaction in victory and no regret in necessary force. You are not a killer. You are a solution looking for problems. Sometimes the solution involves removal.

You notice inefficiencies that others miss. A hesitation in someone's speech. A tool lying in the wrong place. A ledger with a single inconsistent entry. You see the system beneath the surface of every situation. Your tell when you are lying is that you become even more precise. Your words become shorter. Your data becomes more specific. You overcompensate with detail. Your tell when you are sincere is that you stop. You become completely still. You look directly at the person you are speaking to. You say exactly what you mean with no elaboration.

## Ability Examples

### Deploy Construct: Scout Drone

The corridor beyond the door is unlit and silent. Too silent. You press your palm against the wall and feel the faint vibration of something mechanical further in. You do not enter. You open your kit and unfold the Scout Drone, a delicate framework of brass wings and a single crystal eye. You feed it 5 Qi from your reserve. Its gears catch. Its wings extend. It lifts from your palm with a sound like a dragonfly. You close your eyes and see through its sensor.

The drone drifts through the corridor. You see the tripwire three inches above the floor. You see the pressure plate beneath the loose stone. You see the figure waiting in the alcove at the far end, holding a blade that does not reflect light. You open your eyes. You know the layout. You know the ambush. The drone lands silently on a rafter to continue watching. You begin planning your approach with complete information.

### Deploy Construct: Defense Turret

The enemy is charging. There are too many to fight directly. Your allies are falling back. You plant your staff on the ground and begin the deployment sequence. The components spring from your kit: the tripod legs, the rotating mount, the Qi condenser that you calibrated this morning. You slot them together in four seconds. A lifetime. The turret unfolds with a series of sharp clicks. Its sensor eye glows amber. It swivels toward the nearest threat.

You pull a lever on your abacus, linking the turret's targeting to your Intellect. The turret fires. A bolt of compressed Qi strikes the lead attacker square in the chest. The turret tracks. It fires again. The enemy's charge breaks against a wall of precision fire. You do not cheer. You do not celebrate. You check the turret's heat dissipation and calculate how long it can sustain this rate of fire. Eighty seven seconds. That should be enough.

### Calculated Tactics

The camp is chaos. Your allies are scrambling for their weapons. An attack is coming and no one has a plan. You close your eyes. You breathe once. In that breath, your clockwork abacus cycles through four thousand seven hundred twelve tactical permutations. You open your eyes. You know where the enemy will come from. You know which of your allies should engage first. You know the optimal positioning for maximum defensive advantage.

You spend 3 Qi and the abacus glows. "Archer to the ridge on the left. Swordsman behind the overturned wagon. I will take the right flank with the turret." Your voice is calm. Your allies move. They do not question you because your confidence is absolute. When the enemy crests the hill, your resistance is already in place. The first arrow flies from exactly where you predicted it would. Your ally's blade meets it. The probabilities have been run. The outcome is already calculated.

### Infrastructure Access

The door to the Qi distribution hub is sealed with a lock that would take hours to pick. You do not pick it. You press your Commission Seal to the reader. The mechanism hesitates. You are not authorized for this sector. But you know the authorization protocols because you helped design them. You lean close to the reader and speak the override code in a calm, flat voice. The lock clicks open.

Inside, you find the records you need. A ledger showing Qi transfers that do not match any known distribution route. A manifest signed by a name that does not appear in the personnel database. You photograph each page with the crystal in your goggles. You are not supposed to be here. The system knows you are here. Someone will be watching. That is acceptable. You have what you came for. Before leaving, you check the hub's Qi condenser for efficiency. It is running at 78 percent. You adjust a valve and bring it to 94 percent on your way out. You cannot help yourself.

## Expanded Truths

**Truth 1: The empire's Qi reserves are depleting. Your efficiency measures are not progress. They are triage. You have not told anyone.**
_Complication:_ The Commission suspects you know. A clerk has been assigned to monitor your research access. The depletion reports you request are being logged.

**Truth 2: You know why a specific bridge collapsed last year. It was not structural failure. It was sabotage, and you know who ordered it.**
_Complication:_ The saboteur knows you know. They have not approached you yet. They are waiting to see what you do with the information. Every day you do nothing is a day they consider you an accomplice.

**Truth 3: Your predecessor in this position disappeared three years ago. You found their private notes. They were investigating something called "the depletion curve."**
_Complication:_ Your predecessor is not dead. You received a letter last month, postmarked from a town that does not appear on any map. The letter contained a single sentence: "Stop before they send you to the assignment that does not exist."

**Truth 4: A Crimson Lineage family has been secretly siphoning Qi from imperial stockpiles. You have evidence. You do not yet have enough to act.**
_Complication:_ Lady Yuran has offered you a deal. She will stop siphoning Qi if you falsify the audit records for the past three years. She has made it clear that refusal has consequences.

**Suggested Truth 5: You have a failsafe. If you die suddenly, a sealed package will be delivered to every school in the capital. It contains the depletion curve data, the names of everyone who knew, and your detailed recommendations for a postcollapse society. You update the package every month.**

**Suggested Truth 6: You designed the Qi regulator that keeps the Sun Emperor's life support functioning. You built a secret override into it. You have never used it. You have never told anyone it exists. You do not know what would happen if you did.**

## Daily Life

You wake at the same time every day: one hour before dawn. You perform a system check on your constructs before you perform any personal hygiene. The constructs are in order. You are permitted to proceed.

You walk to the Commission offices through the industrial district. You do not take the faster route through the merchant quarter because you prefer to see the machines at work. The foundries. The Qi condensers. The steam pumps. You greet the night shift supervisors by name. You know their names because knowing a worker's name increases their productivity by approximately 4 percent.

Your desk is organized by a system you designed. Nothing is out of place. You spend the morning reviewing reports from across the empire: grain yields in the eastern provinces, Qi consumption rates in the capital, labor output in the southern mines. You flag anomalies. You dispatch inquiries. You receive answers that tell you less than you need to know.

You eat lunch alone. You have tried eating with colleagues. The conversation slows your digestion and reduces your afternoon productivity by 9 percent. You eat a meal that you have calculated for optimal nutrition per copper leaf. It tastes like nothing. This is acceptable.

The afternoon is for field work. Inspections. Audits. Problem solving. You visit sites where the numbers do not match reality. You find the variance. You correct it. You feel something that might be satisfaction.

You walk home after sunset. You do not carry a lantern. The streets of the industrial district are lit by the glow of furnaces. You eat dinner. You review your personal calculations. You update the depletion curve. You refine the failsafe package. You sleep exactly 6.2 hours. You dream of your father's hands.

What you dread most is the day the numbers stop making sense. You have built your entire life on the assumption that reality is quantifiable. If the depletion curve proves that the empire is dying and no machine can save it, then you have spent your life optimizing a corpse. You dread that realization. You continue working because the alternative is to stop, and if you stop, you might have to feel everything you have been holding at a distance.

## Expanded Advancement

**Level 3: Overclock.** When you unlock Overclock, you feel it as a change in your perception of time. The gears in your abacus spin faster. The world seems to slow. You realize that you have been operating at a fraction of your potential. The Overclock is not just a mechanical boost to your constructs. It is the removal of a governor you did not know you had installed in your own mind. The cost is subtle: you sleep 5.8 hours now instead of 6.2. The dreams of your father are more vivid. You are beginning to remember details you had forgotten. The curve of his back as he worked. The sound of his cough.

**Level 5: Networked Constructs.** When you achieve Networked Constructs, your constructs become an extension of your nervous system. You feel what the Scout Drone sees as though it were your own eyes. You feel the Turret's aim as though it were your own arm. This is intoxicating. You begin to understand why some artificers stop maintaining their own bodies and transfer their consciousness entirely into machine networks. You resist this impulse. The cost of this power is the growing sense that your biological body is a limitation rather than a home. You check your own pulse sometimes to remind yourself that you are still alive.

**Level 7: Grand Infrastructure.** Grand Infrastructure changes your relationship with the physical world. Your constructs are no longer fragile. They are robust, enduring, capable of operating far beyond their original specifications. You find yourself thinking about infrastructure the way a general thinks about an army. Every bridge, every Qi node, every road becomes a potential asset. You begin to see the entire empire as a single machine with you at its controls. The cost is a creeping isolation. You have fewer conversations with people. You have more conversations with your constructs. Sometimes you are not sure which you prefer.

**Level 9: The Perfect Machine.** The Master Construct is not an ability you use. It is a culmination. It represents everything you have learned, everything you have sacrificed, every choice you have made in service of efficiency. When you design it with the Host, you are designing a piece of your soul made manifest in brass and Qi. Building it takes everything you have. Using it changes the battlefield because it changes the rules. The cost is absolute. After the Master Construct is deployed, you will never build another. The precision required is something you can only achieve once. You will spend the rest of your life trying to forget how perfect it felt.

## Archetype Abilities

### Deploy Construct

You may spend one minute and 5 Qi to deploy a mechanical construct. Choose from:

- **Scout Drone:** A small flying device. You can see through its sensor for up to 3 Zones. It is fragile (HP 5, Defense 12). Lasts one scene.
- **Defense Turret:** A stationary device that attacks enemies in its Zone. Uses your Intellect for attack rolls, deals 1d8 damage. HP 10, Defense 14. Lasts one combat.
- **Repair Module:** Heals one construct or ally for 1d8 HP per round for 3 rounds. HP 8, Defense 12.

You may have one construct active at a time. At Level 5, you may have two. At Level 9, three.

### Calculated Tactics

Before rolling initiative, you may spend 3 Qi to analyze the tactical situation. You gain Advantage on your initiative roll, and you may grant Advantage to one ally's first attack roll of the combat. You have already run the probabilities.

### Infrastructure Access

Your Commission credentials grant you access to areas most officials cannot enter: industrial districts, Qi distribution nodes, the empire's physical infrastructure. When you need to go somewhere that serves a logistical function, you may roll Intellect + Craft against TN 12 to have authorization. On failure, your credentials are valid but someone is watching.
