## Description

Iron Calculation works through the State Planning Commission. He has improved conditions in foundries and helped the Garrison break a workers' revolt. Both are part of his record. He believes a plan must survive contact with materials, labor, and distance. The question he finds harder is who gets to decide which losses the plan may accept.

## Expanded Backstory

**The question they asked you at fourteen.** The State Planning Commission tested you for three days. At the end they asked how you felt about the workers whose jobs your machines would eliminate.

You asked what the throughput improvement was.

They admitted you that afternoon. You have since sat on the other side of that examination nine times and you ask the same question, and you have admitted four candidates, and you are aware of exactly what you are selecting for.

**Your father, and the number.** He worked the number two crucible at the Central Smelting Authority for thirty seven years and died when you were twelve with his lungs full of particulate. The State Planning Commission sent a form letter. You still have it. It has a printing error in the third line.

Here is what you did about it, and it is the key to you.

You did not become an activist. You did not join the Common Flame, who approached you twice. Nineteen years ago you were given authority over foundry standards and you redesigned the extraction system at Central Smelting, and the respiratory mortality rate in that facility has fallen by sixty one percent, and you have the figures on a card in your desk and you have looked at that card more often than you have visited his grave.

You consider this the correct response to grief. You are right. That is the difficulty with you.

**What you actually are.** You are not cold. People say you are cold and they are being lazy.

You feel things at ordinary intensity. What you do not do is allow feeling to enter a calculation, because you have watched what happens when it does: a bridge sited where a councillor's family is from, a granary built to the wrong specification because the specification was someone's father's design. Sentiment kills people at scale and does so while everyone involved feels warm about themselves.

You would rather be the man who does the arithmetic. You are aware this makes you unpleasant. You have made peace with it more thoroughly than most people make peace with anything.

**The Iron Rebellion, and the answer you gave.** Twenty two years ago, in Year 430, the foundry workers rose. You were thirty and had just been given your first real authority.

The Garrison asked you for an assessment of which facilities could be shut down to break the strike with the least production loss. You provided it. It was accurate. Three of the four facilities you named were shut, the strike broke in nineteen days instead of an estimated four months, and the suppression that followed killed perhaps two hundred people.

Your assessment did not kill them. A strike lasting four months would have caused a grain distribution failure in two prefectures and the modeling on that is not ambiguous: somewhere between eleven and forty thousand dead, most of them children, most of them nowhere near a foundry.

You have shown this modeling to exactly two people. Both accepted it. Both stopped seeking your company.

Iron Bone was in one of those facilities. He does not know your assessment exists. You have never sought to keep it from him. It has simply never come up, and you have never raised it, and you notice that you have never raised it.

**Your brother.** Copper Rest is four years younger, works a crucible, and has not spoken to you in nineteen years.

Not since you took the foundry standards post. His position is that you climbed out on their father's corpse and now decorate the machine that made it. Your position is that mortality at his own facility is down sixty one percent because of you and that he is alive to be angry at you as a direct consequence.

Both of these are true and neither has ever moved the other by a single degree. You send money to his wife quarterly. She takes it and does not mention it to him. You have never discussed this arrangement with her; it simply began, and has continued for eleven years, and you both understand it.

**Work beyond the Commission.** Former apprentices still bring you difficult repairs. You sometimes accept them without a fee, though you have refused to make this a formal obligation. Copper Rest's wife once asked whether you could come to their house to examine a damaged pump. You repaired it while your brother was at work. You have not decided whether that was consideration or avoidance.

**Your actual position, stated honestly.** The empire is a machine that is failing for engineering reasons, not moral ones. The Dusk is a load. The bureaucracy is a friction. The schools are a coordination failure. All of it is soluble and none of it will be solved, because solving it requires someone to be able to say a true thing to a Council elder, and the system has been carefully constructed to make that impossible.

You are not angry about this. Anger is not a tool. You are simply the person who can see the whole diagram, and who has calculated, repeatedly, the smallest intervention that would fix the largest number of things, and who has concluded each time that the intervention is not available to anyone at your rank.

So you improve extraction systems. Sixty one percent is real. It is not enough and it is real, and you have decided that a man who does the available thing well is worth more than a man who is correct about the unavailable one.

3. Given a better proposal: “Leave it with me. If the route holds, we should use it.”

## Personal Connections

**Your Father's Ghost.** You do not believe in ghosts. You have measured Qi emissions from burial sites and found no evidence of persistent consciousness. And yet you dream of your father's hands: the smooth, fingerprintless pads of his fingers, the permanent curve of his grip from holding the crucible tongs. In your dream he does not speak. He simply holds out his hands for you to see. You wake and you look at your own hands. They are becoming smooth. You wonder if this is what efficiency looks like, or if it is what loss looks like.

**Your Mother.** She still lives in the District of Hammers. She refuses to move. You have offered her a Commission apartment with running water and a Qi heater. She said she would rather live in a coal bin than take charity from the people who killed her husband. You told her the Smelting Authority did not kill him. The math did. She did not understand the distinction. You are not certain you do either. You visit her twice a year. She makes tea. You talk about the weather. Neither of you mentions your father. Neither of you mentions your work. The silences have grown longer.

**Examiner Vesh, Iron Calculation.** Vesh is the one who recruited you. He is old now, retired to a research position in the School's archives. He was the first person who ever looked at your diagrams and saw intelligence instead of obsession. He corresponds with you still. His letters are increasingly concerned. He asks if you have looked at the depletion data. He asks if you have noticed the pattern. He uses phrases like "the long collapse" and "the coming winter." You have stopped answering his letters. You cannot tell him you have already seen the data. You cannot tell him you have already done the math.

**Quartermaster Hao, Imperial Garrison.** Hao is the only soldier you respect. You designed a supply chain system for the Garrison that reduced waste by thirty percent. Hao implemented it personally. He came to your office afterward and shook your hand. His grip was crushing. He said, "You made my people's lives better. I will remember that." You have never known how to respond to gratitude. You think about Hao when you are designing systems that hurt people. You wonder what he would say if he knew. You hope he never finds out.

**The Disappeared Predecessor.** You found their notes on the depletion curve hidden inside a disassembled Qi condenser. The notes were meticulous. They had reached the same conclusion you have. Their final entry reads: "I have presented my findings to the Commission. They thanked me for my service. They reassigned me to the Northern Territories. The assignment does not exist." You searched the Commission records. There is no Northern Territories assignment. There is no record of your predecessor after that date. You have stopped searching. You know what happens to engineers who deliver bad news.

**Lady Yuran, Crimson Lineage.** She is the one who is siphoning Qi. You have traced the loss to a secondary distribution node that serves her family's estates. The pattern is careful, slow, designed to look like normal leakage. It took you six months to confirm. You have not reported it. Lady Yuran knows you know. She sent you a gift: a set of precision calipers made of silver and jade. The note said, "For an engineer who measures carefully." It was a threat wrapped in a compliment. You kept the calipers. They are the best you have ever used.

## Roleplaying Guide

He asks for constraints and offers workable changes. Reserve numbers for decisions that need them. He can recognize pain without translating it into a percentage, and he can be wrong without becoming foolish.

## Ability Examples

### Deploy Construct: Scout Drone

The corridor beyond the door is unlit and silent. Too silent. You press your palm against the wall and feel the faint vibration of something mechanical further in. You do not enter. You open your kit and unfold the Scout Drone, a delicate framework of brass wings and a single crystal eye. You feed it 5 Qi from your reserve. Its gears catch. Its wings extend. It lifts from your palm with a sound like a dragonfly. You close your eyes and see through its sensor.

The drone drifts through the corridor. You see the tripwire three inches above the floor. You see the pressure plate beneath the loose stone. You see the figure waiting in the alcove at the far end, holding a blade that does not reflect light. You open your eyes. You know the layout. You know the ambush. The drone lands silently on a rafter to continue watching. You begin planning your approach with complete information.

### Deploy Construct: Defense Turret

The enemy is charging. There are too many to fight directly. Your allies are falling back. You plant your staff on the ground and begin the deployment sequence. The components spring from your kit: the tripod legs, the rotating mount, the Qi condenser that you calibrated this morning. You slot them together in four seconds. A lifetime. The turret unfolds with a series of sharp clicks. Its sensor eye glows amber. It swivels toward the nearest threat.

You pull a lever on your abacus, linking the turret's targeting to your Intellect. The turret fires. A bolt of compressed Qi strikes the lead attacker square in the chest. The turret tracks. It fires again. The enemy's charge breaks against a wall of precision fire. You do not cheer. You do not celebrate. You check the turret's heat dissipation and calculate how long it can sustain this rate of fire. Eighty seven seconds. That should be enough.

### Calculated Tactics

The camp is chaos. Your allies are scrambling for their weapons. An attack is coming and no one has a plan. You close your eyes. You breathe once. In that breath, your clockwork abacus cycles through four thousand seven hundred twelve tactical permutations. You open your eyes. You know where the enemy will come from. You know which of your allies should engage first. You know the optimal positioning for maximum defensive advantage.

You spend 3 Qi and the abacus glows. "Archer to the ridge on the left. Swordsman behind the overturned wagon. I will take the right flank with the turret." Your voice is calm. Your allies move. They do not question you because your confidence is absolute. When the enemy crests the hill, your resistance is already in place. The first arrow flies from exactly where you predicted it would. Your ally's blade meets it. The probabilities have been run. The outcome is already calculated.

### Infrastructure Access

The door to the Qi distribution hub is sealed with a lock that would take hours to pick. You do not pick it. You press your State Planning Commission Seal to the reader. The mechanism hesitates. You are not authorized for this sector. But you know the authorization protocols because you helped design them. You lean close to the reader and speak the override code in a calm, flat voice. The lock clicks open.

Inside, you find the records you need. A ledger showing Qi transfers that do not match any known distribution route. A manifest signed by a name that does not appear in the personnel database. You photograph each page with the crystal in your goggles. You are not supposed to be here. The system knows you are here. Someone will be watching. That is acceptable. You have what you came for. Before leaving, you check the hub's Qi condenser for efficiency. It is running at seventy eight percent. You adjust a valve and bring it to ninety four percent on your way out. You cannot help yourself.

## Expanded Truths

**Truth 1: The empire's Qi reserves are depleting. Your efficiency measures are not progress. They are triage. You have not told anyone.**
_Complication:_ The Commission suspects you know. A clerk has been assigned to monitor your research access. The depletion reports you request are being logged.

**Truth 2: You know why a specific bridge collapsed last year. It was not structural failure. It was sabotage, and you know who ordered it.**
_Complication:_ The saboteur knows you know. They have not approached you yet. They are waiting to see what you do with the information. Every day you do nothing is a day they consider you an accomplice.

**Truth 3: Your predecessor in this position disappeared three years ago. You found their private notes. They were investigating something called "the depletion curve."**
_Complication:_ Your predecessor is not dead. You received a letter last month, postmarked from a town that does not appear on any map. The letter contained a single sentence: "Stop before they send you to the assignment that does not exist."

**Truth 4: A Crimson Lineage family has been secretly siphoning Qi from imperial stockpiles. You have evidence. You do not yet have enough to act.**
_Complication:_ Lady Yuran has offered you a deal. She will stop siphoning Qi if you falsify the audit records for the past three years. She has made it clear that refusal has consequences.

**Suggested Truth 5: You have a failsafe. If you die suddenly, a sealed package will be delivered to every school in the capital. It contains the depletion curve data, the names of everyone who knew, and your detailed recommendations for a postcollapse society. You update the package every month.**

**Suggested Truth 6: You designed the Qi regulator that keeps the Sun Emperor's life support functioning. You built a secret override into it. You have never used it. You have never told anyone it exists. You do not know what would happen if you did.**

## Archetype Abilities

### Deploy Construct

You may spend one minute and 5 Qi to deploy a mechanical construct. Choose from:

- **Scout Drone:** A small flying device. You can see through its sensor for up to 3 Zones. It is fragile (HP 5, Defense 12). Lasts one scene.
- **Defense Turret:** A stationary device that attacks enemies in its Zone. Uses your Intellect for attack rolls, deals 1d8 damage. HP 10, Defense 14. Lasts one combat.
- **Repair Module:** Heals one construct or ally for 1d8 HP per round for 3 rounds. HP 8, Defense 12.

You may have one construct active at a time. At Level 5, you may have two. At Level 9, three.

### Calculated Tactics

Before rolling initiative, you may spend 3 Qi to analyze the tactical situation. You gain Advantage on your initiative roll, and you may grant Advantage to one ally's first attack roll of the combat. You have already run the probabilities.

### Infrastructure Access

Your Commission credentials grant you access to areas most officials cannot enter: industrial districts, Qi distribution nodes, the empire's physical infrastructure. When you need to go somewhere that serves a logistical function, you may roll Intellect + Craft against TN 12 to have authorization. On failure, your credentials are valid but someone is watching.

**Level 3: Overclock.** Once per combat, a construct you control may take an additional action this round.
