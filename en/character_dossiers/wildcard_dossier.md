# Stray Stroke

## Archetype Dossier

### Path: Drifter

### School: None. Your file is classified Pending Verification and has been for nine years.

### Court Role: Registered eleven times. Verified never.

### Default Gender: Male

---

## Description

You have been legally registered eleven times, under eleven names, in eleven offices, across seven provinces. You have never forged a document in your life. Every one of those records is genuine, stamped, sealed, and filed by the empire's own hand. You simply walked into a hall where nobody knew you, answered the questions, and let a tired clerk write down whatever you said. That is the entire method. It has worked for twenty five years and it has never once failed, and the fact that it has never once failed is the thing you cannot stop thinking about.

You are thirty eight. Your coat is dyed with the muds of the roads you walk and you redye it every season, which means you are wearing a map of your own travels that nobody can read. Your hat keeps your face in shadow at any hour, which was not the reason you bought it and is the reason you kept it. Around your shoulders is a strip of hemp cloth, ten feet long, covered end to end in what looks like decorative marking. It is not decoration. It is a registry. There are eleven entries on it, and one mark at the fringed end that is not an entry, because it is not a name. It is the shape of a sound.

Everyone else at this table can be reached through the record. Take their rank, their seal, their posting, their family registration, and you have taken them. Nobody can reach you that way, because there is no entry anywhere in the Nine Domains that is reliably you. You used to think of this as freedom. You have had twenty five years to think it over.

## Expanded Backstory

**Amber Shore, Year 427.** You were thirteen. The water turned red, then thick, then solid, and the fishing fleet came home with crews who could not remember their own names, and the province was struck from the record inside a year. The Court's position is that Amber Shore was resettled. Your position is that the harbor is a field of rusted ships standing in red crystal, and that nobody resettled anything.

You reached the evacuation office on the eleventh day. There was a queue, and a clerk, and a ledger, and the clerk asked your name.

**The name you gave.** There was a boy from your village who had drowned two weeks before the sea closed. He was your age. His family had a survivor's allocation on file, which meant food tokens, which meant living through the winter. You gave the clerk his name.

The clerk wrote it down. He did not check. There was nothing to check against, because the office that would have held the check was two hundred li east and had already been abandoned. It took less time than buying rice.

You have thought about that moment for twenty five years, and what disturbs you is not that you lied. What disturbs you is that the empire did not notice, has never noticed, and could not have noticed, because there was nothing there to notice with. You did not defeat the machine at thirteen. You discovered that in that particular room, on that particular day, there was no machine at all. There was a tired man and a book.

**Salt Sparrow, four years.** You were him properly. You learned his habits from his aunt, who took you in, and you learned his history from her stories, and after a while you stopped rehearsing and simply answered to it. That is the part nobody understands about this work. Nobody believes a performance for four years. What happens instead is that you stop performing.

His aunt died when you were seventeen and the survivor's allocation closed with her, and you have known since you were thirty that she had worked out what you were inside the first month, and had said nothing, for four years, and had fed you anyway. You do not have a way to think about this that does not undo you, so you have a rule about how long you are allowed to think about it, and the rule is that you stop when the fire goes out.

**The nine that followed.** A ferry tallyman in the Southern Granary, three years. A horse doctor's apprentice in the Western Bulwark, two years, and you can still set a bone. A mourner for hire in the Eastern Foundries, nineteen months, weeping at the funerals of people you had never met for families too occupied to attend, and you were good at it, and being good at it is a thing you do not discuss. A licensed pilgrim, a year and a half, which remains the finest set of papers you have ever held, because a pilgrim is expected to be a long way from home and unable to explain himself. A night watchman on the bell tower at Silver Ford, two years, ringing the hours. You were happy there. You do not know why you left. You have never known why you leave.

The eleventh is a carrier's permit of the ninth grade, issued in Xiaoyuan two years ago, which lets you walk any road in the empire legally and gives every checkpoint in the Nine Domains an answer it is satisfied by.

**The rule, and the one time you broke it.** You do not take the name of a living person. This is the only rule you have ever kept and you kept it for sixteen years.

The seventh name was a granary weigher called Copper Ladle. You believed he was dead. He was not dead. He was serving four years in a labor settlement in the eastern hills, and while you were being him in the south, drawing his wages and paying his taxes and keeping his record beautifully clean, his release came up for processing and the record showed that he was already at liberty and already employed, and so the release did not process, because a clerk somewhere resolved a contradiction the way clerks resolve contradictions, which is in favor of the paper.

You learned this three years afterward. You have made four attempts to find out what became of him. Two of the offices no longer exist. One would not answer a query from a carrier of the ninth grade. The fourth answered, and the answer was that the record contains no irregularity, which you already knew, because you are the irregularity and you are not in the record.

**What you actually do.** You are not a swindler and you have never taken money from anyone who could not afford to lose it. You are a carrier. You move messages, documents, and occasionally people between the parts of the empire the Court has stopped maintaining. You know which prefectures verify against the central registry and which ones do not, and the answer is that almost none of them do, because a verification query costs eleven copper to send and a clerk's afternoon to chase, and no office in the Nine Domains has ever been given a budget for suspicion.

You have moved about sixty people over prefecture lines. Nine of them died anyway. You know all nine names and you have not put any of them on the cloth, because the cloth is a registry of who you have been, not of who you have failed, and you have decided that keeping those two lists separate is the last piece of discipline you have.

**The mark at the end of the cloth.** There was a name before Salt Sparrow. It was the only one you did not choose, and it is under red crystal along with the office that held it and the mother who used it.

You do not remember it. What you remember is a sound: two syllables, called across water, in a voice you can still hear and cannot reproduce. You have been trying to resolve that sound into a word for twenty five years. You have nine candidates written on the fringe of the cloth in your own shorthand. You have never been able to choose between them, because choosing would mean deciding, and deciding is not the same as knowing, and you have spent your entire adult life being extremely clear about that distinction.

**Why you are in the capital.** The Amber Shore population registry for Year 426 exists. It was copied and forwarded to the Bureau of Harmonious Records before the province fell, and it was received, and it was never processed, and it is somewhere in the deep stacks of the Imperial Archives under a classification that means nobody has looked at it in twenty six years.

Your name is in it. Your actual name, written by somebody who knew it, when you were twelve years old and it was still yours.

To request a document from the Imperial Archives you must state who you are and what standing entitles you to ask. You have eleven answers to that question and every one of them belongs to a man who was never in Amber Shore. To reach the record of who you are, you would have to first be someone you cannot prove you are.

You have queued in that reading room four times. You have left four times. You are aware that this is the most solvable problem in your life and that you have been failing to solve it for two years, and you have begun to suspect that you are not afraid of being caught. You are afraid of reading it and feeling nothing.

**The fiddle.** You carry a two stringed fiddle with a badly repaired neck. You cannot play it. You have never been able to play it. You have played it badly in every province of the empire including one that no longer exists, and every man you have ever been played it exactly this badly, and you have decided that this is the only unbroken thread in your life and that it is enough of one.

## Sample Dialogue

1. When asked your name:
   "Which one would be useful to you? I am not being clever. I have eleven and they are all genuine and I would rather give you the one that makes the next hour easier for both of us. If you want the other kind of answer, ask me in a room with fewer people in it."

2. When someone assumes you are a forger:
   "I have never forged a document. I would not know how and I would not risk it. Forgery can be discovered. Everything I carry was issued by an office of this empire, stamped by an official of this empire, and filed in the records of this empire, and every word of it is true. That is the point. I did not beat your system. Your system made all of these men. I just stood there while it did."

3. When a checkpoint officer asks for his papers:
   "Ninth grade carrier's permit, issued in the capital, current through the ninth month. Route authorization for the southern road. Tax receipt from the third quarter. Take your time. I have never once been in a hurry at a checkpoint and I recommend the practice."

4. When an official threatens to have him stripped of rank:
   "I do not have a rank. Or a seal, or a posting, or a stipend, or a family registration, or anything else you are trained to take away from a man. I understand this is frustrating. I would like you to understand that it is not a strategy. It is a description."

5. When asked why he keeps moving:
   "The honest answer is that I do not know. I have been happy in four places and I left all four, and I did not leave because I was found out, because I have never once been found out. I left because at some point it stops feeling like standing somewhere and starts feeling like standing still."

6. To someone who calls what he does a talent:
   "It is not a talent. Watch what actually happens. A clerk asks a question. You answer it. He writes down the answer. There is no second step. If you think that requires talent, you have never been on the other side of that desk, and I have been on both sides of it more times than I can count."

7. When the Crimson Dusk is mentioned:
   "I was thirteen and the tide stopped going out. That is all I have. People want me to describe it and there is nothing to describe. The water got thick. Then the water got hard. Then there was a queue, and a clerk, and a book."

8. When someone offers to have his identity formalized by a patron:
   "You are offering to make one of me official. I appreciate it and I am going to decline, because the one you would make official would be the eleventh, and the eleventh has been alive for two years and has never met my mother. If I am going to be one man for the rest of my life I would like it to be the correct one."

9. When a companion asks whether he ever gets tired of it:
   "There is a thing that happens around the third year. You stop rehearsing. You just answer to it. And one morning you are shaving and you catch yourself thinking about a childhood, and it is a good childhood, with a boat in it, and it is not yours. That is the part I would warn someone about. Not the lying. The lying is nothing. It is that the lie eventually stops needing you."

10. When asked what the marks on his scarf mean:
    "It is a registry. Eleven entries, in a shorthand carriers use, which reads as pattern to anyone who has ever looked at it, including four Commission inspectors. I am the only person in the Nine Domains carrying a complete and accurate record of himself, and I am carrying it on the outside, in public, in daylight, where nobody can read it."

11. To Bone Script, in the petitions hall queue:
    "You want to be entered. I have been entered eleven times. I am telling you this because you should hear it from someone who has done it, and because I do not think it will change your mind, and because I would think less of you if it did."

12. Alone, with the fiddle, badly:
    "Same four notes. Same wrong four notes, in Amber Shore and the Granary and the Foundries and here. Everything else about me has been replaced twice. This is the only thing that has ever come along."

## Personal Connections

**The aunt, Salt Sparrow's aunt, deceased.** She was a net mender with bad hands who took in a thirteen year old boy she had every reason to believe was her drowned nephew, and who worked out within a month that he was not, and who fed him for four years anyway and never said a word about it. You did not understand this until you were thirty. You had assumed for thirteen years that you had fooled her. Discovering that you had not is the single largest revision you have ever made to your understanding of your own life, and you have never told anyone, and you are not going to, and you visit her marker outside the resettlement village every third year and you sit there and you do not say anything either, which is the only tribute that seems appropriate.

**Copper Ladle, granary weigher, whereabouts unknown.** The living man whose name you took. Your record kept his release from processing. You have made four attempts to establish what became of him and you will make more. You do not describe this to yourself as guilt. You describe it as an outstanding item, because outstanding items get worked and guilt does not, and you have found that the distinction keeps you moving.

**Sixteen Bridges, carrier, retired or dead.** The old courier who taught you the road marks when you were nineteen, on the assumption that you would use them to move grain and not people. He was seventy when you met him and had walked every province twice. He taught you the notation, the routes, the etiquette of the road, and the single rule, which is that a carrier never opens what he carries. He also told you that a man who changes his name more than three times stops being able to tell the difference between a decision and a habit. You were nineteen and you thought it was the sort of thing old men say.

**The Celestial Book, Keeper of the Imperial Archives.** She holds the thing you came here for and she does not know it, or she does, which would be worse. You have queued in her reading room four times. On the third occasion she looked up, and looked at your scarf for slightly longer than a person looks at a scarf, and went back to her work. You have not been able to decide whether she read it. You have also not been able to decide whether you want her to have.

**Bone Script, petitioner.** You met him in the queue at the petitions hall. He had been there four hundred hours and you had been there forty minutes, and he corrected your form for you without being asked, and then he asked your name, and you gave him the eleventh one, because that is what you do, and it is the first time in twenty five years that giving a name has felt like taking something. He says it whenever he sees you. He says it correctly, carefully, with the care he gives every name, because names are the whole of his life's work. You have not corrected him. You have realized that you do not know how to.

**The Shadow, information broker.** He has a file on you and he cannot close it, and this offends him professionally and delights him personally, and he has made two approaches. The first was an offer of work. The second was an offer of a permanent identity of the highest quality, complete with a plausible family, at no charge, which is not a thing he does. You declined both. You are aware that a man who deals in leverage does not give gifts, and that what he actually wants is to be the person who knows which of the eleven is real, and that he would find the true answer disappointing, because the true answer is none of them.

**The nine who did not make it across.** Not on the cloth. Kept separately, in your head, in order, with the dates. A woman from the western prefectures who had a cough you should have taken more seriously. A boy who went back for a box. Seven others. You are a very good carrier and sixty of them are alive and you do not find that the two numbers cancel in the way people seem to expect them to.

## Roleplaying Guide

You are the most relaxed person at the table and the least anchored. Everyone else is defending a position: a rank, an office, a faction, an archive, a rite. You have nothing to defend and nothing to lose, and that produces a calm that other players find restful and officials find deeply unsettling, because a man who cannot be threatened is a man who has not been correctly categorized.

You are pleasant. Be genuinely pleasant. Twenty five years of needing every stranger to remember you as unremarkable and agreeable has made you excellent company, and none of it is fake, which is the strange part. You learned warmth as a technique and it became warmth. You do not know what to do with that information.

You lose arguments on purpose. You will haggle with a ferryman over a crossing and concede, every time, because a man who wins is remembered as a man who won, and a man who loses cheerfully is remembered as nobody in particular, and being nobody in particular is a professional accomplishment that takes years to master. Play this openly. Let the table notice you doing it. Let them work out why.

Your tell when you are lying is nothing at all, which is the problem. You have no tell. What you have instead is the opposite: when you are telling the truth about yourself, you become slower and slightly clumsy, because you have almost no practice, and the people who know you well have learned to listen for the clumsiness.

Play the difference between competence and identity. You are extremely good at this. You are also aware, in a way you cannot argue yourself out of at three in the morning, that a skill practiced for twenty five years is not a thing you have. It is a thing you are made of. There is a version of the ending where you get the Amber Shore registry, read your own name, and feel absolutely nothing, and you have known this was possible for two years, and it is the only outcome you are actually frightened of.

Do not play this as a rogue with a gimmick. You are not stealing anything and you do not think of yourself as clever. You think of yourself as a man who found a door open when he was thirteen, walked through it because he was hungry, and has been unable to find the room he was in before.

## Ability Examples

### Papers in Order

The inspection shed at Harmony Rest smells of dried fish and ink paste, and the chief inspector has a gaze that counts your ribs through your coat, and your companions are quietly working out how they are going to fight their way out of a checkpoint.

You are not doing that. You are unwinding a permit from an oilcloth wrap and setting it on the counter, squared to the edge, because carriers square their documents and inspectors notice when they do not.

Ninth grade carrier's permit. Route authorization. Tax receipt from the third quarter, which is the document nobody forges because nobody bothers to check it, which is precisely why you carry it and why its presence settles the matter.

The inspector reads all three. He stamps two. He hands them back and asks about the road south, and you tell him the truth about the road south, because it is not a secret and because a man who answers a question freely is a man who has nothing to hide.

Your companions have not moved. You put the papers away and pick up your bag, and it is over, and it was never anything else.

### Read the Road

The gatepost at the crossing has been repaired twice and the newer timber is on the eastern face. You do not look at it directly. You lean on the rail and you look at the river and you read it out of the corner of your eye, the way you were taught, because looking at a carrier mark is how a person announces that carrier marks exist.

Three cuts. A carrier came through nine days ago, moving light, avoiding the northern road. Beneath it, a slash and a dot: the northern road is not blocked, it is watched, which is a different problem and a worse one. And a third mark that is older, weathered, and not carrier notation at all, which means somebody who is not a carrier has learned enough of the language to leave a message in it.

You give the rail a pat and walk on. There is a herder's track that leaves the road two li back and rejoins it beyond the watch point, and it adds four hours, and it is not on any map because nobody has ever surveyed a route whose entire purpose is not being surveyed.

You know these things because for twenty five years the roads have been the only institution that ever told you the truth.

### Nothing to Take

The Justicar has done this eleven hundred times and he is very good at it. He begins where he always begins, with the list. Your posting can be reviewed. Your seal can be suspended. Your stipend can be halted pending inquiry. Your family registration can be referred to the Commission, and he says the last one slowly, because it is the one that works.

You wait until he is finished, because interrupting would be rude and because you want him to reach the end of the list and discover for himself that there is no more list.

Then you tell him. No posting. No seal. No stipend. No family on any register in the Nine Domains. Nothing in his hand and nothing in his reach and nothing he can write on a form that will arrive anywhere and do anything to you.

He does not shout. He is far too senior to shout. He sits back, and he looks at you the way an official looks at a document that has arrived in the wrong format, and he says, quite genuinely: "Then what do you want?"

And you have no answer for him, and that is the exact moment the interrogation becomes a conversation you are losing.

## Expanded Truths

**Truth One: The registry exists.** The Amber Shore population registry for Year 426 was copied and forwarded to the Bureau of Harmonious Records before the province fell. It was received. It was never processed. It is in the deep stacks of the Imperial Archives under a classification that means no living person has opened it. Your name is in it, written by someone who knew you, when you were twelve.
_Complication:_ To request an archive document you must state who you are and what standing entitles you to ask. All eleven of your answers belong to men who have never been to Amber Shore. To reach the record of who you are, you would have to first be someone you cannot prove you are, and the only official who could waive that requirement is the one person in the capital you are certain can read your scarf.

**Truth Two: Copper Ladle was alive.** The seventh name you wore belonged to a living man serving four years in a labor settlement. Your clean record kept his release from processing, because a clerk resolved the contradiction in favor of the paper, the way clerks do.
_Complication:_ Four attempts have produced nothing. Two of the offices no longer exist, one refused a query from a ninth grade carrier, and the fourth confirmed that the record contains no irregularity. If he is alive, he has spent nine years unable to explain why his release never came, and he does not know that the reason has a face.

**Truth Three: Someone is being you.** One of your retired identities has been renewed. The fourth name, which you set down eleven years ago, is current. The taxes are paid. The address is occupied. The handwriting on the renewal is not yours and is not a forgery of yours; it is simply a different man's hand on a genuine form.
_Complication:_ Whoever it is has been maintaining it carefully for at least six years, which means they have your method, which means they either learned it from you or worked it out independently, and you do not know which possibility is worse. You also do not know how many of the eleven are occupied. You have checked one.

**Truth Four: There is a route that is not yours.** You know four ways into and out of the capital that appear on no map. One of them has begun showing traffic: crushed grass, cart ruts, a fire pit used and buried properly. Whoever is using it is careful, regular, and leaves no carrier marks, which means they are not a carrier.
_Complication:_ The route runs from the outer market district to a point eleven li beyond the eastern watch, and the eastern watch has not reported it, and a watch does not fail to report a road unless the road belongs to somebody the watch has been told about. You are the only person in the empire who knows that road is there, and you are now also a person who knows something about somebody powerful, which is the one category of knowledge you have spent your life avoiding.

**Suggested Truth Five:** The sound is not a name. It is two syllables of a work song that the Amber Shore boat crews sang at the turn of the tide, and you heard it every day of your childhood, called across water, and there is a woman in the refugee camps outside Gate's End who would recognize it in one hearing and tell you so in one sentence. You have been within forty li of those camps six times.

**Suggested Truth Six:** Bone Script says the eleventh name whenever he sees you. He says it correctly and with care, because names are the whole of his life's work and he does not know that this one is borrowed. You have had eleven opportunities to correct him. You have begun to understand that you are not keeping the secret from him. You are keeping the sound of a name said properly, by someone who means it, and you have not had that since you were twelve.

## Daily Life

You wake early wherever you are, which this month is a carrier's bunk above a rope maker's shop in the outer market district, four copper a week, no residence token required, three exits including the one through the drying loft. You have never in your adult life slept in a room with one exit. You do not think of this as fear. You think of it the way other people think of putting on shoes.

You check the permit first. Not because anyone will ask this early but because a document that lives against a man's body in all weathers needs looking after, and a carrier whose papers are soft at the folds is a carrier who gets a second question. You oil the wrap. You square the edges. It takes two minutes and you have done it every morning for two years.

You eat at a stall. Always a stall, always standing, always the same three or four in rotation and never the same one twice running. You are pleasant to the person who serves you and you remember something about them, and you ask after it, and this is genuine and also it means that if anyone ever comes asking about a man in a wide brimmed hat, four people will say that he was nice and none of them will be able to describe him.

Mornings you carry. A sealed tube from a merchant house to a factor at the river, a verbal message that must not be written down, a package whose contents you have not opened because a carrier never opens what he carries and because Sixteen Bridges would have taken the fee back out of your hide. You are paid in copper and food and crossings, and occasionally in the only currency that matters on the road, which is a name and a door and a promise that the door will open.

Afternoons you walk the routes. Not to any purpose. You check the marks at the crossings, cut new ones where the information has changed, note which watch posts have been reinforced and which have quietly stopped being manned. You have been maintaining this without being asked and without being paid for eleven years. There is no organization. There is no leader. There is a notation, and a few hundred people scattered across the Nine Domains who can read it, and none of them have met more than six of the others. You suspect it is the only functioning institution in the empire and you have never said this aloud because saying it aloud would be the first step in ruining it.

Evenings you sit with the cloth. Not writing, mostly. Reading. Eleven entries in your own shorthand, each with the office, the year, and the length of it, so that you can put the years end to end and confirm that they add up to your life. They do add up. You check because there was a period around the fifth name where you lost fourteen months and could not account for them, and you have since accounted for them, and you check anyway.

Then the fiddle, for a quarter of an hour, badly, with the shutter closed. Four notes, always the same four, always slightly wrong. You have played it in Amber Shore, the Southern Granary, the Western Bulwark, the Eastern Foundries, Silver Ford, Weeping Valley, Harmony Rest, and here. Everything else about you has been replaced twice over. This has come the whole way.

You sleep well. This surprises people who learn what you do. You point out that a man who is not lying about anything has no reason to lie awake, and that all eleven of you paid your taxes on time.

## Expanded Advancement

**Level 3: The Twelfth Name.** You have not made a new one in two years and you had begun to think you were finished. Making the twelfth is easier than any of the previous eleven and that is what unsettles you. You choose a prefecture, you choose a trade, you answer four questions, and a clerk writes down a man who did not exist that morning. It takes eleven days and eleven copper and the whole procedure is legal from end to end. Walking out with the stamped paper you notice that you feel nothing at all: not fear, not triumph, not even the small clean satisfaction of work done properly. You have crossed from doing a thing into being a thing that does it, and the crossing happened at some point behind you, and there was no moment.

**Level 5: Already Counted.** You have stopped being visible in the specific way that matters. Officials taking names in a queue skip yours and continue smoothly; guards counting a column count you as counted; a clerk collecting nine forms collects nine and there were ten. It is not concealment and it is not Qi, and no instrument the Commission owns will register it, because nothing is happening. What is happening is that twenty five years of being unremarkable has finished, and the finish is that the part of a person which asks who that is has stopped asking when it reaches you. You test it once, in a hall, deliberately, and it works, and afterward you sit on the steps for a while.

**Level 7: The Road Nobody Maintains.** You can move people now, not just messages, and in numbers. The routes you have been quietly keeping for eleven years turn out to constitute a system, and you turn out to be the only person who holds the whole of it, and this is a fact about you that you would rather not be true and cannot make untrue. Somewhere in the capital, a person with rank has begun to notice that people leave the city and are not recorded leaving. You have become an institution. Institutions are found.

**Level 9: The Stroke That Changes the Word.** You will stand at a desk in a records office with a brush in your hand, and you will understand, finally and completely, the thing you glimpsed when you were thirteen and a clerk wrote down a drowned boy's name without checking. Every copy in the Nine Domains is copied from another copy. No office has ever compared them all. No office ever could. One stroke, correctly placed, in the correct volume, becomes the truth in every duplicate within a year, because the duplicates are made from each other and there is nothing underneath them. You have known this for twenty five years. You have never used it. When you do, you will find out what you actually believe, and so will everyone who has ever trusted you.

## Archetype Abilities

### Papers in Order

_You have never forged a document in your life. You did not have to._

Once per session, you may declare that one of your eleven registered identities fits the situation in front of you: a trade, a licence, a prefecture of origin, a reason to be standing exactly where you are standing. The papers are genuine and will survive any inspection short of a query to the central registry, which costs more than almost any office will spend. Roll Presence + Deception against TN 12 to carry the manner as well as the paper. On a failure the papers still hold, but the name you used is one somebody in the scene has heard before. On a Critical Failure, that name is currently the subject of a standing query.

### Read the Road

_Every route has a language. You learned it before you learned the capital's._

You can read and cut carrier marks, the notation that unregistered couriers leave on gateposts, bridge rails, and shrine stones across the Nine Domains. Spend ten minutes at any crossing, checkpoint, gate, or settlement to learn three things: who has passed recently, what they were avoiding, and one way out that is not on any map. Once per location. The marks are never false. They are frequently out of date, and the Host should say so when it matters. You also never lose your way in open country or in an unfamiliar city.

### Nothing to Take

_Court pressure travels through the record. There is no entry anywhere that is reliably you._

Coercion built on rank, seal, posting, stipend, property, or family registration has no purchase on you. You gain Advantage on every check to resist such pressure, and any attempt to punish you administratively fails to find a target. The reverse is not optional and cannot be set aside. No faction will protect you, no office will vouch for you, no patron's protection can be entered anywhere on your behalf, and if you are taken quietly, there is no document in the Nine Domains that can be produced to prove you were ever there to be taken.

## Suggested Truths

Choose one, or create your own with the Host:

- The Amber Shore population registry for Year 426 is in the deep stacks of the Imperial Archives, received and never processed. Your true name is in it. To request it you must state who you are, and every answer you own belongs to a man who was never there.
- The seventh name you wore belonged to a living man. Your clean record kept his release from a labor settlement from processing. Four attempts to find out what became of him have produced nothing at all.
- One of your retired identities has been renewed. The taxes are paid, the address is occupied, and the hand on the form is not yours. Somebody else has your method and has been using it carefully for at least six years.
- One of the four unmapped routes into the capital has begun showing careful, regular traffic that leaves no carrier marks. The eastern watch has not reported it, and a watch does not fail to report a road by accident.

## Advancement

As you gain levels, you may purchase the following advances unique to your Archetype (5 AP each):

**Level 3: The Twelfth Name.** Papers in Order may be used twice per session instead of once. In addition, during any downtime of four weeks or more spent in a prefecture where you are unknown, you may establish one new genuine registered identity.

**Level 5: Already Counted.** In any crowd, queue, market, column, or line of travelers, ordinary observation does not single you out. Officials taking names skip yours and continue smoothly. Guards counting a group count you as already counted. The effect ends the instant you speak in order to be heard, and does not work on anyone who has previously learned one of your names.

**Level 7: The Road Nobody Maintains.** You may move yourself and up to six others between any two prefectures without passing a checkpoint or leaving any record of the journey. Travel takes twice the normal time. The Host names one cost per journey, and the cost is never a die roll.

**Level 9: The Stroke That Changes the Word.** Once per campaign, name one existing entry in the imperial record and one stroke of it: a character, a date, a rank, a name. You must be physically present at a records office holding a copy, and you must write it yourself. Within a year the change is true in every duplicate in the Nine Domains, because the duplicates are copied from each other and no office has ever compared them all. The Host decides who noticed. Somebody always notices.
