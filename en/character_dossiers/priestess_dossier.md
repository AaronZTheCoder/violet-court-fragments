## Description

Incense Crown is the youngest of the Temple's nine living officiants and the current performer of the Continuance. The Court needs her service but does not own her consent. She protects people within the Temple while negotiating the terms that keep its doors open. The Seventh Niche asks her to distinguish an honor she may freely offer from authority she is being asked to surrender.

## Connection to the Shared World

The same Temple that receives grain also receives people and confers legitimacy. Incense Crown can consent to an admission, withhold a civil rider, and protect testimony as separate acts. The Continuance and the niche liturgy remain different rites.

## Expanded Backstory

**Four years old.** You were given to the Temple at four. Your family was poor, the Temple fed you and taught you six scripts, and your mother wept for a week and also lived because of what the Temple sent back.

You do not remember choosing this. Every other archetype at this table made a decision. You were placed.

**The years you were unbearable.** Between about nineteen and twenty five you were, by the account of everyone who survived it, insufferable.

You had just received the ninth year teaching. You had worked out the arithmetic of the Temple's position. And you concluded, with the total confidence of a clever young person who has understood something real, that the entire institution was a fraud sustained by the Court's need for it, that Mother Nine Bells knew, and that the honest course was to say so.

You said so. Repeatedly. To her, at length, with citations.

She let you. For six years she let you argue, and she answered when the argument was good and did not when it was not, and she never once used her authority to stop you. You did not recognize that as an extraordinary act of patience until your early thirties.

You would like it recorded somewhere that you were not always this composed. There was a version of you that shouted.

**Mother Nine Bells, and the last hour.** She was not warm. She taught you the forty one phrases by making you repeat them until you wept and then continuing.

She died at ninety one, four years ago. In the last hour she told you she had never believed a word of it and had never once performed it carelessly, and that these two facts were the whole of her teaching. Then she asked for water and died.

What is not in your usual account of this: you were angry. Not moved. Angry, for months, because she had held that back for most of your life and delivered it as a parting gift when there was no time to ask her even one more question. You have come to understand that this was deliberate, and you are still not sure it was kind.

**What the crown is like from inside.** You are the instrument and you know precisely how the instrument is built.

You also enjoy it. That is the part omitted from every account of you, including your own.

You are beautiful and you know the effect and you deploy it, and the deployment is a skill you have refined across eleven years of public rites. There is a real pleasure in walking into a room of powerful men and watching the temperature change before you have said anything. You would not describe this as vanity because you have constructed a professional frame around it. It is vanity.

The first time you used a procedural question to destroy a case, you felt a satisfaction so sharp it alarmed you. It has not stopped feeling good. You are careful about that and you are not sure careful is enough.

**The body nobody writes about.** You have not been touched with intention since you were a child.

You are dressed by two people every morning. You are attended, arranged, and observed continuously. The last time another person made physical contact with you for a reason that was not liturgical or logistical was approximately nineteen years ago and you remember the date.

You are thirty four. You want things you have no framework for wanting. There is a physician who attends the Inner City residences, a woman of about your age with cold hands and a dry sense of humor, and you have found reasons to require four consultations this year, and you know exactly what you are doing and have not stopped.

You eat almost nothing at formal dinners and have perfected the appearance of eating. Alone, at night, you eat enormously and badly: salt fish, cold rice, the cheap sour pickle from the market stalls that your attendants would find horrifying. It is the food of a fishing prefecture. You have never once ordered it in daylight.

**The sanctuary, and the rule you made.** The undercroft holds six. It has held nineteen.

You have a rule: nobody whose pursuers are Commission rather than a school. You made this rule in your fourth year of sheltering people and you have told yourself it is because Commission pursuit means a search, and a search means everyone in the undercroft is lost, so the rule saves more people than it costs.

That is true. It is also the exact rule that keeps you personally safe, and you constructed it yourself, and you have never asked another person to review the reasoning.

Two winters ago you turned away a scholar with the Commission four hours behind him. You sent him to a safe house you knew had been compromised the previous week. You have his name. It is not in any of your registers because you keep no registers.

**The eighth phrase, which is not a small matter.** Nine Bells put a rising tone on the eighth phrase. Her teacher did not. The Duke of Eternal Night witnessed both and will not say which was correct, and you have concluded he does not know either.

This sounds like scholarship. It is not.

If the tone has been wrong for a hundred and forty years and nothing has happened, then either the rite does nothing, or something on the other side of that door has been patiently tolerating an error for a hundred and forty years.

You do not know which possibility frightens you more, and you think about it during the rite, and during dinners, and at the fourth hour when nobody is watching.

**What you are bad at.** You cannot tolerate being contradicted by someone you respect.

You are famously gracious under insult; a hostile censor can say anything and you will thank him. But when the Bright Mirror disagrees with you on something small, precisely and correctly, you feel it in your chest for two days. You have never let it show. You are aware that a woman whose entire authority rests on being the calm center of a room has organized her life around never being in a room with someone who might be right.

## Personal Connections

**Mother Nine Bells, your predecessor.** She died at ninety one, four years ago, and she was the closest thing to a parent you have and she was not a warm woman. She taught you the forty one phrases by making you repeat them until you wept, and then past that. She told you, at the end, that she had never believed a word of it and had never once performed it carelessly, and that these two facts were the whole of her life's teaching, and then she asked for water and died an hour later. You have not decided how you feel about this. You perform exactly as she taught you.

**White Reed and Little Pine, your attendants and informants.** Twenty two and seventeen. They report on you weekly to the Commission and you have known since the first week and have never mentioned it. You have taught them both to read. You feed Little Pine harmless truths to file so that her reports stay useful and she stays employed. You are aware that you have made yourself responsible for the welfare of your own surveillance, and that this is either an act of grace or a very sophisticated form of control, and that you cannot tell from inside.

**The Bright Mirror, High Justicar.** He holds the standing order to close the Temple and has not executed it in thirteen years. You have met eleven times, always formally, always with witnesses. Neither of you has ever said anything that could be written down. You believe he is the only genuinely honest person in the Grand Council's orbit, you believe he will destroy the Temple the day the arithmetic changes, and you believe he will be right to and will not forgive himself. You have prayed for him, privately, by name, and would be humiliated if he learned it.

**The Duke of Eternal Night.** He attended your first Continuance. Afterward he said, "Nine Bells put the rising tone on the eighth phrase. Her teacher did not." He was present when her teacher performed it. He is the only living witness to a version of the rite older than your entire lineage, and he will not tell you which was correct, and you have come to understand that he does not know either, and that this is the closest thing to comfort anyone has offered you.

**Ash, the woman from the fallen province.** She came to the shrine of the drowned in the third month, Dusk Touched, red at the eyes, asking whether the last rite could be performed for someone who was not entirely dead. You said you did not know. She asked you to try. You tried. Something answered, and it was not the thing that usually answers, and you have not told anyone, and you have not slept properly since.

**The one you did not shelter.** A scholar came to the shrine two winters ago with the Commission four hours behind him. Sheltering him would have meant a search, and a search would have meant the discovery of the other six people in the undercroft. You made the arithmetic. You sent him to a safe house you knew had been compromised the week before. You have his name. It is not in any of your registers, because you do not keep registers, because keeping registers is how people are found. You keep him somewhere else.

## Roleplaying Guide

Let formal precision give way to direct speech when the ceremony is over. She can seek counsel without surrendering her judgment. Neither beauty nor loneliness substitutes for her obligations and competence.

## Ability Examples

### The Rite That Cannot Lapse

The Censor lays out the charges and asks whether you have anything to add. You identify a ritual duty within your appointment and name the official who can verify it.

The hearing is suspended until the next sunset. The accusation remains. Outside, you ask a companion to obtain the missing witness statement while you find someone willing to represent you. The ability has given the party time to act; it has not decided the case.

### Sanctuary of the Ten Thousand

The Commission's search party is in the forecourt at the second hour of morning, and there are six people in the undercroft, and there is no time to move them.

You go out to meet the party yourself, unveiled, which they have never seen. You greet the captain by name and ask after his mother's illness, because you asked after it four months ago and you remembered. You tell him the Temple is open to him as it is open to everyone, and that you will accompany him personally, and that you will need to ask him to remove his boots at the inner threshold, as everyone does, including the Grand Council, including you.

He removes his boots. And in that moment the search has become a visit, because a man in his stocking feet, being walked politely through a shrine by the woman who confirms the Emperor's life, cannot easily become a man tearing up floorboards. You have not obstructed him. You have reframed him.

He searches. He finds nothing, because he is now searching the way a guest searches. He leaves. You go down to the undercroft and you are shaking so badly you have to sit on the stairs.

### The Hearing of Names

The dying man in the camp has no registry entry, no prefecture code, and no one who will claim him. He is frightened in the specific way of a person who believes he is about to stop having ever existed.

You kneel in the dirt in a gown worth more than the camp. You ask him his name and the names of his parents and the place he was born, and he tells you, and the place has a tonal fall you have to ask him to repeat twice, and you get it right on the third attempt and he corrects you once more and then nods.

Then you say it. All of it, correctly, in the old form, aloud, at the proper cadence, with his hand in yours.

Nothing supernatural occurs. You are almost certain nothing supernatural occurs. He dies about an hour later, and he dies having heard his own name said correctly by someone whose office it is to say it correctly, and you have come to believe that this is not a small thing and may in fact be the only thing.

## Expanded Truths

**Truth One: The rite has an answer.** The Continuance is not a commemoration. You ask a question at a sealed door and you report the answer, and the Court believes the answer is a formula.
_Complication:_ The answer changed four years ago, at your fifteenth performance. It has given the same new answer at every Continuance since. You have reported the old formula each time, because reporting the change would require you to explain how you know there is a difference.

**Truth Two: You are hiding people.** The undercroft of the shrine of the drowned currently holds six. It has held as many as nineteen.
_Complication:_ One of the six is not who they claim to be. You have known for two weeks. You have not yet decided whether they are Commission, a rival school, or something worse, and every day you do not decide is a day the other five are exposed.

**Truth Three: The ordination cannot be reconstructed.** The Bureau of Celestial Inquiry spent nine years and failed. The transmission is oral, physical, and requires the presence of a living officiant.
_Complication:_ There are nine of you left and the next youngest is eighty one. You have not taken a successor. You know precisely how long the empire's doctrinal foundation outlives you, and so does everyone else, and it has begun to occur to certain parties that a hostage with no replacement is worth more alive than a hostage who has trained one.

**Truth Four: Something answered Ash.** You performed the last rite for a woman who was Dusk Touched and not entirely dead, and something responded, and it was not what responds at the door.
_Complication:_ It knew the forty one phrases. It said the eighth with a rising tone.

**Suggested Truth Five:** You learned the Temple's ninth year teaching at nineteen. It offers an explanation for why the Sun Emperor did not abolish the Temple. You have kept it private for fifteen years. Decide with the Host what evidence supports it and who might dispute the interpretation.

**Suggested Truth Six:** You have begun composing the ordination in writing. This is forbidden, it is the one thing your entire lineage exists to prevent, and it would end the Temple's leverage and therefore its protection within a year of discovery. You are doing it anyway, one phrase a month, in a cipher of your own devising, because you have watched two elder officiants die before the present nine were counted and you have done the arithmetic and you would rather the empire lose its hostage than lose the rite.

## Archetype Abilities

### The Rite That Cannot Lapse

_A duty owed elsewhere can buy you time here._

Once per session, when formally accused, detained, sanctioned, or subjected to a Harmonization Audit, invoke a ritual duty within your agreed appointment. The Court body suspends the proceeding until the next sunset while that obligation is verified. You cannot use this twice on the same body in one campaign arc, and it protects only you. State the duty and the official who can verify it. This buys time for representation or evidence, not permanent immunity.

### Sanctuary of the Ten Thousand

_The Temple is open to everyone. That is not a slogan. It is a structural fact on which everything depends._

You may declare any consecrated ground a sanctuary. While within it, you and those under your protection cannot be lawfully seized; a search may be conducted only with your presence and consent. Roll Presence + Persuasion (TN 16) to reframe a hostile search party as guests. On success they search politely and find nothing they were not already certain of. On failure they search properly, and you learn what the Commission does when the arithmetic finally changes.

### The Hearing of Names

_Say it correctly, aloud, and it will have been said._

Spend 6 Qi and perform the naming for a person living or newly dead. If living, they recover 1d6 Sanity and are immune to fear and compulsion for one scene. If dead, their name enters the Temple's oral register, which no Bureau can seize, edit, or strike, and the Host must permit that name to matter later. You must have the tones correct. If you do not know the correct pronunciation of their birthplace, you must ask, and you must be told the truth.

## Advancement

As you gain levels, you may purchase the following advances unique to your Archetype (5 AP each):

**Level 3: The Weight of the Crown.** The Rite That Cannot Lapse may be used to shield one other person instead of yourself.

**Level 5: The Undercroft Grows.** You may maintain a hidden sanctuary population. The Host tracks it. You may call on it once per session for information, shelter, or hands, and you must accept what protecting it costs.

**Level 7: The Answer Changes Again.** Once per session, ask one question about the Sun Emperor, the Spire, or the Dusk. The Host gives a truthful observation relevant to it, phrased in a dead language. After translation, distinguish the observation from possible explanations. This ability cannot settle an intentionally unresolved cause or withhold an available answer merely to preserve suspense.

**Level 9: The Unveiling.** Once per campaign, disclose a substantiated ritual teaching in a witnessed scene. Those present must answer its specific challenge to their authority before invoking that authority against you in the scene. This does not compel belief or loyalty. The Host records who accepts, contests, or suppresses the evidence and follows those people's actions. The disclosure does not predetermine your death or the Court's collapse.
