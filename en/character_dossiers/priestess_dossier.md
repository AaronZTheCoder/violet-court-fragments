# Incense Crown

## Archetype Dossier

### Path: Oracle

### School: The Temple of Ten Thousand Gods (tolerated, unaligned, older than the Court)

### Court Role: Officiant of the Rites the Court Cannot Perform

---

## Description

You wear a crown of pierced silver and river jade, hung with drops that chime at a frequency chosen four hundred years ago to be inaudible to anyone standing further than three paces away. Beneath it, a veil of unbleached silk. Around your throat, six strands of turquoise, amber, and carnelian, each one a completed rite. Your gown is white, which in the Nine Domains is the color of mourning, embroidered at the placket and hem in cinnabar red and gold thread in a pattern that predates the imperial script and has never been satisfactorily translated.

You are beautiful in a way that is entirely constructed and entirely deliberate. Every element of what people see when they look at you was designed, by people long dead, to produce a specific effect on the nervous system of a frightened human being. It works. It has worked for eight centuries. You are the instrument, and you are also the only person alive who knows precisely how the instrument is built.

The Celestial Court is three hundred years into governing without its Emperor. It can seize, tax, imprison, and erase. It has never once managed to manufacture legitimacy. Twice a year it needs a rite performed that predates the Founding Charter, and there are nine people left in the Nine Domains who can perform it correctly, and you are the youngest of them by forty years.

This makes you untouchable. It also makes you a hostage. You have never been entirely certain which is the larger fact.

## Expanded Backstory

**Four years old.** You were given to the Temple at four. Your family was poor, the Temple fed you and taught you six scripts, and your mother wept for a week and also lived because of what the Temple sent back.

You do not remember choosing this. Every other archetype at this table made a decision. You were placed.

**The years you were unbearable.** Between about nineteen and twenty five you were, by the account of everyone who survived it, insufferable.

You had just received the ninth year teaching. You had worked out the arithmetic of the Temple's position. And you concluded, with the total confidence of a clever young person who has understood something real, that the entire institution was a fraud sustained by the Court's need for it, that Mother Nine Bells knew, and that the honest course was to say so.

You said so. Repeatedly. To her, at length, with citations.

She let you. For six years she let you argue, and she answered when the argument was good and did not when it was not, and she never once used her authority to stop you, and you did not recognize that as an extraordinary act of patience until you were about thirty five.

You would like it recorded somewhere that you were not always this composed. There was a version of you that shouted.

**Mother Nine Bells, and the last hour.** She was not warm. She taught you the forty one phrases by making you repeat them until you wept and then continuing.

She died at ninety one, four years ago. In the last hour she told you she had never believed a word of it and had never once performed it carelessly, and that these two facts were the whole of her teaching. Then she asked for water and died.

What is not in your usual account of this: you were angry. Not moved. Angry, for months, because she had held that back for thirty years and delivered it as a parting gift when there was no time to ask her a single follow up question, and you have come to understand that this was deliberate, and you are still not sure it was kind.

**What the crown is like from inside.** You are the instrument and you know precisely how the instrument is built.

You also enjoy it. That is the part omitted from every account of you, including your own.

You are beautiful and you know the effect and you deploy it, and the deployment is a skill you have refined for twenty years, and there is a real pleasure in walking into a room of powerful men and watching the temperature change before you have said anything. You would not describe this as vanity because you have constructed a professional frame around it. It is vanity.

The first time you used a procedural question to destroy a case, you felt a satisfaction so sharp it alarmed you. It has not stopped feeling good. You are careful about that and you are not sure careful is enough.

**The body nobody writes about.** You have not been touched with intention since you were a child.

You are dressed by two people every morning. You are attended, arranged, and observed continuously. The last time another person made physical contact with you for a reason that was not liturgical or logistical was approximately nineteen years ago and you remember the date.

You are thirty four. You want things you have no framework for wanting. There is a physician who attends the Inner City residences, a woman of about your age with cold hands and a dry sense of humor, and you have found reasons to require four consultations this year, and you know exactly what you are doing and have not stopped.

You eat almost nothing at formal dinners and have perfected the appearance of eating. Alone, at night, you eat enormously and badly: salt fish, cold rice, the cheap sour pickle from the market stalls that your attendants would find horrifying. It is the food of a fishing prefecture. You have never once ordered it in daylight.

**The sanctuary, and the rule you made.** The undercroft holds six. It has held nineteen.

You have a rule: nobody whose pursuers are Commission rather than a school. You made this rule in your fourth year of sheltering people and you have told yourself it is because Commission pursuit means a search, and a search means everyone in the undercroft is lost, so the rule saves more people than it costs.

That is true. It is also the exact rule that keeps you personally safe, and you constructed it yourself, and you have never asked another person to review the reasoning.

Two winters ago you turned away a scholar with the Commission four hours behind him. You sent him to a safe house you knew had been compromised the previous week. You have his name. It is not in any of your registers because you keep no registers.

**The eighth phrase, which is not a small matter.** Nine Bells put a rising tone on the eighth phrase. Her teacher did not. The Duke of Eternal Night witnessed both and will not say which was correct, and you have concluded he does not know either.

This sounds like scholarship. It is not.

If the tone has been wrong for a hundred and forty years and nothing has happened, then either the rite does nothing, or something on the other side of that door has been patiently tolerating an error for a hundred and forty years.

You do not know which possibility frightens you more, and you think about it during the rite, and during dinners, and at the fourth hour when nobody is watching.

**What you are bad at.** You cannot tolerate being contradicted by someone you respect.

You are famously gracious under insult; a hostile censor can say anything and you will thank him. But when the Bright Mirror disagrees with you on something small, precisely and correctly, you feel it in your chest for two days. You have never let it show. You are aware that a woman whose entire authority rests on being the calm center of a room has organized her life around never being in a room with someone who might be right.

## Sample Dialogue

1. When asked whether you believe:
   "You are asking the wrong question and I say that gently. Whether I believe changes nothing about what the rite does. Two hundred million people sleep better in the eighth month because a woman in a silver crown said the words. My sincerity is not an ingredient. I used to find that unbearable. Now I find it restful."

2. To an official who threatens the Temple:
   "Of course. Close it. I will need to know the date, because the Continuance falls in the eleventh month and I will want to be sure the Council understands that the tablets after that date will be, in the Orthodoxy's own doctrinal language, posthumous. Shall I put my question in writing, or would you prefer I raise it at the rite, in the antechamber, where the acoustics carry?"

3. When a player asks what the rite actually does:
   "Eleven minutes. Forty one phrases in a language nobody has spoken since the Founding. I open the third door of the antechamber, I make the offering, I ask the question, and I report the answer. That is all I will tell you, and I will tell you that I have not told you everything, so that you know the shape of the space I am leaving."

4. To someone frightened, in the shrine, at night:
   "Sit. No, do not kneel, you have been kneeling all day. Sit. I am going to ask you nothing. There is no register here, no form, and no one is going to arrive. You may have this hour and do whatever you like with it, including nothing."

5. When the Bright Mirror questions her loyalty:
   "High Justicar, I have been performing the Continuance for eleven years, which means I am the only person in the empire who has knelt at that door more recently than you have. If you wish to test my loyalty, you will have to define it first, and I do not believe the Court has ever agreed on a definition. I am loyal to the rite. You will have to decide whether that is enough for you."

6. Regarding the two attendants who report on her:
   "Ah, White Reed and Little Pine. They file weekly. White Reed is thorough and Little Pine invents things to seem useful. I make sure Little Pine has small true things to report so that she does not have to invent large false ones. She has a mother. It is a manageable arrangement and I am fond of them both, and if you find that grotesque then you have understood it correctly."

7. When offered a faction alliance:
   "Every school has made this offer. I give them all the same answer, which is that I will pray for their endeavor with complete sincerity and commit to nothing. The moment I belong to one of you, I am a partisan, and a partisan can be opposed, and a thing that can be opposed can be removed. My uselessness to all of you is the only reason I am alive."

8. On the Crimson Dusk:
   "The old liturgy has a phrase for it. It is usually translated as the unrecorded debt. That translation is wrong. The word does not mean debt, it means the portion set aside at a meal for someone who has not arrived. The Temple has been setting that portion aside for eight hundred years and this is the first century anyone has come to collect."

9. When someone calls her a collaborator:
   "Yes. That is accurate and I would like you to keep using it, because the alternative words people use are worse and less true. I stand beside them at the rite. I take their silk. And forty one people are alive this year who would not be, and I cannot tell you their names, and so you will have to decide what to call me on incomplete information, which is the position everyone in this empire is in about everyone else."

10. To a dying person, performing the last rite:
    "I am going to say your name and the names of your parents and the name of the place you were born. That is the whole of it. The gods are not the point. The point is that it will have been said aloud, correctly, by someone whose job it is to say it correctly, and that is a different thing from being forgotten. Tell me the place. Say it slowly. I will get the tone right."

11. Removing the crown at the end of a rite:
    "Eleven minutes. Do you know what I think about, in there? Whether the eighth phrase should have a rising tone. My teacher said rising. Her teacher said falling. One of them is wrong and it has been wrong for a hundred and forty years and nothing has happened, and I do not know what that means and I think about it constantly."

12. Alone, unveiled, to no one:
    "I was four. I would like the record to show, somewhere, to someone, that I was four years old and nobody asked me."

## Personal Connections

**Mother Nine Bells, your predecessor.** She died at ninety one, four years ago, and she was the closest thing to a parent you have and she was not a warm woman. She taught you the forty one phrases by making you repeat them until you wept, and then past that. She told you, at the end, that she had never believed a word of it and had never once performed it carelessly, and that these two facts were the whole of her life's teaching, and then she asked for water and died an hour later. You have not decided how you feel about this. You perform exactly as she taught you.

**White Reed and Little Pine, your attendants and informants.** Twenty two and seventeen. They report on you weekly to the Commission and you have known since the first week and have never mentioned it. You have taught them both to read. You feed Little Pine harmless truths to file so that her reports stay useful and she stays employed. You are aware that you have made yourself responsible for the welfare of your own surveillance, and that this is either an act of grace or a very sophisticated form of control, and that you cannot tell from inside.

**The Bright Mirror, High Justicar.** She holds the standing order to close the Temple and has not executed it in thirteen years. You have met eleven times, always formally, always with witnesses. Neither of you has ever said anything that could be written down. You believe she is the only genuinely honest person in the Grand Council's orbit, you believe she will destroy the Temple the day the arithmetic changes, and you believe she will be right to and will not forgive herself. You have prayed for her, privately, by name, and would be humiliated if she learned it.

**The Duke of Eternal Night.** He attended your first Continuance. Afterward he said, "Nine Bells put the rising tone on the eighth phrase. Her teacher did not." He was present when her teacher performed it. He is the only living witness to a version of the rite older than your entire lineage, and he will not tell you which was correct, and you have come to understand that he does not know either, and that this is the closest thing to comfort anyone has offered you.

**Ash, the woman from the fallen province.** She came to the shrine of the drowned in the third month, Dusk Touched, red at the eyes, asking whether the last rite could be performed for someone who was not entirely dead. You said you did not know. She asked you to try. You tried. Something answered, and it was not the thing that usually answers, and you have not told anyone, and you have not slept properly since.

**The one you did not shelter.** A scholar came to the shrine two winters ago with the Commission four hours behind him. Sheltering him would have meant a search, and a search would have meant the discovery of the other six people in the undercroft. You made the arithmetic. You sent him to a safe house you knew had been compromised the week before. You have his name. It is not in any of your registers, because you do not keep registers, because keeping registers is how people are found. You keep him somewhere else.

## Roleplaying Guide

You are the most powerful person at this table and the least free, and you understand both facts with total clarity, and you have organized your entire personality around not letting either one show.

You are unfailingly gracious. Not warm: gracious, which is a technique. You have been trained since four in the management of frightened people, and it has made you extraordinarily good at making a room feel calm, and it has made it very difficult for you to have an unmanaged conversation with anyone. When a player character earns a genuinely unguarded response from you, treat it as a significant event. It should happen perhaps twice in a campaign.

You speak slowly and you never fill silence. Silence is your primary instrument. Officials who are used to the Court's constant verbal maneuvering find themselves talking into your pauses and saying more than they meant to. You do this deliberately. You have done it since you were nineteen.

You are physically still. Trained stillness, ritual stillness, the kind that reads as serenity to observers and is actually enormous sustained effort. Your tell is the third strand of your necklace: when you are calculating, you move one bead. Your attendants have noticed. The Commission has not, because the Commission watches your face.

Play the contradiction and refuse to resolve it. You are a collaborator. You stand beside the Grand Council and lend them the legitimacy they cannot make. You are also running the only functional sanctuary in the capital. Neither of these cancels the other. Do not let the table push you toward being secretly good or secretly compromised. You are publicly compromised and privately decent and the two are load bearing for each other: the collaboration is what makes the sanctuary possible.

You do not know if you believe. Play this as a genuinely open question rather than as concealed atheism. Something answered Ash in the shrine. You have knelt at that door twenty two times. You are not a skeptic pretending. You are a person who has deliberately declined to look directly at the thing at the center of her own life, because she suspects that looking would require her to choose, and choosing would end the arrangement that keeps forty one people alive.

## Ability Examples

### The Rite That Cannot Lapse

The Censor has been building this case for four months and it is a good case, and he lays it out in the Council chamber with the documents in order, and the elders are listening, and you can feel the room deciding.

You wait for him to finish. You always wait for them to finish.

Then you say that you have no response to the charges, that you will of course submit to whatever the Council determines, and that you will need guidance on one administrative matter: whether the Continuance in the eleventh month should be performed before or after your detention, and if after, whether the Council would prefer you perform it from custody, as there is no precedent and the Temple would want to observe the correct form.

Silence. You have not threatened anyone. You have asked a procedural question. There is no sentence in what you said that could be read aloud as disloyal.

The eldest elder says the matter can be reviewed at a later session. The case does not come back.

### Sanctuary of the Ten Thousand

The Commission's search party is in the forecourt at the second hour of morning, and there are six people in the undercroft, and there is no time to move them.

You go out to meet the party yourself, unveiled, which they have never seen. You greet the captain by name and ask after his mother's illness, because you asked after it four months ago and you remembered. You tell him the Temple is open to him as it is open to everyone, and that you will accompany him personally, and that you will need to ask him to remove his boots at the inner threshold, as everyone does, including the Grand Council, including you.

He removes his boots. And in that moment the search has become a visit, because a man in his stocking feet, being walked politely through a shrine by the woman who confirms the Emperor's life, cannot easily become a man tearing up floorboards. You have not obstructed him. You have reframed him.

He searches. He finds nothing, because he is now searching the way a guest searches. He leaves. You go down to the undercroft and you are shaking so badly you have to sit on the stairs.

### The Hearing of Names

The dying man in the camp has no registry entry, no prefecture code, and no one who will claim him. He is frightened in the specific way of a person who believes he is about to stop having ever existed.

You kneel in the dirt in a gown worth more than the camp. You ask him his name and the names of his parents and the place he was born, and he tells you, and the place has a tonal fall you have to ask him to repeat twice, and you get it right on the third attempt and he corrects you once more and then nods.

Then you say it. All of it, correctly, in the old form, aloud, at the proper cadence, with his hand in yours.

Nothing supernatural occurs. You are almost certain nothing supernatural occurs. He dies about an hour later, and he dies having heard his own name said correctly by someone whose office it is to say it correctly, and you have come to believe that this is not a small thing and may in fact be the only thing.

## Expanded Truths

**Truth One: The rite has an answer.** The Continuance is not a commemoration. You ask a question at a sealed door and you report the answer, and the Court believes the answer is a formula.
_Complication:_ The answer has changed. It changed four years ago, at your eighth performance. It has been the same new answer three times since. You have reported the old formula each time, because reporting the change would require you to explain how you know there is a difference.

**Truth Two: You are hiding people.** The undercroft of the shrine of the drowned currently holds six. It has held as many as nineteen.
_Complication:_ One of the six is not who they claim to be. You have known for two weeks. You have not yet decided whether they are Commission, a rival school, or something worse, and every day you do not decide is a day the other five are exposed.

**Truth Three: The ordination cannot be reconstructed.** The Bureau of Celestial Inquiry spent nine years and failed. The transmission is oral, physical, and requires the presence of a living officiant.
_Complication:_ There are nine of you left and the next youngest is eighty one. You have not taken a successor. You know precisely how long the empire's doctrinal foundation outlives you, and so does everyone else, and it has begun to occur to certain parties that a hostage with no replacement is worth more alive than a hostage who has trained one.

**Truth Four: Something answered Ash.** You performed the last rite for a woman who was Dusk Touched and not entirely dead, and something responded, and it was not what responds at the door.
_Complication:_ It knew the forty one phrases. It said the eighth with a rising tone.

**Suggested Truth Five:** The Temple's ninth year teaching explains why the Sun Emperor did not abolish it. You received that teaching at nineteen. You have told no one, including the Grand Council, including under direct question, and you have structured eleven years of your life around never being asked it by someone you cannot refuse.

**Suggested Truth Six:** You have begun composing the ordination in writing. This is forbidden, it is the one thing your entire lineage exists to prevent, and it would end the Temple's leverage and therefore its protection within a year of discovery. You are doing it anyway, one phrase a month, in a cipher of your own devising, because you have watched two of the nine die and you have done the arithmetic and you would rather the empire lose its hostage than lose the rite.

## Daily Life

You wake at the fourth hour, before your attendants, which is the only hour of the day you are unobserved. You use it badly: you sit at the window and you do not meditate and you do not pray and you think about the eighth phrase.

White Reed comes at the fifth hour with water and the day's schedule. You are dressed by two people. This takes fifty minutes and it is not vanity; the crown alone requires a second pair of hands, and the placket embroidery has an order of fastening that is itself liturgical. You have not dressed yourself since you were eleven and you sometimes think about that while it happens.

Mornings are the Court. You attend openings, confirmations, and the endless ceremonies by which the Celestial Court reassures itself that it is still legitimate. You say very little. Your presence is the content. You have learned to read the Council's internal politics entirely from seating and from who approaches you first, and you are better informed than most elders, because everyone speaks freely near a woman they have decided is furniture.

Midday you return to the Temple. This is the real work and none of it is written down. You receive petitioners in the outer hall, perform small rites, bless what needs blessing. In the third hour of afternoon your attendants take their meal, and for forty minutes you are in the shrine of the drowned, and what happens there does not exist.

Afternoons you study. Six scripts, four of them dead. You are the empire's foremost authority on liturgical languages and this is genuinely your pleasure, the one part of your life that was not assigned. Lately your study has drifted: you have been reading the Founding era material, looking for the Temple in it, looking for why He let it stand.

Evenings are formal dinners you cannot refuse. You eat almost nothing at them. You have perfected the appearance of eating.

You return at the second hour of night, are undressed, and then you are alone with your hair down and your face bare, and this is the only time you look like a woman from a fishing prefecture. You write one phrase of the ordination into the cipher. It takes about twenty minutes. Then you burn the working notes, and you sleep about four hours.

Twice a year, in the eleventh and the fifth month, you walk into the Spire's antechamber and kneel at a sealed door and ask a question in a language that has been dead for four hundred and fifty two years. Those are the only twenty two minutes of your year in which nobody is watching you, because during the rite nobody is permitted to raise their head.

## Expanded Advancement

**Level 3: The Weight of the Crown.** You begin to use your position deliberately rather than defensively. You had thought of your untouchability as a wall you stood behind. You discover it is a tool you can point at things. The first time you deploy a procedural question to destroy a case against someone who is not you, you feel a satisfaction so sharp it alarms you, and you understand that Mother Nine Bells felt it too, and that this is why she warned you about it exactly once and never again.

**Level 5: The Undercroft Grows.** Word has spread through channels you did not open and cannot close. People arrive now. You have had to build systems: signals, sponsors, a rule about how many, a rule about who decides. You have become an administrator of mercy, which means you have become someone who turns people away by criteria, and you have discovered that having criteria is worse than having none, because criteria are a thing you chose.

**Level 7: The Answer Changes Again.** The response at the door alters a fourth time, and this time it is not a variation, it is an address. Something on the other side of that door is aware that the woman kneeling there is a specific person, and has begun, in a language dead for four and a half centuries, to say something that is meant for you.

**Level 9: The Unveiling.** You will stand at some point in a room, before the Grand Council or before the empire, and you will have to decide whether to say aloud the thing you received in your ninth year. Saying it ends the Court's doctrinal foundation, the Temple's protection, and your life, in that order, over about six weeks. Not saying it means the arrangement continues, and the sanctuary continues, and the rite continues, and you go on being the most useful liar in the Nine Domains. There is no third option and the Host must not invent one for you.

## Archetype Abilities

### The Rite That Cannot Lapse

_They cannot remove you. The arithmetic will not permit it._

Once per session, when you are formally accused, detained, sanctioned, or subjected to a Harmonization Audit, you may raise a procedural question regarding the continuity of the rite. The proceeding is suspended and deferred indefinitely. This works on any Court body including the Commission for Celestial Purity. It does not work twice on the same body in the same campaign arc, it does not protect anyone but you, and each use raises your visibility: the Host advances one faction's private plan to solve the problem you represent.

### Sanctuary of the Ten Thousand

_The Temple is open to everyone. That is not a slogan. It is a load bearing structural fact._

You may declare any consecrated ground a sanctuary. While within it, you and those under your protection cannot be lawfully seized; a search may be conducted only with your presence and consent. Roll Presence + Persuasion (TN 16) to reframe a hostile search party as guests. On success they search politely and find nothing they were not already certain of. On failure they search properly, and you learn what the Commission does when the arithmetic finally changes.

### The Hearing of Names

_Say it correctly, aloud, and it will have been said._

Spend 6 Qi and perform the naming for a person living or newly dead. If living, they recover 1d6 Sanity and are immune to fear and compulsion for one scene. If dead, their name enters the Temple's oral register, which no Bureau can seize, edit, or strike, and the Host must permit that name to matter later. You must have the tones correct. If you do not know the correct pronunciation of their birthplace, you must ask, and you must be told the truth.

## Advancement

As you gain levels, you may purchase the following Archetype specific advances (5 AP each):

**Level 3: The Weight of the Crown.** The Rite That Cannot Lapse may be used to shield one other person instead of yourself.

**Level 5: The Undercroft Grows.** You may maintain a hidden sanctuary population. The Host tracks it. You may call on it once per session for information, shelter, or hands, and you must accept what protecting it costs.

**Level 7: The Answer Changes Again.** Once per session you may ask the Host one question about the Sun Emperor, the Spire, or the Dusk, and receive an answer that is true, complete, and phrased in a dead language you must then interpret.

**Level 9: The Unveiling.** Once per campaign you may speak the ninth year teaching aloud. Every faction's plans reorganize around it within the scene. The Court's doctrinal foundation begins to fail. The Host determines how long you live afterward, and the answer is measured in weeks.
