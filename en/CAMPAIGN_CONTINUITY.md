# The Violet Court Fragments

## Campaign Continuity

For the Host. This is the shared calendar and consequence record for one continuing world. The manuscripts provide different views of it, not independent settings. Begin with One Living Chronicle in the Host's Codex, then consult the relevant date, clue, or obligation here.

### One Case, Three Questions

The **Twenty Seventh Relief Docket** authorizes an emergency grain allocation for Gate's End. Its distribution annex is attached to the Seventh Niche's ceremonial account because the Temple has offered its precincts as relief depots. The Sixth Reconstruction would let the Bureau close the same account as a completed act of thanksgiving before the missing have been traced.

This attachment is the practical connection between the books. The rite can remain a moving commemoration while its paperwork does harm. The landslide was not arranged. Officials chose to put the existing relief depots into a ceremony already on the calendar because it offered a fast authorization and a public success. Each subsequent office treats the attachment as a fact someone else has checked.

Ask three separate questions: who receives grain, who remains in the record, and who controls admission to the Temple? The players can improve one answer without resolving all three. Relief need not wait for proof of who commissioned the Sixth Reconstruction.

### Three Documents the Players Can Hold

**The routing slip.** Give this to the party without a roll when it accepts the docket.

> Twenty Seventh Relief Docket. Gate's End emergency grain.
>
> Attach the provisional relief roll to the Seventh Niche ceremonial account. Stores may be received in the Temple precincts pending distribution.
>
> Close outstanding entries at the Hinge under the register then in force.

The date and the attachment are plain facts. A Lore or Investigation check at TN 12 finds the registry that explains the closing clause, or obtains a copy without logging the character's interest. Failure records the inquiry; it does not hide the existence of that registry.

**The distribution annex.** The old version says, “Receive the names before thanksgiving.” The proposed Sixth version says, “Receive the account as thanksgiving.” Under the next Key, an account entered as complete ends provisional grain claims. People marked missing do not become magically dead or nonexistent. Their families lose a route to relief.

The wording alone proves a risk, not that every clerk intends it. The Temple's depot ledger and an actual rejected claim establish how the rule will work. Let players test their reading against both.

**The admission rider.** A separate civil clause delegates future allocation of Temple niches to the Court. Previous rites admitted named individuals with the Temple's consent. The rider would make that consent standing rather than individual. It is new, it can be struck out, and consecrating one statue does not itself transfer the Temple's authority.

### People Who Carry the Connection

**Reed Basket**, a temporary clerk at the Bureau of Border Harmony, has been compiling the provisional roll. Her sister's household is missing at Gate's End. She asks the party to keep the entry open. She does not ask it to overthrow anything. She knows the routing and the roll; she has no secret explanation for the Dusk.

“I wrote missing. They returned it as incomplete. I know it is incomplete. That is why I wrote missing.”

**Dry Ink**, 128, survives through the longevity elixirs available to senior Court scholars. He helped draft the Widening as a young man. He can identify the routine registry hand copied into both unsigned requisitions. He cannot tell the party who wanted the later document to mature. He is proud of the work he remembers and uneasy about the work now done in its name.

“That line is mine. The instruction beneath it is not. Put the sheets side by side, please.”

**Incense Crown** knows the Temple's law and the Continuance. She has not seen the relief annex or the civil admission rider. Give her the documents and she recognizes their significance. She can propose a conditional consecration, request a stay, or ask for witnesses. She has attendants, colleagues, obligations, and doubts. Her decision is not an examination for which the players must supply the approved answer.

“I can speak the name at the niche. I cannot speak for a family whose name you have not let me see.”

If a player takes one of these named roles by agreement, transfer the relevant decisions to that player. Do not place a second version of the same person in the scene.

| Temple authority changes only through the rider     | Read the rider      | Temple's record of an earlier admission | A Bright Mirror examiner states the objection |

Give an essential clue when a character reaches a source that contains it and takes a reasonable action. Roll for speed, discretion, an extra concession, or the danger of removing the original. Announce the stakes before rolling. A failed check can cost time or attract scrutiny; it cannot make every other source disappear.

The matched notation is a copied registry entry, not proof that one hidden author has controlled eighteen years of history. In Year 434 it forwarded an internal request for testimony. In Year 452 it was reused in a reconciliation batch that included the Sixth draft. The clerk who forwarded it lacked authority to commission either policy. Players can establish that chain. Whether an earlier instruction was deliberately exploited or became a carry remains a further question, supported by evidence rather than settled by a final speech.

### Opening Scene: Two Copies

Read aloud when the party first examines the docket:

> The lower half of the sheet is still damp. Someone has put sand over the last signature and folded it before the ink dried.
>
> A second sheet lies beneath it. Same columns, same date. Where the first says “missing,” the second has an empty space.
>
> The clerk across the desk holds out her hand. “I need one copy back.”

Ask which sheet they examine, what they want to establish, and what they return. Do not assign their reaction. Reed Basket wants a usable record and fears losing her post. A supervisor wants the batch finished before the Hall opens. Neither knows what the party will do.

### Bridge Scene: The Storehouse Door

After the Hall address, or whenever the party follows the routing slip, offer this scene at a Temple depot:

> The doors stand open. Grain dust catches in the afternoon light. A porter sits on a sack with a child asleep against his shoulder.
>
> At the desk, a shrine attendant turns the relief slip over. “This is a ceremonial receipt.”
>
> “They told us to come here,” the porter says.
>
> “I believe you.” She sets the slip beside her ledger. “I need the distribution order.”

The grain is present. The depot lacks a valid release order. The characters may obtain a countersignature, find an existing emergency authority, buy or borrow supplies, take responsibility for an unauthorized issue, or pursue another plan. State who can approve each route and what it risks. A stolen sack helps one household and may expose the porter; a lawful order can help everyone named in it. Neither action requires a particular career.

If the players decline, the attendant begins an ordinary petition. It takes time. Report what happens when the party next checks, rather than using a catastrophe to scold it.

### Keeping the Journey Possible

Ordinary courier travel from Xiaoyuan to Gate's End takes about two weeks each way. Do not offer a leisurely return before the Hinge. A field party can send a preliminary roll back by the public lantern relay from Iron Gate, with the original following by courier. Establish that relay's capacity and the cost of authenticated transmission before departure. Allies in the capital can file the receipt. A party staying in Xiaoyuan can work from arriving witnesses, dispatch a survey team, or seek an extension.

Lantern relays carry scheduled public recitations and short reports through staffed stations. They are not a private conversation network. Expensive paired Qi Resonance Towers carry confidential, immediate messages. A public bulletin can arrive quickly while an original sealed roll remains on the road.

Keep three separate entries in your notes: **relief**, **the roll**, **Temple consent**. Write who holds an effective order, which names are confirmed, missing, or disputed, and what the Temple has actually agreed to. Advance time for travel and completed tasks, not to force the next speech. At the Hinge, resolve the documents the players have changed, not an untouched default ending.

### Carrying the World Beyond the Relief Case

After a consequential choice, note who observed it, which institution must answer, and what materially changed. Bring one into the next scene. This is an annotation to the existing record, not another points system.

If the party follows **work and water**, reuse the delivery's crew, route, or freight contract to reach the Ledger of Fifteen, the Silt Papers, and the Great Weir's compensation claims.

If it follows **names and protection**, reuse a claimant or witness to reach Scorch Field's withdrawn code, Amber Shore's displaced communities, or the consent of Nine Willow Bend's survivors. Bone Script's private knowledge remains private until shared.

If it follows **authority and memory**, reuse the admission agreement or a disputed date to reach the old Temple records, the original Charter, and witnesses to the Spire decisions.

Each route changes what can be asked about the same empire. It does not require all routes to converge on one villain or all people to share one secret. Keep one chosen direction active and at most one additional invitation.

The opening arc may end while those horizons remain. Describe a real result for the family, witness, or crew, and preserve it when the scope widens.

### The Starter Adventure as an Optional Door

If using The First Harmonization, begin its warning on Hinge Gate 21 and hold its Audit seven days later on Hinge Threshold 3. Tao Chen's existing evidence concerns Qi theft and earlier Audits. It does not secretly prove the landslide was planned. A legitimate later allocation of relief grain uses the same reserve accounting office; its routing slip can reach the party through a clerk, the Temple, or a docket request. That administrative connection leads forward without rewriting Chen's crime.

Run the two starter versions as alternatives. Their two to three or four to six sessions cover the same seven fictional days. Players can leave Xiaoyuan, refuse a faction, rescue Chen, or lose the original ledger without losing every path into the later case.

### What the World Knows

The founding and sealing have witnesses and surviving records. The claim that they caused the Crimson Dusk is a strong theory, not a proven law. The record's effects on relief and legal standing are observable; its power to alter reality remains uncertain. The Celestial Book knows documents, not every cause. A living elder may remember a ceremony imperfectly. A named figure's interpretation is evidence to examine, not the Host's final answer.

Keep voices rooted in purpose. Reed Basket needs an entry preserved. Dry Ink distinguishes what he wrote from what others used. Incense Crown distinguishes consent from obedience. Iron Calculation asks for a workable allocation. Bright Mirror asks who can attest to an order. Their phrasing changes with the person in front of them; none needs a recurring gesture, numerical tic, or compulsory catchphrase to be recognizable.
