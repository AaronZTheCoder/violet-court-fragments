# The Violet Court Fragments

## Starter Adventure

# THE FIRST HARMONIZATION

> **Recommended full story:** Use [The First Harmonization: Complete Cinematic Narrative](./the_first_harmonization_cinematic_cut.md) for the complete table narrative presented in extended story form. This document remains the full production bible and mechanical reference.

> _An introductory adventure for one Host and three to six players._
> _Designed for new characters. Estimated playing time: two to three sessions._

## Adventure Overview

The players are newly assigned officials in the Outer Court of Xiaoyuan. They have been in the capital less than a month. They know the basic shape of the Court: the six schools, the major figures, the constant background dread of saying the wrong thing to the wrong person. They have not yet been tested.

This adventure tests them.

A Harmonization Audit is coming. The players have one week to prepare. During that week, they will navigate a web of competing demands from three schools, investigate a mystery that could give them leverage or destroy them, and ultimately face the Audit itself. Those who survive will emerge changed. Those who do not will begin their first legacy cycle.

### Adventure Structure

The adventure unfolds in five parts:

1. **The Warning.** The players learn an Audit is imminent.
2. **The Three Demands.** Three schools approach the players with competing requests.
3. **The Investigation.** The players uncover a hidden truth that changes the Audit's stakes.
4. **The Preparation.** The players make their final arrangements before the Audit.
5. **The Audit.** Judgment Day. The dice decide.

### Mechanical Foundations

This section defines the core mechanical starting state for the adventure. Reference it throughout play as Faction Standing values and Risk levels shift.

**Starting Ritual Harmony**

The adventure begins with each player having Ritual Harmony 50, as per the Core Rulebook default. The Host may adjust this based on character creation choices, but 50 is the recommended starting point for this introductory adventure.

**Starting Faction Standing**

Each player begins with Faction Standing values derived from their character dossier. Record these before play begins. The schools most relevant to this adventure are:

- **Golden Orthodoxy** (Bureau of Harmonious Narrative)
- **Verdant Path** (reformist/underground network)
- **Iron Calculation** (State Planning Commission)
- **Crimson Lineage** (old families)
- **Common Flame** (workers and refugee organizers)
- **Bright Mirror** (Commission for Celestial Purity)

Consult each player's dossier for specific starting Faction Standing values. Default starting positions, if not specified in a dossier, place all major factions at +0, with up to +10 from background choices.

**Faction Standing and Audit Protection Map**

Faction Standing with a given school maps directly to the protection that school can offer during the Audit:

- **+30 or higher: Strong Protection.** The school actively shields the player. One automatic Risk reduction per Audit phase when invoking this school's name.
- **+10 to +29: Moderate Protection.** The school offers token resistance on the player's behalf. The player may call on this protection once, reducing Risk by one level, but the school will not expend significant resources.
- **minus 9 to plus 9: No Protection.** The school offers nothing. The player is on their own.
- **minus 10 or below: Enemies.** The school actively works against the player during the Audit. Risk increases by one level automatically. Commission agents pay extra attention.

**Starting Risk Level Formula**

A player's starting Risk Level is determined by two factors: the current Ritual Harmony baseline and the protection or enmity of the schools watching them.

_Base Risk by Ritual Harmony:_

- Ritual Harmony 60+: **Minimal Risk.** The Court is calm. Audits are procedural.
- Ritual Harmony 40 to 59: **Low Risk.** Standard Audit conditions. Some scrutiny, but the machine is not hunting.
- Ritual Harmony 20 to 39: **Moderate Risk.** The Commission is active. Questions are pointed.
- Ritual Harmony below 20: **High Risk.** The Court is paranoid. Every official is suspect.

_Adjustments from Protection Column:_

- Apply the highest protection bonus: Strong Protection (minus 2 Risk), Moderate Protection (minus 1 Risk), No Protection (+0), Enemies (+1 Risk).
- Apply the lowest Faction Standing penalty if multiple schools have negative Faction Standing, with an additional +1 Risk for each school at minus 10 or below, to a maximum of +3.

For this adventure, the Ritual Harmony baseline begins at approximately 50 (Low Risk). Adjust per player based on Faction Standing from their dossier. Record each player's starting Risk before Part One begins.

**Encounter Stat Blocks.** The combat encounters in this adventure use a compact shorthand: Combat is the attacker's bonus on attack rolls, Defense is the target number to hit them, Resilience is the bonus on their Endurance checks, and Wounds is their Hit Points. Convert freely to full NPC sheets if your table prefers them.

## Adventure Atmosphere

### Tonal Notes for the Host

THE FIRST HARMONIZATION works best when the Host leans into three tonal notes: cosmic dread, bureaucratic terror, and the fragile hope of people trying to do good in a system designed to break them.

The Court of Xiaoyuan is not evil in the way a monster is evil. It is evil in the way gravity is evil. It does not hate you. It does not know you exist. It simply applies pressure from all sides until something gives. This is the feeling to cultivate at the table: the sense that the machinery of the empire is vast, indifferent, and capable of grinding anyone into component parts. The horror of this adventure is not in what leaps out from shadows. It is in what sits calmly across from you, offering you a pen, asking you to sign.

Pacing should follow a deliberate rhythm. The first session is slow and paranoid. The players should feel the weight of not knowing who to trust. The second session accelerates as demands collide and the players realize they cannot satisfy everyone. The third session is the Audit itself: sharp, merciless, and over before anyone fully understands what happened. Let silences stretch across the table. Let players sit with their choices. Do not rush the moment when a player must decide whether to sign a colleague's death warrant.

### Opening Read Aloud for Session One

The air in Xiaoyuan tastes of ink and incense and something else. Something metallic, like old blood that has been scrubbed away but never fully cleaned. The sun is a pale disc behind the perpetual haze that hangs over the capital. You have been here three weeks. You have learned the names of your superiors. You have learned which corridors to avoid. You have learned that questions are dangerous. And still, every morning when you enter your office, you feel like a stranger wearing someone else's clothes.

A junior scribe is approaching your door. They do not knock. That has never happened before.

### Opening Read Aloud for Session Two

A week is not a long time. A week is seven days. One hundred and sixty eight hours. You have spent forty of them asleep, forty more in briefings and ceremonies and the endless small rituals that keep the Court running. The remaining time has been a blur of messages and meetings and choices that felt small at the time and now feel like stones tied to your ankles. The note arrived seven days ago. You have not burned it yet. You will not burn it. You cannot bring yourself to destroy the only thing that told you the truth.

Today the first school made their move. Today you learned that the cost of doing nothing may be the same as the cost of doing something. Today you will choose.

### Opening Read Aloud for Session Three

The morning of the Audit is quiet. The corridors are empty. The officials who usually bustle past with their scrolls and their schedules have vanished. A single figure stands at the end of the hall. They are wearing the grey and silver of the Commission for Celestial Purity. They are holding a list. They are walking toward you. The list has your name on it. You have known this for seven days. You are not ready.

The Commission does not care if you are ready. The Commission cares only about what is true. Or rather, what they can prove is true. Or rather, what they decide is true. These are not the same thing. You will learn the difference today.

### Zone Reference

The following zones define the key locations in this adventure. Zones enable spatial abilities such as Shadow Step, Wood Walking, and combat maneuvers. Reference this section whenever players enter a new location.

**The Bureau of Harmonious Records (Office and Corridors)**

- Zone 1: **Office.** A cramped chamber with a single desk, shelves, a faded calligraphy scroll, and a window overlooking a grey courtyard containing a dead tree (no living plants). One door leads to the corridor.
- Zone 2: **Corridor.** Stone halls connecting ministry offices. Narrow, poorly lit, with occasional alcoves and side passages. No living plants.
- _Wood Walking: Not possible. The only plants are dead or absent._

**The South Gate Checkpoint**

- Zone 1: **Guard Post.** A wooden barrier across the road with a small guardhouse. Four Garrison soldiers. The guardhouse contains a desk, a ledger, and a brazier for warmth.
- Zone 2: **Crowd Area.** The open space before the barrier where travelers queue. Carts, peddlers, and milling civilians provide cover.
- Zone 3: **Far Side.** The road beyond the checkpoint, leading out of the capital. Once across, a player is outside the city proper.
- _Wood Walking: Not possible in the immediate checkpoint area, but scattered market stalls have potted plants that could provide a brief anchor within the Crowd Area._

**The Warehouse (Northern Qi Reserve)**

- Zone 1: **Office.** The entrance area with a clerk's desk. A single oil lamp. Ledgers on shelves. Doors lead outside and to the Storage Floor.
- Zone 2: **Storage Floor.** A large open space filled with crates and Qi containers, thirty feet high. Iron doors, no windows. Stacked crates provide cover and climbing opportunities. Dimly lit by hanging lanterns.
- Zone 3: **Locked Room.** A small room at the back with a nameplate scratched beyond recognition. Contains sealed manifests and ledgers. The key is held by the night inventory supervisor.
- _Wood Walking: No living plants. The storage floor has wooden crates but nothing rooted and alive._

**The Judgment Chamber (Commission for Celestial Purity)**

- Zone 1: **Chamber.** A bare grey stone room with a single chair facing a raised dais. No windows, no decorations. The ceiling is lost in shadow. The only doors are the entrance and a hidden side door used by agents.
- Zone 2: **Antechamber.** A waiting area outside the Judgment Chamber. Stone benches. A single Commission scribe at a desk. Grey stone walls. No decoration.
- _Wood Walking: Not possible. The Chamber is deliberately bare of all organic material._

**The Teahouse of Whispered Petitions (Eastern Market)**

- Zone 1: **Main Room.** A small front room with a few tables, warm air, jasmine scent. Dim lighting from paper lanterns. A back door leads to the private alcove.
- Zone 2: **Private Alcove.** A screened space at the rear with a single table. Paper walls, bamboo mat flooring. A window overlooks a narrow alley.
- _Wood Walking: Possible. The teahouse has bamboo matting and living plants in decorative pots in the main room, providing anchor points._

### Atmosphere Reference: Expanded Read Aloud Passages

The following descriptions provide sensory detail for each major location. Read these aloud when the players first enter a place, or when the scene needs atmospheric reinforcement.

**The Bureau of Harmonious Narrative (Golden Orthodoxy Office)**

Censor Wei's office is on the fourth floor of the eastern tower, accessed by a staircase that grows narrower and colder with each step. The door is lacquered a deep gold, the color of old honey, and it opens without a sound.

The room inside is larger than it should be. The ceiling rises into a dome painted with the eight trigrams in concentric circles, the gold leaf catching what little light filters through a single high window. The window faces north. The light is always grey here, always thin, as if the sun has decided this room is not worth illuminating. A heavy desk sits at the room's center, bare except for a single inkstone, a brush rest, and a stack of documents tied with red cord. The inkstone is obsidian. The brush rest is carved from bone.

The air is cold and still and smells of sandalwood and old paper, with an undertone of something chemical: the fixative used to preserve official seals. The silence in this room has weight. Footsteps do not echo. Voices do not carry. The walls absorb sound the way dried earth absorbs water.

Along the far wall, a series of scrolls hang from wooden rods. They are covered in dense calligraphy: Bureau regulations, procedural codes, lists of prohibited associations. The ink is so black it seems to drink the light. The scrolls nearest the window have faded to brown. No one has replaced them. No one has looked at them in years. They hang like forgotten witnesses, recording rules no one reads but everyone is expected to follow.

A single detail breaks the room's symmetry: a teacup on the corner of the desk, half full, cold. Censor Wei was interrupted. He was drinking tea when something required his immediate attention. The cup has a hairline crack from the rim to the base. He has not replaced it. He has not noticed it. A man who does not replace a cracked teacup is a man who does not believe he will be in this office much longer.

**The Teahouse of Whispered Petitions (Verdant Path Meeting Place)**

The teahouse hides at the end of an alley so narrow you can touch both walls with your elbows. The alley slopes downhill, the stones worn smooth by centuries of footsteps. Water trickles somewhere beneath the pavement; a hidden stream the city was built over and forgot. The air changes as you descend: the dust and smoke of the market thins, replaced by damp stone and the faint, sweet breath of jasmine.

The door is a slab of dark wood set into a wall of grey brick. It has no handle, only a recessed ring of black iron worn bright at the top where countless hands have pulled it open. Beyond it, warmth washes over you like a wave.

The main room is small, intimate, lit by three paper lanterns hanging at different heights. Their light is amber, soft, making every face look kinder than it is. The walls are made of rice paper stretched over bamboo frames, and through them you hear the city's sounds muffled and transformed: cart wheels become the murmur of distant water, voices become the rustle of wind through reeds. The floor is covered in tatami mats that smell of sun dried grass, each one worn to a gentle concavity by years of kneeling. A single decorative pot stands in each corner, each containing a bamboo plant whose leaves reach toward the ceiling with quiet determination. The plants are healthy. Someone waters them regularly. Someone cares for this place even though it has no sign, no name, no official existence.

The private alcove at the rear is separated from the main room by a screen painted with a scene of mountains and mist. The paint has faded almost to invisibility in places. The mountains look like ghosts. The mist looks like the Grey. The painting is very old. No one has commissioned a new one. Perhaps no one has noticed how much it resembles a dying landscape.

**The Northern Qi Reserve Warehouse (Iron Calculation Investigation)**

The building squats on the northern edge of the city like an animal waiting to be fed. It is made of grey stone, windowless, with a roof of dark tiles that seem to absorb the sky above. The iron doors are eight feet tall and banded with rusting steel. No merchant stalls line these streets. No children play in the gutters. The area has been deliberately emptied, the way a room is cleared before something dangerous is brought inside.

Inside, the air is cold and dry and tastes of metal. Refined Qi leaves a faint tang on the tongue, like ozone after a thunderstorm, but thinner, older, as if the storm passed through here years ago and the air has never fully cleared. The ceiling is lost in shadow. Oil lamps hung from iron hooks cast pools of yellow light that do not reach the upper darkness. The walls are lined with wooden shelves warped by the weight of years.

The crates fill the main floor in neat rows arranged according to a logic only the inventory system understands. They are made of untreated pine, pale and splintered, each one stamped with a serial number and a date. Some of the crates have not been moved in so long that dust has settled into a soft grey blanket over their lids. Others have been recently shifted, their dust patterns broken, the wood beneath still clean. The floor is packed earth covered in a layer of sawdust that muffles footsteps. In the quiet, you can hear the creak of settling wood and the drip of water somewhere distant. And beneath that, a sound almost below the threshold of hearing: a low hum, the resonance of stored Qi vibrating in its containers. It is the sound of a held breath.

The locked room at the back has a door of heavier iron than the front entrance. The nameplate has been scratched out with something sharp and violent; not removed, not painted over, but gouged, repeatedly, as if the person who did it wanted to erase not just the name but the fact that there had ever been a name at all.

**The Judgment Chamber (Commission for Celestial Purity)**

You descend. The corridors grow narrower as you go deeper beneath the Ministry. The walls change from painted plaster to bare stone to bedrock. The torches in their sconces burn with a low, steady flame that does not flicker. They are not ordinary torches. The flame is white at the core, blue at the edges, and it casts shadows that point away from the flame regardless of where you stand. The light has no source; it is simply present, a property of the air itself.

The chamber at the bottom is a perfect cylinder carved from living rock. The walls are smooth, seamless, as if the room was not built but excavated by something patient and precise. The ceiling is invisible. The light that fills the room comes from nowhere and everywhere, grey and uniform, stripping all color from skin and cloth alike. Your own hands look unfamiliar in this light. They look like someone else's hands.

The single chair at the center of the room is made of dark wood, unadorned, armless. It sits on a circular stone platform slightly raised from the floor. The grain of the wood seems to move when you look at it indirectly, a slow swirling pattern like water drawn toward a drain. The chair has been used many times. The arms are polished to a dull gleam by countless palms.

The temperature changes as you sit. The stone beneath your feet is cold. The air at waist level is cool. At shoulder height it is warmer, almost body temperature. The chamber is layered with invisible strata of heat and cold. You are caught between them, held in a column of air that belongs to neither. No sound reaches this room from the world above. The silence is absolute. You can hear your own heartbeat reflected from the stone walls. You can hear the blood moving in your veins. The figure on the dais exists in this silence as a disturbance, a presence that the quiet must make room for. When she speaks, the silence does not break. It simply rearranges itself around her words.

**The Imperial Archives Reading Room**

The reading room occupies the southwest corner of the Ministry's west wing, where the afternoon sun reaches through a row of tall, narrow windows set high in the wall. The windows are grimy with decades of accumulated dust, and the light that passes through them is amber and soft, the color of old varnish. It falls in long rectangles across the reading tables, illuminating the grain of the wood and the faint, circular stains left by generations of teacups.

The room is warm. The heat comes from a single iron stove in the corner, fed with coal that burns with a low, steady glow. The stove's pipe rises through the ceiling through a hole cut decades ago. The hole is lined with tin that has turned black with soot. The warmth has a particular smell: hot iron, coal dust, and the slow release of fragrance from the camphor blocks placed between the book stacks to repel insects. It is the smell of preservation, of things kept safe from time.

The tables are massive, each one a single slab of oak supported by iron legs bolted to the floor. They are scarred with the marks of study: ink stains, knife cuts, circular burns from candles placed too close to the edge. One table bears a deep groove running its entire length, as if someone once drew a blade across it in a moment of frustration or despair. No one has repaired it. No one has commented on it.

The shelves rise from floor to ceiling on all walls, their tops lost in the amber gloom. The books are bound in leather and cloth, their spines cracked, their titles faded. Some are so old that their bindings have crumbled to dust, leaving only naked signatures of paper held together by string. A small window near the archivist's desk is open a crack, letting in a thin stream of cold air that moves the dust motes in slow spirals. The air carries the faint scent of wet stone and, from somewhere distant, woodsmoke. Someone is burning something in the courtyard. The smoke mixes with the camphor and the coal and the old paper, and the combination becomes the taste of knowledge: dry, patient, interminable.

**The Abandoned Room (Unused Ministry Wing)**

The room is at the end of a corridor that does not appear on any official map. The Ministry has three floors. This corridor leads to a fourth floor that does not exist, or no longer exists, or was never meant to exist. The door is warped in its frame, swollen by decades of humidity, and it sticks when you push it open. The wood groans in protest. The sound is very loud in the emptiness.

Inside, the room is small, ten feet square, with a single window that looks out onto an air shaft. The window glass is so thick with grime that the sky beyond is visible only as a suggestion, a lighter patch of grey. The air is stale and tastes of dust and the faint, sweet decay of old paper. It is the taste of a place that has been forgotten by the people who maintain the Ministry. The cleaning staff do not come here. The inspectors do not check this room. It exists in a blind spot, a pocket of neglect in a building where every official inch is accounted for.

A single desk sits against the far wall. It is plain, unadorned, Ministry issue from thirty years ago. The varnish has cracked and peeled in long strips, revealing pale wood beneath. The surface is bare. There are no ink stains, no paper cuts, no signs of use. But the desk has been moved recently. The dust on the floor shows drag marks leading from the center of the room to the wall. Someone pulled it into position. Someone has been using this room.

A burned candle stub sits at the center of the desk, its wax pooled and hardened in a rough circle. The wick is black and curled. The candle was allowed to burn down completely, not extinguished. A small pile of ash on the desktop is all that remains of the papers they burned. The ash is grey, fine, unreadable. No words survived. No secrets remained. The floorboards near the desk are scored with the marks of a chair being shifted back and forth. The pattern suggests long hours of sitting, of writing, of waiting. Someone spent many nights in this room.

The air smells faintly of sandalwood. The same expensive, imported sandalwood from the warning note. He was here. He was here recently. He is gone now.

**The Teahouse of Whispered Petitions at Midday (Eastern Market)**

At midday, the teahouse at the end of the Eastern Market alley becomes a different place. It is louder, less careful. The door stands open during business hours, revealing a narrow room packed with small tables where merchants take their midday rest. The bamboo matting is worn soft by sandals and stained by years of spilled tea. Menus and notices are pinned to the beams, and a handwritten poem about the moon hangs above the door.

The air is thick and warm and carries the smell of roasted barley, osmanthus flowers, and the sharp bite of cheap tea boiled too long. A large kettle sits on a charcoal brazier near the back, its lid rattling with the force of the steam inside. The woman who runs the teahouse is old, perhaps sixty, with hands that have been burned by hot water so many times that the skin is smooth and pale. She does not look at customers directly. She watches them in reflections: in the polished surface of the kettle, in the window glass, in the surface of her tea.

The patrons at midday are a cross section of the city's middle layers. A paper merchant argues with a supplier over a shipment that arrived damaged. Two clerks from the Ministry of Rites share a plate of steamed dumplings, eating in the careful, silent way of people who are used to being overheard. An old soldier sits alone at the corner table, his tea untouched, his eyes fixed on something only he can see. No one looks at anyone else for too long. That is the etiquette of this place. You come here to be alone among others.

The light comes from the open door and from the three paper lanterns hanging at different heights. Their light is amber, soft, pooling on some tables and leaving others in shadow. Every table has a story. Every shadow holds a conversation that was never meant to be heard. The teahouse does not judge. The teahouse only listens.

## Part One: The Warning

### The Morning Briefing

The adventure opens on a grey morning in the Outer Court. The players are gathered in their shared office, a cramped chamber in the Bureau of Harmonious Records.

**Read Aloud:**

The office smells of old paper and the cheap tallow candles that burn in every ministry. The walls are bare stone, unadorned except for the cracks that map the building's slow decline. A single window looks out onto a grey courtyard where a dead tree stands. No one has removed it. No one has commented on it. The desk at the center of the room is stacked with scrolls you have not finished cataloguing. You have been here three weeks and you are already behind.

The only decoration is a faded calligraphy scroll on the far wall. The characters read: "Harmony is the thread that binds the empire." The ink has faded to a pale brown. The scroll hangs slightly crooked. No one has fixed it. No one has commented on that either.

A junior scribe enters without knocking, which never happens.

The scribe is pale. Their hands are shaking. They deliver the morning briefing scroll, bow, and flee.

**The Scribe's Words:**

Their voice is barely above a whisper: "I apologize for the intrusion, Honored Ones. I was told to deliver this directly. I was told not to speak to anyone on the way." They press the scroll into the nearest player's hands. Their fingers are cold. "Please. Read it carefully. I have to go. I should not be here." They are gone before anyone can respond. The sound of their footsteps fades down the corridor. Then silence.

**Host Note:** The scribe is genuinely terrified. They know something is coming, though not the details. They were chosen because they are the most junior, the most expendable. If the players try to stop them or ask questions, the scribe apologizes repeatedly and says nothing more. They were told what would happen if they spoke.

The scroll contains the usual bureaucratic notices: promotions, reassignments, ritual schedule changes. The full text reads:

**The Morning Briefing Scroll:**

_By order of the Grand Secretariat, the following promotions are confirmed: Third Archivist Hu to Second Archivist, effective the first day of the eighth month. Scribe Lin to Senior Scribe, subject to ritual confirmation. Junior Censor Zhou to Censor, with all attendant responsibilities and liabilities._

_The following appointments are rescinded: Sixteenth Assistant Recorder Fei, for failure to complete the required purification rites. The position remains vacant pending review._

_Ritual schedule for the coming month: The Festival of Ancestors requires full attendance from all Outer Court officials. Appropriate vestments are mandatory. Inappropriate vestments will be noted._

_A reminder: Unauthorized discussion of Audit procedures is prohibited. Unauthorized discussion of Audit results is prohibited. Unauthorized discussion of the Commission for Celestial Purity is prohibited._

_A second reminder: The Commission for Celestial Purity has requested complete personnel records for all departments in the Outer Court. Departments are reminded to ensure their records are current and accurate. Inaccuracies will be investigated._

**Host Note:** The second reminder is the hidden signal. The players may not recognize its significance immediately, but it means the Commission is already gathering data. The Audit has begun in secret before it begins in public.

But clipped to the scroll is a smaller, unsealed note, handwritten in a rushed script:

> _"It begins in seven days. The Commission has already started collecting names. Burn this after reading. If anyone asks, you never received it."_

There is no signature. The handwriting is precise, with a slight tremor on the downstrokes.

**Host Note:** The note was sent by the Shadow. He sends these warnings to people he considers potentially useful. The players do not know this yet. Let them speculate.

### The First Decision

The players now know an Audit is imminent. They have one week. What do they do?

Let them talk. This is a roleplaying scene, not a dice scene. Encourage them to:

- Discuss what they know about Audits (roll Intellect + Lore, TN 10 for basic information, TN 16 for details about how they work).
- Consider their own vulnerability (review their Ritual Harmony and Faction Standing values).
- Reach out to any contacts they may have from character creation.
- Speculate about who sent the note.
- Examine the note itself for physical clues: the paper quality, the ink composition, the fold pattern.
- Observe the behavior of colleagues in the Ministry after the scribe's visit. Does anyone seem to be watching? Does anyone avoid eye contact?
- Discuss what each character fears most about the Audit. This is a character development opportunity, not a mechanical one.

**The Atmosphere After the Note:**

### What the Players Can Do Now

The players have received the warning. They have one week. Here are obvious avenues they might pursue in the first hour after receiving the note:

3. **Try to trace how the note was delivered.** The junior scribe was sent through a chain of intermediaries. ### NPCs the Players Might Consult

**Master Scribe Xun (Consultation NPC)**

Master Scribe Xun has served in the Outer Court for forty seven years. He remembers the previous Harmonization Audit and the one before that. He can be found in the Archive reading room, where he spends his days copying texts no one will read. His hands are stained with ink. His eyes have the distant look of someone who has seen too much.

His dialogue:

"I saw you coming. You have the look of people who have heard a bad song and cannot forget the tune. Sit down. Do not tell me what you know. Tell me what you fear."

If asked about Audits: "The Audit is a name for a process that has no name. They come every few years. They take who they take. The rest of us pretend we did not see. You want my advice? Make yourself small. Make yourself useful. Make sure someone above you wants you to stay. That is the only protection that works."

If pressed about the handwriting on the note: "That script. That tremor. I have seen it before. On old files. On records from fifteen years ago. There is a man in the Court. He has worked in the shadows for years. He writes warnings. I do not know his name. I do not want to. Knowing his name would be a liability I cannot afford at my age."

If asked who the man is: "He is called the Shadow by those who know of him. He knows this place better than I do. He sees things. He knows things. He tries to warn people. Sometimes they listen. Sometimes they cannot be saved. I have never met him. I have seen his work."

**Xun's Expanded Dialogue:**

_If the players challenge his authority or accuse him of withholding information:_

He does not flinch. His hands, stained with ink, remain still on the table. He looks at the speaker with the patience of someone who has been looked at this way before. "You are young. You have been in the Court for weeks. I have been in the Court for forty seven years. I have seen people who asked the wrong questions disappear from their desks before lunch. I have seen people who pushed too hard reassigned to border posts where the Dusk eats them from the outside in. I am not withholding information. I am withholding danger. If you cannot tell the difference, you will not last until your first anniversary."

_If the players show genuine empathy, asking about his health, his sleep, the toll of forty seven years:_

His eyes change. Something flickers behind the distant gaze. He is quiet for a long moment. When he speaks, his voice is softer. "No one has asked me that in years. They come to me for information, for warnings, for the names of the dead. No one asks how I am. The answer is that I am tired. I am very tired. I have copied texts that no one will read. I have watched Audits consume the talented and the kind and the unlucky. I have outlived everyone who entered this Ministry with me. That is not a triumph. That is an accident of timing. I stay because someone must remember. When I stop remembering, I will die. And that will be a kindness."

_If the players offer a creative solution (such as asking him to secretly copy records or provide a safe place to hide documents):_

He considers. He does not answer immediately. He picks up his brush, dips it in ink, and writes a single character on a scrap of paper. He turns it so the players can see. The character means "listen." "I am a scribe. I copy documents. That is my function. No one notices what a scribe copies because no one believes a scribe understands what they write. I have been copying documents for forty seven years. I have copies of things that would burn this Ministry to the ground. I do not show them to anyone because showing them would mean choosing a side. I have avoided choosing a side for forty seven years. That is how I have survived. If you ask me to choose one now, you ask me to end a streak that has carried me through five sweeping reorganizations of the Court, six Audits, and the deaths of everyone I ever worked with. I will think about it. Do not ask me again until I have decided."

_His mask slips. His true feelings emerge:_

He is writing when he says it, not looking up, as if he is talking to the paper instead of the players. His brush moves in steady strokes. "You want to know what I really think? I think the Court is a machine that turns people into fuel. I think the Golden Orthodoxy knows this and uses it. I think the Verdant Path knows this and tries to stop it. I think neither of them will succeed because the machine does not care about intentions. It only cares about momentum. I have copied more Audit records than I can count. I have seen the same patterns repeat for decades. The names change. The accusations change. The outcome never changes. Someone is always taken. The machine is always fed. And I am still here, copying the same words, knowing they will not save anyone. The ink on my hands is not ink. It is the residue of everything I have failed to prevent." He stops. He sets down his brush. He does not look up. "I said too much. Forget what I just said. I am an old man who talks to himself. It means nothing."

_His mask slips. His true feelings emerge:_

## Part Two: The Three Demands

Within hours of the warning, three schools reach out to the players. Each has a request. Each request is an opportunity and a trap. The players cannot satisfy all three. They must choose.

### The Golden Orthodoxy's Request

A senior official from the Bureau of Harmonious Narrative arrives at the players' office. He is Senior Censor Wei.

**Read Aloud:**

The knock at your door is precise: three taps, evenly spaced, exactly one second apart. The man who enters wears the deep gold robes of the Bureau of Harmonious Narrative. His sleeves are embroidered with the eight trigrams. His face reveals nothing. He stands in the center of your office as if he owns it. He probably does. The air around him is cold. He does not apologize for arriving unannounced. He does not wait to be invited. He simply stands there, looking at each of you in turn, and begins.

**Censor Wei's Opening Dialogue:**

"Good morning. I am Senior Censor Wei of the Bureau of Harmonious Narrative. I apologize for the unannounced visit. The matter is urgent."

He waits for acknowledgment before continuing.

"The Bureau is compiling a comprehensive report on heterodox influences within the Outer Court. This report will inform the Commission's decisions during the coming Audit. Your cooperation would be valued. Your hesitation would be noted."

If the players ask what he wants:

He reaches into his sleeve and produces a folded document. "I need you to confirm the associations of five individuals. You work alongside most of them. You know their views. Simply sign the statement confirming what you have observed. Nothing more. Nothing less."

If the players ask what happens to the five:

"That is not your concern. Your concern is the accuracy of your testimony. The rest belongs to the Commission for Celestial Purity. They will do what they do. It is not my place to speculate on outcomes."

If the players hesitate:

His tone shifts. It does not become loud. It becomes quieter. Colder. "Let me be clear. This is not a request for assistance. This is an invitation to demonstrate your loyalty to orthodox doctrine. The Bureau is watching. The Commission will see your name either on this document or on another document. The difference is which side of the page your name appears."

If the players ask who the five are:

He unfolds the document and reads the names without emotion. "Second Archivist Yu. Scribe Min. Clerk Fen. Recorder Jia. Archivist Chen. You know them. You work alongside the first four. You have seen them at their desks. The fifth name is different; Archivist Chen is no longer at his desk. That is precisely why the Bureau wants his name confirmed. You know what they believe. Sign the document and return it to me by the end of the day."

If the players refuse:

His face does not change. He retrieves the document, folds it precisely, and places it back in his sleeve. "I see. The Bureau will note your independence." He turns to leave. At the door, he pauses. His back is still to the players. "I hope, for your sake, that the Audit finds you as principled as you pretend to be." He leaves without closing the door.

**The Document Wei Asks the Players to Sign:**

> **Denunciation Statement**
>
> _I, the undersigned, do hereby confirm the following statements to be true and accurate to the best of my knowledge:_
>
> _That Second Archivist Yu has expressed views regarding the distribution of Qi that diverge from the teachings of the Harmonious Way, specifically advocating for broader access to Qi cultivation among the common classes._
>
> _That Scribe Min has been observed in the company of known members of the Verdant Path on multiple occasions, including meetings at locations outside the Ministry complex._
>
> _That Clerk Fen has been heard to question the necessity of the annual Purification Tithe, using language that suggests disloyalty to the Throne and sympathy with reformist factions._
>
> _That Recorder Jia has failed to report the heterodox statements of colleagues, thereby participating in a conspiracy of silence that undermines the integrity of the Outer Court._
>
> _That Archivist Chen has been found in possession of texts not sanctioned by the Bureau of Harmonious Narrative, including works by authors whose names appear on the List of Prohibited Voices._
>
> _I affirm that these statements are made freely, without coercion, and in full knowledge of the penalties for false testimony. I understand that the Commission for Celestial Purity may use this testimony in its proceedings._
>
> _Signature: _____________________
> _Date: _____________________
> _Seal: _____________________

**Host Note:** The five names are real people. Four of them are colleagues the players work alongside every day; the fifth, Archivist Chen, is already a fugitive, which is why the Bureau wants his name on a document. If the players sign, these five will be investigated. They will almost certainly be Disappeared. The players will see their desks empty. If the players refuse, Wei will remember. The Golden Orthodoxy will remember. This is not a choice that goes away.

If the players sign, they gain +10 Faction Standing with the Golden Orthodoxy and +5 Ritual Harmony. They will also carry the weight of what they have done.

If the players refuse, the official's cordiality evaporates. "I see. The Bureau will note your... independence." The players lose 10 Faction Standing with the Golden Orthodoxy. The official leaves. The players now have an enemy.

**Censor Wei's Expanded Dialogue:**

_If the players challenge his authority, questioning whether the Bureau has the right to demand testimony:_

_If the players show genuine empathy, acknowledging the difficulty of his position:_

For a fraction of a second, something cracks. His carefully composed expression wavers. He looks down at his hands. When he speaks, his voice is quieter, less certain. "You think I enjoy this. _If the players offer a creative solution, such as agreeing to sign a modified statement that names only the most clearly guilty party:_

He listens. He does not interrupt. When they finish, he is silent for a long moment, studying them as if seeing them for the first time. "That is not how this works. _His mask slips. His true feelings emerge:_

This happens only if the players press him on the name Archivist Chen, the last name on the list. The name he lingered on. The name that made his heartbeat change.

His voice drops. "Tao was my student. Fifteen years ago. He was the best copyist I ever trained. He had a gift for the precise phrase, the exact word, the perfect rendering of meaning into text. That someone will be less kind than I am. I am trying to save him, in the only way the system allows. By being the one who takes him. Because I will make it quick. Someone else would make it last."

His composure returns like a mask being lowered. His face goes blank. He squares his shoulders. "We are done here. You have my offer. You have until sundown."

### The Verdant Path's Request

A representative of the Verdant Path approaches the players more quietly: a note slipped under a door, asking for a meeting in a teahouse far from the Court. The representative is Junior Archivist Lin.

**The Note:**

The note is written on cheap paper in a hurried hand. It reads:

> _I need to speak with you. This is about the warning you received. I know who sent it. I can help you. Come to the Eastern Market teahouse. The one with no sign. Come alone. Come at dusk. Tell no one._

The handwriting is different from the Shadow's note. This is younger, less practiced, more urgent.

**The Teahouse Meeting:**

**Read Aloud:**

The address leads you through the Eastern Market, past stalls being closed for the night, past workers sweeping the streets, past a group of Garrison soldiers sharing a flask in a doorway. The teahouse is at the end of a narrow alley. It has no sign above the door. The windows are dark. You would have walked past it if you were not looking for it.

Inside, the air is warm and smells of jasmine tea. The room is empty except for a single figure at the back table. She is young, maybe twenty, wearing the plain grey robes of a junior archivist. Her hands are wrapped around a cup of tea that has gone cold. She stands when you enter, almost knocks over the table, sits down again. She is not good at this.

**Junior Archivist Lin's Dialogue:**

"Thank you for coming. I was not sure you would. I am Lin. Junior Archivist Lin. I work in the Eastern Repository. I do not normally do this."

She takes a breath. She closes her eyes for a moment. When she opens them, she seems to have steadied herself.

"There is a man. A former official. He worked for the Ministry of Works until three months ago. He discovered something about the Golden Orthodoxy. Something about money. About imperial funds that were supposed to go to border reconstruction but instead went to a private account."

If the players ask his name:

"His name is Tao Chen. Former Third Inspector of Works. He fled when they started asking questions. The Verdant Path has been hiding him. But the Audit will change everything. They will search everywhere. They will find him. We need to get him out of the capital before the Commission starts its work."

If the players ask why she trusts them:

"I do not trust you. I trust that you received the warning note from the Shadow. Anyone he warned is worth taking a risk on. He does not waste his warnings on people who do not matter."

If the players ask what they need to do:

"We need him through the South Gate checkpoint. The guards have been told to watch for him. His description has been circulated. He has a chance to survive this."

If the players ask what Tao Chen knows:

"He has records. Copies of the financial documents. The Golden Orthodoxy has been taking imperial funds for years. The money goes to accounts controlled by the Crimson Lineage. Specific accounts. Specific amounts. Specific dates. He can prove it. That is why they want him so badly. That is why they will kill him if they catch him."

If the players ask why the Verdant Path is helping:

"Because it is right. Because the Golden Orthodoxy should not be above the law. Because if the Court is to be saved, it must first be cleaned. I know that sounds naive. I do not care. Someone has to do what is right, even if they do it badly."

### The Iron Calculation's Request

The Iron Calculation's request arrives through official channels: a formal memorandum from the State Planning Commission requesting "temporary reassignment of personnel" for a "special inventory project."

**The Memorandum:**

The memorandum arrives on heavy paper bearing the seal of the State Planning Commission. It is stamped with the personal seal of Commissioner Zhang. The text reads:

_To the Office of Harmonious Records, Outer Court. Re: Temporary Personnel Reassignment._

_The undersigned requests the temporary reassignment of the following personnel for a period of no more than seven days, effective immediately: [player names]._

_Purpose: Special Inventory Project, Imperial Qi Reserve. Classification level: Internal. Authorized by: Commissioner Zhang, State Planning Commission._

_The selected personnel have been identified as possessing the requisite discretion and analytical competence. Their regular duties are to be suspended during the reassignment period. Any questions or concerns should be directed to the undersigned in writing._

_Commissioner Zhang does not respond well to questions. This is made clear by the absence of any contact information._

**Meeting Commissioner Zhang:**

**Read Aloud:**

Commissioner Zhang is not what you expected. He is a small man in plain grey robes, sitting behind a desk buried in papers. He does not look up when you enter. He does not greet you. He simply points to a stack of ledgers on a side table and says: "Those are the records for the Imperial Qi Reserve for the last three years. Look at the third quarter of last year and the fourth quarter. Tell me what you notice. Do not tell me what you think. Tell me what the numbers say."

He still has not looked at you. He does not need to. The numbers are all that matter.

**Host Note:** Commissioner Zhang (Presence 8, Intellect 18) is clipped, efficient, and utterly uninterested in social niceties. He wants data. He dislikes excuses. He used to work for a Crimson Lineage family. He knows their methods. He left because he could not stomach them. This investigation is personal for him, though he will never admit it.

**Ledger Examination Scene 1: The Receiving Records**

Commissioner Zhang leads the players to a windowless room in the State Planning Commission. The walls are lined with shelves, each shelf packed with identical ledgers bound in leather. He points to a table where three ledgers are already open.

**Read Aloud:**

"The Imperial Qi Reserve receives contributions from the Nine Domains quarterly. These are the receipts for the last two years. Look at the totals for the third quarter of last year. Then compare them to the fourth quarter. Tell me what you notice."

Intellect + Investigation TN 12 reveals that the third quarter total is 8,720 units of refined Qi. The fourth quarter total is 7,920 units. The difference is 800 units. The discrepancy is not noted in any official summary. No one has flagged it. No one has commented on it. It is simply there, waiting to be noticed.

Zhang's response: "Eight hundred units. That is enough to power a city ward for a month. It is not an accounting error. Someone altered the receipts. Someone received the full shipment and recorded a lower amount. The question is who, and where the Qi went."

**Ledger Examination Scene 2: The Transport Logs**

**Read Aloud:**

The second set of records is from the Imperial Transport Bureau. Each shipment of refined Qi requires a manifest signed by three officials: the sender at the provincial depot, the transporter, and the receiver at the capital warehouse.

Commissioner Zhang points to a series of entries from the past six months. "The manifests appear to be in order. But look at the signatures. Compare the transporter's signature across these five documents."

Intellect + Investigation TN 14 reveals that the transporter's signature is identical on all five documents. The ink is the same color. The pressure is the same. The angle is the same. They were signed by the same hand, despite being dated months apart and involving different shipment routes from different provinces. The transporter's signature is a forgery.

Zhang's response: "Someone is signing for shipments that were never transported. The Qi is being diverted before it reaches the capital. The forged signature belongs to a man named Sun Jiao, a transport supervisor who died six months ago. Someone has been using his name."

**Host Note:** The name Sun Jiao will appear again in Part Three. The Shadow's note to the Disappeared predecessor mentions this name.

**Ledger Examination Scene 3: The Warehouse Records**

**Read Aloud:**

The third set of records takes you to an actual warehouse on the northern edge of the city. It is a grey stone building with iron doors and no windows. The surrounding streets are empty. There are no merchants, no carts, no pedestrians. The area has a feeling of being forgotten.

A single clerk sits at a desk near the entrance. He is young, maybe twenty five, and he is sweating despite the cold. His name is Wei Liang. He is the night inventory supervisor. He does not meet your eyes.

**Warehouse Clerk Dialogue (Wei Liang):**

If questioned directly, Wei Liang stammers and claims everything is in order. He offers to show the players the inventory books. He talks too much. He fills silences with unnecessary details. He is clearly trying to be helpful in a way that is unhelpful.

Presence + Insight TN 14 reveals that he is terrified. Not of the players. Of someone else. He keeps glancing at a locked door at the back of the warehouse. There is a nameplate on the door, but the name has been scratched out.

If pressed further (Presence + Intimidation TN 14 or Presence + Persuasion TN 16), he breaks:

"I did not know. I swear I did not know. They told me it was surplus. They said it was authorized. The manifests came from the top. I just signed where they told me to sign. I have a family. My wife. My daughter. They said if I talked, my family would be reassigned to the border garrisons. You know what that means. You know what happens at the border."

If asked who "they" are:

"I do not know their names. They wore masks. Every time. But the seal on the manifests. I recognized it. It was the seal of the Crimson Lineage. The old families. They have their own seals. They do not need permission from anyone. They do not answer to the Commission."

If asked to testify:

He refuses absolutely. His face goes pale. "I will deny everything. You cannot protect me. No one can protect me. The old families own this city. They own the Commission. They own the guards who will come for me the moment you leave this room. If I testify, I am dead. My family is dead. I am sorry. I cannot."

He will not be moved. Not by threats. Not by promises. He has seen what happens to people who cross the old families.

**The Discovery: Crimson Lineage Connection**

After the warehouse visit, the pieces connect. The forged signatures. The missing Qi. The manifests sealed with a family crest. The name Sun Jiao, dead for six months, still signing for shipments.

Commissioner Zhang summarizes the findings:

"An old family is stockpiling Qi. This is not theft for profit. This is not simple corruption. This is preparation. They are gathering power for something. Qi on this scale is not wealth. It is fuel. The question is what they are fueling."

He pauses.

"I worked for the Crimson Lineage once. I know how they think. They do not steal for pleasure. They steal for purpose. And they do not cover their tracks this well unless the purpose is large."

**Host Note:** This is the moment the players realize the Audit is not the only threat. Something larger is moving beneath the surface of the Court. The Crimson Lineage is preparing for something. The Audit may be a distraction, or it may be a tool, or it may be unrelated. The players do not know yet. Let them wonder.

### Creative Solutions and Third Options

Players are resourceful. They may attempt approaches not covered above. Here is guidance for common creative solutions:

**Trying to Play the Schools Against Each Other:** A player might attempt to tell the Golden Orthodoxy about the Verdant Path's smuggling plan while also helping the Verdant Path. This is dangerous but possible. Presence + Deception TN 18 to manage the double cross. On failure, both schools learn the player is untrustworthy. Faction Standing decreases with both. On success, the player gains temporary advantage but has created enemies who will eventually compare notes.

**Attempting Blackmail:** If the players gather evidence against a school (the embezzlement documents, the forged signatures, the Qi theft), they might try to blackmail their way out of the demands. Presence + Intimidation TN 16. On success, the school backs down and does not press the issue. On failure, the school reports the blackmail attempt to the Commission. The player faces additional scrutiny during the Audit.

**Fleeing the Capital:** A player might try to leave before the Audit. This requires getting past the gate checkpoints without proper documentation. Swiftness + Stealth TN 18. On success, the player escapes but is marked as a deserter. Their character becomes an NPC, hunted by the Commission. On failure, they are arrested and taken directly to the Audit. Their Risk increases by two levels.

**Appealing to a Higher Authority:** If a player has a patron or faction connection from character creation, they might appeal for protection. This costs 10 Celestial Favor and provides immunity from one school's demand. However, the patron's attention is now on the player. The patron will expect repayment. The player has acquired a debt they cannot predict the cost of.

**Doing Nothing:** Refusing all three requests is a valid choice. The players face the Audit without faction support but also without faction obligations. No one owes them anything. No one has leverage over them. Their fate rests entirely on their own actions and testimony. This is a clean path, but it is not a safe one.

### The Choice

The players can, with creativity, satisfy two of the three requests. Satisfying all three is nearly impossible: the Golden Orthodoxy and Verdant Path requests are in direct conflict. The Iron Calculation's request is unrelated to that conflict but requires considerable time.

Let the players strategize. Do not rush them. This is the adventure's central decision point.

### Weather and Time: The Morning Before

The Host may read this passage at the start of the day before the Audit, when the players enter the Preparation phase.

The sun rises late over Xiaoyuan on the morning before the Audit. The haze that usually begins as a thin veil and thickens through the day is already dense at dawn, swallowing the eastern towers of the Imperial complex before the first bells have finished ringing. The light that reaches the streets is diffuse, sourceless, like the glow inside a tent made of dirty canvas. The city is wrapped in a cocoon of grey.

The air is warmer than it has been all week. An unnatural warmth, damp and oppressive, as if the city is breathing on you. The frost that coated the cobblestones yesterday has been replaced by a fine layer of moisture that makes every surface gleam. The stones look oiled. The rooftops look wet. Your own skin feels slick within minutes of stepping outside. The warmth is not a relief from the cold. It is a different kind of pressure, heavier, more intimate. It presses against your chest and makes each breath feel like a negotiation.

The Ministry corridors are quieter than you have ever heard them. The usual bustle of scribes and messengers has been replaced by a careful, deliberate silence. People move as if walking on eggshells. Voices are kept low. No one meets anyone else's eyes. The building itself seems to be holding its breath, waiting for the axe to fall.

As you pass the main entrance, you notice a detail that stops you cold. The scroll that has hung in the entrance hall for as long as anyone can remember, the one that reads "Harmony is the thread that binds the empire," has been taken down. The hooks remain. The nails remain. The rectangle of darker paint where the scroll protected the wall from the sun remains. But the scroll itself is gone. No one has explained why. No one has commented on it. The absence is more powerful than the presence ever was.

A junior clerk passes you in the corridor. They do not look up. They are carrying a stack of files. The top file has a name you recognize. It is one of your colleagues. The file is going to the Commission. The clerk does not meet your eyes. They do not need to. The message is clear. The machine is already in motion. The Audit has begun before it has begun.

## Part Four: The Preparation

### The Final Day

The day before the Audit is announced. The players have one final day to act. Use the Host's Codex Audit Preparation rules (Phase One: The Whisper) to structure this session.

The players may:

- Call in favors to reduce their Audit risk.
- Hide or destroy incriminating evidence.
- Make final deals with schools or figures.
- Attempt to flee (not recommended for a first adventure, but allowed).
- Attempt to throw someone else to the wolves (see Audit rules).

### The Final Day Timeline

Use this timeline to pace the session and create a sense of mounting pressure. Each hour brings a new development.

**Dawn (First Light):** The players wake to find a notification slipped under their doors. The Audit will begin at dawn tomorrow. All personnel are to remain in their assigned quarters until summoned. Travel between ministries is restricted without a writ. The writ must be obtained from the Commission. The Commission is not issuing writs.

**Early Morning (Hour Two):** A messenger arrives with a formal summons for each player. The summons is written in formal calligraphy and bears the seal of the Commission for Celestial Purity. It reads: "You are hereby summoned to appear before the Commission for Celestial Purity at the ninth hour on the day following this notice. Failure to appear will be treated as an admission of guilt." The calligraphy is beautiful. The message is death.

**Mid Morning (Hour Four):** The players' office is visited by a Commission agent who catalogs all documents on the premises. The agent is professional and silent. They wear grey gloves. They do not answer questions. They do not make eye contact. They sort through the players' desks, opening drawers, reading notes, examining everything. They take three scrolls and leave. The players do not know which scrolls were taken.

**Noon (Hour Six):** The city falls quiet. The markets are ordered to close early. People retreat indoors. The streets empty. The players have their last opportunity to reach out to contacts. Anyone they wish to speak to must be sought out now.

**Afternoon (Hour Eight):** The players receive any final messages from NPCs. Last minute deals can be struck. Last minute betrayals can be arranged. The Commission's patrols increase. Movement becomes more difficult.

**Dusk (Hour Ten):** Curfew begins. The players should be in their quarters. Commission agents patrol the corridors of the Ministry. Any player caught outside their assigned area faces additional scrutiny. Risk increases by one level.

**Night (Hour Twelve):** The quiet before. Let the players talk among themselves. This is a roleplaying moment. There are no more actions to take. There are only choices already made. Let them sit with those choices.

## Part Five: The Audit

### Judgment Day

The Audit is announced. The Commission for Celestial Purity publishes the list of departments under review. The players' department is on the list.

Run the Audit using the Host's Codex procedures:

1. **Phase Two: Investigation.** Each player is summoned for questioning. This is a structured social encounter. The investigator is a junior Justicar (Presence 14, Insight 14). The questions focus on the player's associations, their recent activities, and any "irregularities" in their record.

   For each player, run a brief interrogation scene. The player may answer honestly, lie (Presence + Deception vs the Justicar's Insight of 14), deflect (Presence + Persuasion TN 14), or invoke a patron's protection (costs 5 Celestial Favor, automatic pass).

   The quality of the player's performance in the interrogation adjusts their Risk by one level in either direction.

2. **Phase Three: Judgment.** Resolve the Audit for each player using the Risk table. Read the results publicly. Describe who is cleared, who is sanctioned, and who disappears.

3. **Phase Four: Aftermath.** The Audit ends. The office is quieter than before. Some desks are empty. The surviving players receive new assignments (the work of the disappeared must be done by someone). The players have survived their first Harmonization Audit.

> **Host Toolbox: Running the Audit Smoothly**
>
> The Audit is the climax of the adventure. It can be the most satisfying session or the most stressful, depending on how it is handled. Here are practical suggestions drawn from playtesting:
>
> **Pacing.** The Audit should feel inexorable, not rushed. Give each player their own interrogation scene. Do not combine them. The waiting players should feel the tension of not knowing what is happening behind the closed door. Let them sit with that. The silence between scenes is as important as the scenes themselves.
>
> **Tone.** Justicar Meng is professional, not cruel. She is not enjoying this. Her questions are methodical, not personal. If the players feel personally attacked, the scene becomes adversarial in a way that undermines the Court's chilling bureaucracy. The horror of the Audit is that it is routine. Meng has done this hundreds of times. She will do it hundreds more. The players are not special to her. That is the point.
>
> **Player Agency.** Even during the interrogation, players have choices. They can answer honestly, lie, deflect, invoke protection, or remain silent. Each choice has a mechanical consequence and a narrative consequence. Remind players of their options if they seem stuck. The Audit is a test, not a cutscene.
>
> **The Final Question.** Question 11 ("Do you believe the Court is just?") is designed to have no correct answer. It is a trap. The player's response does not change the mechanical outcome of the Audit, but it does shape the narrative aftermath. A player who answers "yes" has committed to a lie that will haunt them. A player who answers "no" has stated a truth that the Commission will remember. A player who refuses to answer has shown courage that the Court will punish. Let each player discover this on their own. Do not warn them in advance.
>
> **After the Verdict.** Once the Audit results are read, give the players space to react. Do not rush to the aftermath. The moment when a player is Disappeared and led away by the grey figures is the emotional peak of the adventure. Let it land. Let the silence stretch. Let the remaining players sit with what they have witnessed. Then, and only then, move to the aftermath scenes.

### The Judgment Chamber

**Read Aloud:**

The Judgment Chamber is not what you expected. It is not a courtroom. There are no benches for spectators. There is no raised platform for a judge. There is a single chair in the center of the room, facing a raised dais. The dais is empty. The walls are bare grey stone, unadorned, unmarked. The ceiling is lost in shadow. You cannot tell how high it goes.

There are no windows. There are no decorations. There is nothing but the chair, the dais, and you.

You are told to sit in the chair. You sit. It is hard and cold. The door closes behind you with a sound that is louder than it should be. You are alone.

Then the figure on the dais speaks. You did not see her enter. She is simply there. A woman in grey and silver, with a face that reveals nothing. She holds a folder. She opens it. She reads something you cannot see. Then she looks at you.

Junior Justicar Meng, investigator for the Commission for Celestial Purity, begins.

### The Interrogation: Justicar Meng's Questions

She does not introduce herself. She does not explain the procedure. She begins immediately. Each question is delivered in a flat, professional tone. She does not raise her voice. She does not show emotion. She watches. She waits. She takes notes.

**Question 1:**

"State your name, your position, and the names of everyone in your immediate department."

Further Question: "You hesitated on the last name. Why? The Commission notices hesitation. Hesitation is a form of speech. What were you thinking about in that moment before you spoke?"

**Question 2:**

"How long have you held your current position?"

Further Question: "And before that? Where did you serve? Who was your superior? What was their opinion of your work? Do you have a letter of reference from them?"

**Question 3:**

"Do you know why you are here today?"

Further Question (if yes): "Then you understand the seriousness of this proceeding. Tell me what you believe we have found. Tell me in your own words. I will compare your account to ours."

Further Question (if no): "Then let me explain. The Commission has received reports regarding your activities during the past week. We are here to determine their veracity. Your answers will determine your fate."

**Question 4:**

"Have you, at any time during your service, expressed views that could be interpreted as critical of the Harmonious Way?"

Further Question: "I ask again. Have you expressed such views? Silence is not an answer. The Commission interprets silence as confirmation. Speak."

**Question 5:**

"Do you recognize this document?"

She holds up a page. The content depends on what the players have done:

- If they investigated the Qi siphoning, it is a copy of the warehouse log with their names circled.
- If they helped the Verdant Path, it is a witness statement describing people matching them near the route they actually used, either the South Gate or the Riverside jetty.
- If they signed the denunciation, it is a copy of the document they signed.
- If they did nothing, it is simply their personnel file, marked with underlines and question marks.

Further Question: "Where did you obtain this? Who gave it to you? What did you do with it after you had it?"

**Question 6:**

"Who is your patron in the Court? Name them."

Further Question: "And what favors have you asked of them recently? What have they asked of you? The Commission is interested in the flow of obligations. Obligations reveal loyalties. Loyalties reveal motive."

**Question 7:**

"During the week before this Audit, you were observed meeting with individuals associated with the Verdant Path. Describe the nature of those meetings."

Further Question: "I have witnesses. Do not waste my time with denials. The Commission has eyes everywhere. The Commission has ears in every room. The Commission has memory that does not fade."

**Question 8:**

"Your predecessor held this same position. Do you know what happened to them?"

Further Question: "Do you know why they were Disappeared? Do you know what they were doing in the weeks before their Audit? Have you followed a similar path? Think carefully before you answer."

**Question 9:**

"What do you know about the Crimson Lineage's activities in this city?"

Further Question: "I ask because your name appears on several documents that mention the family. Explain the connection. If there is no connection, explain why your name appears."

**Question 10:**

"Is there anything you wish to confess before the Commission reaches its conclusion?"

Further Question (if yes): "Then speak. This is your only opportunity to offer a full accounting. The Commission values confession. Confession is the first step toward reintegration."

Further Question (if no): "Very well. You have made your choice. The Commission will now deliberate."

**Question 11 (Final):**

"One last question. Do you believe the Court is just?"

**Host Note:** This is a test. There is no correct answer.

If the player says yes: "Then you accept whatever judgment follows. Your acceptance is noted."

If the player says no: "Then you have just confirmed what the Commission suspected. Disbelief in the Court's justice is itself a form of heterodoxy. Your answer has been recorded."

If the player deflects: "An evasion. The Commission does not accept evasions. Answer the question directly."

If the player refuses to answer: "Refusal to answer is an answer. It has been recorded."

### Interrogation Variations

Depending on the player's approach, the tone and direction of the interrogation shifts.

**Variation 1: The Cooperative Player**

A player who answers honestly and respectfully, who shows deference to the Commission and does not challenge the Justicar's authority, can shift the tone of the interrogation. Justicar Meng is professional but not cruel. She respects honesty, even when the honesty is damaging.

Tone adjustment: Meng becomes slightly less cold. She does not smile, but her questions become less pointed. She may offer a warning: "Your honesty serves you well. But honesty alone does not protect you from the Audit. The Commission sees all. Remember that. I am telling you this because you have earned the truth."

Mechanical effect: The player's Risk decreases by one level. They gain one additional opportunity to explain any incriminating evidence that arises.

**Variation 2: The Defiant Player**

A player who challenges the Justicar, refuses to answer, shows hostility, or lectures the Commission triggers a different approach. Meng becomes icier. She takes notes more deliberately. She asks the same question multiple times in different ways, looking for inconsistencies.

Tone adjustment: "You are angry. I understand. Many people are angry when they sit in that chair. Anger is not a defense. Anger is evidence of something unresolved. The Commission will resolve it. That is our purpose."

Mechanical effect: The player's Risk increases by one level. The interrogation lasts longer. Additional questions arise from the player's attitude, and Meng probes deeper into any area where the player showed resistance.

**Variation 3: The Deceptive Player**

A player who attempts to lie must succeed on contested Presence + Deception checks against Meng's Insight (14). Each successful lie requires another check to maintain the deception. A failure means Meng catches the contradiction.

On detection: "You are lying. I will ask again. Do not insult this Commission with another fabrication. The truth will emerge regardless of your efforts. The only question is whether you will be the one to speak it."

Mechanical effect: A single detected lie increases Risk by one level. Multiple lies increase it further. However, a player who successfully maintains a deception throughout the entire interrogation gains +5 Ritual Harmony for "successful integration." They have convinced the Commission of a falsehood. That is a kind of victory.

You have faced better interrogators. _If the Shadow is a player character, you have a technique for this: answer every question with a truth that implies a different falsehood. The truth satisfies Meng's Insight; the falsehood protects you. Requires Presence + Deception TN 16._

> **Duke of Eternal Night (Vampire):** The Cooperative variation is the easiest path for you. You have centuries of practice in deference to authority you do not respect. Bowing to the Commission costs you nothing. You have bowed to worse powers for longer.

> **Iron Bone (Werewolf):** The Defiant variation is the most dangerous for you. Hostility triggers your wolf. If the party chooses defiance, you should not be the one to speak. Let someone else take point. Make a Resolve TN 14 check to stay silent during the hostile exchanges.

> **Celestial Book (Wizard):** The Cooperative variation has a hidden benefit that only a wizard would notice: Meng's Qi guard drops when she is engaged in a respectful conversation. If the players need to make a subtle Qi reading of her aura or emotional state, the Cooperative approach provides the best opportunity.
>
> **Bone Script (Druid):** You will not lie and you will not perform anger, and Meng will find both harder to work with than either. Your variation is precision. When she summarizes your answer inaccurately, correct the summary. Every time. Politely. The Host should track how many corrections it takes before she stops summarizing.

> **Stray Stroke (Wildcard):** The deceptive player variation does not apply to you, and the Host should say so plainly at the table. You are not lying. No contested Deception check is called for, because there is nothing to contest: the papers are real. What Meng can catch is a manner that does not match the trade printed on the permit, so roll Presence + Performance against her Insight of 14, once, at the start.

**Justicar Meng's Expanded Dialogue:**

_If the players challenge her authority, questioning the legitimacy of the Commission or the Audit itself:_

Her pen stops moving. She looks up. Her face is unreadable, but her eyes sharpen. "You question the legitimacy of this proceeding. That is a dangerous line of inquiry. The Commission for Celestial Purity derives its mandate from the Grand Secretariat, which derives its mandate from the Throne. Do you wish to proceed?"

_If the players show genuine empathy, acknowledging the difficulty of her position:_

She is quiet for a moment. Her pen hovers above the page. Then she sets it down and folds her hands on the desk. "You are the first person to sit in that chair who has looked at me and seen a person instead of a function. I do not know whether to thank you or to warn you. So I will do both. Thank you. And be careful. Compassion in this room is a vulnerability. I am trained to notice vulnerabilities. I will note yours, even though I appreciate it. The Commission does not allow me to ignore what I have seen. You were kind to me. I will remember that. But I will still do my job."

_If the players offer a creative solution, such as presenting evidence that redirects the investigation toward the actual perpetrators:_

She examines the evidence carefully. She turns each page and reads every word. If the players worked with Commissioner Zhang, she checks his seal against the State Planning Commission registry and sends for the reserve ledgers and bank transfer records tied to the listed accounts. Otherwise, she marks each account and date for independent verification. When she finishes, she sets the papers down and looks at the player with a new expression: respect. "This evidence is carefully assembled. If these claims are verified, they will change the direction of this investigation. I must confirm them through the relevant registries, and through Commissioner Zhang's authentication if you obtained it. I will recess the proceeding for one hour. During that time, you will remain in the antechamber. Do not leave the building. Do not speak to anyone. If the evidence proves genuine, your standing in this Audit will be reconsidered." She pauses. "I hope it is genuine. I would rather investigate a corrupt official than Harmonize someone who tried to expose corruption."

_Her mask slips. Her true feelings emerge:_

This happens only if the player asks a specific question: "Do you believe the Court is just?" and asks it with genuine curiosity rather than defiance.

She looks at the player for a long, searching moment. Her professional composure wavers. She glances at the door. She lowers her voice. "I have served the Commission for seven years. I have conducted four hundred and thirty seven interrogations. I have sent seventy eight people to narrative erasure. Some of them were guilty. Some of them were not. I know this because I reviewed their files afterward. I know which ones were innocent. I know because I saw the gaps in the evidence, the witnesses who recanted too late, the documents that were presented out of context. I saw all of it. And I did nothing. Because doing something would have meant joining them in the cells below this chamber." Her voice drops to barely a whisper. "My Ritual Harmony is 39. I check it every morning. I am closer to that chair than you are. I know what waits for me if I step out of line. I have made peace with it. But I have not made peace with the seventy eight. I dream about their faces. I will dream about yours too, if you become the seventy ninth." She straightens. Her professional mask slides back into place. "I said nothing. You heard nothing. The record will show that you answered my questions cooperatively. That is all."

### The Judgment Announcement

**Read Aloud:**

The Justicar closes her folder. She stands. For a long moment, she says nothing. She looks at you. Not through you. Not past you. At you. As if seeing you for the first time.

"The Commission for Celestial Purity has reached its conclusion. The judgment is final. There is no appeal. There is no reconsideration. There is only what comes next."

She pauses.

Then she reads the judgment.

**Host Note:** Resolve each player's Audit individually using the Risk table from the Host's Codex. For each outcome, read or paraphrase the appropriate description below.

**Outcome 1: Cleared**

**Read Aloud:**

"The Commission finds no evidence of heterodoxy. You are cleared. Return to your duties. You will receive formal notification within three days."

That is all. There is no relief in her voice. No congratulations. No warmth. You are simply dismissed. The door opens. You walk out into the corridor. The world looks the same. It does not feel the same.

**Host Note:** A cleared player has survived with their Faction Standing intact. They are free to continue their career in the Outer Court. But they are now on the Commission's radar. They will be watched. Future Audits will be more thorough.

**Outcome 2: Sanctioned**

**Read Aloud:**

"The Commission finds evidence of minor irregularities. You are sanctioned. Your rank is reduced by two grades. You are reassigned to the Border Documentation Office in Northbridge Prefecture, effective immediately. You will depart within seven days."

She looks at you without expression.

"This is a mercy. Remember that."

**Host Note:** A sanctioned player is removed from the central Court and sent to a provincial posting. They are still playable, but their influence is drastically reduced. They can return to the capital in a future adventure, but it will require significant effort, patronage, or a major faction's support. The Border Documentation Office is a punishment posting. The work is tedious. The conditions are harsh. The Dusk is closer there.

**Outcome 3: Disappeared**

**Read Aloud:**

"The Commission finds evidence of deliberate heterodoxy. You are Disappeared."

The word hangs in the air like smoke. The Justicar does not explain what it means. She does not need to. Two figures enter from a side door that you did not notice before. They wear grey. They carry no weapons. They do not speak. They do not look at you. They take the player by the arms. The player is led out. The door closes. The sound of footsteps fades.

The other players never see them again.

**Host Note:** A Disappeared character is gone. The player should create a new character for the next adventure. The Disappeared character's fate is deliberately ambiguous. They may be executed, imprisoned, or subjected to something worse. Leave it unclear. The mystery is more frightening than any answer the Host could provide.

### After the Audit: Aftermath Scenes

The Audit is over. The players who survived gather in their office. The room is emptier than before. Some desks are silent. Some faces are missing.

**If the player was Cleared:**

**Read Aloud:**

You return to your desk. The same desk. The same stack of scrolls. The same grey light through the same window. Nothing has changed. And yet everything has changed. You know now what the Court can do to you. You have seen it happen. You have felt its shadow pass over you. You will never be as safe as you were before.

A new scroll sits on your desk. It is a notification of reassignment. The workload of the disappeared must be distributed. You have inherited three new cases. There is more work now. There are fewer people to do it. The Court does not pause. It does not mourn. It simply continues.

**If the player was Sanctioned:**

**Read Aloud:**

You have seven days to gather your belongings. Seven days to say goodbye to the capital. The Border Documentation Office is a thousand li away, in a province you have never visited. The assignment is for three years. It might as well be forever.

On your last night, when your quarters are bare and your bags are packed, a messenger arrives. They do not identify themselves. They do not knock. They slip a small package under your door. Inside is a travel allowance, a letter of introduction to the border prefect, and a single sheet of paper with an address written in the border town.

The handwriting on the address is familiar. You have seen it before. On a note that warned you, seven days ago.

The Shadow has not forgotten you.

**If the player was Disappeared (for surviving players):**

**Read Aloud:**

Their desk is empty. The Commission has already collected their personal effects. The drawers are bare. The shelves are empty. It is as if they never worked here.

No one speaks their name. Not in your office. Not in the corridors. Not in the dining hall. It is as if they never existed. The files will be sealed. The records will be amended. By next week, there will be no evidence that they ever worked in this Ministry. The Court is efficient in its erasures.

But you remember. You remember their face. You remember their voice. You remember the choices that led to that chair. You will carry that memory. It will change you. It will change how you move through this Court. You have learned the first lesson of Xiaoyuan: no one is safe. No one is protected. No one matters except insofar as they are useful.

You will be useful. Or you will be next.

### Part Five Faction Standing Texture

As the Audit concludes and the dust settles, the world reshapes itself around the new reality:

- **If the player was Cleared:** You are free, but you are watched. The Commission's file on you now has a notation: "Cleared; monitor." A junior clerk you have never seen before appears at the edge of your vision in every corridor. They are always there. They never speak.
- **If the player was Sanctioned:** The border posting is a punishment, but it is also an opportunity. The officials who sent you there expect you to vanish into obscurity. They do not know that the border is where the Dusk is closest, where secrets are cheapest, and where a person with nothing left to lose can become very dangerous.
- **If the player was Disappeared:** The silence is absolute. Your name is not spoken. Your desk is cleared before the day ends. But somewhere in the city, the Shadow makes a note of your name. He will remember. In future adventures, a fragment of your fate may surface; a message, a clue, a whisper from the depths of the Commission's facilities. The Disappeared are not always gone forever.
- **If the players gained Faction Standing with a school during the Audit:** A formal invitation arrives within a week. The school wants to debrief, to thank, to bind you closer. The invitation is polite, but the subtext is clear: you owe them, and they intend to collect.
- **If the players lost Faction Standing with a school during the Audit:** That school's agents watch from a distance. They do not approach. They do not threaten. They wait. In Xiaoyuan, patience is a weapon, and the old families and schools have centuries of practice.
- **Regardless of outcome:** The city continues. The markets reopen. The scribes return to their scrolls. The dead tree in the courtyard is removed and replaced with a new sapling; a gesture of renewal that feels obscene in its optimism. The new tree will grow in poisoned soil. Everyone knows this. No one comments on it. The Court does not acknowledge its failures.

### Where Do We Go From Here

THE FIRST HARMONIZATION is designed as an introduction to the Court of Xiaoyuan. The players have learned the rules. They have felt the pressure. They have made choices with consequences. Now the campaign continues.

**Immediate Hooks (Next Session):**

1. **The Shadow's Summons.** A player finds an envelope marked in black under their door. Inside is a single sentence: "The person who warned you would like to meet. Come alone. Come at midnight. Come to the Archive." This begins the next adventure, introducing the Shadow as a major figure and potential ally.

2. **The Crimson Lineage's Revenge.** If the players investigated the Qi siphoning, Lady Feng does not forget. A player's relative in the provinces is taken. A message arrives: "We have something of yours. You have something of ours. The records you copied. Return them." This creates immediate personal stakes for the next adventure.

3. **The Bright Mirror's Interest.** If the embezzlement evidence reached him, the Bright Mirror summons the players for a rigorous review. He asks where every page came from, who authenticated it, and what the players want done with it. If their account holds, he offers formal cooperation and an exchange of verified information. Refusing the offer does not make them targets, but lying to him or manipulating the evidence will earn his scrutiny. The players have caught the attention of one of the most exacting figures in the Court.

4. **The Fugitive's Return.** Tao Chen, if he escaped, does not stay hidden. He resurfaces with more evidence. The Golden Orthodoxy wants him silenced permanently. The Verdant Path wants him protected. The players find themselves caught between the schools again, this time with higher stakes and fewer options.

**Campaign Hooks:**

5. **The Dusk Breach.** Ash is only the beginning. Reports arrive from the border: the Dusk is spreading faster than predicted. Towns are falling. Refugees are arriving with stories of something moving in the Grey. The Court is not prepared. The players may be sent to investigate, or the Dusk may come to Xiaoyuan itself. The stolen Qi from the Crimson Lineage warehouses may be connected to the Dusk's advance. Someone has been stockpiling refined Qi, and the Dusk has been growing stronger. The two facts cannot be unrelated.

6. **The Predecessor's Legacy.** The Disappeared official Yun Shu had a family. A sibling arrives in the capital, asking questions. They know the players now hold the position their sibling once held. They want answers. They want justice. They may become allies or enemies, depending on how the players respond. The sibling carries a sealed letter from Yun Shu, written before the Audit, containing a name the Shadow did not include in his warning. A name that connects the Qi theft, the Golden Orthodoxy, and the Crimson Lineage in a single thread.

7. **The Iron Calculation's Crusade.** Commissioner Zhang does not stop. The Qi siphoning investigation continues. He needs allies he can trust. He approaches the players with evidence of a larger conspiracy involving multiple old families. The Crimson Lineage is just one piece of a much larger puzzle stretching back decades. Zhang has identified a pattern: the Qi thefts accelerated in the months before each Harmonization Audit. The Audits are not unrelated to the thefts. The Audits may be the cover for the thefts.

8. **The Golden Orthodoxy's Grudge.** If the players refused to sign the denunciation, Senior Censor Wei waits. He is patient. He is methodical. He does not forget. In a future adventure, his machinations surface again. The players face a choice: make peace with the Golden Orthodoxy, or destroy them before they destroy you. Wei has his own superiors, and they are not as patient as he is. If he fails to deliver results, he will be replaced by someone less reasonable. The players may come to miss dealing with Wei. His eventual successor rarely appears in person at all. Petitioners who once faced one patient bureaucrat now find themselves denounced in the open squares by strangers who call themselves the Loyal Chorus. They swear no official sent them. It is almost believable.

9. **The Justicar's Crisis.** Junior Justicar Meng has her own secrets. Her Ritual Harmony is 39. She is one bad Audit away from the other side of the table. In a future adventure, she may approach the players for help. She has seen something she was not meant to see. She needs allies she can trust. She is not good at trust. She may be the players' most unlikely ally. She has access to Commission files that no one else can reach. She also has a conscience, buried deep but not yet dead. If the players can reach that conscience, they gain a source of information worth more than any faction's favor.

10. **The Nature of the Audits.** The deeper mystery remains: why do the Harmonization Audits happen? Who truly controls the Commission? What is being hidden? Each Audit reveals another piece of the truth. Each piece is more disturbing than the last. The players may eventually discover that the Audits serve a purpose no one in the Court speaks of aloud. The Disappeared are not simply executed or imprisoned. They are processed, their Qi extracted, their memories cataloged, their spiritual essence fed into something that sustains the Court's power. The Audits are not inspections. They are harvests.

11. **The Teahouse Keeper's Network.** The old woman who runs the Teahouse of Whispered Petitions has been watching the players. She knows what they did during the Audit. She knows who they helped and who they refused. She sends a message: a single dried jasmine flower, delivered to the player's desk, with no note attached. The flower is a summons. The teahouse keeper has her own network, independent of the Verdant Path and the Shadow. She has been in Xiaoyuan longer than both. She knows where the bodies are buried, literally and metaphorically. An invitation to tea from her is not a social call. It is an offer that cannot be refused.

> **Iron Wall (Knight):** The campaign hooks are not paths; they are oaths. The Crimson Lineage's revenge (Hook 2) means someone needs protection. The Justicar's crisis (Hook 9) means someone needs rescue. You know which hooks call to you. You do not choose them. They choose you.
>
> **Bright Mirror (Paladin):** The future hooks are tests of principle. The Shadow's summons (Hook 1) tests whether you trust a stranger. The embezzlement evidence (Hook 3) tests whether you use power responsibly. The Dusk Breach (Hook 5) tests whether you help those who cannot help themselves. Your responses define you.
>
> **Shadow (Rogue):** The hooks are threads in a web. You can pull any of them. But pulling one moves all the others. Helping the Bright Mirror (Hook 3) will anger the Golden Orthodoxy (Hook 8). Protecting the fugitive (Hook 4) will deepen the Crimson Lineage's grudge (Hook 2). You see the connections before the others do. _If the Shadow is a player character, you have already begun working one of these hooks. During the aftermath, you sent a message, arranged a meeting, or planted a piece of information. The next adventure has already begun for you, whether the party knows it or not._
>
> **Duke of Eternal Night (Vampire):** The campaign hooks stretch across time in ways the mortal players do not fully grasp. The Crimson Lineage (Hook 2) was old when you were turned. The nature of the Audits (Hook 10) may connect to events centuries past. You have patience the others lack. You will see these stories through to their end, whatever that end may be.
>
> **Iron Bone (Werewolf):** The pack has been tested and has survived. The hooks ahead will test it again. But a pack that survives together grows stronger. You watch your companions as they discuss the future, reading their scents, their postures, their fears. You know who will break and who will hold. You will be the one who holds.
>
> **Celestial Book (Wizard):** The deeper mystery of the Audits (Hook 10) resonates with your studies. Narrative erasure has a ritual component that the Court does not acknowledge publicly. The rites, seals, and Commission grey robes are all part of a larger working. You have begun to see the pattern. The next adventure will bring it into focus.
>
> **Bone Script (Druid):** Yun Shu's sibling arrives in the capital asking questions and will be given, over four months, exactly the answers you were given about your valley. You know the shape of what is coming for them. You could shorten it by two years by teaching them the procedure, and teaching someone the procedure is how the Court manufactured you.

> **Incense Crown (Priestess):** You have now used ritual authority in front of the Commission and it worked, which has converted you from a necessity into a problem that somebody will eventually decide to solve. The Host should begin the count: nine ordained officiants, and the first of the deaths mentioned in one sentence, without comment, three sessions from now.

> **Stray Stroke (Wildcard):** The Amber Shore registry is still in the deep stacks and you are two years closer to nothing. But you have now stood beside people who watched you produce genuine papers for a life that was not yours and did not flinch, and one of them holds the standing to request an archive document, and you are going to have to ask, and asking will be harder than any of this was.

## NPC Quick Reference for This Adventure

### The Golden Orthodoxy Official (Senior Censor Wei)

**Profile:**

- Presence 14, Intellect 12. Coldly polite. Genuinely believes he is protecting the empire.
- Wants: Signed statements confirming heterodoxy. Dislikes: Independent thought.
- Secret: The official he is asking the players to condemn was once his finest student. Wei trained him, sponsored his career, attended his wedding, and held his first child. He has not slept well in months.

**Physical Description:** Wei is in his late forties, with a lean face and grey hair cut close, receding at the temples. He carries himself with the rigid posture of a man who has spent decades perfecting his public appearance. His robes are always immaculate, the gold thread of the Bureau's eight trigrams precisely embroidered on each sleeve. He moves with economy: no wasted gestures, no unnecessary steps, no hesitation. His pale, carefully tended hands are those of someone who has never done physical labor. His eyes are his most telling feature; they are pale brown, almost amber, and they do not blink as often as normal eyes do. He has trained himself to hold eye contact without wavering. He has trained himself to reveal nothing. The training is good, but it is not perfect. When he is stressed, a small muscle at the corner of his left eye twitches.

**Tells:** His left eye twitches when he is lying or under pressure. He touches his signet ring when he is about to make a threat, as if drawing authority from the metal. He breathes through his mouth when preparing to deliver bad news.

**Behind the Scenes for the Host:** When the players are not watching, Wei returns to his office and closes the door. He pours himself a cup of cold tea from the pot on his desk. He does not drink it. He holds it, letting the warmth seep into his palms, staring at the cracked teacup he has not replaced. He thinks about Archivist Chen, his former student. He thinks about what will happen to him if his superiors learn that he hesitated. He thinks about the cost of loyalty and the cost of betrayal. Then he drinks the tea, sets the cup down, and prepares for the next meeting. He will not sleep tonight. He will not sleep many nights. He has made peace with this. He has made peace with the arrangement: his conscience for his survival.

### The Verdant Path Representative (Junior Archivist Lin)

**Profile:**

- Presence 10, Intellect 14. Earnest, terrified, deeply committed. She is not good at this.
- Wants: Safe passage for the fugitive official. Dislikes: Violence. She came to the Verdant Path through reading banned texts, not through factional games.
- Secret: The fugitive official is her uncle.

**Physical Description:** Lin is twenty two years old, though she looks younger. She has a round face, large dark eyes, and an expression of permanent anxiety that softens only when she talks about books. Her hair is pulled back in a severe bun that is already coming loose, strands escaping around her face. She wears the standard grey robes of a junior archivist, but her sleeves are rolled up to the elbow, revealing forearms stained with ink and, on closer inspection, a thin white scar running from her wrist to the midpoint of her forearm; a paper cut from an encounter with a badly bound book. She moves in a state of controlled panic, her hands never still, her eyes always scanning for threats. When she is nervous, she twists a strand of hair around her finger. When she is very nervous, she repeats the last word of every sentence under her breath, as if checking that she said it correctly.

**Tells:** She repeats the last word of sentences under her breath when anxious. She twists her hair when thinking. She closes her eyes before making a difficult decision, as if praying to a god she does not believe in.

**Behind the Scenes for the Host:** When the players are not watching, Lin returns to the safe house where Tao Chen is hiding. She brings him food, medicine, news from the outside. She sits with him in silence, letting him sleep while she watches the door. She is terrified, but she does not show it in front of him. She is the brave one now, the one who must hold the fear at bay for both of them. After the players leave, she makes a note in a small journal she keeps hidden in her sleeve. She writes down everything the players said, every promise they made, every hesitation she noticed. She is learning to read people. She is learning to survive. She does not know if she is doing it right.

### The Iron Calculation Liaison (Commissioner Zhang)

**Profile:**

- Presence 8, Intellect 18. Clipped, efficient, utterly uninterested in social niceties.
- Wants: Data. Dislikes: Excuses.
- Secret: He used to work for a Crimson Lineage family. He knows their methods. He left because he could not stomach them. This investigation is personal.

**Physical Description:** Commissioner Zhang is a small man in his early sixties, with thinning grey hair and the kind of face that is easy to overlook in a crowd: nondescript, forgettable, designed by nature for a life of quiet observation. He wears plain grey robes with no insignia, no decoration, no indication of rank. He could be a clerk, a messenger, a retired schoolteacher. He is not. The power he wields is invisible, written in ledgers and memoranda and the quiet accumulation of information. His hands are calloused in specific places: the thumb and first two fingers of his right hand, from decades of holding a brush. The calluses are deep, permanent, a permanent record of his labor. He moves slowly, deliberately, as if conserving energy for tasks that matter. He does not waste movement. He does not waste words. He does not waste anything.

**Tells:** He sets down his brush before speaking, never during. He pauses exactly two seconds before answering a question, using the time to formulate a precise response. When he is angry, his writing becomes smaller, tighter, the characters pressing together like people in a crowded room.

**Behind the Scenes for the Host:** When the players are not watching, Zhang works late into the night. His office lamp burns until the small hours. He compares the warehouse records with the transport logs and the Crimson Lineage financial statements he copied before leaving their employ. He is building a case. He has been building it for fourteen years, since the morning they took his predecessor. He has collected enough evidence to destroy a family. But he needs a trigger, a moment, a catalyst. The Audit may be that moment. Or it may be another piece of evidence he adds to the pile, waiting for the day when the pile is large enough to collapse under its own weight. He does not know if that day will come in his lifetime. He continues anyway.

### The Dusk Refugee (Nameless, calls herself "Ash")

**Profile:**

- Presence 6, Resolve 18. She has walked across a dead province. She is stronger than she looks.
- Wants: Safety. Or answers. She is not sure which.
- Secret: Her shadow knows things. It whispers to her at night. It has been telling her about the players.

**Physical Description:** Ash is in her late twenties, though the Dusk corruption makes her age difficult to read. She has the look of someone who has been weathered by forces that do not respect human timelines. Her skin is pale, almost grey, with a network of darker veins visible at her temples and wrists where the corruption has spread closest to the surface. Her hair is dark and matted, unevenly cut with a blade she does not remember using. Her eyes are her most striking feature: pale grey with flecks of amber, the irises too large, the pupils slow to react to changes in light. She moves with an economy born of exhaustion: no wasted energy, no unnecessary motion. Her hands are calloused and scarred from the journey, the nails cracked and dirty. When she stands still, she sways slightly, as if the ground beneath her is not entirely solid.

**Tells:** Her shadow moves when she does not. She tilts her head when the shadow speaks to her. She touches her chest above her heart when she is about to tell a difficult truth, as if checking that the words are still there.

**Behind the Scenes for the Host:** When the players are not watching, Ash sits in whatever shelter she has found and talks to her shadow. The conversation is soft, continuous, like the murmur of a stream. The shadow responds in ways only she can perceive. She is not insane. The shadow is real. It is a fragment of the Dusk that attached itself to her during her journey through the Grey. It knows things about the Court, about the players, about the Crimson Lineage's connection to the Dusk. It tells her these things in fragments, images, conversations only partly remembered. She does not understand most of what it shows her. But she remembers. She remembers everything. If the players befriend her, they have access to a source of information no one else in the capital possesses: the memory of the Dusk itself.

### The Junior Justicar (Justicar Meng)

**Profile:**

- Presence 14, Resolve 16. Young, zealous, desperate to prove herself to the Bright Mirror.
- Wants: To find heresy. She genuinely believes heresy is everywhere. She is not wrong.
- Secret: Her own Ritual Harmony is 39. She is one bad Audit away from the other side of the table.

**Physical Description:** Meng is thirty four years old, with a face that has aged faster than her years. She has sharp cheekbones, a narrow jaw, and dark eyes set deep in their sockets. Her hair is pulled back so tightly that it stretches the skin at her temples, giving her a perpetually alert expression. She wears the grey and silver of the Commission without adornment; no jewelry, no pins, no marks of personal taste. The uniform is her identity. She has worn it so long that she forgets she is wearing it. She moves with the controlled precision of someone who has trained herself to suppress all involuntary gestures. She does not fidget. She does not shift her weight. She sits as still as a statue, her hands flat on the desk, her pen moving only when she takes notes. Underneath the professional exterior, there are signs of strain: a faint tremor in her left hand when she thinks no one is looking, dark circles under her eyes that powder cannot fully conceal, a tendency to press her lips together when she hears a disturbing answer.

**Tells:** Her left hand trembles when she is under stress. She presses her lips together when she hears something that disturbs her. She glances at the ceiling when she is about to deliver a judgment, as if seeking approval from a source only she can see.

**Behind the Scenes for the Host:** When the players are not watching, Meng returns to her small office in the Commission headquarters. She locks the door. She sits at her desk and opens a locked drawer. Inside is a personal Ritual Harmony tester, a small jade disc that changes color based on the user's Ritual Harmony. She places her palm on it. The color shifts. It is worse than yesterday. It has been worse every day for months. She records the number in a private journal, then closes the drawer and locks it. She is counting down. She knows she has perhaps two years before her Ritual Harmony drops below the threshold that triggers a mandatory Audit of her own conduct. She has begun making arrangements: letters to be delivered after she is gone, evidence to be released, a confession to be read. She is preparing for her own disappearance. She does not expect anyone to save her. She does not expect anyone to try.

### Closing Notes for the Host

This adventure was written to introduce the Court of Xiaoyuan as a setting and the Harmonization Audit as an engine of tension. It works best when the Host trusts the atmosphere. Do not explain the Court's politics in detail. Let the players discover them through experience. Do not soften the consequences of failure. The Court is not fair. The adventure should not be either.

For groups that prefer less political intrigue, focus on the Investigation and the Audit. The Three Demands section can be streamlined: pick the two most interesting schools and let the third approach fail on its own. The players cannot do everything. That is the point.

For groups that prefer more social interaction, extend Part One. Let the players interview multiple NPCs. Let them build relationships before the pressure begins. The more they care about the people in the Ministry, the harder the choices in Part Two become.

For groups with a player who was Disappeared, offer that player the chance to continue as an NPC contact. The Disappeared are not always gone forever. A fragment of their fate may surface in a future adventure: a message smuggled out, a witness who saw them in the Commission's lower levels, a rumor that someone matching their description was seen on the border road heading east. Hope is a powerful tool, even false hope.

The threads planted in this adventure are designed to grow. The Shadow's network, the Crimson Lineage's conspiracy, the Dusk's advance, the Bright Mirror's investigation: each is a seed for a longer campaign. Let the players choose which thread to pull. The Court is large enough to contain many stories.

Finally, remember that the Court of Xiaoyuan is not a monolith. It is made of people: frightened clerks, ambitious officials, compromised idealists, tired soldiers, and a few rare individuals who have not yet surrendered their conscience. The players are among those rare individuals. That is why their story matters.

When in doubt, return to the sensory details. Describe the quality of light in a room. Describe the temperature of the air. Describe the sound of footsteps in a silent corridor. The Court comes alive in its small details. A single detail can do more to create atmosphere than a page of explanation. Trust your descriptions. Trust your players. Trust the story you are building together.

There is no single correct way to run this adventure. Some groups will revel in the political maneuvering and spend an entire session debating which school to support. Others will want to move quickly to the Audit, treating the preparation as a tactical problem to be solved. Both approaches are valid. The adventure is designed to flex. Lean into what your group finds compelling. The Court will still be waiting when you return.
