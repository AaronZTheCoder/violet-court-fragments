# The Violet Court Fragments

## Starter Adventure: Full Cinematic Story

# THE FIRST HARMONIZATION

## A Playable Tragedy in Seven Bells

> _For one Host and three to six players._
>
> _Designed for new characters and four to six sessions._
>
> _Every answer saves something. Every answer abandons something else._

## The Story Promise

Seven days before a Harmonization Audit, a group of newly appointed officials receives a warning that should not exist.

Before nightfall, three factions ask them to perform three apparently separate acts.

The Golden Orthodoxy wants five names placed beneath a denunciation.

The Verdant Path wants one fugitive moved beyond the city walls.

The Iron Calculation wants a missing ledger page recovered from the Imperial Qi Reserve.

All three requests concern the same man.

All three conceal the same crime.

All three lead to the same chair.

This is not a story about proving innocence.

It is a story about deciding which truth deserves to survive when the state has already prepared the lie.

## How to Use This Story

This document is a complete table narrative. It contains the full dramatic path, substantial narration, character dialogue, investigation routes, checks, failures, optional encounters, verdicts, and campaign continuations.

The source adventure in [the_first_harmonization.md](./the_first_harmonization.md) remains the production bible for additional setting detail, wider rules context, and alternate material.

Use the following cues.

1. **READ ALOUD** is spoken directly to the players.

2. **DIRECTOR** controls rhythm, focus, sound, and performance.

3. **HOST** explains hidden information and scene purpose.

4. **CHECK** presents a meaningful test and what success changes.

5. **CONSEQUENCE** records what follows from a choice or failure.

6. **CUTAWAY** shows an image the players may witness as an audience even when their characters do not.

Read the prose slowly enough for players to inhabit it. Stop whenever a player interrupts with action. The script exists to create pressure, not to prevent choice.

## The Dramatic Engine

The Court claims that a Harmonization Audit discovers impurity.

The truth is more terrible.

The Audit manufactures certainty.

It turns fear into testimony.

It turns testimony into records.

It turns records into reality.

At the beginning, the characters still believe that records describe the world.

By the end, they must decide whether records create the world, and whether survival requires them to become authors of another person's erasure.

## The Three Stories Moving Together

### The Public Story

The Court says the coming Harmonization Audit will protect Xiaoyuan from heterodoxy.

The Court needs a list of names large enough to calm a political crisis and small enough to look precise.

### The Hidden Story

The Court says Archivist Chen is a fugitive who stole state property.

Tao Chen possesses evidence that connects Golden Orthodoxy embezzlement, Crimson Lineage Qi theft, and a pattern of earlier Harmonization Audits.

### The Personal Story

The characters believe they are minor officials who can remain neutral.

Every decision they make writes a sentence in another person's fate.

Neutrality is merely a sentence written by someone who refuses to sign their name.

## Canonical Terms in This Story

Use these terms exactly in play.

1. The full proceeding is a **Harmonization Audit**.

2. The character resource is **Celestial Favor**.

3. The orthodoxy measure is **Ritual Harmony**.

4. Political relationship scores are **Faction Standing**.

5. Supernatural energy is **Qi**.

6. Its formal study is **Qi Theory**.

7. People altered by the Crimson Dusk are **Dusk Touched**.

8. The three formal outcomes are **Cleared**, **Sanctioned**, and **Disappeared**.

9. The removal of a person from public memory is **narrative erasure**.

10. Tao Chen is called **Tao Chen** in private and **Archivist Chen** in formal accusation.

## The Human Anchor

Tao Chen was once Third Inspector of Works.

He is not fearless.

He is not pure.

He did not expose the crime when he first found it.

He copied the evidence, hid it, returned to work, and spent months pretending that delay was caution rather than fear.

He folds discarded paper into birds.

When he was a child, his mother told him that paper remembers every shape forced upon it.

Whenever the characters find evidence of his passage, place a paper bird in the scene.

The players should remember the birds before they remember the ledger.

## The Seven Bells

Each bell reduces time and removes one comforting interpretation of events.

Ring a real bell, tap a glass, or strike the table once at every transition.

1. **Bell Seven:** Seven days remain. The warning arrives.

2. **Bell Six:** Six days remain. The Golden Orthodoxy asks for names.

3. **Bell Five:** Five days remain. The Verdant Path asks for a life.

4. **Bell Four:** Three days remain. The Iron Calculation asks for proof.

5. **Bell Three:** One day remains. The three requests become one buried history.

6. **Bell Two:** One night remains. The characters decide what they will carry into judgment.

7. **Bell One:** No time remains. The Court manufactures truth.

## Recurring Images

1. **The dead tree** represents witness, memory, and the possibility that poisoned ground may still remember rain.

2. **The cracked teacup** represents a person who continues to function after becoming unsafe.

3. **The paper bird** represents truth made fragile, portable, and easy to burn.

4. **The empty chair** represents the shape left in a room after narrative erasure.

5. **The crimson petal** represents power that wants its victims to know who touched them.

## Recurring Sounds

1. A brush moving across paper.

2. A bell whose vibration remains after the scene has ended.

3. Footsteps stopping outside a closed door.

4. A breath taken immediately before a lie.

5. A chair leg scraping stone.

## Performance Law

No powerful official needs to shout.

The more dangerous the statement, the more quietly it should be delivered.

No frightened person should be played as foolish.

Fear is accurate information arriving inside a body that has nowhere safe to put it.

## Mechanical Foundation

Begin every player at Ritual Harmony 50 unless character creation establishes another value.

Begin every player at Low Risk, then apply protection or hostility from Faction Standing as defined in the Host's Codex.

Track Risk openly enough that players know why it changes.

The conspiracy may remain mysterious.

The rules must not.

The following events usually increase Risk by one level.

1. Public defiance witnessed by an official.

2. A lie contradicted by established evidence.

3. Possession of an incriminating original document.

4. Hostile Faction Standing with an active faction.

5. An unexplained disturbance during curfew.

The following events usually reduce Risk by one level.

1. Credible testimony supported by evidence.

2. A patron spending political capital.

3. Ten Celestial Favor spent through an appropriate contact.

4. Protective Faction Standing invoked at real cost.

5. Evidence that redirects the investigation toward a more powerful target.

No player may reduce Risk below Minimal.

No single scene should raise Risk by more than two levels unless a player knowingly chooses open rebellion.

## Principal Cast

### Senior Censor Wei

Wei speaks for the Golden Orthodoxy and the Bureau of Harmonious Narrative.

He believes order prevents catastrophe.

He also knows that order has made him a collector of names.

Tao Chen was once his finest student.

Wei touches his signet ring before making a threat.

He looks at his cracked teacup whenever Tao is mentioned.

### Junior Archivist Lin

Lin serves in the Eastern Repository and works with the Verdant Path.

She is earnest, terrified, and still learning how to lie.

Tao Chen is her uncle.

She repeats the last word of a sentence under her breath when panic rises.

### Commissioner Zhang

Zhang is a senior investigator of the State Planning Commission.

He does not trust speeches.

He trusts numbers because numbers do not ask him to forgive himself.

He once balanced accounts for a Crimson Lineage family.

He left after discovering what the payments purchased.

### Tao Chen

Tao is a former Third Inspector of Works and the formal Archivist Chen named in Wei's denunciation.

He possesses copied evidence of embezzlement and Qi diversion.

He believes truth matters.

He no longer believes truth automatically makes its carrier brave.

### Ash

Ash is a Dusk Touched refugee who crossed a dead province on foot.

Her shadow moves before she does.

It remembers things the Court has erased.

### Scribe Hui

Hui works in Gate Records.

She has seen the players' names flagged by the Commission and the Crimson Lineage.

She is not prepared to become a martyr.

She warns the characters anyway.

### Justicar Meng

Meng conducts the final interrogation for the Commission for Celestial Purity.

She has performed four hundred and thirty seven interrogations.

Seventy eight people did not leave.

Her own Ritual Harmony is 39.

She is one bad Harmonization Audit away from sitting in the chair.

# PROLOGUE: THE CHAIR

## Scene P1: A Room Built for One Truth

**Location:** Judgment Chamber 7

**Time:** Unknown

**Dramatic Question:** What remains of a person when the record has already decided what they are?

> [!DIRECTOR]
>
> Begin before character introductions.
>
> Do not explain whose memory this is.
>
> Let the players hear the room before they understand it.
>
> Keep every voice quiet.

> [!READ ALOUD]
>
> There is a chair in the center of a room built to contain only one truth.
>
> It is made from ironwood polished by the weight of people who were told to sit still.
>
> The floor is grey stone.
>
> The walls carry no seal, no scripture, and no image of the Sun Emperor.
>
> Nothing in this room is permitted to compete with judgment.
>
> Someone is sitting in the chair.
>
> You cannot see their face.
>
> You see only their hands.
>
> One hand is closed around something small.
>
> The other rests open on the arm of the chair, palm upward, as if waiting to receive a verdict that has been falling toward it for years.
>
> Across from the chair, a woman in grey and silver opens a file.
>
> The first page bears a name.
>
> Her hand covers it.
>
> Somewhere behind the stone, a brush begins to move.
>
> The sound is delicate.
>
> The sound is final.

Meng lifts her eyes.

Deliver the next line without anger.

> [!READ ALOUD]
>
> "The Court does not ask whether you are innocent."
>
> "Innocence is private."
>
> "The Court asks whether your continued existence remains compatible with harmony."

Pause.

Ask every player to close one hand around an imaginary object.

Do not ask what the object is yet.

> [!READ ALOUD]
>
> Beyond the walls, a bell begins to ring.
>
> Its first vibration enters the room.
>
> Before the sound can end, the woman turns one page.
>
> The person in the chair takes a breath.
>
> The world cuts backward seven days.

# BELL SEVEN: THE DEAD TREE

## Scene 7.1: Morning Without Warmth

**Location:** Bureau of Harmonious Records, shared office

**Time:** Seven days before the Harmonization Audit

**Dramatic Question:** When fear enters the room, who admits they recognize it?

> [!DIRECTOR]
>
> Begin with ordinary work.
>
> Let the first minute feel almost dull.
>
> Terror becomes sharper when it interrupts routine rather than replacing it.

> [!READ ALOUD]
>
> Morning enters your office without warmth.
>
> Grey light passes through a dirty window and settles across unfinished reports, cooling tea, and a desk built for fewer people than now use it.
>
> You have served in the Outer Court for three weeks.
>
> You are already behind.
>
> The office smells of old paper, wet stone, and the cheap tallow candles burned by every ministry that considers good oil an indulgence.
>
> A faded scroll hangs crooked on the far wall.
>
> Its calligraphy reads, "Harmony is the thread that binds the empire."
>
> No one has straightened it.
>
> No one has commented on that.
>
> Through the window stands a dead tree in the courtyard below.
>
> It has stood there for thirty years.
>
> No bird nests in it.
>
> No gardener removes it.
>
> The Court finds it easier to ignore a dead thing than to explain why it died.

Invite each player to describe one ordinary task their character is performing.

Ask what detail of their desk reveals the life they had before the Court.

Let two characters exchange a few lines before the interruption.

Then knock once beneath the table.

> [!READ ALOUD]
>
> The office door opens without a knock.
>
> A junior scribe stands in the threshold.
>
> Their face is pale.
>
> Their hands are shaking.
>
> They carry the morning briefing as though it is heavier than paper.

The scribe is Ren, nineteen years old, three months in service.

Ren speaks too quickly and never looks behind them.

> [!READ ALOUD]
>
> "Forgive the intrusion, Honored Ones."
>
> "I was told to deliver this directly."
>
> "I was told not to speak to anyone on the way."
>
> Ren presses the scroll into the nearest pair of hands.
>
> Their fingers are cold enough to hurt.
>
> "Please read it carefully."
>
> "I have to go."
>
> "I should not be here."

If a player blocks the door, Ren freezes rather than fighting.

If a player speaks kindly, Ren whispers:

> "They chose me because no one important would notice if I failed."

If a player demands a name, Ren looks toward the corridor and says:

> "The message passed through six hands. Mine is the only hand you can still find."

If a player reaches for the scroll before Ren offers it, Ren flinches as though struck.

Then Ren leaves.

Do not let the scene continue comfortably.

The sound of Ren's footsteps should stop too soon.

## Scene 7.2: The Morning Briefing

The scroll contains promotions, reassignment notices, ritual schedules, and reminders written in the polished language of institutional indifference.

Read enough for normality to become obscene.

> [!READ ALOUD]
>
> "By order of the Grand Secretariat, the following promotions are confirmed."
>
> "Third Archivist Hu advances to Second Archivist."
>
> "Scribe Lin advances to Senior Scribe, subject to ritual confirmation."
>
> "Junior Censor Zhou advances to Censor, with all attendant responsibilities and liabilities."
>
> "The appointment of Sixteenth Assistant Recorder Fei is rescinded for failure to complete the required purification rites."
>
> "The position remains vacant pending review."
>
> "Unauthorized discussion of Harmonization Audit procedures is prohibited."
>
> "Unauthorized discussion of Harmonization Audit results is prohibited."
>
> "Unauthorized discussion of the Commission for Celestial Purity is prohibited."
>
> "The Commission has requested complete personnel records for all departments of the Outer Court."
>
> "Inaccuracies will be investigated."

Clipped beneath the briefing is a smaller note.

The handwriting is feminine, precise, and strained by a tremor that appears only on certain words.

Hand a folded sheet to the nearest player if possible.

> [!READ ALOUD]
>
> _It begins in seven days._
>
> _The Commission has already started collecting names._
>
> _Burn this after reading._
>
> _If anyone asks, you never received it._

Do not speak for several seconds.

Let the players decide who touches the note, who reads over another shoulder, who checks the door, and who looks at the fire.

## Scene 7.3: The First Questions

Ask every player in character:

> **What is the first thing you believe the Court would use against you?**

Do not require a full confession.

A name is enough.

A look is enough.

A refusal is also an answer.

Record every response exactly.

Meng will return to these words during Bell One.

Then ask:

> **Who in this room would you trust to destroy evidence without reading it?**

The second question is not about evidence.

It is about the shape of trust before the story tests it.

> [!HOST]
>
> The warning came from the Shadow.
>
> She has sent similar notes for decades.
>
> She chose the characters because their newness makes them difficult to predict and because at least one of them has already shown an unwillingness to look away.

## Scene 7.4: Reading the Paper

Allow the following approaches.

### Ink Analysis

> [!CHECK]
>
> Intellect plus Alchemy, target 14.
>
> Success identifies crushed iron oxide found in high quality official ink.
>
> Exceptional success reveals a trace of sandalwood oil used in riverside offices.
>
> Failure still reveals that the note was written inside the Court, but the reagent stains one corner red.

### Handwriting Analysis

> [!CHECK]
>
> Intellect plus Investigation, target 16.
>
> Success identifies an educated career bureaucrat with decades of practice.
>
> The tremor appears around the words Commission, names, and never.
>
> Failure suggests the writer is elderly, which is incomplete but useful.

### Qi Reading

> [!CHECK]
>
> Intellect plus Qi Theory, target 14.
>
> Success reveals fear, determination, and a deeper residue of guilt.
>
> Exceptional success shows the emotional image of a small room facing north, with running water audible beyond one wall.
>
> Failure causes the note to grow cold and alerts the reader that some older ward once protected it.

### Tradecraft

> [!CHECK]
>
> Presence plus Intrigue, target 14.
>
> Success recognizes a six link delivery chain and a professional dead drop method.
>
> The final courier was chosen to be visible while every earlier link remained hidden.
>
> Failure identifies only that Ren was never meant to survive scrutiny.

Whatever the checks reveal, the note remains a hook.

The sender wants the characters frightened enough to act and curious enough to keep going.

## Scene 7.5: The Missing Scribe

If the characters look for Ren immediately, they find the corridor empty.

A service door at the first turn rocks gently on its hinge.

Beyond it, steps descend toward the unregistered floors beneath the Bureau.

Following now requires urgency.

> [!CHECK]
>
> Swiftness plus Stealth, target 14.
>
> Success catches the echo of two sets of footsteps and a glimpse of grey cloth turning below.
>
> Exceptional success finds a brass button stamped with the old seal of the Commission for Celestial Purity.
>
> Failure reaches the lower landing after the footsteps have vanished.
>
> A wet footprint remains on dry stone.

If the characters search Ren's quarters later, read:

> [!READ ALOUD]
>
> Ren's room is smaller than your office and cleaner than any place occupied by someone who left willingly.
>
> The bedding is folded.
>
> The official shoes remain beneath the bed.
>
> A chipped bowl sits beside a jar containing three copper petals.
>
> There are no clothes.
>
> No letters.
>
> No family charm.
>
> No travel pack.
>
> The room has not been abandoned.
>
> It has been edited.

On the desk is a paper bird folded from a discarded duty roster.

Inside one wing, written too small to see before unfolding:

> _I only carried the message._

If the characters preserve the bird, it becomes evidence that Ren expected danger.

If they burn it, ask who watches until the name is gone.

If they leave it, the bird disappears before morning.

## Scene 7.6: The Room That Does Not Exist

Tracing the delivery chain leads to an unused wing of the Ministry.

The official building has three floors.

The service stair continues to a fourth.

No map includes it.

> [!READ ALOUD]
>
> The staircase rises into a corridor that should be outside the roof.
>
> The air is colder here.
>
> Damp has swollen the doors in their frames.
>
> Water moves behind one wall though no pipe is marked on any plan.
>
> At the far end, one door stands open.
>
> Inside waits a writing desk, a burned candle, a cold brazier, and the ash of many pages.
>
> The chair has been pushed back recently.
>
> Whoever worked here left in haste.
>
> Whoever cleaned the room believed ash was the same as absence.

The room offers five discoveries.

Reveal one without a check.

Reveal more through skill or sacrifice.

1. A strand of silver grey thread from a Commission robe caught beneath the desk.

2. A cup ring scented faintly with jasmine and sandalwood.

3. A fragment of a personnel request bearing the first two words of the Bureau of Harmonious Records.

4. A nail driven beneath the desk where a packet was recently tied.

5. A narrow window facing north toward the river.

> [!CHECK]
>
> Intellect plus Investigation, target 15.
>
> Success reconstructs the room as a long used message station.
>
> Exceptional success finds a phrase impressed into the desk by years of writing on thin paper.
>
> The phrase reads, "If you are still reading, someone has failed to erase you."
>
> Failure disturbs the ashes and destroys one physical clue, but reveals that one page was burned less than an hour ago.

If the characters remain too long, footsteps stop outside.

The door does not open.

After one breath, the footsteps continue.

Increase tension without forcing combat.

Someone wants them to know they were observed.

## Scene 7.7: Master Scribe Xun Remembers

Xun can be found in the Imperial Archives copying a text no one requested.

His hands are black with ink.

His eyes belong to a person who has survived by refusing the shape of surprise.

> [!READ ALOUD]
>
> Xun does not look up when you approach.
>
> His brush continues across the page.
>
> "I knew you would come," he says.
>
> "You have the faces of people who heard a tune they cannot stop hearing."
>
> He finishes the character before lifting his eyes.
>
> "Do not tell me what you know."
>
> "Tell me what you fear."

Let the players answer or refuse.

If shown the warning note, Xun studies it without touching it.

> "That hand has been writing warnings longer than you have been alive."
>
> "Old files carry the same tremor."
>
> "People call the writer the Shadow."
>
> "Knowing her name is a debt I am too old to afford."

If asked how to survive:

> "Make yourself small."
>
> "Make yourself useful."
>
> "Make sure someone above you wants you to remain."
>
> "Those are three different disciplines."
>
> "Most officials master only two."

If accused of cowardice:

Xun places the brush down with great care.

> "I have served here for forty seven years."
>
> "I have watched talented people become sealed files before lunch."
>
> "I am not hiding information from you."
>
> "I am hiding danger from you."
>
> "If you cannot distinguish those things, you will not reach your first anniversary."

If treated with empathy, Xun's voice changes.

> "No one asks how I am."
>
> "They ask for warnings, names, and the dead."
>
> "I am tired."
>
> "I have outlived everyone who entered this ministry beside me."
>
> "That is not victory."
>
> "It is an accident of timing."
>
> "I remain because someone must remember."

Xun can hide one copied document for the characters if they earn his trust.

He does not promise to return it.

He promises only that the Court will not find it first.

## Scene 7.8: Officer Tien and the Waiting Cat

Tien patrols the outer perimeter with a spear polished more carefully than his armor.

He is young enough to believe fear should embarrass him.

> [!READ ALOUD]
>
> Tien speaks without turning toward you.
>
> "I should not be talking to you."
>
> "I am saying that first so you know I know."
>
> "The Commission has been meeting behind closed doors."
>
> "That never happens before an ordinary Harmonization Audit."
>
> "It happens before something worse."

Tien has seen three officials transferred out of the characters' department during the last month.

He does not know where they went.

If asked to pass messages or watch a door:

> "For you, once."
>
> "Because you bought me tea when I was new."
>
> "After that we are even."
>
> "I have a daughter."
>
> "I need to go home at night."

If the characters mention Ren, Tien looks toward the kitchen yard.

> "The cat behind the kitchens waits for Scribe Min every noon."
>
> "She fed it the best part of her lunch."
>
> "Min was transferred yesterday."
>
> "The cat does not understand reassignment."

If the players refused to sacrifice someone earlier in play, Tien may add:

> "People notice who signs."
>
> "People notice who does not."
>
> "The Court pretends only the official record matters."
>
> "That is not true."

Tien can create one distraction during the South Gate scene.

His signal is a dropped spear.

If used, the characters must promise to protect his family if he is exposed.

Do not allow a casual promise.

Ask the speaker to look Tien in the eye.

## Scene 7.9: A Meal Before the Demands

The characters reach the Teahouse of Whispered Petitions near midday.

The teahouse does not ask what they want.

Food arrives because the keeper has learned that frightened officials forget hunger until hunger makes their decisions for them.

> [!READ ALOUD]
>
> The private alcove glows with amber light filtered through rice paper walls.
>
> A pot of jasmine tea steams between you.
>
> There are pickled vegetables cut almost transparent, cold tofu dressed in dark sauce, and two buns warm enough to soften beneath your fingers.
>
> Beyond the wall, a merchant calls prices.
>
> A cart wheel complains against stone.
>
> A child laughs at something none of you can see.
>
> For a few minutes, the city contains no Harmonization Audit.
>
> It contains tea.
>
> It contains food.
>
> It contains the people sitting across from you.

Do not introduce a new demand until each player answers one question.

> **What did your character believe a government record was for before today?**

Let the meal breathe.

When the conversation slows, the old keeper places an empty cup at the table.

No one ordered it.

She says:

> "For the person who will be missing next time."

Then she walks away.

Ring the sixth bell.

# BELL SIX: THE GOLDEN ROOM

## Scene 6.1: Three Perfect Knocks

**Location:** Bureau of Harmonious Records, shared office

**Time:** Six days before the Harmonization Audit

**Dramatic Question:** Is a signature still violence when the hand holding the brush remains clean?

> [!DIRECTOR]
>
> Let Wei enter the characters' space as though ownership is a matter of posture.
>
> He is never rude.
>
> Courtesy is the blade he has chosen.

> [!READ ALOUD]
>
> The knock arrives in three precise sounds.
>
> Each is separated from the next by exactly one second.
>
> The man who enters wears the deep gold of the Bureau of Harmonious Narrative.
>
> Eight trigrams are embroidered on his sleeves.
>
> His robes are immaculate.
>
> His face is calm.
>
> He stands in the center of your crowded office and makes the room appear to have been arranged for him.
>
> His gaze passes over every desk, every cup, every unfinished report.
>
> It stops for half a breath on the dead tree beyond the window.
>
> Then it returns to you.

Wei bows exactly as much as rank requires.

> "Good morning."
>
> "I am Senior Censor Wei of the Bureau of Harmonious Narrative."
>
> "I apologize for arriving without notice."
>
> "The matter is time sensitive."

He waits for acknowledgment.

If no one offers it, he continues after the silence becomes an accusation.

> "The Bureau is preparing a comprehensive report on heterodox influence within the Outer Court."
>
> "The report will inform the coming Harmonization Audit."
>
> "Your cooperation would be valued."
>
> "Your hesitation would be noted."

## Scene 6.2: Five Names

Wei removes a folded document from his sleeve.

Place a real sheet of paper at the center of the table.

He unfolds it with the care of a surgeon laying down a knife.

> [!READ ALOUD]
>
> The paper is heavy.
>
> The seal is real.
>
> Five empty spaces wait at the bottom for names, dates, and personal marks.
>
> Above them are five accusations written in language so polished that suffering has nowhere to cling.

Wei reads the names.

Pause after each.

> "Second Archivist Yu."
>
> "Scribe Min."
>
> "Clerk Fen."
>
> "Recorder Jia."
>
> "Archivist Chen."

The first four are colleagues the characters have seen.

Give each one a human detail.

Yu keeps medicine for a sick child in the lowest desk drawer.

Min saves food for the cat behind the kitchens.

Fen repairs other clerks' broken brushes without asking payment.

Jia sings quietly when working late and stops whenever anyone enters.

The fifth name receives no detail yet.

Wei taps the paper beside it.

> "You work near these people."
>
> "You have heard them speak."
>
> "You have seen the company they keep."
>
> "I require confirmation of what the Bureau has already established."

If asked whether the statements are true:

> "Truth is not a private possession."
>
> "It is a relationship between observation and authorized interpretation."
>
> "The Bureau provides interpretation."
>
> "You provide observation."

If asked what will happen to the five:

> "That belongs to the Commission for Celestial Purity."
>
> "My concern is the accuracy of testimony."
>
> "Your concern should be the location of your own name when the report closes."

## Scene 6.3: The Denunciation

Read the document in full if the players examine it.

> [!READ ALOUD]
>
> **Denunciation Statement**
>
> _I, the undersigned, confirm the following statements as true and accurate to the best of my knowledge._
>
> _Second Archivist Yu has advocated broader access to Qi cultivation among the common classes in terms that diverge from the Harmonious Way._
>

## Scene 1.6: How Meng Conducts the Audit

Meng is professional.

She is not theatrical.

She has asked these questions hundreds of times.

The horror lies in routine.

Use three modes according to the player's approach.

### Cooperative Mode

Meng becomes less pointed but not warm.

Honest answers supported by evidence may reduce Risk by one level.

She grants one extra chance to explain a contradiction.

> "Your honesty serves you."
>
> "Do not mistake that for safety."

### Defiant Mode

Meng repeats questions and studies inconsistency.

Sustained hostility raises Risk by one level.

Moral clarity supported by evidence may still affect her personally.

> "Anger is not a defense."
>
> "It is evidence of something unresolved."
>
> "The Commission exists to resolve."

### Deceptive Mode

Every material lie requires Presence plus Deception contested by Meng's Insight 14.

Each successful lie creates a detail that must remain consistent.

A detected lie raises Risk by one level.

A complete deception maintained through the final question increases Ritual Harmony by 5.

> "You are lying."
>
> "I will ask again."
>
> "Do not insult the Commission with a weaker version."

# HOST REFERENCE

## The Essential Truth

The Harmonization Audit is partly political theater and partly a concealed harvest.

Before major Audits, high grade Qi is routed beneath the Commission.

The Qi powers a process that extracts memory, spiritual resonance, and identity from selected people.

The Court uses the result to sustain protective structures around Xiaoyuan and to reinforce the official narrative.

Most officials know only one fragment.

Wei knows the narrative is manipulated.

Lin knows people are erased.

Tao knows money and Qi move before Audits.

Zhang knows the transfers form a system.

Ash knows the Crimson Dusk recognizes the energy.

Meng knows innocent people vanish through procedures she administers.

The Shadow knows the pattern has repeated for decades.

No one knows the whole truth at the start.

The players may become the first group to assemble it.

## The Core Clue Path

The story should never depend on one successful check.

Each essential conclusion has at least three routes.

### The Audit Was Already Underway

1. The morning briefing requests full personnel records.

2. Officer Tien reports closed Commission meetings.

3. Hui reveals record requests began one month earlier.

### Tao Is More Than a Fugitive

1. Wei's grief reveals a personal past.

2. Lin names him as family and witness.

3. Zhang identifies his seal as copied rather than criminal.

### The Qi Theft Is Systematic

1. The eight hundred unit discrepancy.

2. Sun Jiao's repeated dead signature.

3. The reserve's hidden conduits and Commission seal.

### The Pattern Predates the Characters

1. Yun Shu's personnel file.

2. The Shadow's thirty year old note.

3. Annual wear on the hidden volume.

### The Crimson Dusk Is Connected

1. The corrupted frequency in the reserve.

2. Ash's matching residue.

3. The second dawn underground during earlier Audits.

### The Commission Manufactures Outcomes

1. The denunciation contains curated fragments.

2. Verdict preparation begins before questioning.

3. An attacker knows the intended Disappeared result before the Audit.

## Failure Forward Law

Failed checks change ownership, timing, exposure, or cost.

They do not remove the story.

If the players miss the ledger discrepancy, Zhang points it out and costs them time.

If they fail to find Yun Shu's packet, Qiao places it in their path later.

If Tao is captured, his torn bird directs them onward.

If Ash vanishes, her testimony appears in a Commission note.

If Hui Disappears, her copied request remains.

If all evidence burns, Xun has one hidden copy.

If every player is Disappeared, the Shadow receives the black thread packet and the next characters inherit the case.

Do not treat Faction Standing as moral score.

Increase Risk by one level for:

1. A detected lie.

2. Public refusal of authority.

3. Possession of the original evidence.

4. Curfew disturbance.

5. Failed impersonation.

6. Open aid to a fugitive discovered by witnesses.

## Pacing the Crosscuts

Crosscut only when the emotional meaning changes.

Good cut points include:

1. A name is revealed.

2. A promise is made.

3. A door closes.

4. A character chooses not to answer.

5. An object changes owner.

6. A bell begins before its source is shown.

Do not cut away from a player who has finally chosen to speak honestly.

Let the truth finish.

## Final Direction to the Host

The climax is not the verdict.

The climax is the sentence a player chooses when silence would be safer.

Do not measure success by how many characters remain in office.

Measure it by whether the table understands what survival cost.

Let the Court remain vast.

Let the full conspiracy remain partly hidden.

Never let the human consequence become abstract.

A system can remove a name from a file in one stroke.

A story exists to make that stroke last long enough for everyone to see the person inside it.

When the session ends, leave the blank page on the table.

The record is not finished.

_May it fail to contain them._
