# The Violet Court Fragments

## Core Rulebook

> _A tabletop roleplaying game for one Host and three to six players._
> _Set in the twilight of a celestial empire that forgot its Emperor._
> _Dice. Paper. The stories you tell together._

---

## Chapter One: What Is This Game?

### The Pitch

The Violet Court Fragments is a tabletop roleplaying game set in the dying days of the Celestial Empire, a vast and ancient realm ruled for more than four centuries by the Celestial Court in the name of the Sun Emperor, who has not spoken publicly for about one hundred and sixty four years. The players take on the roles of figures drawn into the Court's web: a brush scribe who discovers a forbidden truth, a soldier who swore an oath to an absent Emperor, a reformer whose ideas are too popular to ignore and too dangerous to tolerate.

Beyond the borders, the Crimson Dusk creeps inward, a corruption of reality itself that turns fields to salt and people into hollow things that weep red. Within the capital, six schools of thought compete for control of the Court, each believing they alone know how the empire should be governed. And somewhere in the depths of the Imperial Archives, a truth waits that could save the empire or destroy it.

This is a game about survival in a system more ancient than anyone understands. About bonds forged under pressure and tested by circumstance. About the stories people tell themselves to justify what they do, and what happens when those stories collapse.

### What You Need to Play

- This rulebook
- At least one d20, plus a few d6 and d8 dice
- Pencil and paper for each player
- Printed character dossiers (one per player, found in the Character Dossiers supplement)
- A printed copy of the Host's Codex for the person running the game
- Three to six players, plus one Host
- A quiet place to gather, a table, and several hours

### The Host and the Players

One person takes the role of the **Host**. The Host describes the world, portrays every figure the players encounter, adjudicates the rules, and keeps the story moving. The Host is not the players' enemy. The Host is their collaborative partner in telling a memorable story. The game's systems exist to support the Host, not to constrain them.

Everyone else takes the role of a **Player**. Each player controls one character: a person with a history, a faction allegiance, secrets they carry, and choices to make. Players describe what their characters do. The Host describes what happens as a result.

When the outcome of an action is uncertain, the dice resolve it. When the outcome is certain, no roll is needed. A character walking across a room does not roll. A character walking across a room while the room is on fire and the floor is collapsing might.

### The Three Pillars

This game rests on three pillars, each equally important:

**Intrigue.** The Court is a labyrinth of competing schools, personal vendettas, and hidden truths. Social encounters are as consequential as combat. The right word can end a career. A poorly chosen alliance can end a life.

**Discovery.** The empire is full of secrets. The truth about the Sun Emperor's silence. The origin of the Crimson Dusk. The founding crime buried in the Imperial Archives. Finding these truths and deciding what to do with them is a central thread.

**Peril.** The Crimson Dusk advances. Harmonization Audits sweep through the Court. Dusk Touched horrors stalk the provinces. Combat, when it comes, is lethal and decisive. Survival is not guaranteed.

### Your First Game of the Violet Court

Welcome to tabletop roleplaying. If you have never played a game like this before, here is what you need to know.

A tabletop roleplaying game is a conversation. One person, the Host, describes the world. The players describe what their characters try to do. Sometimes the dice decide whether those attempts succeed or fail. Everything that happens emerges from this conversation. There is no script. There is no screen. There is no winner. The goal is to tell an unforgettable story together.

**What a session looks like.** A typical session runs three to four hours. You gather at a table with your character sheet, a pencil, and your dice. The Host begins by describing where the story picks up. Maybe the characters are standing in a courtyard slick with rain outside the Hall of Harmonious Edicts. Maybe they are huddled in a safe house while Dusk Touched things scratch at the shutters. Maybe they are walking through a market that will be attacked in moments, though they do not know it yet. The Host sets the scene, and the players respond.

**How to speak in character.** When you want to say something your character says, speak in first person and use your character's voice. The Host answers as the person you are speaking to. If you want to do something your character does, describe it: "I want to search the desk for hidden drawers," not "Do I find anything?" The Host then tells you what happens.

**How the Host and players interact.** The Host describes what the characters perceive. The players describe what their characters do. The Host describes the consequences. Dice only enter when the outcome is uncertain and meaningful. Everything else flows from the shared imagination of the group.

**What you need to bring.** Bring your character sheet, your dice, and a willingness to make decisions under pressure. Your character will face hard choices. Make the choice you think your character would make, not the choice that is safest.

**A note on collaborative storytelling.** The Host is not your enemy. The Host wants your character to succeed. But the Host also wants the story to be compelling, and compelling stories involve setbacks. When your plans go wrong, do not treat it as a loss. Treat it as an opportunity for your character to show who they really are.

**A note for the Host.** Your role is demanding but rewarding. You are the world. You are every person the players meet. You are the weather, the architecture, the politics, and the horror. You do not need to be a professional actor or a seasoned writer. You need to listen to what your players find interesting and give them more of it. When in doubt, describe how things smell. That detail alone will make your world feel real.

**Example of Play: The First Scene**

Here is what a first session might sound like at the table. The Host has prepared an opening scene. The players have their characters ready.

Host: "The rain has been falling for three days. It falls now in sheets across the Outer City, turning the unpaved streets to mud. You stand in the courtyard of the Azure Lotus Teahouse, a modest establishment in the shadow of the great walls of the Inner City. The lanterns are lit. Warm light spills from the windows. Inside, you can hear someone playing a stringed instrument badly. The air smells of wet stone and frying oil. Two of you arrived together. One of you was already here, nursing a cup of tea and watching the rain. How do you want to begin?"

Player 1 (Duke of Eternal Night): "I am the one inside. I have been here for an hour, sitting at a table near the window where I can see the door. I am not drinking the tea. I am just watching the rain. And the other patrons."

Host: "The teahouse is quiet. Four other tables are occupied. A merchant and his bodyguard argue over a bill of lading. Two clerks huddle over documents. An old woman sits alone, muttering to herself. The door opens."

Player 2 (Night Warbler): "I step through the door, shaking water from my cloak. I scan the room quickly. I am looking for exits, for threats, for faces that watch too closely."

Player 3 (Celestial Book): "I am right behind him. I close my umbrella and tap it twice on the floor to shake off the water. I nod to the proprietor."

Host: "The proprietor, a sturdy woman with broad shoulders and greying hair tied in a severe knot, nods back. She gestures to an empty table near the hearth. The warmth of the fire reaches you even from across the room. The Duke of Eternal Night at the window table has not turned to look at you."

Player 1: "I have. I turn now. I meet the eyes of the Night Warbler and incline my head slightly. I recognize him. We have worked together before."

Host: "Good. The three of you are here because a message found each of you in the night, folded into a plain envelope with no seal. The message said only: 'The Jade Iris blooms at midnight. Ask for the woman who weeps.' Each of you followed the thread here. What do you do next?"

This is the rhythm of the game. The Host sets the scene. The players respond. The story moves forward.

## Chapter Two: The Core Mechanic

### The Resolution Roll

When a player declares an action whose outcome is uncertain, the Host may call for a **Resolution Roll**. The player rolls a d20, adds modifiers from the relevant Facet and any applicable Skills, and compares the total against a **Target Number** (TN) set by the Host.

**d20 + Facet Modifier + Proficiency Bonus versus Target Number**

If the total meets or exceeds the TN, the action succeeds. If it falls short, the action fails, and the Host describes the complication.

### Target Numbers

| Difficulty  | TN  | Example                                          |
| ----------- | --- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Trivial     | 5   | Remembering a famous official's name             |
| Routine     | 8   | Filing a standard report without errors          |
| Moderate    | 12  | Convincing a neutral official to share gossip    |
| Challenging | 16  | Detecting a lie told by a trained operative      |
| Formidable  | 20  | Escaping a burning building during a Dusk attack |
| Legendary   | 24  | Convincing the Bright Mirror to show mercy       |
| Impossible  | 28  | Lying to the Celestial Book and succeeding       |

### Advantage and Disadvantage

When circumstances are unusually favorable, the Host grants **Advantage**: roll two d20 and take the higher result. When circumstances are unusually unfavorable, the Host imposes **Disadvantage**: roll two d20 and take the lower result.

Advantage and Disadvantage cancel each other out. If you have both, roll normally regardless of how many sources of each exist.

### Critical Success and Critical Failure

A natural roll of 20 on the d20 is a **Critical Success**. The action succeeds exceptionally well, often with an additional benefit determined by the Host.

A natural roll of 1 on the d20 is a **Critical Failure**. The action fails, and a complication occurs. The Host is encouraged to make complications interesting rather than merely punitive: a failed attempt to bribe an official might result in the official becoming intrigued rather than offended, creating a different kind of trouble.

### Degrees of Success

For particularly significant actions, the Host may use degrees of success:

- **Beat TN by 5 or more:** Exceptional success. Additional benefit.
- **Beat TN by 0 to 4:** Standard success. You get what you wanted.
- **Miss TN by 1 to 4:** Near failure. You fail but learn something or gain a lesser outcome.
- **Miss TN by 5 or more:** Failure with complication.

### When Not to Roll

Do not roll when:

- The action is impossible (the Host simply says no)
- The action is trivial (the Host simply says yes)
- Failure would not create an interesting outcome (skip to success)
- The dice would override a better story (the Host rules in favor of the story)

The dice serve the narrative, not the other way around.

### Examples of Resolution Rolls at the Table

The following examples show how Resolution Rolls play out in actual conversation at the table. Each example includes the Host setting the scene, the player declaring their action, the dice being rolled, and the outcome narrated.

**Example 1: Routine (TN 8): Filing a Report**

A player character, a Celestial Book archivist named Scholar Lin, needs to file a routine acquisition report to avoid scrutiny.

Host: "The Bureau of Celestial Inquiry requires every document transfer to be recorded on Form 7B in triplicate. The clerk at the desk is half asleep. He slides the forms toward you without looking up and mutters something about ink."

Scholar Lin's player: "I fill out the forms quickly. I have done this hundreds of times. I want to get through this without drawing any attention to my request."

The Host sets the TN at 8 for this routine task. The player rolls a d20, getting a 7. Scholar Lin has Intellect 14 (modifier +2) and proficiency in Lore, which applies to bureaucratic forms. Total: 7 + 2 + 2 = 11. Beats TN 8 by 3.

Host: "Your brush moves smoothly. The clerk takes the forms, stamps them without reading, and gestures for you to pass through. A routine success. The guard at the inner door does not even glance at you."

**Example 2: Moderate (TN 12): Gathering Gossip**

A Night Warbler named Kestrel tries to extract information from a neutral clerk in the Hall of Records.

Host: "The clerk is a nervous young woman with fingers stained by ink. She is sorting scrolls and clearly eager to be done with her work. She barely glances up when you approach. 'Can I help you?' she asks, in a tone that suggests she hopes the answer is no."

Kestrel's player: "I lean against the counter casually and compliment the calligraphy on the scroll she is holding. I ask who the scribe was. I am trying to start a conversation that leads to information about the Director's visitors last night."

The Host sets the TN at 12 for convincing a neutral official to share gossip. The player rolls a d20, getting a 9. Kestrel has Presence 12 (modifier +1) and no proficiency in Persuasion. Total: 9 + 1 = 10. Misses TN 12 by 2.

Host: "The clerk gives you a flat look. 'The scribe was me. Is there something you actually need, or are you here to waste my time?' She has not taken the bait. You may try a different approach, but she is more guarded now."

**Example 3: Challenging (TN 16): Detecting a Lie**

An Iron Bone named Steelhand interrogates a merchant who may be feeding information to a rival faction.

Host: "The merchant's smile does not waver. 'I have never met the Director in my life,' he says. 'I am a simple spice trader. Politics is poison to my business.' He holds your gaze steadily. His hands are perfectly still on the table."

Steelhand's player: "I do not believe him. I study his face, his breathing, the micro expressions he cannot control. I use my Insight."

The Host sets the TN at 16 for detecting a lie told by a trained operative. The player rolls a d20, getting a 14. Steelhand has Resolve 12 (modifier +1) and proficiency in Insight (+2). Total: 14 + 1 + 2 = 17. Beats TN 16 by 1.

Host: "You catch it. A tiny flicker in his left eye when he said 'never.' And his hands are too still, a controlled stillness that speaks of someone hiding nerves. He is lying. He has met the Director, and recently. What do you do with this knowledge?"

**Example 4: Formidable (TN 20): Escaping a Burning Building**

A Duke of Eternal Night named Veren is trapped on the second floor of a teahouse as fire consumes the structure. Dusk Touched shadows move through the flames below.

Host: "The floorboards groan beneath you. Smoke pours up the stairwell, thick and black. The window overlooks a drop of three stories to a cobblestone courtyard. The fire is spreading from the kitchen. You have perhaps two minutes before the whole structure collapses. Outside, through the smoke, you can see shapes moving that do not move like people."

Veren's player: "I tear the bedsheet into strips, knot them together, and tie one end to the heavy bronze brazier. I throw the sheet out the window and prepare to climb down."

The Host sets the TN at 20 for escaping a burning building during a Dusk attack. The player rolls a d20, getting a 2. Veren has Swiftness 10 (modifier +0) and no proficiency in Athletics. Total: 2 + 0 = 2. Misses TN 20 by 18.

Host: "Critical Failure. The brazier tips as you test your weight on the sheet. It crashes through the weakened floor. You fall with it, landing hard in the ground floor kitchen. You take 1d6 damage from the fall and the flames. You are surrounded by fire and the Dusk Touched shapes are closing in. You have made your situation worse."

**Example 5: Legendary (TN 24): Pleading Before the Bright Mirror**

A player character must convince the Bright Mirror to spare a reformer who has been accused of heterodoxy.

Host: "The Bright Mirror sits motionless on the judgment seat. His face reveals nothing. The accused kneels to your left, wrists bound. The courtroom is silent except for the drip of water from the ceiling. The Bright Mirror speaks: 'You have one argument. Make it count.'"

Player: "I step forward. I do not plead for mercy. I argue that executing this reformer would be strategically unwise. I cite three precedents where the Commission's harshness created martyrs. I keep my voice calm and my arguments precise."

The Host sets the TN at 24 for convincing the Bright Mirror to show mercy. The player rolls a d20, getting a 17. A Duke of Eternal Night with Presence 20 (modifier +5) and proficiency in Persuasion (+2). Total: 17 + 5 + 2 = 24. Exactly meets TN 24.

Host: "The Bright Mirror's expression does not change. For a long moment, the only sound is the dripping water. Then he speaks. 'You argue from strategy rather than sentiment. That is wise. The prisoner will be remanded to house arrest rather than execution. You have saved a life today. I suggest you ensure that life is worth saving.'"

**Example 6: Impossible (TN 28): Lying to the Celestial Book**

A Night Warbler tries to deceive the Keeper of the Imperial Archives, a being who has spent centuries cataloging human falsehood.

Host: "The Celestial Book regards you with eyes that have seen the empire rise and fall and rise again. She knows every document you have ever filed, every request you have ever made, every person you have ever contacted through official channels. She waits."

Player: "I tell her I was in the archives researching the legitimate lineage of a minor noble house. I have a forged writ of authorization. I keep my story simple."

The Host sets the TN at 28 for lying to the Celestial Book. The player rolls a d20, getting a 7. Night Warbler with Presence 14 (modifier +2) and proficiency in Deception (+2). Total: 7 + 2 + 2 = 11. Misses TN 28 by 17.

Host: "The Celestial Book does not accuse you. She simply tilts her head and says, 'You were not in the archives. You were in the Western Repository, which is outside your clearance level. You accessed a file marked with the Crimson Sigil. You have been very busy tonight.' She does not raise her voice. She does not need to. What do you say now?"

**Example 7: Advantage from Clever Planning (TN 12)**

A Guest Among Forests named Willow attempts to follow a target through the night market.

Host: "The Night Market of Whispers is packed. Stalls crowd every available surface. Lanterns swing overhead. The smell of grilled fish and burning incense fills the air. Your target, a thin man in a grey coat, weaves through the crowd. If you lose sight of him, he is gone."

Willow's player: "Before we came here, I bought a small jar of pungent fish oil from a vendor. I dabbed some on the inside of my sleeve earlier. When I bumped into the target at the entrance, I transferred a trace to his collar. Now I can follow the smell."

The Host grants Advantage for this clever preparation. Willow rolls two d20: a 5 and a 16, taking the 16. Willow has Swiftness 12 (modifier +1) and proficiency in Stealth (+2). Total: 16 + 1 + 2 = 19 against TN 12.

Host: "You catch the scent even through the market's chaos. The fish oil is faint but unmistakable. You track him through three turnings, past the spice merchants, past the fortune tellers, to a nondescript door behind a noodle stall. He knocks twice, pauses, knocks three times. The door opens a crack."

**Example 8: Disadvantage from Poor Circumstances (TN 12)**

An Iron Calculation artificer named Gear tries to repair a broken communication device during a rainstorm.

Host: "The rain pours through the hole in the roof. The device sits on a table in a pool of water. Its inner mechanisms are exposed. You can see a cracked brass gear and a snapped filament. Your tools are getting wet. Your fingers are cold."

Gear's player: "I need this device working. I have to send a warning before the patrol walks into the ambush. I work as fast as I can."

The Host imposes Disadvantage because of the rain, cold, and fading light. Gear rolls two d20: a 6 and a 13, taking the 6. Gear has Intellect 16 (modifier +3) and proficiency in Craft (+2). Total: 6 + 3 + 2 = 11 against TN 12. Misses by 1.

Host: "Your fingers slip on a wet gear. The filament snaps completely. The device goes dark. However, in the process, you noticed something: the filament was already damaged before you touched it. This device was sabotaged, not broken by accident. The patrol is walking into a trap, and now you know someone wanted them to walk into it."

## Chapter Three: Character Creation

### Making Your Character

Character creation is designed to be completed in under thirty minutes. You will need a Character Dossier (a printed sheet for your chosen Archetype) and a pencil.

### Step One: Choose Your Archetype

The game provides thirteen Archetypes. Twelve of them correspond to a major figure in the Celestial Court. The thirteenth corresponds to a man the Court has never managed to count, which is why the Court still says twelve. When you choose an Archetype, you are not playing as that figure. You are playing as someone who shares their path, their training, and their general role in the empire. The Archetype provides your mechanical foundation.

The thirteen Archetypes are:

| Archetype             | Path             | Role in the Empire                     | Default Gender |
| --------------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| Cinnabar Heart        | Alchemist        | Bureau of Harmonious Narrative         | Male           |
| Night Warbler         | Shadow Hand      | Bureau of Internal Harmony             | Male           |
| Guest Among Forests   | Long Lived       | Representative of the Northern Expanse | Female         |
| Iron Calculation      | Artificer        | State Planning Commission              | Male           |
| Iron Wall             | Bastion          | Imperial Garrison Command              | Male           |
| Bright Mirror         | Justicar         | Commission for Celestial Purity        | Male           |
| Shadow                | Night Walker     | Independent Intelligence               | Male           |
| Duke of Eternal Night | Blood Cultivator | Crimson Lineage Aristocracy            | Male           |
| Iron Bone             | Fury Adept       | Red Banner Revival Movement            | Male           |
| Celestial Book        | Archivist        | Bureau of Celestial Inquiry            | Female         |
| Bone Script           | Hedgewarden      | None. You have no file.                | Male           |
| Incense Crown         | Oracle           | Temple of Ten Thousand Gods            | Female         |
| Stray Stroke          | Drifter          | None. You are eleven files, not one.   | Male           |

Full details for each Archetype are provided in the Character Dossiers supplement.

These defaults govern the named Court figures, the Stray Stroke, and every unmarked example in this build. A player may choose any gender for a player character without changing mechanics, history options, or advancement.

**How to choose your Archetype.** Think about the kind of story you want to tell. Do you want to be the person who speaks truth to power? The Bright Mirror or the Celestial Book fits well. Do you want to be the person who operates from the shadows? The Night Warbler or the Shadow suits that. Do you want to be the one who endures, who takes hits and keeps standing? The Iron Wall or the Iron Bone. Do you want to stand outside the machine entirely and look at it from underneath? The Bone Script. Do you want to hold a power the empire cannot take, copy, or replace, and discover that this is also a cage? The Incense Crown. Do you want to be the one person the record cannot resolve into a single man, and find out what that costs? The Stray Stroke. Your Archetype is not your destiny. It is your starting point.

Three of these Archetypes sit differently from the rest. The Bone Script has no rank, no seal, and no personnel file, which means the ordinary tools of Court pressure do not reach him and the ordinary protections do not either. The Incense Crown holds an office the empire depends on and cannot reproduce, which makes her both the safest person in the capital and the most closely watched. The Stray Stroke has been entered into the record eleven times under eleven genuine names, so that the empire holds eleven true accounts of him and cannot assemble a single one, which leaves him unreachable and unprotected in exactly equal measure. If your table wants a character who is outside the hierarchy rather than inside it, these are the three doors.

**Example choice.** A player named Mira wants to play a character who is a scholar drawn into dangerous politics. She reads the Archetype descriptions. The Celestial Book offers access to forbidden knowledge and a position in the archives, which appeals to her. The Cinnabar Heart offers alchemy and the manipulation of information through the Bureau of Harmonious Narrative. She chooses the Celestial Book, deciding her character is an archivist who found a document she was not meant to see.

### Step Two: Assign Your Facets

Every character has six **Facets** that measure their core capabilities:

| Facet         | Measures                              | Used For                                                             |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Might**     | Physical power, melee combat          | Striking, lifting, breaking, intimidation through strength           |
| **Swiftness** | Speed, agility, stealth               | Dodging, sneaking, ranged combat, initiative, picking locks          |
| **Endurance** | Toughness, stamina, resilience        | Resisting damage, surviving harsh conditions, holding breath         |
| **Intellect** | Knowledge, reasoning, Qi manipulation | Investigation, conducting rituals, crafting, recalling lore          |
| **Presence**  | Charisma, bearing, social force       | Persuasion, deception, leadership, performance, commanding attention |
| **Resolve**   | Willpower, courage, perception        | Resisting fear, detecting lies, maintaining sanity, staying focused  |

Your Archetype provides a baseline array of Facet scores. You then have **six additional points** to distribute among your Facets as you wish. You may not raise any Facet above 18 with these points. A few Archetypes begin with a signature Facet of 20: the Celestial Book's Intellect and Resolve, the Incense Crown's and the Duke's Presence. That score is the Archetype's gift rather than something you bought, and it cannot be raised further.

**Facet Modifier:** Your Facet Modifier equals (Facet Score minus 10) divided by 2, rounded down. A Facet of 14 gives a modifier of +2. A Facet of 8 gives a modifier of -1.

**Example assignment.** Mira's Celestial Book baseline is Might 6, Swiftness 8, Endurance 12, Intellect 20, Presence 16, Resolve 20. She has 6 bonus points. Intellect and Resolve are already at the Archetype's signature 20 and cannot be raised, so she spends elsewhere. She puts 4 points into Endurance, raising it to 16 (modifier +3), because an archivist who faints during an Audit is no use to anyone. She puts 2 into Presence, raising it to 18 (modifier +4). Might stays at 6 and Swiftness at 8. She decides her character is brilliant and immovable and physically hopeless, and that this is the point of her.

### Step Three: Select Skills

Skills represent specific areas of training and expertise. Each Skill is tied to a Facet. When you make a Resolution Roll using a Skill you are proficient in, you add your **Proficiency Bonus** (+2 at character creation, increasing with advancement) to the roll in addition to your Facet Modifier.

Your Archetype grants proficiency in three Skills. You may choose **two additional Skills** from the full list.

**Skill List:**

_Might Skills:_

- Athletics: Running, climbing, swimming, feats of strength
- Brawling: Unarmed combat, improvised weapons, grappling
- Intimidation: Coercion through physical presence or threats

_Swiftness Skills:_

- Stealth: Moving unseen, hiding, shadowing a target
- Acrobatics: Balancing, tumbling, escaping restraints
- Precision: Ranged weapons, throwing, delicate manual tasks
- Larceny: Pickpocketing, lockpicking, disabling traps

_Endurance Skills:_

- Survival: Foraging, tracking, enduring elements, navigating wilderness
- Fortitude: Resisting poison, disease, exhaustion, physical interrogation

_Intellect Skills:_

- Investigation: Searching for clues, analyzing evidence, research
- Lore: Knowledge of history, ritual, the Court, the Empire
- Qi Theory: Understanding and manipulating Celestial Qi
- Craft: Building, repairing, forging documents, creating devices

_Presence Skills:_

- Persuasion: Convincing others through reason or charm
- Deception: Lying convincingly, maintaining cover stories
- Performance: Public speaking, artistic expression, impersonation
- Connections: Knowing who to talk to, calling in favors, navigating bureaucracy

_Resolve Skills:_

- Insight: Reading people, detecting lies, sensing motives
- Discipline: Resisting temptation, maintaining composure, meditation
- Vigilance: Noticing danger, staying alert, detecting ambushes

**Pairing a Skill with a different Facet.** The Facet listed beside each Skill is its default, not its only pairing. When the fiction clearly calls for a different one, the Host may pair a Skill with whatever Facet the situation demands: Intimidation with Presence when a character menaces by sheer bearing rather than muscle, Craft with Swiftness when the work is delicate rather than clever, Lore with Resolve when the question is whether you can recall it under interrogation. Proficiency still applies. The Host should name the pairing aloud before the roll so the table can see the logic.

**Example selection.** The Celestial Book Archetype grants proficiency in Lore, Investigation, and Qi Theory. Mira needs to choose two more. Her character is an archivist, but she wants her to have some practical skills for when things go wrong. She chooses Insight (Resolve) to read dangerous people, and Deception (Presence) because archivists who cannot lie do not survive long in the Court. She records these five skills on her dossier.

### Step Four: Determine Derived Values

**Hit Points (HP):** Your capacity to withstand physical harm. Calculated as: 10 + Endurance Modifier + Archetype Bonus. When HP reaches 0, you are dying.

**Qi Pool:** Your reserve of Celestial Qi for techniques and rituals. Calculated as: 10 + Intellect Modifier + Archetype Bonus.

**Sanity:** Your mental and spiritual stability. Starts at 20 + Resolve Modifier. When Sanity reaches 0, your character suffers a breakdown and may become unplayable.

**Initiative:** Swiftness Modifier plus any Archetype bonuses. Used to determine turn order in combat.

**Defense:** 10 + Swiftness Modifier. The base TN for attacks targeting you.

**Example calculation.** Mira's Celestial Book has Endurance 16 (modifier +3) and an Archetype bonus of +1 HP. Her HP is 10 + 3 + 1 = 14. Her Qi Pool: Intellect 20 (modifier +5) with an Archetype bonus of +8 Qi. Total Qi Pool: 10 + 5 + 8 = 23. Sanity: 20 + Resolve modifier (+5) = 25. Initiative: Swiftness 8 (modifier -1), with no Archetype bonus, so -1. Defense: 10 + (-1) = 9. She is the most fragile character at the table and the hardest to break.

### Step Five: Starting Resources

Every character begins with:

- **Celestial Favor:** 10 points. Your currency of influence within the Court. Spend to call in favors, access restricted areas, or survive investigations.
- **Ritual Harmony:** 50. A measure of how orthodox you are perceived to be. The scale runs from 0 (Heterodox: under active investigation) to 100 (Exemplary: a threat to everyone less pure than you, which is everyone).
- **Equipment:** Your Archetype provides starting equipment. You may also begin with one personal item of significance, agreed upon with the Host.

### Step Six: Your Truth

Every character carries a **Truth**: something they know that complicates their relationship with the Court. Your Archetype suggests a Truth, but you may work with the Host to create your own. This Truth is a narrative hook. It may be a secret you protect, a discovery you made, or a connection you have that would be dangerous if revealed.

Examples:

- You know a senior official's secret dealings.
- You have seen a document that contradicts established doctrine.
- You are related by blood to someone who was Disappeared.
- You have a contact in a fallen province who still sends messages.

Your Truth should be something the Host can use to create personal stakes in the larger story.

**Example Truth.** Mira decides that her Celestial Book, Scholar Jinhai, found a fragment of a journal in the archive stacks. The journal appears to be written in the Sun Emperor's own hand and dated fifty years after the Sun Emperor withdrew from public life. The fragment mentions something called "the First Omission." Jinhai has told no one about this discovery. She keeps the fragment hidden in her quarters. The Host now has a plot hook: what is the First Omission, and who else is looking for this journal?

### Step Seven: Faction Standing

Your Archetype has a default starting relationship with each of the six Schools of Thought. Record these on your Character Dossier. During play, these Faction Standing values will shift based on your actions.

### Step Eight: Name and History

Give your character a name. Write two or three sentences about who they were before they became entangled in the Court's machinations. Where were they born? What did their family do? What event pushed them into the path they now walk?

Answer these three questions:

1. What do you want most? (Your ambition)
2. What would you never do? (Your line)
3. Who matters to you? (Your bond)

Share your answers with the Host. They will use them.

**Example history.** Mira writes: "Jinhai was born in a small town in the eastern province of Serene Waters. Her family were paper makers. She was recruited into the Bureau of Celestial Inquiry at age twelve because a visiting examiner noticed her ability to memorize documents after reading them once. She has spent eighteen years in the archives. She has always believed the archives exist to preserve truth. The fragment of the Emperor's journal has made her question everything."

Her answers: "I want most to understand the truth about the Emperor's silence. I would never destroy knowledge, even knowledge that condemns me. My bond is to my younger sister, a clerk in the Bureau of Harmonious Narrative who does not know what I have found."

### A Complete Character Build from Start to Finish

Here is a complete walkthrough of creating a character from nothing to ready for play.

**Player:** Alex
**Archetype Chosen:** Night Warbler (Shadow Hand)

**Step 1: Archetype.** Alex reads the Archetype descriptions and picks the Night Warbler. The Shadow Hand appeals because Alex wants to play an infiltrator, someone who moves through the Court unseen and uncovers secrets. The Night Warbler baseline is Swiftness 18, Might 14, Resolve 14, Intellect 14, Endurance 12, Presence 10.

**Step 2: Facets.** Alex has 6 points to distribute. Night Warbler abilities rely on Swiftness and Presence, but Swiftness is already at 18 and cannot be raised with these points. Alex spends all 6 on Presence, raising it from 10 to 16 (modifier +3), because an infiltrator who cannot talk her way back out is a corpse with good shoes. Might stays at 14 (modifier +2). Endurance stays at 12 (modifier +1). Intellect stays at 14 (modifier +2). Resolve stays at 14 (modifier +2).

**Step 3: Skills.** The Night Warbler grants Stealth, Precision, and Insight. Alex chooses two more: Deception (to maintain a cover story) and Acrobatics (to escape through windows and across rooftops).

**Step 4: Derived Values.** Night Warbler has HP bonus +2 and Qi bonus +3. With Endurance 12 (modifier +1): HP = 10 + 1 + 2 = 13. With Intellect 14 (modifier +2): Qi Pool = 10 + 2 + 3 = 15. Sanity = 20 + Resolve modifier (+2) = 22. Initiative = Swiftness modifier (+4) + Night Warbler bonus (+2) = 6. Base Defense = 10 + 4 = 14, rising to 15 while she wears concealed armor.

**Step 5: Resources.** Starting equipment: concealed armor, two hidden blades, six daggers, a dark cloak, a grappling hook with silk rope, and forged identification papers. Plus one personal item: a jade pendant that belonged to Cinder's mother. Plus 15 Copper Leaves.

**Step 6: Truth.** Alex decides: "I know that a senior member of the Golden Orthodoxy has been meeting with a representative of the Crimson Dusk. I witnessed the meeting by accident while following a different target. I have not reported it because I am not sure who to trust."

**Step 7: Faction Standing.** Night Warbler baseline: Neutral with the Golden Orthodoxy, slightly negative with the Verdant Path, Neutral with the Iron Calculation, slightly positive with the Crimson Lineage, strongly negative with the Common Flame, and slightly negative with the Bright Mirror. The Bureau of Internal Harmony is Cinder's nominal employer, but it is an institution rather than one of the six Schools.

**Step 8: Name and History.** "My name is Cinder. I was a street child in the capital's outer districts. The Bureau of Internal Harmony recruited me at age fifteen because I could get into places I should not be able to get into. I have served them for twelve years. I am good at my job. I am starting to wonder if I am on the right side."

Ambition: "I want to know who is really running the Bureau." Line: "I will not harm children, not even for the mission." Bond: "The old woman who runs the noodle stall where I ate as a child. She never asked questions. She fed me anyway."

The character is ready for play.

### Creating Compelling Character Concepts

A character concept is more than a collection of numbers. It is the answer to the question: why should anyone at this table care about this person?

**Start with a contradiction.** The most memorable characters contain opposing impulses. A soldier who is afraid of combat. A spy who believes in honesty. A scholar who distrusts knowledge. Contradictions create internal conflict, and internal conflict creates drama.

**Give them a flaw that matters.** Not a cute flaw like "I talk too much." A real flaw that creates problems at the table. A hot temper that makes them burn bridges. A loyalty that makes them protect the wrong people. A curiosity that leads them into danger they know they should avoid.

**Connect them to the world.** Your character should have at least one relationship that matters to them. A friend, a rival, a family member, a mentor, a student. The Host can use these relationships to create personal stakes. A player whose only relationship is to their own ambition has given the Host nothing to work with.

**Ask: what would make this character betray their principles?** If you cannot imagine the circumstances, your character is not yet fully formed. Every character has a breaking point. Knowing what it is before play begins helps you make consistent choices when pressure mounts.

**Ask: what would make this character change?** Characters who do not change are not characters. They are statues. Your character should enter the game with beliefs that could be challenged, assumptions that could be overturned, loyalties that could be tested.

**Example concepts.**

A Duke of Eternal Night who is secretly funding reformist pamphlets because he fell in love with a revolutionary. He hates what he is doing but cannot stop.

An Iron Wall who has never been tested in real combat, only in exercises. He believes he is ready. Everyone else wonders.

A Cinnabar Heart who uses his position in the Bureau of Harmonious Narrative to hide evidence of imperial crimes. He tells himself he is preserving stability. He is no longer certain he believes it.

A Guest Among Forests who has lived for more than four centuries and is deeply, profoundly tired. She has seen empires rise and fall. She struggles to care about the current crisis but feels obligated to try.

A Shadow who was once a member of every school and was expelled from all of them. He works alone because he has burned every bridge. He tells himself he prefers it that way.

## Chapter Four: The Six Facets in Detail

Each Facet represents a dimension of your character's capability. This chapter explains what each Facet governs and how it manifests in play.

### Might

Might measures raw physical power. Characters with high Might can break down doors, wield heavy weapons, carry wounded allies, and physically dominate opponents. Might is not merely muscle: it is the capacity to project force into the world.

In combat, Might determines melee attack rolls and melee damage. Outside combat, Might governs feats of strength and physical intimidation.

**What Might feels like at different scores.**

**Might 6 (modifier -2).** You are physically frail. A heavy stack of books winded you. You struggle to open a stubborn window. In a fight, you rely entirely on speed, guile, or weapons of convenience. A shove from an ordinary person might knock you off balance. You have learned to avoid physical confrontations because you rarely win them.

**Might 10 (modifier +0).** You are an ordinary person with ordinary strength. You can carry your own belongings, walk all day, and hold your own in a bar fight if you have to. You are not impressive, but you are not a liability. The average soldier, laborer, and farmer all share this level.

**Might 14 (modifier +2).** You are noticeably strong. You can lift a full grown person with effort. You break a wooden chair over someone's back and the chair breaks. When you grab someone by the collar, they stay grabbed. Laborers respect your strength. Guards think twice before physically challenging you.

**Might 18 (modifier +4).** You are extraordinarily powerful. You can bend iron bars with your hands. You punch through wooden doors. You carry an injured comrade under each arm and still move at a jog. People step aside when you walk through a crowd. In combat, your blows send enemies reeling.

**Might 20 (modifier +5).** You are a force of nature. You can tear a door from its hinges with one pull. You lift the edge of a collapsed cart to free someone trapped beneath. Your unarmed strikes are as dangerous as most people's sword swings. There are perhaps a dozen people in the capital with your raw physical power, and you are one of them.

### Swiftness

Swiftness measures speed, coordination, and grace. Characters with high Swiftness move first in combat, dodge attacks that would fell others, slip through shadows unnoticed, and land precise shots from across a battlefield.

In combat, Swiftness determines initiative, Defense, and ranged attack rolls. Outside combat, Swiftness governs stealth, acrobatics, and any task requiring fine motor control.

**What Swiftness feels like at different scores.**

**Swiftness 6 (modifier -2).** You are clumsy and slow. You trip on uneven floors. You knock things over when reaching for them. You cannot catch a thrown object. You are always the last to react in a crisis. You have bruises from walking into door frames and you do not remember how you got most of them.

**Swiftness 10 (modifier +0).** You move like an average person. You can catch a ball if you concentrate. You can walk quietly across a wooden floor if you try. You are neither graceful nor clumsy. You simply exist in space and occasionally bump into furniture.

**Swiftness 14 (modifier +2).** You are quick and coordinated. You catch falling objects before they hit the ground. You walk across a room without making a sound when you want to. You climb walls and leap gaps that make others hesitate. In a crowd, you move like water flowing around stones.

**Swiftness 18 (modifier +4).** You are extraordinarily fast. You snatch a document from a desk while the clerk is still turning to see who entered. You dodge a crossbow bolt fired from across a courtyard. You run across rooftops without slowing. People describe your movements as "blurring."

**Swiftness 20 (modifier +5).** You are preternaturally quick. You catch arrows out of the air. You move so quietly that people do not realize you are in the room until you speak. In the time it takes an opponent to draw a weapon, you have crossed the room, taken it from them, and stepped back.

### Endurance

Endurance measures physical resilience and stamina. Characters with high Endurance can take blows that would kill lesser people, survive in harsh environments, resist poison and disease, and keep fighting when others have collapsed.

Endurance directly determines Hit Points and governs resistance to physical harm of all kinds.

**What Endurance feels like at different scores.**

**Endurance 6 (modifier -2).** You are fragile. A hard fall leaves you winded for minutes. A minor cut becomes infected. You tire before others on a long march. You catch every illness that passes through the city. Your HP is dangerously low, and every fight carries the risk of your death.

**Endurance 10 (modifier +0).** You are an average healthy person. You can work a full day without collapsing. You recover from a cold in a few days. A fight leaves you bruised but functional. You are not tough, but you are not made of glass either.

**Endurance 14 (modifier +2).** You are resilient. You can march through a night and a day without rest. A blow that would fold an ordinary person barely makes you grunt. You shrug off diseases that put others in bed. You hold your breath for an impressively long time.

**Endurance 18 (modifier +4).** You are exceptionally tough. You survive falls that should break your legs. You keep fighting with wounds that would send others into shock. You can go three days without food and still function. Poison affects you weakly and briefly.

**Endurance 20 (modifier +5).** You are nearly unbreakable. You have been stabbed and kept fighting. You have been thrown through a wall and gotten up. You have walked through a blizzard in clothing meant for mild weather and arrived tired but alive. Your HP is vast, and your threshold for pain is unimaginable to most people.

### Intellect

Intellect measures reasoning, knowledge, and the capacity to manipulate Celestial Qi. Characters with high Intellect are scholars, investigators, ritualists, and strategists. They see patterns others miss and understand systems others take for granted.

In combat, Intellect determines the effectiveness of Qi Techniques. Outside combat, Intellect governs investigation, lore, crafting, and ritual performance.

**What Intellect feels like at different scores.**

**Intellect 6 (modifier -2).** You are unlettered and slow to grasp new concepts. You struggle with written language. You cannot follow complex arguments. You rely on instinct and memory rather than analysis. People call you simple. You have learned to let them underestimate you.

**Intellect 10 (modifier +0).** You are an ordinary person of ordinary intelligence. You can read and write, do basic arithmetic, and follow a conversation about politics if it is not too complicated. You are not a scholar, but you are not a fool either.

**Intellect 14 (modifier +2).** You are sharp and well informed. You catch inconsistencies in documents that others skim past. You follow three conversations at once. You can reconstruct a sequence of events from partial evidence. People come to you for advice because you see angles they miss.

**Intellect 18 (modifier +4).** You are brilliant. You memorize documents after a single reading. You recognize obscure references and make connections between disparate fields. You can design a device, draft a legal argument, and decode a cipher in the same afternoon. Scholars respect your mind.

**Intellect 20 (modifier +5).** You are a genius. You see systems within systems, patterns within patterns. You can predict an opponent's strategy three moves ahead. You grasp new fields of knowledge in days. Your understanding of Celestial Qi is deep enough that you can attempt techniques others consider impossible.

### Presence

Presence measures social force: the weight a person carries when they enter a room. Characters with high Presence are leaders, diplomats, deceivers, and performers. They can sway crowds, talk their way past guards, and make people want to help them.

Presence governs social interaction of all kinds: persuasion, deception, performance, and the ability to project authority or warmth as needed.

**What Presence feels like at different scores.**

**Presence 6 (modifier -2).** You are invisible or repellent. People forget you are in the room. When you speak, others talk over you. You struggle to make eye contact. Your jokes fall flat. Your attempts at persuasion come across as awkward or desperate. You have learned to let others do the talking.

**Presence 10 (modifier +0).** You are unremarkable in social settings. You can hold a conversation. You can make a request. You can tell a small lie without being obvious. You are neither charismatic nor off putting. You exist in the social space without dominating it.

**Presence 14 (modifier +2).** You are likeable and persuasive. People enjoy talking to you. When you enter a room, some people notice. You can calm a tense situation with a few carefully chosen words. You are good at reading a room and adjusting your approach. Your lies usually hold.

**Presence 18 (modifier +4).** You are magnetic. When you enter a room, conversation shifts. People want your approval. You can charm information out of hostile sources. You can command a crowd. Your words carry weight. Officials remember you after a single meeting.

**Presence 20 (modifier +5).** You are legendary. Your presence changes the atmosphere of any room you enter. You can make people believe things they know are false. You can calm a mob or incite one. Your voice carries authority that transcends your actual position. People follow you because being near you makes them feel significant.

### Resolve

Resolve measures inner strength: willpower, courage, perception, and the ability to withstand psychological pressure. Characters with high Resolve cannot be broken by fear, cannot be deceived by lies, and maintain their sanity in the face of the Crimson Dusk.

Resolve determines Sanity and governs resistance to fear, deception, and supernatural horror.

**What Resolve feels like at different scores.**

**Resolve 6 (modifier -2).** You are easily shaken. Loud noises make you jump. You believe what people tell you because questioning them is exhausting. You avoid conflict and confrontation. At night, you lie awake thinking about everything that could go wrong. The Dusk would break you quickly.

**Resolve 10 (modifier +0).** You have ordinary willpower. You can stand your ground in an argument. You resist obvious manipulation. You sleep through the night most of the time. You are not especially brave, but you are not a coward either. You can look at something horrible without screaming.

**Resolve 14 (modifier +2).** You are steady. Panic does not take you easily. You maintain composure when others lose theirs. You notice when someone is trying to manipulate you. You can look into the face of horror and keep functioning. People rely on you in a crisis.

**Resolve 18 (modifier +4).** You are iron willed. You have stared into the Dusk and not looked away. You maintain your principles under torture. You detect lies like others detect changes in the weather. You inspire courage in those around you simply by remaining calm.

**Resolve 20 (modifier +5).** You are unshakeable. Your sense of self is so strong that effects which warp reality struggle to take hold. You can resist the temptation of the Dusk when others succumb. You walk into hopeless situations without despair because you have accepted the possibility of death. Your calm in the face of annihilation is almost disturbing to witness.

## Chapter Five: Skills and Proficiency

Skills represent specialized training. They add granularity to the Facet system, allowing characters to be good at specific applications of their broader capabilities.

### Proficiency Bonus

Your Proficiency Bonus starts at +2 and increases as your character advances:

| Character Level | Proficiency Bonus |
| --------------- | ----------------- |
| 1 to 4          | +2                |
| 5 to 8          | +3                |
| 9 to 12         | +4                |
| 13 to 16        | +5                |

When you make a Resolution Roll using a Skill you are proficient in, add your Proficiency Bonus in addition to the relevant Facet Modifier.

### Using Skills Without Proficiency

Anyone can attempt any skill. If you are not proficient, you still add your Facet Modifier. Proficiency represents formal training or extensive experience, not the baseline ability to try.

### Gaining New Proficiencies

At the Host's discretion, characters may gain new Skill proficiencies through extended training, study, or significant experiences. As a guideline, learning a new Skill proficiency requires dedicating significant downtime (weeks or months) to focused practice with a teacher or through intense independent study.

## Chapter Six: Combat

Combat in the Violet Court is lethal and decisive. Most fights end quickly, and every battle carries the risk of permanent death. The system is designed to be fast at the table while preserving tactical depth.

### The Combat Round

Combat unfolds in rounds. Each round represents roughly six seconds of action. Within a round, every participant takes one turn, in order of initiative.

### Step One: Determine Initiative

At the start of combat, every participant makes an **initiative check** (d20 + Initiative: Swiftness Modifier plus any Archetype bonuses). The Host may grant Advantage to characters who have prepared an ambush or Disadvantage to characters who are surprised. Results are ranked highest to lowest to determine turn order.

The Host may also choose to use **Side Initiative**: all players act in any order they choose, then all enemies act, or vice versa. This is faster for large encounters and encourages teamwork.

### Step Two: On Your Turn

On your turn, you may take **one Action** and **one Movement**. You may also take one **Free Interaction** (speaking a few words, drawing a weapon, dropping an item).

**Actions:**

- **Strike:** Make a melee or ranged attack against a target. Roll d20 + Might Modifier (melee) or Swiftness Modifier (ranged) against the target's Defense. On a hit, roll damage based on your weapon.
- **Use Technique:** Activate a Qi Technique. See Chapter Seven.
- **Guard:** Until your next turn, attacks against you have Disadvantage, and you gain +4 Defense. You cannot Strike this turn.
- **Assist:** Grant Advantage to the next ally who attacks or acts against a target you designate.
- **Improvise:** Attempt something not covered by the other actions. The Host adjudicates.

**Movement:**

- Move to an adjacent Zone. See Zones below.
- Stand up from prone.
- Take cover behind an object within your Zone.

**Free Interaction:**

- Speak a short phrase.
- Draw or sheathe a weapon.
- Pick up an item at your feet.
- Make a quick gesture.

### Zones

Combat in the Violet Court uses abstract **Zones** rather than precise grid measurements. A Zone is a narratively coherent area: "the courtyard," "the archive chamber," "the burning rooftop." Zones are connected to adjacent Zones. Moving from one Zone to another uses your Movement for the turn.

Ranged attacks can target anyone in the same Zone or an adjacent Zone. Attacking someone two Zones away requires a weapon with sufficient range and may impose Disadvantage.

Zones allow the Host to describe combat in vivid terms without counting squares. "The assassin leaps from the balcony Zone down to the courtyard Zone" is more evocative than "the assassin moves 30 feet."

### Damage and Dying

When you hit with an attack, roll the weapon's damage dice and add the relevant Facet Modifier (Might for melee, Swiftness for ranged, Intellect for Qi Techniques). Subtract the total from the target's HP.

When a character reaches **0 HP**, they are **Dying**. At the start of each of their turns while Dying, they make an Endurance check against TN 15. On a success, they stabilize but remain unconscious. On a failure, they worsen. After three cumulative failures before a success, the character dies.

An ally can stabilize a Dying character with a successful Intellect check (TN 12) using appropriate supplies, or by spending one use of a healing technique or item.

### Weapon Reference

| Weapon       | Damage | Properties                                                                       |
| ------------ | ------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Unarmed      | 1d4    | Light                                                                            |
| Dagger       | 1d6    | Light, Concealable, Throwable                                                    |
| Sword        | 1d8    | Versatile (1d10 with both hands)                                                 |
| Greatsword   | 1d12   | Heavy, Requires Two Hands                                                        |
| Bow          | 1d8    | Ranged, Requires Two Hands                                                       |
| Crossbow     | 1d10   | Ranged, Requires Two Hands, Loading                                              |
| Staff        | 1d6    | Versatile (1d8 with both hands), Qi Focus                                        |
| Hidden Blade | 1d4    | Concealable, First Strike (+1d6 on the first round if you act before the target) |

**Properties:**

- **Light:** Can be dual wielded. When dual wielding, you may make a second attack as a Free Interaction, but it deals half damage.
- **Concealable:** Can be hidden on your person. Guards do not find it on a casual search.
- **Throwable:** Can be used for ranged attacks within the same Zone.
- **Versatile:** Deals the higher damage die when used with two hands.
- **Heavy:** Requires Might 13+ to wield effectively. Otherwise, attacks have Disadvantage.
- **Requires Two Hands:** This weapon requires both hands.
- **Qi Focus:** Can channel Qi Techniques through this weapon, adding +1 to Technique attack rolls.
- **First Strike:** On the first round of combat, if you act before your target, this weapon deals additional damage.
- **Loading:** Requires a Free Interaction to reload between shots.

### Combat Example 1: Surprise Ambush in the Alley

**The Setup.** The player characters have been following a courier who carries documents for the Golden Orthodoxy. They cornered him in a narrow alley behind the Temple of Echoing Prayers. They intended to question him quietly. He intended to deliver his package. Neither plan survived contact with the enemy. The courier's three escorts, hidden in the shadows, attack first.

**Zones.** The alley (Zone A). The temple courtyard (Zone B, adjacent). The rooftop (Zone C, above the alley, adjacent to Zone A with a climb).

**Participants.** Cinder (Night Warbler, Swiftness 18). Jinhai (Celestial Book, Intellect 20). Steelhand (Iron Bone, Might 18). Three Orthodoxy thugs (minions). The courier (noncombatant).

**Round 1.**

Host: "The alley is narrow, barely wide enough for two people to walk side by side. Rain water drips from eaves overhead. The courier stops when he sees you block the far end. He says, 'You are making a mistake.' That is when you hear the scrape of boots on stone behind you. Three figures step out of the recessed doorway you just passed. They have knives."

Initiatives: Cinder rolls 19 + 6 = 25. Steelhand rolls 14 + 2 = 16. Thugs roll 12 + 1 = 13. Jinhai rolls 4 + (-1) = 3.

Cinder acts first.

Cinder's player: "I do not wait for them to close the distance. I spin and throw a dagger at the nearest thug. Then I move toward the courier. If I can grab the documents, this fight becomes pointless."

Host: "The thug is in the same Zone, so no range penalty. Roll your attack."

Cinder rolls 15 + Swiftness modifier (+4) + Proficiency in Precision (+2) = 21 against the thug's Defense of 12. A hit. Dagger damage: 1d6. Rolls a 4, plus Swiftness modifier (+4). Total damage: 8. The thug has 10 HP.

Host: "The dagger catches him in the shoulder. He staggers but does not fall. Blood spreads across his grey tunic. He snarls and keeps coming. Your dagger is now in his shoulder, not your hand."

Cinder moves to the courier's Zone. Free Interaction: she draws one of her hidden blades.

Steelhand's turn.

Steelhand's player: "I charge the thugs. No finesse. I punch the injured one square in the face."

Steelhand rolls 12 + Might modifier (+4) + Proficiency in Brawling (+2) = 18 against the thug's Defense of 12. A hit. Unarmed damage: 1d4. Rolls a 4, plus Might modifier +4. Total: 8.

Host: "Your fist connects with his jaw. You feel something crack. His eyes roll back and he collapses. One down. The other two thugs look at each other, reassessing."

The remaining two thugs act.

Host: "The two remaining thugs move together. One slashes at you, Steelhand, while the other tries to grab Cinder before she reaches the courier."

Thug 1 attacks Steelhand: rolls 8 + 1 = 9 against Steelhand's Defense of 12. Miss.

Thug 2 attempts to grapple Cinder: rolls 11 + 1 = 12 against Cinder's Defense of 15, including her concealed armor. Miss.

Host: "The first thug's knife skids off your armored forearm, Steelhand. Sparks fly. The second thug lunges for Cinder but grabs only air as she sidesteps. The courier is backing away, fumbling for something in his coat."

Jinhai's turn.

Jinhai's player: "I am at the back of the alley. I cannot reach the fight directly. But I can see the courier reaching for something. I use Qi Sense to check if he has an object charged with Qi on him."

The Host sets the TN at 12 for basic Qi detection. Jinhai rolls 8 + Intellect modifier (+5) + Proficiency in Qi Theory (+2) = 15. Success.

Host: "Your Qi Sense flickers. The courier has something in his coat that glows with Qi. It is faint but focused, like a talisman. And it is getting brighter. He is activating it."

**Round 2.**

Initiative order remains the same.

Cinder's turn.

Cinder's player: "I do not give him time to finish. I close the distance and drive my hidden blade into his wrist, the hand reaching into the coat. I am trying to stop the activation, not kill him."

Cinder rolls 17 + Might modifier (+2) = 19 against the courier's Defense of 10. A hit. Damage: 1d4 (rolls 2) + Might modifier (+2). First Strike no longer applies. Total: 4. The courier has 8 HP.

Host: "Your blade punches through his sleeve and into his forearm. He screams and drops whatever he was holding. A jade talisman clatters to the wet stones. The glow fades. The courier clutches his arm, blood running between his fingers. The remaining thugs freeze, seeing their principal wounded."

Steelhand's turn.

Steelhand's player: "I raise my fists and speak to the remaining thugs. 'Your employer is bleeding in the mud. Your friend is unconscious. You can still walk away.' I use Intimidation."

Steelhand rolls 10 + Might modifier (+4) + Proficiency in Intimidation (+2) = 16 against TN 12 (Neutral Stance). Success.

Host: "The thugs exchange a look. One of them drops his knife. It hits the stones with a wet clatter. 'We were just paid to escort him,' he says. 'This is not our fight.' They back away slowly, then turn and run."

**Aftermath.** The party has the courier, the jade talisman, and the documents. The fight lasted two rounds. One thug is unconscious. The courier is wounded but alive. The party can now question him.

### Combat Example 2: Social Encounter Turns Violent

**The Setup.** The player characters are attending a formal reception at the Estate of Far Horizons, hosted by a neutral faction. They are here to gather information about a conspiracy. A rival agent from the Crimson Lineage confronts them publicly, hoping to provoke them into a scene that will get them expelled.

**Zones.** The grand hall (Zone A). The balcony overlooking the hall (Zone B, adjacent to Zone A by stairs). The garden terrace (Zone C, adjacent to Zone B through open doors). The wine table (obstacle in Zone A).

**Participants.** Cinder (Night Warbler). Jinhai (Celestial Book). Lady Crimson, a Duke of Eternal Night (NPC antagonist). Various guests (noncombatants and obstacles).

Host: "The hall is magnificent. Candlelight reflects off lacquered pillars. A dozen minor nobles and officials circulate with cups of imported wine. Lady Crimson approaches your group with a smile that does not reach her eyes. She speaks loudly enough for others to hear. 'I heard the most interesting rumor about you, Scholar Jinhai. Something about a document you found in the archives. Something about the Emperor's handwriting.'"

Jinhai's player: "I keep my expression neutral. I say, 'The archives contain many documents. You would need to be more specific.' I try to sound bored rather than defensive."

Host: "Lady Crimson's smile widens. 'A journal fragment, I believe. With handwriting that matches the Sun Emperor's personal correspondence. But that cannot be right, can it? No private writing has been verified since the Emperor withdrew.' She has made this public. Other guests are turning to listen."

The Host calls for a social check. This is a social attack. Lady Crimson has chosen to damage Jinhai's standing publicly.

Jinhai's player: "I match her tone. 'If I had found such a document, I would have filed it with the Bureau of Celestial Inquiry, where it would be properly cataloged and studied. Are you suggesting I have not done my duty?' I am trying to shift the narrative from 'I found something secret' to 'I am a professional doing my job.'"

Roll: Jinhai rolls Presence + Persuasion. d20 shows 11. Jinhai has Presence 18 (modifier +4) and is not proficient in Persuasion. Total: 15 against Lady Crimson's Stance of Suspicious (TN 16 for persuasion). Misses by 1.

Host: "Lady Crimson laughs lightly. 'Oh, I am sure you filed it properly. The question is who else has seen it before you filed it. The question is who you showed it to. The question is why a scholar of your modest reputation would be the one to discover something so significant.' The guests are murmuring. She has turned your professionalism into a point against you. You have lost Social Standing."

Cinder's player: "I step forward and interject. 'Lady Crimson, you seem remarkably well informed about the contents of a document you claim not to have seen. How exactly do you know what Scholar Jinhai found?' I am making a social counterattack."

Cinder rolls Presence + Deception (she is pretending to be merely curious, but the real purpose is to undermine Lady Crimson). d20 shows 18. Cinder has Presence 16 (modifier +3) and proficiency in Deception (+2). Total: 23 against Lady Crimson's Insight of 15 (10 + Resolve modifier +3 + proficiency +2). Success.

Host: "Lady Crimson's smile falters for just a fraction of a second. The guests notice. Some of them turn to look at her instead. 'I have my sources,' she says, but her voice has lost its easy confidence. 'We all have our sources.' She takes a drink of wine to cover her recovery."

Lady Crimson, angered, decides to escalate. She sets down her wine glass and speaks coldly.

Host: "Lady Crimson sets down her glass with a deliberate click. 'You know, I have had enough of this farce. You come into my city, you skulk through my archives, you interfere in matters you do not understand. And then you have the audacity to question me in my own Court.' She snaps her fingers. Four armed attendants step forward from the walls. The reception is over. Roll Initiative."

The social encounter has collapsed into combat. Party members who were not present in the main hall must spend their first turn moving Zones.

Initiatives: Cinder 23. Lady Crimson's attendants 18. Lady Crimson 15. Jinhai 9.

Cinder's turn.

Cinder's player: "The balcony is right there. I vault over the railing onto the balcony Zone to get elevation and a better position. From there, I draw a dagger and throw it at the nearest attendant."

Cinder rolls Acrobatics (for the vault). d20 shows 12 + Swiftness modifier (+4) + Proficiency in Acrobatics (+2) = 18 against TN 8 (routine). Success.

Host: "You flow over the railing like water. Guests scatter. You land softly on the balcony and immediately go for your blade."

Cinder attacks: d20 shows 9 + 4 + 2 = 15 against attendant Defense of 12. Hit. Damage: 1d6 (rolls 3) + 4 = 7.

Host: "Your blade catches the attendant in the shoulder. He grunts and keeps coming but is favoring that arm."

Attendants' turn.

Host: "Two attendants move toward Jinhai. Two move toward the balcony stairs to flank Cinder. They draw curved swords."

Attendant 1 attacks Jinhai: d20 shows 14 + 2 = 16 against Jinhai's Defense of 9. Hit. Damage: 1d8 (rolls 5). Jinhai has 14 HP. Now at 9.

Host: "The blade opens a cut across Jinhai's forearm. Blood soaks into her robe sleeve. She hisses in pain but stays on her feet."

Lady Crimson's turn.

Host: "Lady Crimson does not draw a weapon. Instead, she raises her hand and Qi begins to gather around her fingers. She is preparing a Technique. You have one round before she releases it."

The party now knows they have one round to disrupt Lady Crimson's technique or take cover.

Jinhai's turn.

Jinhai's player: "I am wounded and not a fighter. I use Bolstered Step (Qi cost 2) to cross two Zones. I move from the grand hall to the garden terrace. I need distance and I need to tend this wound."

Jinhai has Qi Pool 23. Spends 2, remaining 21. She moves two Zones, from the grand hall to the garden terrace.

Host: "Qi flares around your feet and you move faster than should be possible. You race up the balcony stairs, cross the balcony, and pass through the open doors onto the terrace before the attendants can react. The night air is cold. You can hear the reception devolving into chaos behind you."

**Round 2.** The fight continues with Cinder on the balcony, Jinhai on the terrace, and Lady Crimson charging her technique in the hall. The outcome depends on whether the party can disrupt her or escape before the technique completes.

### Combat Example 3: Dusk Touched Boss Fight with Zone Manipulation

**The Setup.** The party has tracked the source of a corruption to an abandoned temple on the outskirts of the capital. The temple was once dedicated to a forgotten celestial virtue. Now it houses a Dusk Touched creature that has been consuming the memories of local residents. The creature, once a priest, has fused with the temple itself.

**Zones.** The temple entrance (Zone A, strewn with rubble, partial cover). The central nave (Zone B, high ceiling, pools of stagnant water on the floor). The altar platform (Zone C, raised three feet above the nave, the creature's starting position). The collapsed eastern wing (Zone D, unstable, falling debris). The underground crypt (Zone E, accessible through a trapdoor in Zone B, dark and cramped).

**Participants.** Cinder (Night Warbler), Jinhai (Celestial Book), Steelhand (Iron Bone), and the Dusk Priest (boss creature).

**Special mechanics.** The Dusk Priest is anchored to the altar (Zone C). It can use an Action to cause a Zone to become "Dusk Saturated," dealing 1d4 Sanity damage to anyone in that Zone at the start of their turn. The temple has a bell that, if rung, suppresses active saturation and prevents new saturation until the end of the Dusk Priest's next turn.

Host: "The temple door hangs askew on broken hinges. Beyond it, darkness deeper than the absence of light. The air is cold and smells of wet stone and something metallic, like old blood. Faint movements ripple across the stagnant water on the floor. On the raised altar at the far end, a figure kneels. It was once a man. Its skin has taken on a crimson sheen. Its eyes, when it opens them, are entirely black."

Initiative: Cinder 22. Dusk Priest 19. Steelhand 14. Jinhai 7.

Cinder's turn.

Cinder's player: "I do not announce myself. I slip through the entrance, keeping to the shadows along the wall. I move toward the eastern pillars where I have cover. I want to get close enough to strike but not stand in the open."

Cinder rolls Stealth. d20 shows 16 + Swiftness modifier (+4) + Proficiency (+2) = 22 against the Dusk Priest's Vigilance of 13 (10 + Resolve modifier +1 + proficiency +2). Success. The Dusk Priest does not notice Cinder's movement.

Host: "Your feet find the silent spots on the stone floor. The Dusk Priest's head is bowed. It is murmuring something, a prayer or a chant, in a language that makes your teeth ache. You reach the shadow of a collapsed pillar. You are within striking distance."

Dusk Priest's turn.

Host: "The Dusk Priest raises its head. It does not look at Steelhand or Cinder. It looks directly at Jinhai, still standing in the entrance. 'You carry knowledge,' it says. Its voice is layered, like two people speaking at once. 'I can taste it. Give me your memories and I will let the others leave.'"

Jinhai's player: "I do not answer. I grip my staff and prepare to defend myself."

Host: "The Dusk Priest stands. The water at its feet begins to steam. Crimson light pulses from the altar. The Dusk Priest uses an Action to saturate the central nave (Zone B) with Dusk energy. The water turns red. Anyone standing in Zone B at the start of their turn takes 1d4 Sanity damage."

Steelhand's turn.

Steelhand's player: "I am in Zone A, the entrance. I do not want to wade into that corrupted water. I look around for something I can use. The bell. I saw a bell pull in a stone alcove just inside Zone B when we entered. I move into the alcove and yank it."

Host: "The bell is old but the mechanism still works. A deep, resonant tone rings through the temple. The sound is pure. It cuts through the Dusk energy like a blade through smoke. The Dusk Priest recoils, clutching its head. The red glow in the water dims. The bell's resonance suppresses the active saturation and prevents the Priest from creating more until the end of its next turn. Good thinking."

Jinhai's turn.

Jinhai's player: "I am still in Zone A. The bell bought us time. I focus my Qi and use Mending Touch on myself. I need to heal the arm wound from the reception before this fight gets worse."

Jinhai spends 3 Qi (now 18 remaining). Heals 1d8 + Intellect modifier. d8 shows 6. Total healing: 6 + 5 = 11. Jinhai's HP goes from 9 to 14 (full).

**Round 2.**

Cinder's turn.

Cinder's player: "I attack from the shadows. The Priest is distracted. I throw a dagger at its back, aiming for the base of the skull."

Cinder attacks with Advantage (the Priest is distracted and unaware of her position). She rolls two d20: 4 and 18. Takes 18. Total: 18 + 4 + 2 = 24 against the Priest's Defense of 14. Hit. Dagger damage: 1d6 (rolls 3) + Swiftness modifier (+4). Total damage: 7.

Host: "The blade sinks deep into the Priest's neck. It should have dropped any normal creature. The Dusk Priest staggers, black ichor spraying from the wound, but it does not fall. It turns slowly, and its black eyes find your position in the shadows. 'There you are,' it says. The wound begins to close, the flesh knitting itself back together. You realize the creature is drawing its regeneration from the altar. You need to separate it from the altar."

Dusk Priest's turn.

Host: "The Dusk Priest raises its arms. The water in Zone B boils. Crimson tendrils rise from the water like reaching hands. It is going to attack everyone in Zone B. Anyone still in the nave needs to dodge."

Steelhand's player: "I am in the Zone B bell alcove. The stone recess shields me from the tendrils."

Jinhai's player: "I am in Zone A. Safe."

Cinder's player: "Cinder is in Zone B, behind the pillar. She needs to dodge the tendrils."

Cinder makes a Swiftness check against TN 15 to dodge. d20 shows 11 + Swiftness modifier (+4) = 15. Exactly meets the TN.

Host: "The crimson tendrils lash out, but you were already moving. One passes close enough to your face that you feel the cold of it, like standing before an open grave. But you are clear."

Steelhand's turn.

Steelhand's player: "The altar is the key. If I can smash that altar, the Priest loses its regeneration. I leave the bell alcove and charge up to Zone C. The water burns, but I push through."

Steelhand starts his turn in Zone B, so the water deals 1d4 Sanity damage. Steelhand has Resolve 12 (modifier +1) and Sanity of 21. He takes 3 Sanity damage, reducing his Sanity to 18. He then moves from Zone B to Zone C with one Movement.

Host: "The water is cold and hot at the same time, a contradiction that your mind cannot resolve. It feels like wading through someone else's nightmares. You take 3 Sanity damage. But you reach the base of the altar. You are in Zone C now."

The Dusk Priest screams, a sound that is part human and part something else entirely. The regeneration stops. The wounds on its neck remain open. The red glow in the water begins to fade. ## Chapter Seven: Qi and Techniques

Celestial Qi flows through all things. Those trained in its manipulation can achieve effects that seem miraculous: healing wounds, shrouding themselves in shadow, striking with the force of a landslide, or reading the threads of fate.

### Qi Pool

Your Qi Pool represents your reserve of Celestial Qi. Each Technique you use costs Qi points. Your Qi Pool replenishes fully after a long rest (a full night's sleep in safety) and partially after a short rest (an hour of meditation or light activity).

### Using Techniques

To use a Technique, you must know it (granted by your Archetype or learned through play) and spend its Qi cost. Unless the Technique specifies otherwise, using a Technique is an Action.

For Techniques that require an attack roll, use d20 + Intellect Modifier + Proficiency Bonus (if proficient in Qi Theory). The TN is the target's Defense for combat Techniques or a TN set by the Host for noncombat Techniques.

### Expanded Technique List

Common Techniques are available to anyone who learns them. Thematic Techniques are typically associated with specific Archetypes but can be learned by any character who finds a teacher.

### Creating Your Own Techniques

Players and Hosts are encouraged to invent new Techniques that fit the themes of the campaign. Here are guidelines for creating balanced Techniques.

**Determine the theme.** Every Technique should belong to one of the four major themes: Healing, Combat, Utility, or Divination. A Technique that crosses themes should cost more than either equivalent.

**Set the Qi cost.** As a guideline:

- A minor benefit that can be used once costs 1 to 2 Qi.
- A moderate, scene long benefit costs 3 to 4 Qi.
- A powerful or encounter defining effect costs 5 to 7 Qi.
- A campaign altering or reality bending effect costs 8 or more Qi and should require a quest to learn.

**Compare to existing Techniques.** Does your new Technique do something similar to an existing one? Price it similarly. Does it do something strictly better? Price it higher. Does it do something strictly worse? Consider making it cost less or adding a secondary benefit.

**Add a limitation.** The best Techniques have interesting limitations. "You can read surface thoughts, but only if the target is unaware of your presence." "You can heal, but the healed area scars permanently." Limitations create interesting decisions.

**Test for balance.** A Technique should never make a Skill or Facet irrelevant. If your Technique does what Stealth does but better and cheaper, the Stealth skill becomes meaningless. Techniques enhance existing capabilities. They do not replace them.

**Example custom Technique.** A player wants to create a Technique called "Face of the Court" that lets her temporarily adopt the appearance of any official she has met. The Host determines this is a Utility Technique with a cost of 4 Qi. The limitation: the disguise lasts only ten minutes, and anyone who knows the official well gets an Insight check against the user's Technique TN to notice something wrong. This is balanced and fits the theme.

### Qi Disharmony Examples

Using Qi under adverse conditions is dangerous. Here are three examples of Qi Disharmony going wrong at the table.

**Example 1: The Depleted Healer.**

Jinhai is deep in the crypts beneath the temple. She has already used most of her Qi to heal her companions during the descent. A Dusk Touched creature has wounded Steelhand badly. He is at 3 HP and bleeding. Jinhai has 2 Qi remaining. She attempts Mending Touch, which costs 3 Qi.

Host: "You reach into your Qi Pool and find it nearly empty. You try to draw the energy anyway, pulling harder than you should. The Qi resists. Make a Qi Disharmony check."

Jinhai's player rolls d20 + Intellect modifier (+5) against TN 15. She gets a 6. Total: 11. Failure.

Host: "The Qi backlashes. You feel a sharp pain behind your eyes, like a needle driven through your skull. The 2 Qi you had is gone. The technique fails. Steelhand is still bleeding. And you take 1d6 psychic damage from the backlash."

Jinhai takes 4 psychic damage. Her HP drops from 12 to 8. She is now wounded herself and has no Qi remaining. The party must find another way to stabilize Steelhand.

**Example 2: The Dusk Interference.**

Cinder is trying to use Shroud of Shadow to escape from a pursuing group of Dusk Touched hunters. She is in a forest that has been partially consumed by the Crimson Dusk. The air itself is corrupted.

Host: "You draw on your Qi to wrap yourself in shadow, but the air, saturated with Dusk, resists your manipulation. The shadows around you writhe, but they do not obey. They are corrupted. You are trying to command something that answers to a different master. Make a Qi Disharmony check."

Cinder's player rolls d20 + Intellect modifier (+2) against TN 15. She gets an 11. Total: 13. Failure.

Host: "The shadows lash out at you instead of concealing you. Thorny tendrils of darkness scrape across your arm. You take 1d6 psychic damage. The Qi cost is spent. The technique fails. And the Dusk Touched hunters have seen the flash of corrupted energy. They know exactly where you are."

Cinder takes 3 psychic damage. Her position is now known. She must find a different way to escape.

**Example 3: Emotional Turmoil.**

Steelhand has just witnessed a close ally being taken by the Commission for Celestial Purity. He is furious and desperate. He attempts to use Qi Strike to break through a door and chase after them.

Steelhand spends 2 Qi to use Qi Strike, then drives his fist into the barred door. The attack connects, but the emotional strain makes the Qi unstable before the added force can take hold.

Host: "Your Qi is turbulent. Your emotions are feeding into it, making it unstable. The energy crackles along your arm, but it does not focus. It wants to explode, not to enhance. Make a Qi Disharmony check."

Steelhand's player rolls d20 + Intellect modifier (+1, Steelhand has Intellect 12). He gets a 4. Total: 5. Failure.

Host: "The Qi erupts from you uncontrollably. The door does not break. Instead, a shockwave of raw energy throws you backward into a bookshelf. You take 1d6 psychic damage from the backlash. Books and scrolls tumble down around you. The Commission agents, hearing the explosion, double their pace. Your ally is farther away now. And you are on the floor, covered in paper, with a nosebleed from the psychic shock."

Steelhand takes 6 psychic damage, reducing his HP from 20 to 14. The door remains intact. The situation is worse than before.

## Chapter Eight: Social Encounters

Words are as deadly as steel in the Celestial Court. A well executed social maneuver can destroy a career, forge an alliance, or save a life. The social encounter system provides structure for these exchanges, treating them with the same tactical weight as combat.

### When to Use Structured Social Encounters

Not every conversation needs dice. Use structured social encounters when:

- The outcome is uncertain and meaningful.
- Multiple parties have competing goals.
- The stakes are high enough that failure matters.
- The players want to engage deeply with the social dimension.

A casual chat with a teahouse owner does not need dice. Convincing the Bright Mirror not to investigate your ally does.

### Social Stance

At the start of a structured social encounter, the Host determines the target's **Stance** toward the players:

| Stance     | Meaning                | Base TN for Persuasion |
| ---------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Hostile    | Actively opposes you   | 20                     |
| Suspicious | Distrusts your motives | 16                     |
| Neutral    | No strong feeling      | 12                     |
| Receptive  | Open to your words     | 10                     |
| Allied     | Wants to help you      | 8                      |

### Shifting Stance

Players can shift a target's Stance through successful social actions. Each shift changes the TN for subsequent social actions by 2 in the players' favor.

A single conversation can shift Stance by at most two steps. Going from Hostile to Allied in one conversation is not possible. This reflects the reality that trust is built over time.

### Social Actions

During a structured social encounter, players may use these actions:

- **Persuade:** Make a case using reason, charm, or emotional appeal. Roll Presence + Persuasion against the target's Stance TN. On success, the target's Stance improves by one step toward you. On a Critical Success, two steps.

- **Deceive:** Tell a lie or misrepresent the truth. Roll Presence + Deception against the target's Insight (10 + Resolve Modifier + any proficiency). On success, the target believes you. On failure, the target realizes you are being dishonest, and their Stance worsens by one step.

- **Probe:** Attempt to discern the target's true motives or emotional state. Roll Resolve + Insight against TN 15. On success, the Host reveals something useful: what the target wants, what they fear, or what would shift their Stance.

- **Leverage:** Use information, threats, or blackmail to force compliance. Roll Presence + Intimidation against TN 15. On success, the target complies with a specific demand but their Stance permanently worsens by two steps. On failure, they become Hostile and may retaliate.

- **Reveal:** Voluntarily share a truth about yourself, your motives, or your situation. No roll required. A genuine revelation automatically improves the target's Stance by one step. This can only be done once per encounter. The truth must be genuine and meaningful.

### Social Damage

In prolonged social conflicts (debates, negotiations, trials), characters have **Social Standing** equal to 10 + Presence Modifier + any relevant bonuses. Social "attacks" deal Social Damage (typically 1d6 + Presence Modifier). When Social Standing reaches 0, the character is socially defeated: they concede the argument, lose face publicly, or are forced to accept terms.

Social Standing recovers between encounters.

### Social Encounter Example 1: Negotiating a Safe Passage

**The Setup.** The party needs to move a Dusk refugee through a checkpoint controlled by the Golden Orthodoxy. The guard captain, Commander Fang, is a devout orthodox who believes the Dusk is divine punishment for the empire's moral failings. He will not help out of compassion. He might help if the party can make a convincing argument that serves his interests.

**Participants.** Jinhai (Celestial Book, Presence 18, Persuasion not proficient). Commander Fang (NPC, starting Stance: Suspicious, TN 16).

Host: "Commander Fang stands before the checkpoint gate, arms crossed. He is a man with broad shoulders in polished armor. Behind him, a line of wagons waits for inspection. He looks at your approach without warmth. 'State your business,' he says."

Jinhai's player: "I step forward and bow formally. 'Commander Fang. My name is Scholar Jinhai of the Bureau of Celestial Inquiry. I am conducting research on the movement patterns of displaced populations. The records I have are incomplete. I was hoping you could assist me.' I am using my official position to establish legitimacy."

This is a Persuade action to shift Stance. The Host determines the approach is reasonable. Jinhai rolls Presence + Persuasion (not proficient, just the modifier +4). d20 shows 11. Total: 15 against TN 16. Failure.

Host: "Commander Fang's expression does not change. 'I have no interest in assisting Bureau research. My duty is to secure this checkpoint against Dusk contamination. If you have no official travel papers, you should move along.' His Stance remains Suspicious. He is not convinced."

Cinder's player: "I step up beside Jinhai. I speak more quietly, so only the Commander can hear. 'Commander, the truth is we are trying to move someone out of the city quietly. A child. Her parents were taken in a Harmonization Audit. She is cared for by people who are not her family. They need to get her to safety before the Commission notices she is missing.' I am using a Reveal action. I am sharing a genuine truth."

The Host rules this is a genuine Revelation. The Stance improves by one step from Suspicious (TN 16) to Neutral (TN 12).

Host: "Commander Fang's eyes narrow. He looks at you both for a long moment. Then he gestures to a side gate. 'Wait there. I will come to you when I have a moment.' He walks away to inspect a wagon. Five minutes later, he joins you by the side gate. 'Tell me more about this child.' His voice is lower now. 'Why should I risk my position?'"

Jinhai's player: "'Because you have children, Commander. I see their drawings pinned to the wall of your office through the window there. You understand what it means to protect someone who cannot protect themselves.' I am appealing to his personal experience, not his doctrine."

This is another Persuade action. The TN is now 12 (Neutral Stance). Jinhai rolls d20. Gets a 6. With Presence modifier (+4). Total: 10. Failure.

Host: "Something flickers in his eyes. Your observation about his children was accurate. But he shakes his head. 'My duty is to the empire. The empire's laws exist for a reason. If I make exceptions for one child, I must make exceptions for all. The checkpoint stays closed.' He is struggling. He wants to help. His doctrine will not let him."

Cinder's player: "I speak now. 'Commander. Do you believe the Golden Orthodoxy teaches that mercy is a virtue?' He nods. 'Then let me ask you this. If the Orthodoxy teaches mercy, and you have the power to show mercy, and you choose doctrine over mercy... which virtue are you actually serving?' I am reframing his internal conflict."

This is a Persuade action. Cinder has Presence 16 (modifier +3) and is not proficient in Persuasion. She rolls d20. Gets a 15. Total: 18 against TN 12. Success by 6 (Exceptional Success).

Host: "Commander Fang is silent for a long moment. Then he laughs, a short, bitter sound. 'You argue like a scholar who has spent too much time in the archives and not enough time in the real world. But you are not wrong.' He opens the side gate. 'The child moves at midnight. I will be inspecting the eastern wall then. I will not see anything.' His Stance has shifted to Receptive. He is helping, but he is not happy about it. He needs to maintain deniability."

The party now has a method to move the refugee. They gained cooperation through a combination of revelation and persuasion.

### Social Encounter Example 2: Interrogation in the Iron Cells

**The Setup.** The party has captured a courier who works for the Crimson Lineage. They need information about a shipment of forbidden materials entering the capital. The courier, a man named Jaren, is trained to resist interrogation. The party has limited time before his absence is noticed.

**Participants.** Steelhand (Iron Bone, Might 18, Intimidation proficient). Jaren the courier (starting Stance: Hostile, TN 20). The interrogation room.

Host: "The room is windowless. A single oil lamp hangs from the ceiling, casting long shadows. Jaren sits on a wooden stool, his wrists bound behind him. He has a bloody lip from the capture. He looks at you with flat, unafraid eyes. 'I have nothing to say to you,' he says. 'Do what you will.'"

Steelhand's player: "I do not threaten him. Not yet. I pull up a stool and sit across from him, at eye level. I set my hands flat on the table between us, scarred knuckles catching the lamplight. I say, 'Jaren. I know you have a family. I know where they live. I do not want to involve them. Tell me about the shipment.'"

This is Leverage: using a threat against family to force compliance. Steelhand rolls Presence + Intimidation. d20 shows 9. Steelhand has Presence 14 (modifier +2) and proficiency in Intimidation (+2). Total: 13 against TN 15. Failure.

Host: "Jaren's eyes narrow. 'You do not know where my family lives. You are guessing. And even if you did, you would not hurt them. You are not that kind of person. I can see it in the way you hold your hands. You have never used them on someone who could not fight back.' His Stance worsens to Hostile. He has called your bluff. And he is right."

Steelhand's player: "He is right. I will not hurt his family. I need a different approach. I stand up and pace. 'You are right. I will not touch your family. But the Commission for Celestial Purity will. If I release you, right now, and you walk out that door, the Commission will pick you up within a day. They know you work for the Crimson Lineage. They are just waiting for the right moment. I am not your enemy, Jaren. I am your only chance to get out ahead of this.'"

This is Persuade, framing the party as the less dangerous choice. Steelhand rolls again. d20 shows 14. He adds 2 for Presence and 2 for Persuasion proficiency, reaching 18 against Hostile TN 20.

Steelhand is proficient in Persuasion, but even his full total falls short. Total: 14 + 2 + 2 = 18 against TN 20 (Hostile). Failure.

Host: "Jaren laughs. 'You think I do not know the Commission is watching me? I have known for months. I have been feeding them misinformation the whole time. Your threat is empty, and your offer is not an offer.' He leans back. He is enjoying this. You are not getting what you need from him."

Jinhai's player: "I step forward. I have been standing in the corner, watching. 'Jaren. You mentioned misinformation. That is interesting. Because I have been reviewing the Crimson Lineage's shipping manifests for the past three months. Your name appears on exactly one of them. A shipment of jade funerary ornaments, marked as going to a temple that burned down last year. You are not a trusted courier. You are a decoy. Your own people set you up to be caught.'"

Jinhai uses a Probe action first to see if this is true. She rolls Resolve + Insight. d20 shows 7. Jinhai has Resolve 20 (modifier +5) and proficiency in Insight (+2). Total: 14 against TN 15. Miss by 1.

Host: "You are not certain about the temple burning down last year. It might have been two years ago. Your research is incomplete. But Jaren's face flickers when you say 'decoy.' That reaction is real. You have hit something."

Jinhai's player: "I press the advantage. 'You are a liability to them. They do not protect liabilities. The only person in this room who can keep you alive is me.' I am making a Persuade attempt."

Jinhai rolls. d20 shows 14. Jinhai has Presence 18 (modifier +4), not proficient in Persuasion. Total: 18 against TN 20 (Hostile). Still fails by 2.

Host: "Jaren is silent. He is thinking. Your words landed. But he does not speak. The Hostile Stance holds. He will not break in this conversation. You have planted a seed, but it will take time or another approach."

The interrogation has stalled. The party has not gotten the information, but they have planted doubt that might bear fruit later if they can continue the relationship.

### Social Encounter Example 3: Debate Before the Court

**The Setup.** The party's patron, a reformist official named Lady Mei, has been accused of corruption by the Commission for Celestial Purity. The party has been called to testify in her defense before a panel of three judges. The courtroom is packed with officials, clerks, and observers. This is a formal debate with Social Standing at stake.

**Participants.** Cinder (Night Warbler) as the primary speaker. Accuser Voss (Bright Mirror prosecutor, Presence 16, Persuasion proficient). Three judges (neutral, will rule based on which side makes the stronger case).

**Social Standing:** Cinder has Presence 16 (modifier +3). Social Standing: 10 + 3 = 13. Accuser Voss: 10 + 3 (Presence modifier) + 2 (relevant bonus from Commission position) = 15.

Host: "The Hall of Adjudication is cold. The three judges sit on a raised dais, their faces unreadable. Accuser Voss stands to your left, a thin man in white robes, a sheaf of documents in his hand. He speaks first. 'Lady Mei has been accused of diverting state funds to support reformist pamphlets. We have records showing payments from her account to a printing house known for heterodox publications.' He presents the documents to the judges. The courtroom murmurs."

Accuser Voss makes a social attack. His Presence + Persuasion: d20 shows 14 + 3 + 2 = 19 against the judges' baseline of TN 12 (Neutral). This deals Social Damage to Cinder's position.

Host: "Voss's evidence is compelling. The judges examine the documents. One of them, Judge Hwan, nods slowly. 'The records appear authentic,' he says. You can feel the room shifting against you. Take 4 Social Damage (1d6 rolled 1 + Presence modifier +3 = 4)."

Cinder's Social Standing falls from 13 to 9.

Cinder's player: "I step forward. 'Honored judges. The records are authentic. The payments were made. But the conclusion the Accuser draws is false. Lady Mei did fund a printing house. That printing house printed reformist pamphlets. But it also printed the official notices of this very court, the tax collection forms for three districts, and the educational primers for the Imperial Academy. The Accuser has presented one piece of a larger picture and asked you to see only that piece. I ask you to consider the whole.'"

Cinder makes a Persuade action to counter the accusation. d20 shows 15. Cinder has Presence 16 (modifier +3), not proficient in Persuasion. Total: 18 against the judges' TN 12. Success. Cinder regains ground.

Host: "Judge Hwan raises an eyebrow. 'The Accuser's evidence does appear selective,' he says. 'Do you have documentation of the other work this printing house performed?'"

Cinder's player: "I do. I place a sheaf of papers on the table. 'Here are receipts, work orders, and delivery confirmations for the past eighteen months. The reformist pamphlets represent less than five percent of the printing house's output. The other ninety five percent is routine government work. If Lady Mei is guilty of corruption for funding a printer who occasionally accepts reformist work, then half the court is guilty of the same.'"

Cinder rolls Persuade again. d20 shows 10. Total: 10 + 3 = 13 against TN 12. Success (by 1).

Host: "The judges examine the documents. Judge Rel, the senior judge, speaks. 'The Scholar's argument has merit. The accusation of corruption requires intent. The evidence presented does not demonstrate intent.' Accuser Voss's expression tightens. He has lost this round. He is down to 8 Social Standing."

Voss's turn. He shifts tactics.

Host: "Accuser Voss does not back down. 'Even if the funding was incidental, the question remains: why did Lady Mei choose that particular printing house? She had dozens of options. She chose the one with known reformist ties. That is not incompetence. That is deliberate support, thinly disguised.' He is targeting Lady Mei's judgment rather than her honesty."

Voss attacks again: d20 shows 8 + 3 + 2 = 13 against Cinder's position. Social damage: 1d6 (4) + 3 = 7. Cinder's Social Standing drops from 9 to 2.

Host: "The question hangs in the air. The judges turn to you. Why that printing house? If you cannot answer, the accusation of deliberate support will stick."

Cinder's player: "I was prepared for this. 'Lady Mei chose that printing house because it is the only one in the district with a dedicated proofreader. The educational primers for the Imperial Academy require precision. A single character error in an official text can change the meaning entirely. Lady Mei prioritized quality over optics. That is not corruption. That is diligence.'"

Cinder rolls Persuade. d20 shows 8. Total: 8 + 3 = 11 against TN 12. Failure. Cinder's Social Standing drops from 2 to 0. She is defeated in the debate.

Host: "The argument is good, but it is not enough to overcome the Accuser's framing. Judge Rel raises his hand. 'We have heard enough. The court will recess to deliberate. Lady Mei is remanded to house arrest pending the judgment.' The guards step forward. Lady Mei is led away. You have lost this round. The trial is not over, but the first verdict has gone against you. You have three days to find evidence that changes the outcome."

The party has lost the debate. Lady Mei is under house arrest. The party must now find additional evidence before the final judgment. The social encounter has created a new objective with a time limit.

## Chapter Nine: Ritual Harmony and the Court

Ritual Harmony measures your standing within the Celestial Court's orthodoxy. It is not a measure of virtue. It is a measure of how well you perform the role the Court expects of you.

### The Ritual Harmony Scale

| Score     | Designation | What It Means                                                                                                                                                   |
| --------- | ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0 to 20   | Heterodox   | Under active investigation. The Commission for Celestial Purity is watching. Every action is scrutinized.                                                       |
| 21 to 40  | Suspect     | Cannot hold official positions. Colleagues avoid you. Your mail is opened before delivery.                                                                      |
| 41 to 60  | Acceptable  | Normal standing. Safe from casual scrutiny but vulnerable during Harmonization Audits.                                                                          |
| 61 to 80  | Harmonious  | In good standing. Eligible for advancement. Protected in most Audits. People seek your endorsement.                                                             |
| 81 to 100 | Exemplary   | You are the standard. Also: every faction now sees you as either a tool to be used or a threat to be neutralized. High Ritual Harmony is a shield and a target. |

### Gaining and Losing Ritual Harmony

Ritual Harmony changes based on your actions:

**Gain Ritual Harmony (and why it might not be a good thing):**

- Publicly reciting orthodox doctrine (+1 to +3)
- Reporting a deviant to the Commission (+5, but someone's life is ruined)
- Being endorsed by a senior orthodox figure (+3 to +5)
- Surviving a Harmonization Audit without being named (+2)

**Lose Ritual Harmony (and why it might be necessary):**

- Being seen with known reformers or heterodox figures (-3 to -5)
- Questioning doctrine in public (-5 to -10)
- Being investigated by the Commission (-5, even if cleared)
- Sheltering a Dusk refugee (-5 to -10)
- Publishing a finding that contradicts official records (-15)

### Actions That Shape Your Ritual Harmony

The following concrete examples show how specific actions at the table might affect Ritual Harmony. Each example includes the mechanical change and the narrative context.

**Gaining Ritual Harmony.**

1. A Bright Mirror publicly recites the Five Pillars of Orthodox Governance during a formal dinner. The performance is flawless. The other guests nod approvingly. (+2 Ritual Harmony, now 52.)

2. A Cinnabar Heart notices a clerk in his department reading a reformist pamphlet. He reports the clerk to the Commission. The clerk is taken away. His colleagues now fear him. (+5 Ritual Harmony, now 57. He has gained Ritual Harmony but lost trust.)

3. A Duke of Eternal Night donates a substantial sum to the Temple of Celestial Harmony, a known orthodox institution. The donation is recorded and publicized. (+3 Ritual Harmony, now 64.)

4. An Iron Wall successfully leads a patrol that intercepts a shipment of heterodox texts. His superiors commend his vigilance. (+3 Ritual Harmony, now 59.)

5. A Celestial Book is asked to authenticate an ancient scroll for the Commission. She certifies the scroll as orthodox, even though she suspects it contains veiled criticism of the current regime. (+4 Ritual Harmony, now 63. She has compromised her scholarly integrity.)

6. A Night Warbler provides information that leads to the arrest of a Dusk cult cell. The Bureau of Internal Harmony is pleased. (+5 Ritual Harmony, now 60. The cell's members may have information he needs.)

7. An Iron Bone participates in a public flagellation ritual during a holy festival, demonstrating his devotion. (+1 Ritual Harmony, now 48.)

8. A Guest Among Forests endorses a new orthodox appointment to the Court, lending her centuries of experience to the legitimacy of the selection. (+4 Ritual Harmony, now 66.)

**Losing Ritual Harmony.**

1. A Celestial Book is seen leaving a teahouse that is known as a meeting place for reformers. She was there doing research. The observers do not care about her reasons. (-4 Ritual Harmony, now 46.)

2. During a routine Harmonization Audit, an Iron Bone questions whether the Commission's methods align with the empire's founding principles. The question is overheard and reported. (-8 Ritual Harmony, now 40. He has dropped into Suspect territory.)

3. A Night Warbler shelters a Dusk refugee in his safe house for three nights. The refugee is a child. The risk of discovery is high. (-8 Ritual Harmony, now 32.)

4. A Cinnabar Heart publishes a research paper that includes a factual correction to a widely distributed imperial census. The correction is accurate. It is also politically inconvenient for a faction that benefits from the error. (-15 Ritual Harmony, now 38.)

5. A Duke of Eternal Night is seen in conversation with a known heterodox philosopher at a social gathering. The conversation was brief and accidental. The optics are damning. (-5 Ritual Harmony, now 59.)

6. An Iron Wall fails to prevent a protest outside a government building. The protest was not his fault. He is blamed anyway. (-3 Ritual Harmony, now 56.)

7. A Guest Among Forests refuses to endorse an orthodox appointment, citing concerns about the candidate's qualifications. The refusal is seen as political rather than professional. (-6 Ritual Harmony, now 60.)

8. A Bright Mirror's investigation of a corrupt official is blocked by political pressure. He withdraws the investigation. He is seen as weak. (-5 Ritual Harmony, now 55.)

9. A Shadow is caught breaking into the office of a Golden Orthodoxy official. The break was professional and left no trace except one: the official came back early. (-10 Ritual Harmony, now 25.)

10. A Celestial Book, under questioning, admits she has seen a document that contradicts orthodox history. She does not reveal the document. The admission alone is damaging. (-12 Ritual Harmony, now 37.)

### Living with Your Ritual Harmony

Your Ritual Harmony is not a number you ignore between Audits. It shapes every interaction you have in the Court.

**Heterodox (0 to 20).** You live in a state of siege. The Commission watches your residence. Your mail is intercepted. Colleagues cross the street to avoid you. You cannot hold official employment. Shopkeepers charge you double or refuse service entirely. You move through the city at night or through hidden passages. Every conversation is a risk. Every kindness from a stranger might be a trap. You survive because you are useful to someone powerful enough to protect you, or because you are too dangerous to arrest openly. Friends who acknowledge you in public become targets themselves.

**Suspect (21 to 40).** Your official position is gone or hanging by a thread. You attend meetings where no one sits next to you. Your requests for resources are lost or delayed indefinitely. People speak to you in formal tones and leave quickly. You are invited to gatherings where your presence is noted but not welcomed. You cannot sponsor anyone. You cannot recommend anyone. You cannot be seen to influence anything. The only people who speak to you freely are those who have already been marked as outsiders, and they are desperate.

**Acceptable (41 to 60).** You are a functioning member of the Court with no particular distinction. Your life is ordinary. You go to work. You file reports. You attend the required ceremonies. Your neighbors nod to you. Your superiors do not know your name. You are safe as long as you do nothing remarkable. The danger of Acceptable is that it is fragile. A single accusation, a single guilt by association, can drop you into Suspect. Security is a temporary condition, not a permanent state.

**Harmonious (61 to 80).** You have arrived. People seek your company. Your name carries weight. Invitations arrive daily. Your recommendations matter. You can walk into any government office and receive prompt service. You are protected against routine investigations. The wealthy and powerful want to be seen with you. The danger of Harmonious is that you have something worth losing. Your allies watch you for signs of weakness. Your enemies look for leverage. Every successful person in the Court has a secret or a vulnerability. Yours will be found eventually.

**Exemplary (81 to 100).** You are untouchable. No one dares accuse you. No one questions your decisions. Your words are repeated as wisdom. Your enemies do not attack you directly. They wait. They watch. They look for the moment your protection slips. The danger of Exemplary is that you are an island. People do not confide in you because they fear your judgment. People do not warn you about dangers because they assume you already know. You are admired, celebrated, and isolated. And when you fall, the fall will be from a great height.

### The Harmonization Audit

Periodically, a Harmonization Audit sweeps through the Court. This is a major campaign event that the Host controls, not a random occurrence. A Harmonization Audit should be a set piece: a session where everything the players have built is tested.

During an Audit:

- Each player's Ritual Harmony determines their baseline risk.
- Each player's Faction Standing determines their protection level.
- Players can take actions to improve their position: spending Celestial Favor, hiding evidence, making deals.
- The Audit resolves with some characters cleared, some sanctioned, and some disappearing entirely.

The Host's Codex contains detailed procedures for running Harmonization Audits.

## Chapter Ten: The Crimson Dusk

The Crimson Dusk is the campaign's central threat and primary mystery. It is not an army. It is not a plague. It is a corruption of reality itself, spreading inward from the empire's borders, turning the familiar into the monstrous.

### The Five Phases

The Host controls the Dusk's advance. It progresses at the pace of the story, not by any mechanical clock. The phases are:

| Phase | Name         | The World                                                                                                            |
| ----- | ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0     | Distant      | The Dusk is a rumor. Border reports mention unusual phenomena. Most in the capital do not believe.                   |
| 1     | Approaching  | Provinces begin to fall. Refugees arrive. The Court can no longer entirely deny.                                     |
| 2     | Pressing     | Inner provinces are threatened. Dusk Touched creatures appear within the heartland. Panic spreads.                   |
| 3     | Imminent     | The capital's outer districts are affected. The Court must unite or collapse. The endgame begins.                    |
| 4     | Consummation | The Dusk engulfs all. The final confrontation. What comes after depends on what the players have learned and chosen. |

### Dusk Exposure

Exposure to the Crimson Dusk erodes Sanity. Brief exposure (seeing a Dusk Touched creature, witnessing a Dusk phenomenon) costs 1d4 Sanity. Prolonged exposure costs 1d8 or more. Direct contact with concentrated Dusk corruption costs 2d6 Sanity and may cause physical mutation.

### Dusk Touched

Some beings are altered by Dusk exposure rather than destroyed. Dusk Touched individuals may exhibit strange abilities, physical mutations, or psychological changes. Some become prophets who speak truths no one wants to hear. Others become monsters. The Host's Codex contains rules for Dusk Touched characters and creatures.

## Chapter Eleven: Sanity and Horror

The Crimson Dusk is not the only thing that erodes the mind. Betrayal, isolation, guilt, and the accumulated weight of surviving in a system designed to crush you all take their toll.

### Sanity Loss

Common causes of Sanity loss:

- Witnessing a Dusk phenomenon (1d4 to 2d6)
- Being betrayed by someone you trusted (1d6)
- Committing an act that violates your principles (1d4 to 2d6)
- Surviving a Harmonization Audit in which friends fell (1d8)
- Prolonged isolation or helplessness (1d4 per week)

### Sanity Recovery

Sanity recovers through:

- A full night's rest in safety (+1)
- Meaningful connection with another person (+1d4)
- Accomplishing a significant goal (+1d6)
- Extended rest in a safe, comfortable environment (+2d6 per week)
- Certain Qi Techniques or rare medicines

### Breaking Points

When Sanity falls to 10 or below, the character is at a **Breaking Point**. The Host may call for a Resolve check against TN 15. On a failure, the character suffers a breakdown: they may flee, freeze, lash out, or experience a delusion. The Host and player collaborate to determine the specific manifestation.

When Sanity reaches 0, the character is permanently broken. They may become catatonic, violently insane, or something stranger: a willing vessel for the Dusk. Such characters are generally unplayable and become NPCs under the Host's control.

## Chapter Twelve: Death and Legacy

Death in the Violet Court is permanent. No Qi Technique restores the dead, and no divine intervention reverses death. When a character dies, their story ends. But the campaign continues.

### When a Character Dies

The player creates a new character. The new character inherits:

- **Knowledge:** The truths the previous character uncovered. The player retains this information and may use it.
- **One Heirloom:** A single significant item passed from the dead character to the new one. This is a narrative decision, not a mechanical one: what object carries the weight of the fallen character's story?
- **The Unfinished Work:** The new character is introduced with a connection to the previous character's unfinished business. Perhaps they were an ally who received a final message. Perhaps they found the previous character's journal. Perhaps they are simply the next person to hold the same position.

### Legacy Cycles

Over a long campaign, multiple characters may die. Each death enriches the story. The Host tracks what has been learned, what has been lost, and what remains undone. The accumulated weight of these cycles creates a narrative that no single character could hold alone.

The black market operates in the outer districts and the tunnels beneath the city. It is not a single location. It is a network of vendors, fences, and fixers who communicate through coded messages and trusted intermediaries.

**Finding the Black Market.** A character can locate black market contacts with a successful Connections check (TN 15) or by spending time in the right taverns and teahouses (one evening of investigation, Resolve + Vigilance against TN 12).

**Black Market Prices.** Black market goods cost two to five times the listed price, depending on scarcity and the seller's assessment of the buyer's desperation. Some items cannot be bought at any price on the open market:

| Item                          | Black Market Cost | Notes                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Unregistered Weapon           | 2x list price     | No serial marks, cannot be traced                                                            |
| Poison                        | 10 to 50 Silver   | Varies by type: gradual, rapid, paralytic, or hallucinogenic                                 |
| Dusk Artifact                 | Priceless         | Usually traded for favors rather than coin. Extremely dangerous.                             |
| Forged Identity Papers        | 5 to 20 Gold      | Quality determines durability under scrutiny                                                 |
| Restricted Archive Document   | 10 to 50 Gold     | Cost depends on the document's significance                                                  |

| Purified Qi Crystal           | 30 Gold           | A rare item that can restore 10 Qi when consumed. One use. Highly regulated.                 |

**Black Market Etiquette.** Do not ask where items come from. Do not reveal your real name. Do not draw attention to the transaction. Do not attempt to cheat a fence unless you are prepared to leave the city immediately. Some fences are information brokers first and merchants second. Assume everything you say will be remembered and possibly sold.

## Appendix C: Example of Play

### A Complete Scene: The Night Market Investigation

This example shows a complete scene from start to finish. The party is investigating a series of disappearances in the Night Market district. They have a lead: a spice merchant named Old Chen who may have seen something.

**The Cast:**

- Mira plays Scholar Jinhai, a Celestial Book archivist.
- Alex plays Cinder, a Night Warbler shadow hand.
- Sam plays Steelhand, an Iron Bone fury adept.
- The Host narrates and plays Old Chen and other NPCs.

**Scene Start.**

Host: "The Night Market district at the ninth hour. The crowds have thinned but not vanished. Lanterns swing overhead, casting pools of amber light on the wet cobblestones. The smell of frying fish and incense hangs in the air. Old Chen's stall is at the end of a narrow alley, wedged between a fortune teller's tent and a shop that sells caged birds. Chen is packing up his spice jars for the night. He is a wiry man in his sixties with a nervous way of moving, like someone who expects to be grabbed at any moment."

Jinhai's player: "I approach slowly. I do not want to startle him. I stop a few feet away and bow. 'Old Chen? My name is Scholar Jinhai. I was told you might have seen something unusual a few nights ago. A woman being taken into a carriage against her will.' I keep my voice low and calm."

Host: "Chen's hands freeze on the jar he is packing. He does not turn around. 'I do not know anything,' he says. 'I see nothing. I hear nothing. That is how I have lived this long.' His voice is tight. He is frightened."

Cinder's player: "I hang back in the shadow of the fortune teller's tent. I watch the surrounding area. I am looking for anyone watching Chen or watching us. I make a Vigilance check."

The Host sets the TN at 12. Cinder rolls d20 + Resolve modifier (+2). She is not proficient in Vigilance. d20 shows 16. Total: 18. Success.

Host: "You see them. Two figures at the far end of the alley, partially hidden behind a stacked cart of melons. They are not shopping. They are watching this stall. One of them has his hand inside his coat, gripping something."

Cinder's player: "I make eye contact with Steelhand across the alley. I tap my ear twice, our signal for 'we are being watched.' Then I melt deeper into the shadows and begin circling around to get behind the watchers."

Cinder rolls Stealth. d20 shows 14 + Swiftness modifier (+4) + proficiency in Stealth (+2) = 20 against the watchers' Vigilance (assumed TN 14). Success.

Host: "You slip behind a stack of empty crates, then behind the bird shop's awning. The watchers have not seen you move. You are now positioned behind them, on the other side of the melon cart."

Steelhand's player: "I stay visible. I walk up to Chen's stall openly, as if I am just another customer. I say, 'Old Chen. I am not here to cause trouble. But those two at the end of the alley might be. Do you know them?' I am trying to get information before things escalate."

Steelhand makes a Presence + Persuasion check. He is proficient in Persuasion. d20 shows 10 + Presence modifier (+2) + proficiency (+2) = 14 against Chen's starting Stance of Suspicious (TN 16). Failure.

Host: "Chen glances at the end of the alley and his face goes pale. 'I do not know them. I do not want to know them. Please. Leave. Take your questions somewhere else.' He starts packing faster, fumbling with the jars. He is going to flee."

Jinhai's player: "I step closer. 'Old Chen. We are not the Commission. We are not here to harm you. But those men at the end of the alley are not here to buy spices. If we leave, they will still be here. And they will have questions for you. Questions you do not want to answer.' I am using a Revelation: we are not the threat, but the threat is real."

The Host rules this is a genuine Revelation. Chen's Stance improves from Suspicious to Neutral (TN 12).

Host: "Chen stops packing. He looks at you, then at the watchers, then back at you. He swallows. 'The carriage. Three nights ago. It was not a carriage. It was a cart. A vegetable cart with a false bottom. I saw them load her in. She was not struggling. She was... asleep? Drugged? I do not know. The cart went toward the Old North Gate.'"

Steelhand's player: "Did you recognize the driver?"

Host: "He shakes his head. 'Covered face. But the cart had a mark on the side. A symbol. A circle with seven lines through it.'"

Jinhai's player: "I know that symbol. It is the mark of the Crimson Lineage's logistical corps. They use it on supply shipments. But why would the Crimson Lineage be kidnapping spice merchants?"

The watchers notice Cinder's movement. She is discovered.

Host: "One of the watchers turns suddenly. He has spotted movement behind the melon cart. He says something to his companion. They start moving toward the stall. They are not running. They are walking with purpose. One of them draws a knife."

Cinder's player: "I step out from behind the cart. I have my hidden blade in hand but held low, not threatening. 'Evening,' I say. 'Lovely weather for a stroll.' I am buying time for the others."

The watchers stop. The one with the knife speaks.

Host: "The man with the knife says, 'You are interfering with a Commission operation. Walk away and we forget we saw you.' His voice is flat. Professional. He is lying about being Commission. You can tell by the way he does not show any credentials."

Jinhai's player: "I use Glimmer of Truth to confirm."

Jinhai spends 2 Qi (23 down to 21 remaining). She gains Advantage on Insight checks for one minute.

Host: "Your vision shimmers gold. The man claims to be Commission. You watch him as he speaks. The shimmer flickers. He is lying. He is not Commission."

Steelhand's player: "I step forward, putting myself between Chen and the watchers. 'You are not Commission. You do not have the bearing, you do not have the credentials, and you are wearing the wrong kind of boots. Commission agents wear regulation boots. Yours are civilian. You are kidnappers pretending to be law. And you have picked the wrong alley.'"

Steelhand makes an Intimidation check. d20 shows 14 + Might modifier (+4) + proficiency in Intimidation (+2) = 20 against the watchers' Resolve + Discipline (assumed TN 14). Success.

Host: "The watchers hesitate. They were expecting easy prey. They were not expecting someone who would fight back. The one with the knife takes a step back. 'This is not over,' he says. They retreat around the corner and are gone."

**Resolution.**

Host: "Old Chen lets out a breath he was holding. He grabs your arm, Jinhai. 'The Old North Gate. Check the warehouses on Iron Street. The ones with the new locks. That is where the cart went. I did not tell you anything.' He shoves a jar of dried ginger into your hands, a pathetic gesture of thanks, and begins packing with desperate speed."

Jinhai's player: "I take the ginger. 'Thank you, Old Chen. Stay safe.' I turn to the others. 'We have a lead. Warehouses on Iron Street, near the Old North Gate. The Crimson Lineage is moving people. We need to find out where they are taking them.'"

Host: "The scene ends with the three of you standing in the emptying night market. Old Chen's stall is dark now. He has fled into the maze of alleys. You have a name, a location, and a growing sense that you have stumbled into something much larger than a few disappearances."

**End of Scene.**

## Appendix D: Common Questions from New Players

**What do I actually do during the game?**

You describe what your character says and does. When the Host describes a scene, you respond. You might say "I search the room for anything hidden" or "I ask the merchant where he was last night" or "I draw my sword and step in front of the ambassador." You make decisions for your character, and the Host tells you what happens as a result.

**How do I know what my character can do?**

Your character sheet lists your Facets, Skills, and Techniques. Your Facet scores tell you your general areas of strength. Your Skills tell you what specific training you have. Your Techniques are special abilities that cost Qi. If you are ever unsure, ask the Host. They can help you figure out what actions are possible.

**What if I do not know what my character should do?**

That is normal. Every player gets stuck sometimes. Ask the Host for more information about the scene. Ask the other players what they think. Or just have your character act on instinct. The worst that happens is a complication, and complications make the story more interesting.

**Can I try anything?**

You can try anything that is physically and logically possible for your character. You can try to climb a wall even if you are not proficient in Athletics. You cannot try to fly without a Technique that allows flight. If you want to do something the rules do not cover, the Host will figure out a fair way to resolve it.

**How do I level up?**

The Host awards Advancement Points (AP) at the end of sessions and story arcs. You spend AP to increase Facets, learn Skills, gain Techniques, and improve your Qi Pool. Every 4 AP spent increases your Level, which increases your Proficiency Bonus. Full details are in Chapter Thirteen.

**What happens if my character dies?**

Create a new character. The new character inherits the knowledge your previous character uncovered, one significant item, and a connection to the unfinished business. Death is permanent, but the story continues. See Chapter Twelve.

**How long does a campaign last?**

A full campaign of the Violet Court Fragments typically runs twelve to twenty sessions, or about three to six months of regular play. Shorter stories and adventures designed for one session are also possible.

**Do I need to read the whole rulebook?**

No. Read Chapter One to understand what the game is. Read Chapter Three to create your character. Read the Quick Reference in Appendix A. The rest you can learn as you play. Your Host will guide you.

**What if I disagree with the Host's ruling?**

Talk to the Host after the session, not during the game. The Host's decision at the table is final. A good Host will listen to concerns after the game and adjust if needed.

**Can I play a character who is not one of the thirteen Archetypes?**

The thirteen Archetypes are designed to cover the major roles in the Celestial Court, and the one role that consists of not having one. If you have a concept that does not fit, work with the Host to adapt an existing Archetype or create a new one. The Archetypes are starting points, not cages.

**What dice do I need?**

You need one d20 for most actions, and a few d6 and d8 dice for damage and healing. Most players own a full set of polyhedral dice. A single d20 is enough to start.

**How do I know when to roll?**

The Host will tell you when a roll is needed. You never need to ask "Do I roll for this?" The Host handles that. Generally, if the outcome of an action is uncertain and the stakes matter, you will roll.

**What is the most important thing to remember?**

The goal is not to win. The goal is to tell an interesting story together. Characters who make suboptimal choices because those choices serve the story are playing the game correctly. Embrace failure. It is often more memorable than success.

## Appendix E: Running the Game for the Host

This section is written specifically for the person who will be the Host. It covers practical advice for preparing and running sessions.

### Preparing a Session

You do not need to write a script. You need to know three things before each session:

**What the players want to do.** At the end of each session, ask your players what they plan to do next. This gives you a direction to prepare. If they say they want to investigate the warehouses on Iron Street, you prepare the warehouses.

**What the opposition wants.** Every faction in the game has goals. The Crimson Lineage is smuggling people. The Commission is conducting Audits. The Golden Orthodoxy is consolidating power. Know what each faction is doing in the background, even when the players are not watching. This makes the world feel alive.

**What could go wrong.** Prepare two or three complications for each likely scene. If the players investigate the warehouse, what happens if the guards detect them? What happens if they set off an alarm? What happens if a rival faction arrives at the same time? These complications are not punishments. They are opportunities for the players to make interesting choices.

### Running NPCs

Every person the players meet is an NPC. You play all of them. Here are guidelines for making them feel real.

**Give each NPC a want and a fear.** The spice merchant wants to stay alive and fears the Crimson Lineage. The guard captain wants to be promoted and fears making a mistake. The beggar wants a full meal and fears the coming winter. When you know what an NPC wants and fears, you know how they will react to the players.

**Use vocal and physical markers.** You do not need to be a voice actor. A slight change in pitch, a different pace of speech, or a consistent phrase is enough. "The merchant speaks quickly, running his words together" is enough to make him distinct from "the guard speaks slowly, choosing each word carefully."

**NPCs have their own information.** They do not know everything. They lie, they exaggerate, they forget, they misunderstand. Players should not trust every NPC. They should have to verify information.

### Pacing

A session should have peaks and valleys. Intense scenes (combat, interrogation, chase) should be followed by quieter scenes (travel, investigation, conversation). Players need time to breathe and plan.

**Opening hook.** Start every session with something that demands attention. A knock on the door. A messenger with bad news. A sound in the dark. Do not start with "you wake up and have breakfast." Start in the middle of something.

**Rising action.** Escalate the stakes. If an NPC is suspicious at first, let them become hostile if pushed. If a location seems safe, introduce a complication. The pressure should build.

**Climax and cooldown.** End the major scene of the session with a decisive outcome. Then give the players a moment to recover before the session ends. The final ten minutes should be quiet: planning, recovering, reflecting.

### Handling the Unexpected

Players will do things you did not plan for. This is normal and good.

**When a player asks about something you have not prepared.** Do not panic. Describe something plausible. If they ask what is in a drawer you did not plan, say "old receipts and a dried up ink bottle." If they need more, they will ask further questions.

**When a player wants to pursue a side quest.** Let them. The main plot will wait. Sometimes the side quest reveals something useful for the main plot. Sometimes it is just a fun diversion.

**When combat breaks out unexpectedly.** Default to Side Initiative for speed. Use the Zone system loosely. Focus on what is dramatic rather than what is mechanically precise. You can look up rules after the session.

### The Principle of "Yes, But"

When a player wants to do something that should not simply succeed or fail, use "yes, but." You can pick the lock, but it takes longer than expected and a guard is coming. You can convince the official, but they will hold a favor for later. This keeps the story moving while introducing cost and consequence.

## Appendix F: Optional Rules

These rules modify the base game for different play styles. Use them if they suit your table. Ignore them if they do not.

### Narrative Armor Rule

In a more cinematic campaign, characters have "narrative armor" that prevents them from dying to random attacks. A character reduced to 0 HP by a minor enemy or environmental hazard is knocked unconscious rather than dying. Only significant enemies, major hazards, or deliberately chosen sacrifices can cause permanent death. This rule allows for more heroic storytelling with less fear of random character death.

### Faction Bonds

Each player, during character creation, chooses one faction they have a personal connection to beyond their official standing. This connection grants Advantage on all social actions involving that faction. The player also chooses one faction that their character has a grudge against. Social actions involving that faction have Disadvantage. These bonds can shift through play based on significant events.

### Extended Downtime

Between sessions, characters can pursue downtime activities. Each day of downtime allows one activity:

- Research: Make an Intellect check against TN 15. On success, learn one piece of useful information.
- Train: Spend two downtime days to make progress on learning a new Skill proficiency. The Host determines the total days required (typically twenty to thirty for a new Skill).
- Socialize: Spend time with an NPC. Make a Presence check to improve their Stance by one step.
- Craft: Use Craft proficiency to create an item. Cost is half the item's listed price. Time is one day per 5 Silver of the item's cost.
- Recover: Double the normal Sanity recovery rate for rest.

### Lingering Injuries

When a character is reduced to 0 HP and stabilized, they gain a lingering injury determined by a d6 roll:

1. Broken rib. Disadvantage on Might checks for one week.
2. Deep scar. Disadvantage on Presence checks involving appearance for one month.
3. Concussion. Disadvantage on Intellect checks for one week.
4. Torn muscle. Disadvantage on Athletics and Acrobatics checks for one week.
5. Nerve damage. Disadvantage on Precision checks for one month.
6. Psychological trauma. Lose 1d4 maximum Sanity permanently.

## Appendix G: The City of a Thousand Bells

Xiaoyuan, known to its people as the City of a Thousand Bells, is the primary setting for most Violet Court campaigns. This appendix provides an overview of its districts and notable locations.

### The Inner City

The Inner City is surrounded by the White Wall, a barrier of polished marble sixty feet high that has never been breached. Within it stand the government buildings, the Imperial Palace (empty of the Emperor but full of administrators), and the residences of the high nobility.

**The Celestial Court.** The sprawling complex where the six schools maintain their headquarters. A maze of halls, gardens, and audience chambers. Every corridor has a political dimension. Every doorway leads to a potential ally or enemy.

**The Imperial Archives.** The domain of the Celestial Book. Seven levels of storage, of which only four are officially acknowledged. The lower levels contain documents that contradict official history. Access to the lower levels requires special clearance or exceptional stealth.

**The Hall of Harmonious Adjudication.** The court of the Bright Mirror. A stark white building with no decoration except the symbol of the Commission for Celestial Purity above the entrance. Trials here are public. Verdicts are final.

### The Middle City

The ring between the inner and outer walls. Home to merchants, skilled artisans, officials in the middle ranks, and the offices of the major guilds.

**The Market of Whispers.** The largest market under the open sky in the capital. Anything can be bought here if you know the right vendor. The market has its own laws and its own enforcers. The City Guard does not patrol here.

**The Iron District.** The domain of the State Planning Commission. Factories, forges, and workshops produce the goods that keep the empire running. The air smells of coal and metal. The workers move in shifts, day and night.

**The Garden of a Thousand Regrets.** A public park built around an artificial lake. Once beautiful. Now neglected. The statues are cracked. The fountains are dry. It is a popular meeting place for those who do not want to be overheard.

### The Outer City

The largest district by area. Home to the poor, the desperate, and the forgotten. The streets are unpaved. The buildings lean against each other for support.

**The Night Market.** Described elsewhere in this rulebook. A warren of stalls, tents, and temporary structures. Active from dusk to dawn. The place to find information, stolen goods, and trouble.

**The Drowned Quarter.** The lowest lying part of the city. Flooded during the rainy season. Many buildings have collapsed. Those who live here cannot afford to live anywhere else.

**The Old North Gate.** One of the original gates of the ancient city. Damaged in a siege three centuries ago and never fully repaired. The gate is closed and barred. The area around it is a no man's land of abandoned warehouses and forgotten roads.

### Key Locations Across the City

**The Azure Lotus Teahouse.** A neutral meeting ground in the Middle City. The proprietor, Madame Lian, remembers faces and has a policy of never repeating what she overhears.

**The Respite of Broken Flowers.** A hospice run by an order of healers who take no side in politics. Anyone can receive treatment here, regardless of faction, Ritual Harmony, or ability to pay. The Commission watches the hospice closely.

**The Iron Heart Forge.** The best weaponsmith in the city works here. He charges accordingly. His work is worth the price.

**The Temple of the Silent Bell.** A temple dedicated to a virtue that no one remembers. The priests keep the rituals anyway. The temple has a basement that connects to the old tunnel network.

## Appendix H: Building Your Own Adventures

This appendix provides a framework for creating your own scenarios.

### The Three Question Method

Every adventure can be built by answering three questions:

**What do the players want?** This could be something they chose (investigate the disappearances, protect a reformer, find a lost document) or something thrust upon them (a message arrives, an attack happens, an accusation is made).

**What stands in their way?** An obstacle, an antagonist, a time limit, a moral dilemma. The best obstacles combine external and internal challenges. The guard patrol is an external obstacle. The knowledge that stopping the guard patrol means failing a promise to an ally is an internal obstacle.

**What happens if they fail?** The stakes must be clear and meaningful. If the players know what failure costs, they can make informed choices. If failure has no cost, there is no tension.

### Adventure Structure

An adventure designed for one session follows this shape:

1. **Hook (10 minutes).** The players receive a call to action. A messenger, a discovery, an explosion, a request from an ally.

2. **Investigation (30 to 60 minutes).** The players gather information, talk to NPCs, and identify their target. This section should have at least two distinct ways to proceed. If the players miss one clue, another should lead them forward.

3. **Complication (15 minutes).** Something unexpected happens. A rival faction intervenes. A trusted NPC betrays them. The target is not who they thought.

4. **Climax (30 to 45 minutes).** The confrontation. Could be combat, social encounter, or a race against time. The outcome determines the next phase of the campaign.

5. **Resolution (15 minutes).** The aftermath. Consequences. New leads. Recovery. Setup for the next session.

### Sample Adventure Seed: The Bell That Would Not Ring

**Hook.** The great bell in the Temple of the Silent Bell cracks during a ceremony. From the crack, a crimson liquid drips. The priests are terrified. They send for discreet help.

**Investigation.** The bell was forged from ore mined in a province that has since fallen to the Dusk. Someone recently applied a substance to the bell's inner surface that weakened the metal. The substance can be traced to a specific alchemist in the Middle City.

**Complication.** The alchemist is already dead, killed before the players can question him. His workshop has been searched. A note with a single word is found: "Archives."

**Climax.** The trail leads to the Imperial Archives. Someone is trying to access a record of the bell's original consecration ceremony, which may contain a secret that the Golden Orthodoxy has spent centuries burying.

**Resolution.** The players must decide: pursue the secret in the Archives, exposing themselves to investigation, or let it lie and allow the conspirator to escape?

## Appendix I: Glossary of Terms

**Advancement Points (AP).** Points awarded by the Host for completing objectives. Spent to improve your character.

**Archetype.** Your character's starting role, which determines baseline Facet scores, starting Skills, and equipment.

**Celestial Favor.** A resource representing your influence within the Court. Spent to call in favors or survive investigations.

**Celestial Qi.** The energy that flows through all things. Used to power Techniques.

**Character Dossier.** The printed sheet used to record your character's information. One is needed for each player.

**Crimson Dusk.** The corruption spreading inward from the empire's borders. The campaign's central threat.

**Critical Failure.** A natural roll of 1 on the d20. The action fails with a complication.

**Critical Success.** A natural roll of 20 on the d20. The action succeeds with additional benefit.

**Facet.** One of six core attributes (Might, Swiftness, Endurance, Intellect, Presence, Resolve) that define your character's capabilities.

**Facet Modifier.** A bonus or penalty derived from your Facet score, calculated as (score minus 10) divided by 2, rounded down.

**Harmonization Audit.** A periodic campaign event where the Commission reviews the orthodoxy of Court members.

**Hit Points (HP).** Your capacity to withstand physical harm. When HP reaches 0, you are dying.

**Host.** The player who describes the world, portrays NPCs, and adjudicates the rules.

**Qi Pool.** Your reserve of Celestial Qi for using Techniques.

**Resolution Roll.** The core mechanic: d20 + Facet Modifier + Proficiency vs Target Number.

**Ritual Harmony.** A score measuring how orthodox you are perceived to be within the Celestial Court.

**Sanity.** Your mental and spiritual stability. Damage from horror, betrayal, and the Dusk.

**School of Thought.** One of six competing factions within the Celestial Court.

**Skill.** A specific area of training tied to a Facet. Proficiency grants a bonus on related rolls.

**Social Standing.** A resource in social conflicts. When it reaches 0, you lose the argument.

**Stance.** An NPC's attitude toward the players, ranging from Hostile to Allied.

**Target Number (TN).** The number you must meet or exceed on a Resolution Roll to succeed.

**Technique.** A special ability powered by Celestial Qi.

**Truth.** A secret your character carries that complicates their relationship with the Court.

**Zone.** An abstract area used in combat. Movement between Zones replaces positioning on a grid.

## Appendix J: Advanced Social Tactics

### Reading the Room

Before making a social action, a player can use a Probe to read the room. This reveals the general mood of the group the player is addressing. A successful Insight check against TN 12 reveals:

- Whether the audience is receptive, neutral, or hostile.
- Who in the room holds the most influence.
- What emotional hot buttons to press.

### Social Maneuvers

Beyond the basic social actions, characters can attempt specialized maneuvers:

**Appeal to Authority.** Name drop a respected figure or institution to bolster your argument. Roll Presence + Connections against TN 12. On success, gain Advantage on your next Persuade action. On failure, the target sees you as name dropping and the TN for your next action increases by 2.

**Turn the Tables.** When accused of something, redirect the accusation toward the accuser. Roll Presence + Deception against the accuser's Insight. On success, the accuser must defend themselves for one exchange, giving you breathing room. On failure, you appear desperate and your Social Standing takes 1d4 damage.

**Appeal to Emotion.** Make an argument based on shared feeling rather than logic. Roll Presence + Performance against the target's Resolve + Discipline. On success, the target's Stance shifts one step toward you. This maneuver can be used even if the conversation has already shifted Stance twice, but it can only be used once per encounter.

### Public vs Private

Social actions work differently in public and private settings.

**Private.** In a private conversation, shifts in Stance are genuine. The target walks away believing what you convinced them of. Consequences are personal.

**Public.** In a crowd or court setting, shifts in Stance are performative. The target may be convinced publicly but remain skeptical privately. Public arguments also affect the audience's perception. A player who wins a public debate gains +2 to their next social action with anyone who witnessed the debate.

### Social Recovery

When a character's Social Standing reaches 0, they are not permanently damaged. They can recover through:

- A strategic retreat from the conversation (regain 1d4 Social Standing).
- A successful Probe that reveals useful information (regain 1d4 Social Standing).
- An ally intervening on their behalf (regain 2d4 Social Standing).
- A dramatic revelation that changes the terms of the debate (regain all Social Standing).

## Appendix L: Conditions and States

**Bleeding.** Takes 1d4 damage at the start of each turn. Can be stopped with a successful Medical Kit use (Intellect TN 10) or any healing technique.

**Dazed.** Cannot take Actions. Can still move and speak. Ends at the start of your next turn.

**Dusk Touched.** The character has been altered by exposure to the Crimson Dusk. Effects vary. Common manifestations: strange abilities, physical mutations, occasional loss of control, a persistent whisper in the mind.

**Frightened.** Disadvantage on all rolls while the source of fear is present. Cannot move toward the source. Can be overcome with a Resolve + Discipline check (TN 15).

**Grappled.** Cannot move. Disadvantage on attacks. Can escape with a Might + Athletics or Swiftness + Acrobatics check against the grappler's relevant check result.

**Hidden.** Cannot be targeted by attacks unless the attacker first succeeds on a Resolve + Vigilance check against the hidden character's Stealth total.

**Poisoned.** Disadvantage on all rolls. Duration depends on the poison. A successful Endurance + Fortitude check (TN varies by poison) halves the duration.

**Prone.** Attack rolls against you have Advantage when made from an adjacent Zone. You have Disadvantage on melee attacks. Standing costs your Movement.

**Restrained.** Cannot move. Attacks have Disadvantage. Attackers have Advantage against you. Can escape with a Might + Athletics check (TN varies by restraint).

**Stable.** At 0 HP but no longer worsening. Unconscious. Will regain consciousness at 1 HP after a full rest or healing.

**Unconscious.** Cannot act. Vulnerable to attacks (attackers have Advantage). Will wake after 1d4 hours naturally or when healed above 0 HP.

## Appendix M: The Calendar of the Celestial Empire

Timekeeping in the empire follows an ancient calendar divided into Seasons and Festivals.

### The Three Seasons

**Season of Ashes (winter).** Cold, wet, and grey. The season of endings. Most Audits occur during this season. The poor die in the cold. The rich burn incense and stay indoors.

**Season of Bloom (spring).** Brief and precarious. The snow melts. The first flowers appear. The Court resumes full activity after the winter slowdown. It is considered unlucky to start a major undertaking during Bloom.

**Season of Embers (summer).** Long, hot, and dry. The season of action. Campaigns are launched. Debates are held. Alliances are made and broken when the heat makes everyone irritable.

**Season of Falling (autumn).** The harvest season. The season of reflection. Festivals celebrate the empire's founding. The year's accounts are settled. The Harmonization Audits begin.

### Major Festivals

**The Festival of a Thousand Lanterns (Season of Falling, full moon).** The empire's founding is celebrated with lantern releases. Every window displays at least one lantern. The sky glows orange. It is tradition to forgive one debt and make one promise on this night.

**The Day of Silence (Season of Ashes, winter solstice).** A day of mourning for the Emperor's silence. No work is done. No music is played. The bells do not ring. It is considered deeply unlucky to speak the Emperor's name on this day.

**The Rite of Renewal (Season of Bloom, first new moon).** A day of purification. Officials bathe in the river (or, for the wealthy, in heated pools). Old grievances are publicly forgiven. The Commission for Celestial Purity conducts mass Audits, believing the Rite makes subjects more likely to confess.

## Appendix O: Session Zero Guide

A Session Zero is a meeting before the campaign begins where players and Host agree on expectations, themes, and boundaries. It is the most important session you will run.

### What to Cover in Session Zero

**Tone and Themes.** The Violet Court Fragments is a dark fantasy game. It deals with oppression, corruption, moral compromise, and horror. Discuss what level of darkness your group is comfortable with. Some groups want full grimdark. Others want lighter moments. Both are valid. Agree on the balance.

**Player Boundaries.** Ask each player privately if there are topics they do not want to appear in the game. Common boundaries include graphic violence against children, sexual violence, and detailed torture. Respect these boundaries without question. A player should never feel pressured to describe something that makes them uncomfortable.

**Character Coordination.** Players should create characters who have reasons to work together. A party of characters who distrust each other can be dramatic. A party of characters who actively sabotage each other is not fun for anyone. Encourage players to build connections between their characters before the game starts.

**Scheduling.** Agree on session frequency, duration, and what happens when someone cannot make a session. A consistent schedule is more important than any rule in this book.

**House Rules.** Review any optional rules from Appendix F and agree on which ones to use. Discuss any additional house rules the Host wants to implement.

## Appendix P: Factions of the Celestial Court

The six Schools of Thought compete for influence within the Court. Understanding them is essential to navigating the game's politics.

### The Golden Orthodoxy

**Symbol.** A sunburst with seven rays, each ray representing a pillar of orthodox governance.

**Philosophy.** The empire must be governed according to ancient tradition. Change is corruption. Innovation is dangerous. The Golden Orthodoxy believes the Emperor's silence is a test of faith. Those who wait patiently will be rewarded. Those who seek answers are heretics.

**Leader.** Cinnabar Heart, Director of the Bureau of Harmonious Narrative.

**Allies.** The Commission for Celestial Purity (Bright Mirror). The Imperial Garrison Command (Iron Wall), though the relationship is strained by the Garrison's practical concerns.

**Enemies.** The Crimson Lineage (seen as decadent). The Common Flame (seen as subversive). The Verdant Path (seen as dangerously foreign).

**Influence.** Strongest in the Bureau of Harmonious Narrative and the religious institutions of the capital.

### The Verdant Path

**Symbol.** A tree whose roots and branches form a circle.

**Philosophy.** The empire is part of the natural world, not separate from it. The Crimson Dusk is a wound in reality caused by the empire's disconnection from nature. Healing the empire means healing the land first. Politics is a distraction.

**Leader.** Guest Among Forests, Representative of the Northern Expanse and Voice of the Verdant Path.

**Allies.** No permanent allies. The Verdant Path cooperates with anyone who advances ecological healing.

**Enemies.** The Iron Calculation (exploits natural resources without regard for consequences).

**Influence.** Weak in the capital. Strong in the northern provinces and among rural communities.

### The Iron Calculation

**Symbol.** A gear with visible inner mechanisms.

**Philosophy.** The empire is a machine. Every part must function efficiently. Sentiment, tradition, and bloodlines are irrelevant. Only results matter. The Emperor's silence is a management problem. If the machine is maintained, it can run without an Emperor indefinitely.

**Leader.** Iron Calculation, Director of the State Planning Commission.

**Allies.** The Crimson Lineage (pragmatic partnership). Any faction that can provide resources or access.

**Enemies.** The Golden Orthodoxy (sees tradition as inefficient). The Verdant Path (sees their ecological concerns as obstacles to progress).

**Influence.** Dominant in the industrial and economic sectors. Controls the flow of resources through the empire.

### The Crimson Lineage

**Symbol.** A perfect circle pierced by seven lines, representing the seven noble bloodlines.

**Philosophy.** The empire was founded by great families whose blood carries the empire's strength. Governance is a matter of breeding and inheritance. Commoners cannot rule. The Emperor's silence is a tragedy that must be resolved by finding or creating a worthy heir.

**Leader.** The Duke of Eternal Night, a Grand Council Elder and patriarch of the Crimson Lineage.

**Allies.** The Iron Calculation school (they fund each other's projects). Several independent noble houses.

**Enemies.** The Golden Orthodoxy (competing for religious authority). The Common Flame (directly threatens their class privilege).

**Influence.** Dominant in the nobility, the luxury trade, and the informal networks of power that operate outside official channels.

### The Common Flame

**Symbol.** A flame with a human eye at its center.

**Philosophy.** The empire belongs to all its people, not just the nobility and the orthodox. The Emperor's silence is an opportunity to reform the empire into something better. The Crimson Dusk is a natural consequence of the empire's sins against the common people.

**Leader.** Iron Bone, organizer of the Red Banner Revival movement.

**Allies.** Informal networks of reformers, disaffected officials, and provincial leaders who feel ignored by the capital.

**Enemies.** The Golden Orthodoxy (represents everything wrong with the old system). The Crimson Lineage (represents class oppression).

**Influence.** Weak in official institutions. Strong in the Outer City, the provinces, and among the disenfranchised.

### The Bright Mirror

**Symbol.** A mirror that reflects only the viewer's eyes.

**Philosophy.** Purity must be enforced. The empire's laws exist to maintain cosmic order. Those who break the laws threaten the stability of reality itself. The Emperor's silence is likely the result of insufficient purity among His subjects.

**Leader.** Bright Mirror, High Justicar of the Commission for Celestial Purity.

**Allies.** The Golden Orthodoxy (shared interest in enforcing orthodoxy).

**Enemies.** The Common Flame (by definition). Anyone who questions the Commission's authority.

**Influence.** The Commission has the power to investigate, arrest, and punish anyone in the empire. Their authority is broad. Their reach is limited by the number of agents and the political cost of targeting powerful figures.

## Appendix R: Advanced Combat Tactics

### Environmental Advantages

Combat is not a white room. The environment matters. Encourage players and enemies to use the terrain:

**High ground.** Attacking from a higher Zone grants Advantage on ranged attacks. Climbing to high ground costs a Movement action.

**Cover.** Objects in a Zone provide cover. Attacking a target behind cover imposes Disadvantage. Partial cover (a low wall, a table tipped on its side) grants +2 Defense. Full cover (a pillar, a door frame) grants +4 Defense.

**Hazards.** Fire, toxic fog, collapsing structures, and areas saturated with Dusk can be used tactically. A character can use their Action to trigger a hazard in an adjacent Zone. This requires a relevant check (Might + Athletics to collapse a pillar, Intellect + Lore to activate a ritual ward).

**Darkness and light.** In dim light, attacks at range have Disadvantage. In total darkness, melee attacks have Disadvantage and ranged attacks are impossible. A lantern illuminates the user's Zone and adjacent Zones.

### Teamwork Actions

Players can combine their actions for greater effect:

**Coordinated Strike.** Two or more players attack the same target on the same round. The first successful attack deals normal damage. Each additional successful attack deals an extra +2 damage as the target is overwhelmed.

**Intervention.** If an ally is about to take damage, another character in the same Zone can use their Reaction to take the damage instead. This requires the character to have not yet acted this round and to be aware of the attack.

**Distraction.** A character uses their Action to create a distraction (a thrown object, a shouted insult, a sudden movement). The next ally to make an attack or Stealth check gains Advantage.

### Retreat and Pursuit

Not every fight needs to end in death. Characters can retreat from combat:

**Withdrawal.** A character can use their Action and Movement to flee a combat. The Host determines whether pursuers can catch them. A successful Swiftness check against TN 12 allows escape. Failure means the character is caught and cannot act next round.

**Covering retreat.** One character holds their position while allies flee. The covering character gains +2 Defense and may make attacks of opportunity against any pursuer. Once all allies have fled, the covering character may attempt to withdraw.

## Appendix T: Qi Phenomena and Environmental Effects

The Celestial Qi that flows through all things can be manipulated by skilled practitioners. In some locations, Qi behaves unpredictably.

### Environments Rich in Qi

In certain places, Qi is abundant and active:

**Confluence points.** Where ley lines of Qi cross. Techniques cost 1 less Qi (minimum 1) when used at a confluence point. Confluence points are often marked by ancient shrines or unusual vegetation.

**The Imperial Archives.** Centuries of concentrated knowledge have saturated the air. Qi Sense functions at double its normal range. Divination Techniques are more vivid but more dangerous: a failed Divination check deals 2d4 Sanity damage instead of 1d4.

**Battlefields.** The violent death of many people leaves Qi scars. Combat Techniques are more powerful (add +1 damage per die) but using them risks awakening vengeful echoes.

### Qi Dead Zones

Some areas are drained of Qi or actively suppress it:

**Commission Interrogation Rooms.** Wards built into the walls suppress Qi. Characters in these rooms cannot use Techniques. Qi Pools are inaccessible.

**The Dusk Frontier.** As the Crimson Dusk spreads, Qi becomes erratic. Techniques have a fifty percent chance of costing double their listed Qi cost. Qi Disharmony checks are made with Disadvantage.

**Ancient Silence Zones.** Rare locations where Qi has never flowed. Characters who enter these zones feel a profound emptiness. Techniques cannot be used. Every hour spent in a silence zone costs 1d4 Sanity as the character senses the absence of something fundamental.

### Weather and Qi

Weather conditions affect Qi manipulation:

**Thunderstorms.** Qi is agitated. Combat Techniques deal +1 damage. Healing Techniques heal for 1 less point.

**Fog.** Qi is muffled. Divination Techniques have Disadvantage. Stealth Techniques gain Advantage.

**Clear Night (stars visible).** Qi is calm and balanced. All Techniques function normally. Meditation during a clear night restores an additional 2 Qi.

**Rain.** Qi is washed clean. Purification Techniques (removing Dusk corruption, curing poison) gain Advantage. Fire aligned effects are weakened.

## Appendix V: Running Investigations

Many Violet Court scenarios involve investigation. This appendix provides a simple framework for running investigative scenes.

### The Clue Structure

Every investigation needs at least three clues that point toward the truth. The players should never be blocked by a missed clue. If they miss one, another should lead them forward.

**The Obvious Clue.** Found without a roll or with a very low TN (5 to 8). This clue confirms that something happened and gives a direction to investigate further. A broken lock. A witness who saw someone flee. A document left on a desk.

**The Hidden Clue.** Requires a successful Investigation check (TN 12 to 16). This clue narrows the suspects or reveals the method. A partial footprint. A coded message. A discrepancy in official records.

**The Critical Clue.** Reveals the truth or the identity of the responsible party. This clue should always be discoverable if the players follow the trail. It might be behind a higher TN (16 to 20) or protected by an NPC who needs to be convinced. A confession. A direct witness. A document with a signature.

### Leading Without Railroading

If the players are stuck, do not let the investigation stall. Use these techniques:

**An NPC volunteers information.** A witness who was too scared to speak before changes their mind. A faction member offers a tip in exchange for a future favor.

**A new event occurs.** A second incident happens. It might be connected or a distraction. Either way, it gives the players something to investigate.

**An antagonist makes a mistake.** The person behind the conspiracy grows overconfident and leaves a trace. A message is intercepted. A meeting is witnessed.

**The Host states the obvious.** Sometimes players miss something that would be obvious to their characters. If the players are staring at a clue and not connecting it, the Host can say "Your character would realize that this handwriting matches the document you found earlier."

## Appendix W: The Tone and Voice of the Empire

### Common Phrases

Use these phrases to add flavor to NPC dialogue:

**"The Emperor sleeps. We keep the vigil."** A reminder of duty. Used to end arguments or dismiss complaints.

**"Seven gates stand between us and truth."** A proverb meaning that the truth is always mediated by layers of interpretation and access.

**"A tree with deep roots does not fear the wind."** An expression meaning that a person with solid foundations is not shaken by challenges.

**"The river does not ask permission."** Used to describe inevitable change or the limits of human control over larger forces.

**"One candle cannot light a thousand rooms."** Acknowledging the limits of individual effort. Often said to justify inaction or to dismiss a request for help.

**"The bell knows when to ring."** An expression meaning that everything happens in its proper time. Patience.

### Addressing Authority

The Celestial Court has a complex system of address:

- "Honored" for officials of equal or slightly higher rank.
- "Radiant" for senior officials of the Golden Orthodoxy.
- "Revered" for members of the Commission for Celestial Purity.
- "Elder" for beings with exceptionally long lives and senior faction leaders.
- "Cousin" among nobles of equal standing.
- "Friend" among members of the same faction.
- No title at all for those of lower rank, unless one wishes to be insulting.

### Social Signals

Certain gestures and customs carry meaning in the Court:

**The double bow.** A bow performed twice, once upon arriving and once upon leaving. The depth of the bow indicates respect. A shallow bow to an equal is expected. A deep bow to a superior is required. A deep bow to an equal is either a profound apology or a mocking insult.

**The offered cup.** When an official offers tea, accepting means willingness to hear their proposal. Refusing means the conversation is over before it begins. The tea may be drunk or merely held. Drinking means agreement. Holding means consideration.

**The open hand.** Showing an open hand during conversation indicates honesty. It is a gesture that is performed deliberately. If someone shows their open hand at the wrong moment, it is a tell that they are performing honesty rather than feeling it.

**The silence.** In Court proceedings, silence after a question means the question was improper. The questioner should withdraw the question or face social consequences.

---

**The gift.** Presenting a small gift before making a request is standard practice. The gift should be appropriate to the recipient's station. An inappropriate gift is either an insult or a sign that the giver does not understand the Court. Gifts of jade, tea, or quality paper are always acceptable.

**The refused gift.** If an official refuses a gift, the request is denied. The giver should withdraw immediately and not press the matter.
